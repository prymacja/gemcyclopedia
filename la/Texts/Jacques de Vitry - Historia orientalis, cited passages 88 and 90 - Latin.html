Jacques de Vitry — Historia Orientalis, cited passages

Edition and scope. The base witness is Franciscus Moschus, ed., Iacobi de Vitriaco Libri duo, quorum prior Orientalis sive Hierosolymitanae, alter Occidentalis historia nomine inscribitur (Duaci: Balthazaris Belleri, 1597), a public-domain print edition. The Latin passages below are transcribed from its scan; common u/v and i/j variation is regularized, and punctuation is editorial. OCR was used as a transcription aid and corrected against the printed page images.

Chapter-number crosswalk. These passages are cited on Bestiary.ca using Jean Donnadieu's 2008 chapter numbering: Chapter LXXXVIII, the lynx-stone passage, and Chapter XC, the pearl and oyster passage. In Moschus's 1597 edition, the matching material appears in the broader animal chapters XVIII (De feris...) and XX (De avibus quibusdam et piscibus raris). Only the complete relevant passages are included here; the intervening animal material from those broader chapters is outside this excerpt's scope.

Digital sources: 1597 scan PDF, Internet Archive; Internet Archive item record and scan viewer; Gallica scan record; Donnadieu/Bestiary.ca Latin reference transcription, Chapters 88 and 90. The cited printed matter is on pp. 177–183 of the 1597 edition; the two passages are on pp. 179–180 and 183 respectively.

Chapter LXXXVIII — De feris: lynx and lyncurium

Sunt ibi lynces, quae ita perspicaces habent oculos, quod solida corpora subtilitate visus penetrare dicuntur. Qui autem huius animalis naturam plenius cognoverunt, eius urinam in lapidis pretiosi naturam, qui lyncurius appellatur, converti dicunt. Unde invidia quadam naturali liquorem emissum arenis, in quantum possunt, abscondunt, ne in usus humanos talis urinae egestio convertatur.

Chapter XC — De avibus quibusdam et piscibus raris: pearls and oysters

In quibusdam conchis marinis lapides pretiosi inveniuntur; dicunt enim quod nocturno tempore littus adeunt et rore caelesti margarita concipiunt. Decrescente luna vacuantur, crescente iterum implentur. Ostrea autem species sunt concharum seu cochlearum, quarum carnibus cancri miro ingenio vivunt. Quia enim testas earum aperire non valent, explorant in insidiis quando claustra aperiunt, et latenter iniicientes lapillum, impedita conclusione, carnes earum corrodunt.

Transcription note: The OCR's common long-s misrecognitions were normalized. The original 1597 print's spelling inuen[...] is rendered inveniuntur; punctuation, word division, and capitalization are editorial. No lines from the 2008 critical text are presented as a full-chapter transcription.
