Pliny — Naturalis Historia, lynx-stone passage

Citation note: Gemcyclopedia's source citation follows Bostock and Riley's chapter numbering as Natural History VIII.57. In Mayhoff's Teubner edition, the relevant lynx-stone passage is in Book VIII, chapter 38, §137. The following is the complete paragraph on the passage's cited claim, not the adjacent section about the leontophonus.

Text source: Karl Mayhoff, ed., C. Plini Secundi Naturalis historiae libri XXXVII, vol. II (Leipzig: Teubner, 1906), encoded by the Perseus Digital Library: Book VIII, chapter 38, §137 in the Perseus XML; Perseus license and repository notes. This text is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0; modifications must be offered to Perseus under the same terms.

Textus Latinus

Lyncum umor ita redditus, ubi gignuntur, glaciatur arescitve in gemmas carbunculis similes et igneo colore fulgentes, lyncurium vocatas, atque ob id sucino a plerisque ita generari prodito. Novere hoc sciuntque lynces et invidentes urinam terra operiunt, eoque celerius solidatur illa.

Textual note: The Perseus markup supplies Mayhoff's reading and apparatus. The encoding has editorial punctuation and omits apparatus from this excerpt; lyncurium and the passage wording are retained. Novere is Mayhoff's spelling.
