Speculum lapidum — cited Latin entries

Edition and method. Transcribed from the 1502 Venice first-edition scan digitized by the U.S. Geological Survey Library and hosted by the Internet Archive. Source Library's OCR is used as the transcription aid; the wording below expands common abbreviations and adds modern punctuation, but has not been fully checked against a page image. The source scan is public domain. This OCR-derived excerpt is attributed to Source Library and licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

Egyptilla (Aegyptilla)

Reference: Source Library scan page 66; Internet Archive scan page. The printed folio number is not confidently legible in the OCR.

Egyptilla niger lapis est, in superficie colorem ceruleum cum venis aureis habet. A loco inventionis nomen sumpsit. Croceum colorem vinique saporem, si in aqua teratur, reddit.

OCR note: the print/OCR reads Egyptilla; the article title normalizes it to Aegyptilla. Punctuation is editorial. The OCR's croceum colorem viniqͻ saporem is expanded as croceum colorem vinique saporem; do not silently paraphrase this as “the color and taste of wine.”

Abistos (cited for Absinthus)

Reference: Source Library scan page 51, printed folio XXIIII; Internet Archive scan page.

Abistos nigri coloris lapis est, rubentibus ac niveis venis lineatus. Ab igne calefactus per octo dies calorem in se retinet, gravis ac ponderosus existens, plus quam sibi videtur sua quantitas ministrare.

Name distinction: the entry itself is Abistos. A separate color index on scan page 41 lists Absinthes under “Niger cum punctis rubeis” (black with red spots), and Abistos under “Niger cum venis rubentibus seu albis” (black with reddish or white veins). The Absinthus gem page's eight-day heat and veining description corresponds to the Abistos entry; the index alone does not establish that they are the same stone.
