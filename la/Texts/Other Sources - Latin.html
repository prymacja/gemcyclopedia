Other Sources — Latin passages

This page collects the Latin source texts used by the composite English and Polish source pages, including Bestiary.md, Pearl - Other Sources.md, and the Other-sheet source excerpts. The links open the corresponding transcription pages. Each page identifies the edition or manuscript, gives the digital source link, and states its precise scope and any unresolved reading or licensing note. Entries explicitly marked partial, excerpted, or uncollated do not yet represent a complete Latin counterpart to the full English or Polish source page.

Stones and bestiary passages

  • Albertus Magnus — De mineralibus, Liber II, Tractatus II: complete tractate II, chapters I–XX; this is the Latin section corresponding to the cited gemstone entries, not the full twenty-book De mineralibus.
  • Augustine — De civitate Dei XXI.4: complete chapter XXI.4, including the lime and magnet passages.
  • Aelian — De natura animalium IV.17 and XV.8: complete cited sections in Friedrich Jacobs's 1832 Latin translation from the Greek.
  • Ambrose — Hexaemeron V.33: complete cited section on sea salt, coral, pearls, and purple dye.
  • Aberdeen Bestiary — fire stones and lodestone: complete cited witness passages from fols. 94r and 102v.
  • Physiologus Latinus, Version Y — fire stones and the Indian stone: both complete cited chapters, directly transcribed from the BSB manuscript witness.
  • Isidore — Etymologiae XVI: complete Book XVI, including the diamond passage.
  • Isidore — Etymologiae XII.6.49, 51–53: the pearl and shellfish sections cited; §50 is explicitly excluded.
  • Ovid — Metamorphoses XV.391–417: complete cited lines on lyncurium.
  • Pliny — Naturalis Historia VIII.57, lynx-stone passage: complete relevant paragraph, cited as VIII.57 in Bostock–Riley and VIII.38 §137 in Mayhoff.
  • Pliny — Naturalis Historia IX, pearls: complete Mayhoff IX.35 §§105–124, including the pearl passages and Cleopatra anecdote; the English source sheet's IX.54–58 numbering follows a different division.
  • Gaius Julius Solinus — Collectanea rerum memorabilium: complete prefatory matter and chapters 1–56, including the diamond, lynx-stone, and pearl passages.
  • Jacques de Vitry — Historia Orientalis, lynx and pearl/oyster passages: complete cited passages from a public-domain 1597 edition, with chapter-number crosswalk.
  • Thomas of Cantimpré — Liber de natura rerum: Book XIV reading text and Book VII pearl excerpts; scope and missing material are disclosed on the page.
  • Bartholomaeus Anglicus — De proprietatibus rerum XIII.26 and XVIII.68: complete cited chapters, with the XIII.26 OCR status identified.

Pearl and other sheet sources

  • Camillo Leonardi — Speculum lapidum, cited entries: Egyptilla and the Abistos entry cited for the Absinthus record; not a complete book transcription.
  • Epiphanius — Liber de XII gemmis, Latin version: the complete surviving Latin witness; the lost Greek original and the mutilated ending are noted.
  • Hildegard — Physica, Book IV, stones: complete preface and stone chapters I–XXVI.
  • Marbodus — Liber lapidum: complete digital transcription of the prologue, 60 lapidary chapters, and De annulo et gemma.
  • Thomas Aquinas — De operationibus occultis naturae: complete short treatise; its edition and transcription provenance are stated on the page.

The medieval French Bestiaire passages by Guillaume le Clerc are not included because they were written in Old French rather than Latin.
