Love, affection, and marital concord

Aetites

Aetites makes its bearer amiable, sober, and rich, shields him from adversity, and grants affection and victory.

See Aetites for the full entry and source citations.

Agapis

It derives its name from [the Greek] agape since it brings delight [love], and is thus loved by men for its virtue..

See Agapis for the full entry and source citations.

Alectorius

It makes friends agree, pleases a wife, and increases sexual desire. A bride may use it to bind her husband’s affection.

See Alectorius for the full entry and source citations.

Beryl

Carried, held, and looked upon frequently, it keeps its bearer tranquil and less given to quarrels.

Beryl makes husband and wife agree and strengthens their conjugal love.

When a person has eaten or drunk poison, grate a moderate amount of beryl into spring water or other water.

When drunk, it dispels sighs and heals liver disorders.

When touched, it reconciles enemies and brings friendship to those at odds.

Crayfish — Engraved on beryl, it pacifies disputes and preserves love in marriage.

See Beryl for the full entry and source citations.

Crystal

Woman with hair spread over her breasts and a man approaching her in a loving gesture — Engraved on Hyacinth or crystal, set in gold, with Amber, aloe, and polium beneath it.

Virgin, young woman, or torch — Engraved on crystal.

See Crystal for the full entry and source citations.

Galactite

Mixed with mead, it is drunk by a nursing mother just after rising from the bath and before eating.

To increase milk in ewes and protect them from disease, crush Galactite with salt and mix it with lustral water.

Its effects also include forgetting past quarrels and troubles. When ground three times in water, dried, and drunk with clear water, it reconciles those at odds and joins them in a lasting love.

Carried on the person, Galactite immediately appeases a king’s anger and restores his favor to one who has offended him.

See Galactite for the full entry and source citations.

Hyacinth

Woman with loose hair and a man approaching in a gesture of love: carved on Hyacinth or crystal, set in gold with amber, aloe, and polium beneath it, it makes the wearer obeyed. Touching a woman with it makes her obey, and placing it beneath the pillow brings desired dreams.

Placed beneath the head, it brings desired dreams.

See Hyacinth for the full entry and source citations.

Kabrates

Jupiter — A figure of Jupiter, commonly seated with a baton and globe, or carved as a man with a ram’s head, when engraved on Kabrates, makes its bearer fortunate and amiable, helps him obtain what he desires—especially from clergy—and raises him to dignity and honor.

See Kabrates for the full entry and source citations.

Magnet

When worn, the magnet reconciles wives and husbands, removes fears and suspicions, and makes a person gracious, persuasive, and elegant in speech.

Hidden beneath a sleeping wife’s head, it makes a chaste wife embrace her husband, while an unchaste wife is thrown from the bed in terror.

See Magnet for the full entry and source citations.

Silenite

Silenite reconciles love and accord; Silonites reconciles those who hate one another.

See Silenite for the full entry and source citations.

Chastity and restraint

Amber

Worn, it makes its bearer chaste.

Placed upon a wife's left breast while she sleeps, amber is said to make her confess her misdeeds.

See Amber for the full entry and source citations.

Chalcedony

All kinds curb lust and protect against tempests and similar adversity.

Worn against bare skin over a vein, Chalcedony turns away infirmities, strengthens the blood, and gives a mind strong against wrath.

See Chalcedony for the full entry and source citations.

Chalcophonos

Worn chastely, it gives a melodious voice and protects strained throats from hoarseness.

See Chalcophonos for the full entry and source citations.

Emerald

The emerald inclines its wearer to chastity and checks lascivious desire. A virgin deflowered while wearing it likewise causes it to break. It tempers anger and lust, and makes the wearer gracious to God and men.

See Emerald for the full entry and source citations.

Gelosia

Gelosia moderates anger, licentiousness, and other hot passions and desires.

See Gelosia for the full entry and source citations.

Jasper

Restrains lust. And guards against licentiousness.

Virgin with laurel branch.

Armed Mars or Virgin in toga.

See Jasper for the full entry and source citations.

Jet

For testing virginity, jet is steeped in water for three days, and the draught is given to the woman. The drink is said to remain in the body of a virgin, while a woman who is not a virgin immediately urinates.

See Jet for the full entry and source citations.

Pearl

Macerated in milk and drunk, they heal deadly ulcers and clarify the voice. Wearing them is said to make a person chaste and to strengthen and harmonize body and mind.

See Pearl for the full entry and source citations.

Sapphire

Rub it upon the tongue while fasting to improve understanding, provided the bearer intends no malice.

The sapphire makes a person chaste, cools lust, and quells wrath.

Milk serves in an eye remedy, and the wearer must be chaste. A sapphire in a ring quells wrath only when set in pure gold without tin.

To remove obsessive romantic passion, pour wine over the sapphire with the prescribed invocation and drink it for three or more days.

See Sapphire for the full entry and source citations.

Sardonyx

This also removes great wrath, stupidity, and illiteracy, and the devil flees its purity. To relieve lust, a man should place Sardonyx on his loins and a woman on her navel.

Some sources say it removes lustful inclinations and makes a person chaste, modest, amiable, and merry.

See Sardonyx for the full entry and source citations.

Turquoise

A bent-kneed man looking upward and holding a cloth, engraved on Turquoise. — Useful in buying and selling various things and in behaving chastely.

See Turquoise for the full entry and source citations.

Sobriety and resistance to intoxication

Amethyst

Amethyst checks drunkenness. Worn over the navel, it lessens the effect of wine and drives drunkenness away.

Water in which the stone has been washed makes barren women fruitful when drunk.

A bear drives demons away and protects the wearer from drunkenness.

See Amethyst for the full entry and source citations.

Dionysia

When rubbed with water or ground and mixed with water, Dionysia gives off a wine-like fragrance that wards off drunkenness.

See Dionysia for the full entry and source citations.

Punicus

Taken before drinking wine, it prevents drunkenness.

See Punicus for the full entry and source citations.

Anger, quarrels, and self-command

Androdamas

The silver-coloured Andromaia form is believed to restrain sudden passions and anger; related accounts say this form calms rage and mitigates lust.

See Androdamas for the full entry and source citations.

Balagius

It overcomes and represses troublesome and lustful thoughts, reconciles quarrels among friends, and brings good health. Ground and drunk with water, it heals illnesses of the eyes and aids disorders of the liver.

See Balagius for the full entry and source citations.

Carnelian

Worn around the neck or on a finger, it appeases quarrels and angry minds. Another account adds that it calms anger.

See Carnelian for the full entry and source citations.

Celidonius

They grant eloquence and persuasive power, soften the anger of rulers, and refresh weary sight.

See Celidonius for the full entry and source citations.

Chrysoprase

It aids sight, makes its bearer diligent in good works, banishes avarice, gladdens the heart, and removes strong passions.

Held at the throat until it warms, it quiets anger and prevents wrathful speech.

See Chrysoprase for the full entry and source citations.

Diamond

One preparation requires a goat that has long drunk wine with wild parsley or eaten mountain fenugreek.

Worn on the left arm, it gives victory and courage and guards against enemies, wild beasts, savage men, weapons, quarrels, phantoms, and night terrors.

Held in the mouth, it drives away malice, lying, and wrath, lessens hunger, and permits prolonged fasting. Left for a day in wine or water, it relieves gicht, palsy, apoplexy, or jaundice when the liquid is drunk.

See Diamond for the full entry and source citations.

Epistrites

Worn over the heart with the proper ligatures and spells, Epistrites keeps its bearer safe, restrains sedition, and wards off misfortune.

See Epistrites for the full entry and source citations.

Filaterius

Filaterius drives away terrors and melancholic passions.

See Filaterius for the full entry and source citations.

Hephaestite

It quenches sedition and arms its bearer against every danger, provided that it is worn over the heart.

See Hephaestite for the full entry and source citations.

Onyx

When worn around the neck or on a finger, Onyx brings sorrow and fear, frightful dreams, and quarrels or lawsuits.

See Onyx for the full entry and source citations.

Phengites

Specularis is quarried and split into sheets as thin as desired.

Carried in the left hand, red Fongites removes bodily aches and calms anger.

See Phengites for the full entry and source citations.

Ruby

It also drives away pestilential and poisonous air, quiets lust, preserves bodily health, banishes evil thoughts, reconciles friends who quarrel, and increases prosperity.

See Ruby for the full entry and source citations.

Topaz

Ground and drunk, it is useful for asthmatics.

It increases wealth and drives away anger and sorrow.

See Topaz for the full entry and source citations.

Sexual desire and attraction

Cimedia

Taken with water, it makes a man lusty during the day.

See Cimedia for the full entry and source citations.

Corvina

Ground and drunk, it has the same virtue.

See Corvina for the full entry and source citations.

Pavonius

The person whose sweat it is and who drinks the mixture is made to burn with love.

See Pavonius for the full entry and source citations.

Struxites

Hung around the neck, it aids digestion and stirs carnal desire.

See Struxites for the full entry and source citations.
