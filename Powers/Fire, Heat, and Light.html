Power

Fire, Heat, and Light

This page groups the explicit powers assigned to gemstones in their linked articles. Each gemstone section links back to its article, where the source passages and citations are retained.

Index

  • Heat and cold (8 gems)
  • Fire and ignition (12 gems)
  • Light and radiance (4 gems)

Heat and cold

Absinthus

When heated, Absinthus remains hot for seven days or more. Another account says it preserves heat for eight days.

See Absinthus for the full entry and source citations.

Apsyctos

When heated by fire, Apsyctos retains the warmth for nearly seven days.

See Apsyctos for the full entry and source citations.

Chalazia

Even when placed in fire, it retains its coldness.

See Chalazia for the full entry and source citations.

Drosolithus

When placed close to fire, Drosolithus emits something like sweat.

See Drosolithus for the full entry and source citations.

Gelasia

Gelasia never warms in fire and remains cold.

See Gelasia for the full entry and source citations.

Pumice

Pumice has a powerful cooling nature. Put in a vat of new wine, it stops the wine from bubbling.

See Pumice for the full entry and source citations.

Pyritis

Pyritis scorches the fingers when rubbed against them.

See Pyritis for the full entry and source citations.

Topaz

Topaz resists heat and poison.

Topaz stops boiling water from bubbling so that it soon becomes cool enough to touch.

See Topaz for the full entry and source citations.

Fire and ignition

Asbestos

Amatides makes cloth resist fire and shine in the flames.

See Asbestos for the full entry and source citations.

Chrysolite

Near a fire, Chrysolite catches flame readily; it also sends out fiery sparks and drives away serpents.

See Chrysolite for the full entry and source citations.

Crystal

A cold crystal placed in direct sunlight throws out fire.

See Crystal for the full entry and source citations.

Diamond

The diamond withstands fire and iron, scratches iron and other gems, and its splinters can cut gemstones, iron, and steel.

See Diamond for the full entry and source citations.

Effesstis

Set beneath the sun, it burns matter that can be kindled.

See Effesstis for the full entry and source citations.

Fire Stones

When one draws near the other, fire kindles between them and consumes both stones and everything around them.

See Fire Stones for the full entry and source citations.

Hephaestite

When placed in sunlight, it ignites dry material and emits fiery rays.

See Hephaestite for the full entry and source citations.

Isciscos

A garment woven from Isciscos resists burning and shines brightly in the flames.

See Isciscos for the full entry and source citations.

Pyrites

When struck, pyrites scatters sparks readily. Sparks from the common hearth kind can be caught on sulfur, fungi, or leaves, and they kindle a fire swiftly.

See Pyrites for the full entry and source citations.

Silenite

Silonites cannot be broken by fire.

See Silenite for the full entry and source citations.

Thunderstone

Lusitanian Thunderstone: Defies fire.

See Thunderstone for the full entry and source citations.

Zingutte

Held against a burning tree, Zingutte quenches its flame.

See Zingutte for the full entry and source citations.

Light and radiance

Astrion

Asterites holds a star-like light within and makes sunlight beams appear white; Astrion receives light when set beneath the stars.

See Astrion for the full entry and source citations.

Epistrites

In direct sunlight, Epistrites emits fire and fiery rays.

See Epistrites for the full entry and source citations.

Isciscos

In fire, Isciscos shines with a brightness sometimes likened to lightning.

See Isciscos for the full entry and source citations.

Ruby

Ruby’s light shines intermittently at night, and sparks lead the finder to the stone.

See Ruby for the full entry and source citations.
