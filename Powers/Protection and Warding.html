Protection and Warding

This page groups the explicit powers assigned to gemstones in their linked articles. Each gemstone section links back to its article, where the source passages and citations are retained.

Against poison, venom, and harmful creatures

Aetites

Worn on the left arm, Aetites strengthens pregnant women, prevents miscarriage, and lessens the dangers of childbirth. Placed on the thigh during labor, it removes danger and speeds the birth.

The stone repels poisonous animals and protects eagles’ eggs and eaglets. It tests suspected poison: when given to one about to eat poisoned food, it prevents him from swallowing. Another test says that a guilty poisoner chokes while the stone lies in the dish, but an innocent person eats freely.

Aetites makes its bearer amiable, sober, and rich, shields him from adversity, and grants affection and victory.

No wild beast harms him, and he is loved by all.

See Aetites for the full entry and source citations.

Amandinus

Amandinus counteracts or mitigates all poison and brings victory over adversaries.

See Amandinus for the full entry and source citations.

Amber

When burnt, it drives away serpents. Its smoke also drives away poisonous creatures and its vapors repel epilepsy.

Amber halts flux of the belly, remedies infirmities of the throat, repels poison, and holds loose teeth fast. Women and children wear amber necklaces to ward off throat ailments.

See Amber for the full entry and source citations.

Amethyst

Passing amethyst over the flesh heals a spider bite, and the stone repels serpents and adders.

Amethyst extinguishes poison, protects soldiers, and brings victory over the enemy. During a hunt it helps the bearer capture wild beasts and birds.

A bear drives demons away and protects the wearer from drunkenness.

See Amethyst for the full entry and source citations.

Asinus

Asinus resists all poisonous animals and heals snakebites. It also makes harmless poisoned water that has already been drunk, helps women in childbirth, and aids the expulsion of a fetus that died in the womb.

See Asinus for the full entry and source citations.

Bezoar

Taken inward in a dram, bezoar counteracts and eliminates any poison. It is esteemed as the first remedy against poison.

See Bezoar for the full entry and source citations.

Borax

Swallowed after poison has been drunk, Borax braces the intestines, extinguishes the poison’s force, and carries it out through the lower parts. For snakebites, fever, and ailments of the stomach and kidneys, drink water in which Borax has been placed.

See Borax for the full entry and source citations.

Chrysolite

Chrysolite drives away every kind of serpent.

See Chrysolite for the full entry and source citations.

Draconites

The stone is said to dispel poison, especially venom from snakes and other poisonous creatures. It also grants victory, courage, and invincibility. To obtain it, One account tells of cutting off a sleeping serpent’s head and extracting the stone while its body still quivers.

See Draconites for the full entry and source citations.

Emerald

When sudden pestilential attacks come, moisten it in the mouth and repeatedly touch it to the body.

The emerald counters poisonous bites, especially those of a rabid dog. Taken in powdered form, it serves against leprosy, kidney stones, and poison. It halts demonic illusions and tempests, and relieves tertian fever.

See Emerald for the full entry and source citations.

Falcones

Swallowed, it is poisonous to all animals.

See Falcones for the full entry and source citations.

Gerades

Gerades protects men from birds of prey.

See Gerades for the full entry and source citations.

Haematite

Dissolved in water, Haematite allays the pain of a poisonous serpent’s bite or an asp’s dart. Drunk with wine, it heals those bitten by serpents.

See Haematite for the full entry and source citations.

Isciscos

Isciscos withstands poisoners, especially magicians.

See Isciscos for the full entry and source citations.

Kenne

Kenne is good against poisons.

See Kenne for the full entry and source citations.

Magnet

The source also says that a magnet may attract a piece of iron from one side and repel it from another, that one kind is itself drawn by iron, and that another draws human flesh.

Placed over a wound made by poisoned iron, it heals it.

The magnet also has dangerous and deceptive uses. A dram mixed with snake fat and nettle juice, then given as a drink, drives a person from family, homeland, and home and makes a woman reveal adultery. Powder spread over hot coals in a house’s corners makes the smoke seem to bring the house down, so its sleeping inhabitants flee and thieves may rob the empty house.

This kind attracts iron on one side and repels it on the other.

The Ethiopian magnet rejects and repels all iron.

See Magnet for the full entry and source citations.

Nusae

When both kinds are shut together in the presence of poison, they burn the hand of anyone who touches them. In the presence of poison, the whitish kind takes on varied colors.

See Nusae for the full entry and source citations.

Ophites

When Ophites is hung by the neck, it heals headaches and serpent bites.

Subtype I: Soft White Ophites: Soft, white marble with serpentine spots.

See Ophites for the full entry and source citations.

Oristes

When Oristes is rubbed with rose oil, it preserves its bearer from misfortune and the harmful bites of reptiles.

See Oristes for the full entry and source citations.

Orites

Worn, it turns away fierce wild beasts.

Subtype II: Green with White Spots: When worn, averts danger and adverse accidents.

See Orites for the full entry and source citations.

Pyrophilus

Pyrophilus brings victory and protects its bearer from poison.

Returning from India, he set the girdle aside to bathe in the Euphrates, where a serpent bit off the gem, broke it, and spat it into the river.

See Pyrophilus for the full entry and source citations.

Ranius

Ranius resists poison, as does Bolus Armenus.

See Ranius for the full entry and source citations.

Ruby

The carbuncle’s chief virtue is to disperse poison in the air or vapor. It also drives away pestilential and poisonous air, quiets lust, preserves bodily health, banishes evil thoughts, reconciles friends who quarrel, and increases prosperity.

They flee from it.

See Ruby for the full entry and source citations.

Solis Gemma

Solis Gemma is held to counter deadly poisons that have been drunk.

See Solis Gemma for the full entry and source citations.

Talc Alchimicus

Talc Alchimicus is used through sublimation to make the worst poisons.

See Talc Alchimicus for the full entry and source citations.

Tephrias

When worn bound on the body, Tephrias protects against serpents.

See Tephrias for the full entry and source citations.

Ydrinus

It drives away poisonous worms and heals their bites.

See Ydrinus for the full entry and source citations.

Yrachite

A bearer of Yrachite is not stung by bees or bitten by flies; it is also believed to resist poison.

See Yrachite for the full entry and source citations.

Against violence, enemies, and treachery

Agate

All agates strengthen the wearer, give a healthy complexion, eloquence, grace, and favour, and help him overcome danger.

For venom from a spider or other vermin, heat the stone in the sun or on a hot tile and lay it upon the painful place. A linen cloth dipped in the water and laid over the bite draws out the poison and heals the wound.

To hinder thieves, carry a clearly visible agate through the length and breadth of the house in the form of a cross each night. Burning agate produces fumes that avert storms and halt the flow of rivers.

A man holding a viper repels its venom.

The constellation Serpentarium engraved on Sicilian agate doubles its power against poison.

The Sicilian kind also counters viper and scorpion venom.

Cretan black-and-yellow: Overcomes perils, strengthens the body, protects against adversity, and makes the wearer pleasing, persuasive, eloquent, and of good colour.

Indian blood-spotted: Preserves sight and counters thirst and poison.

Lion-coloured: Ground with water and applied to the place, wards off venom from viper and scorpion bites and like poisons.

Cretan red-and-gold: Counters swallowed poison and venomous snakebites.

It also repels venomous creatures, strengthens sight, grants victory and strength, and repels tempests and lightning.

Cretan coral-like: Sharpens sight, quenches thirst, counters poison, and gives grace, eloquence, preservation, and strength.

See Agate for the full entry and source citations.

Alectorius

Alectorius gives strength and victory in battle and makes its bearer invincible.

See Alectorius for the full entry and source citations.

Beloculus

Beloculus is said to make its bearer invincible in battle.

See Beloculus for the full entry and source citations.

Beryl

Beryl protects against peril from enemies, overcomes disputes, and grants victory. Carried, held, and looked upon frequently, it keeps its bearer tranquil and less given to quarrels.

When a person has eaten or drunk poison, grate a moderate amount of beryl into spring water or other water. The poison will pass through nausea or the bowels.

When drunk, it dispels sighs and heals liver disorders.

When touched, it reconciles enemies and brings friendship to those at odds.

See Beryl for the full entry and source citations.

Carnelian

Worn around the neck or on a finger, it appeases quarrels and angry minds.

See Carnelian for the full entry and source citations.

Gagatromaeus

Alcides braved every danger while protected by the gem and escaped unharmed, but after losing it the mighty victor soon fell.

See Gagatromaeus for the full entry and source citations.

Heliotrope

It checks the flow of blood, expels poisons, and protects against treachery.

See Heliotrope for the full entry and source citations.

Hyacinth

All kinds strengthen the body and heart, drive away grief and vain suspicions, and protect their bearer from perils abroad. They guard travelers from noxious air and contagious disease, defend against lightning and enemies, bring honor and wealth, and help secure a favorable hearing from princes.

Hyacinth quenches a blazing fire without suffering harm. If wrapped in linen and laid over sparks, the stone keeps the cloth from burning or even singeing, though the hand holding the wrapped stone feels painful heat. The stone also eases childbirth and drives away nightmares.

Worn at the neck or finger, it keeps travelers safe, wins the welcome of their hosts, and protects them in unhealthy regions. Saphirinus has a special power against poison.

See Hyacinth for the full entry and source citations.

Liparea

Hunted beasts run to the stone as their protector. So long as they remain in its presence, dogs and hunters cannot harm them.

See Liparea for the full entry and source citations.

Merochites

Merochites keeps children safe from harmful events.

See Merochites for the full entry and source citations.

Nitrum

A composite made by mixing three things in proportion is said to break, lead, drive away, and destroy whatever opposes it, but the ingredients are not named.

See Nitrum for the full entry and source citations.

Pantherus

For that whole day it protects against enemies and keeps them from prevailing.

See Pantherus for the full entry and source citations.

Sard

Worn on a finger, Sard restrains the harm of Onyx. It prevents terrible dreams, increases wealth and cheerfulness, restrains bleeding, and grants victory over enemies.

To avert pestilence, carry the stone.

See Sard for the full entry and source citations.

Against spirits, magic, and illusion

Antiphates

Antiphates is useful against enchantments.

See Antiphates for the full entry and source citations.

Chalcedony

Pierced with Emery (smyris) and hung around the neck, Chalcedony is said to dispel fanciful illusions arising from melancholy. All kinds curb lust and protect against tempests and similar adversity.

Its bearer becomes tranquil, difficult to provoke justly, and safe from unjust harm.

See Chalcedony for the full entry and source citations.

Chrysolite

Set in gold and worn on the left arm, Chrysolite drives away fiends and night terrors; pierced and threaded with donkey hairs, it drives away demons.

See Chrysolite for the full entry and source citations.

Chrysoprase

It aids sight, makes its bearer diligent in good works, banishes avarice, gladdens the heart, and removes strong passions.

Wherever the stone is, death-bearing poison loses its strength.

See Chrysoprase for the full entry and source citations.

Crystal

Hung over a sleeper, it dispels bad dreams and frees those who have been charmed.

Lion’s head, two-headed dragon with a small tail, or a man striking the beast with a baton — Engraved on crystal, set in brass, with musk and Amber beneath it. — Makes everyone obey the bearer and increases his wealth.

See Crystal for the full entry and source citations.

Daemonius

Daemonius is prescribed against fever and expels poison. It makes its bearer safe from enemies and victorious against them.

See Daemonius for the full entry and source citations.

Diacodos

When it is cast into water while the proper charm is spoken, various demons appear and answer questions.

See Diacodos for the full entry and source citations.

Diamond

The diamond repels poison, sorcery, enchantment, and evil arts. It reveals poisons and dispels vain fears, madness, frenzy, and demonic possession. Worn on the left arm, it gives victory and courage and guards against enemies, wild beasts, savage men, weapons, quarrels, phantoms, and night terrors.

Held in the mouth, it drives away malice, lying, and wrath, lessens hunger, and permits prolonged fasting.

Engraved on a diamond, the head of a long-bearded man with blood about his neck gives victory and courage and protects the body from bodily harm.

See Diamond for the full entry and source citations.

Emetrem

Emetrem is used to make charms that ward off the evil eye.

See Emetrem for the full entry and source citations.

Epistrites

Worn over the heart with the proper ligatures and spells, Epistrites keeps its bearer safe, restrains sedition, and wards off misfortune. It drives locusts, harmful birds, clouds, hailstorms, and tornadoes away from fertile land and crops.

See Epistrites for the full entry and source citations.

Iris

An Armed Man with bow and arrow engraved on Iris protects both its bearer and the place where it is kept from every evil.

See Iris for the full entry and source citations.

Isciscos

Isciscos withstands poisoners, especially magicians.

See Isciscos for the full entry and source citations.

Jasper

And guards against licentiousness. Incantations recited over it make the bearer pleasing, powerful, and safe.

Carrying Jasper disperses phantasms and threats that appear in dreams, especially thunder and lightning. A mother should keep it on her hand from childbirth through the child’s infancy to guard both from malign spirits. Where a serpent breathes, place Jasper: it weakens the breath and causes the serpent to stop breathing there.

Another account calls belief in Jasper’s protection of pregnant women superstition.

Worn at the neck, Jasper eases labor, guards against misfortune and night phantoms, and gains strength from blessing and silver.

Green warrior trampling serpent.

See Jasper for the full entry and source citations.

Jet

Its smoke is said to drive away serpents, demons, and spells. When burned, it also chases snakes.

Jet is used against dropsy, stomach and belly disorders, and melancholy’s phantasms. It tightens loose teeth, and when the stone is powdered and mixed with water it is used to fix them; One account says that wearing it also keeps them firm. Its odor or smoke provokes menstruation. When washed in water, or when its fumes are applied from below, it brings on menstruation; Another account ascribes this effect to its smell.

One account adds that it cures epilepsy and that jet mixed with a stag’s marrow heals a snakebite.

See Jet for the full entry and source citations.

Lignites

Hung around a child’s neck, Lignites protects the child from charms and spells of witches.

See Lignites for the full entry and source citations.

Malachite

Malachite protects its bearer from harm and guards the cradles of infants against mischance and witchcraft. It protects children from accidents and hurtful phantasms, so that they may grow up in prosperity.

See Malachite for the full entry and source citations.

Obtalius

When carried with a laurel leaf and the proper charms, it sharpens the wearer’s sight and darkens the sight of those around, until they are unable to see.

See Obtalius for the full entry and source citations.

Onyx

When worn around the neck or on a finger, Onyx brings sorrow and fear, frightful dreams, and quarrels or lawsuits.

A smooth, polished stone may be put into the eye without harm, provided it does not touch the sensitive pupil.

The stone’s good heat mixed with the vinegar’s heat drives away the noxious humors that cause fever.

To aid cattle during pestilence, hold Onyx over steaming water so its sweat enters the water, then leave the stone in the water for three days.

See Onyx for the full entry and source citations.

Ophthalmius

It clouds all other eyes, so that a thief may carry away plunder openly and securely.

See Ophthalmius for the full entry and source citations.

Sapphire

It preserves health and turns away betrayal, harm, envy, and fear.

See Sapphire for the full entry and source citations.

Sardachat

The passage links Sardachat with Agate, which it calls a driver-away of evil things.

See Sardachat for the full entry and source citations.

Sardonyx

This also removes great wrath, stupidity, and illiteracy, and the devil flees its purity.

See Sardonyx for the full entry and source citations.

Topaz

Pierced and threaded with an ass’s bristle, then bound on the left wrist, it drives away terrors and melancholy. Set in gold, it dispels phantasms, expels stupidity, and gives wisdom.

Set in gold, Chrysolite drives away the terrors of night.

Set in gold and carried in the left hand, Crisoletus drives away demons, nightmares, and melancholy visions. It weakens the enchantments of evil women and ends their charms. Held in the hand, it dispels feverish heat.

Set in gold, the Ethiopian Crisolitus drives away terrors of the night, gives wisdom and honors, and dispels folly.

Topaz resists heat and poison. When poison is in food or drink, the stone sweats if brought near it.

For protection, place Topaz over the heart each morning and say: “May God, who is magnified above all things and in all ways, not reject me from his honor, but may he preserve, strengthen, and establish me with his blessing.” Evil will abhor the bearer, and the stone deflects assaults.

One account says that water cooled by the stone drives away lasciviousness.

It increases wealth and drives away anger and sorrow.

See Topaz for the full entry and source citations.

Against disease and bodily affliction

Aquilinus Limphaticus

Hung around the neck or carried on the person, Aquilinus Limphaticus wards off the distress of quartan fever.

See Aquilinus Limphaticus for the full entry and source citations.

Celidonius

Wrapped in yellow linen and worn around the neck, the stone banishes fevers and obstructive watery humors.

See Celidonius for the full entry and source citations.

Chalcophonos

Worn chastely, it gives a melodious voice and protects strained throats from hoarseness.

See Chalcophonos for the full entry and source citations.

Chemites

Chemites is said to preserve the bodies of the dead for a long time, preventing putrefaction and keeping worms from consuming them.

See Chemites for the full entry and source citations.

Corvina

The fish-head Corvina keeps away intestinal disorders when carried against the body. Ground and drunk, it has the same virtue. It is taken from the still-living fish while the moon is waxing in May.

See Corvina for the full entry and source citations.

Dionysia

When rubbed with water or ground and mixed with water, Dionysia gives off a wine-like fragrance that wards off drunkenness. Its own scent dispels the fumes of wine and makes the smell of wine evaporate.

See Dionysia for the full entry and source citations.

Filaterius

Filaterius drives away terrors and melancholic passions.

See Filaterius for the full entry and source citations.

Galactite

To increase milk in ewes and protect them from disease, crush Galactite with salt and mix it with lustral water. It makes the ewes’ udders abound with milk and drives away murrain or scab.

Its effects also include forgetting past quarrels and troubles.

It brings victory in legal causes, makes its bearer astute, amiable, and eloquent, and guards against enchantment.

See Galactite for the full entry and source citations.

Garnet

Garnet gladdens the heart and dispels sorrow. Some say it protects its wearer from plague.

See Garnet for the full entry and source citations.

Hiena

Carried on the person, it guards against quartan ague and gout.

See Hiena for the full entry and source citations.

Kabrates

It protects against evil accidents and heals dropsy.

See Kabrates for the full entry and source citations.

Limphicus

Wrapped in silk, it protects against infections of the eyes, jaw, and throat, and against coughs and headaches. This protection extends into the future as well as the present.

See Limphicus for the full entry and source citations.

Ophthalmus

Ophthalmus protects the wearer against all bad eye diseases.

See Ophthalmus for the full entry and source citations.

Poeanites

Pregnant women trust Poeanites for protection during childbirth, especially at the dangerous moment of delivery.

See Poeanites for the full entry and source citations.

Silenite

Silonites is said to cure consumption and those weakened by long illness.

See Silenite for the full entry and source citations.

Spongius

Hung around a child’s neck, it protects the child from coughing.

See Spongius for the full entry and source citations.

Turquoise

Turquoise is said to preserve sight and protect the wearer from misfortunes. Another account says that a rider who carries Turchion will not have a tired horse or suffer riding accidents. Its appearance strengthens sight, and its bearer is protected from outward and evil accidents.

See Turquoise for the full entry and source citations.

Vulture Stone

The stone heals the one who wears it, protects against every kind of misfortune, fills a woman’s breasts with milk, and ensures success for those who ask for it.

See Vulture Stone for the full entry and source citations.

Zigrites

Worn around the neck, Zigrites reduces bleeding and dispels delirium.

See Zigrites for the full entry and source citations.

Against storms and natural forces

Balagius

It overcomes and represses troublesome and lustful thoughts, reconciles quarrels among friends, and brings good health. Touching the four corners of a house, garden, or vineyard with it is said to protect them from lightning, storms, and worms.

See Balagius for the full entry and source citations.

Bronia

Bronia resists lightning.

See Bronia for the full entry and source citations.

Brontea

Brontea counters the lightning-bolt.

See Brontea for the full entry and source citations.

Ceranius

Worn, Ceranius saves its bearer from drowning and protects against lightning and tornadoes.

See Ceranius for the full entry and source citations.

Ceraunium

Lightning does not strike where Ceraunium is present.

See Ceraunium for the full entry and source citations.

Cimedia

Cimedia foretells calm or stormy weather in the air and at sea.

See Cimedia for the full entry and source citations.

Cinaedia

Cinaedia foretells calm or storm at sea.

See Cinaedia for the full entry and source citations.

Coral

Coral protects the wearer at home or aboard ship. It drives away ghosts, demons, illusions, and nightmares, restrains lightning, winds, tempests, and whirlwinds, and protects against wild beasts. Coral also guards against storms and hail.

Hung among fruit trees or mixed with seed in the field, it increases fertility and keeps away hail and rain.

See Coral for the full entry and source citations.

Hephaestite

It shields fields from locusts, hail, whirlwinds, and rust. It quenches sedition and arms its bearer against every danger, provided that it is worn over the heart.

See Hephaestite for the full entry and source citations.

Pearl

Thunder and lightning frighten the shells shut and can produce small, hollow, misshapen, or aborted pearls.

They comfort the heart, aid fainting and epilepsy, stop the flux of the belly, and when taken with sugar help against pestilential fevers.

See Pearl for the full entry and source citations.

Thunderstone

Wear the stone in purity to be spared from lightning. Its presence protects a city, house, and farm from such harm. During a voyage over the sea, it guards the ship from lightning and destructive whirlwinds. The stone also confounds enemies in lawsuits and battle and brings sweet sleep.

See Thunderstone for the full entry and source citations.
