Changing substances and mixtures

Aegyptilla

When ground in water, Aegyptilla gives the water the color and taste of wine.

See Aegyptilla for the full entry and source citations.

Chrysocolla

Chrysocolla has the nature of a Magnet. One account says it increases or coagulates gold.

See Chrysocolla for the full entry and source citations.

Magnosia

Once melted, it is mixed with glass to purify the glass’s substance.

See Magnosia for the full entry and source citations.

Pearl

Pearls are soft in water and harden when brought into the air.

Pearls dissolve in vinegar.

See Pearl for the full entry and source citations.

Pumice

Put in a vat of new wine, it stops the wine from bubbling.

See Pumice for the full entry and source citations.

Sifinus

When the stone is smeared with oil and put over a fire, it hardens and turns black.

See Sifinus for the full entry and source citations.

Topaz

Topaz stops boiling water from bubbling so that it soon becomes cool enough to touch.

See Topaz for the full entry and source citations.

Attraction and repulsion

Amber

Warmed by rubbing on cloth, amber attracts leaves, straws, threads, chaff, and cloth fringes, as the Magnet attracts iron.

See Amber for the full entry and source citations.

Carchedonia

When Lychnis is warmed by the sun or rubbed with the fingers, it draws chaff and scraps of paper to itself. It is also resistant to carving.

See Carchedonia for the full entry and source citations.

Diamond

Placed upon a magnet, the diamond restrains it from drawing iron and can make it release iron already held. Yet the Indian or Arabian diamond is also credited with the magnet's virtue: turning an iron needle toward the Arctic pole.

See Diamond for the full entry and source citations.

Jet

Warmed by friction, jet draws straw and chaff to itself.

See Jet for the full entry and source citations.

Ligurius

Rubbed, Ligurius attracts straw. A form of Ligurius is said to resemble Electorius, and it too attracts straw.

See Ligurius for the full entry and source citations.

Magnet

The magnet attracts iron and transfers its virtue to it, so the first piece may draw a second, and rings or needles may hang from one another in a chain. It is also said to draw molten glass. When a needle floats in a basin on straw or wood, drawing the magnet around the vessel makes the needle follow.

Garlic and Diamond prevent the magnet from drawing iron. A small diamond may restrain a great magnet. The source also says that a magnet may attract a piece of iron from one side and repel it from another, that one kind is itself drawn by iron, and that another draws human flesh.

When worn, the magnet reconciles wives and husbands, removes fears and suspicions, and makes a person gracious, persuasive, and elegant in speech.

The magnet also has dangerous and deceptive uses.

The magnet is said to call up phantoms when used with incantations and magic signs, and to hang a great iron cult-statue in a temple as though it floated in the air.

This kind attracts iron on one side and repels it on the other. When touched to a suspended needle, its opposite sides draw or drive the needle.

In this kind, iron does not follow the stone.

The Ethiopian magnet rejects and repels all iron. The deeper blue the magnet, the better its quality.

See Magnet for the full entry and source citations.

Sagda

Sagda clings to a ship’s hull with a force compared to the Magnet’s attraction of iron.

See Sagda for the full entry and source citations.

Preservation, decay, and corrosion

Chemites

Chemites is said to preserve the bodies of the dead for a long time, preventing putrefaction and keeping worms from consuming them.

See Chemites for the full entry and source citations.

Haematite

Haematite preserves rather than corrodes the body.

See Haematite for the full entry and source citations.

Ruby

Placed in clothing or another object, the carbuncle makes it last longer and decay with difficulty.

See Ruby for the full entry and source citations.

Breaking, flotation, and movement

Nitrum

It is used in instruments of war to launch stones. When kindled by fire, it rarifies and expands violently. By resistance and pressure it hurls stones into the air.

See Nitrum for the full entry and source citations.

Okitokius

Liquefy Okitokius with echium’s juice. When a person dips a finger into the unguent and touches any hard wood, metal, or stone, that object immediately breaks.

See Okitokius for the full entry and source citations.

Syrius

The whole stone floats in water, but a cut piece sinks.

See Syrius for the full entry and source citations.
