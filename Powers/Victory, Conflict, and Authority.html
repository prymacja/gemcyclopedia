Honors, reputation, and public favor

Aetites

Aetites makes its bearer amiable, sober, and rich, shields him from adversity, and grants affection and victory. It preserves honors and healthy offspring.

See Aetites for the full entry and source citations.

Corvina

It increases riches, brings honors, and foretells future events.

See Corvina for the full entry and source citations.

Heliotrope

Heliotrope gives its bearer a good reputation, honor, health, and long life.

See Heliotrope for the full entry and source citations.

Nicolus

Worn, Nicolus makes its bearer victorious and well-liked by people.

See Nicolus for the full entry and source citations.

Sapphire

A man engraved as if playing a musical instrument raises the bearer to greater honours and makes him gracious to all.

See Sapphire for the full entry and source citations.

Topaz

Set in gold, the Ethiopian Crisolitus drives away terrors of the night, gives wisdom and honors, and dispels folly.

For protection, place Topaz over the heart each morning and say: “May God, who is magnified above all things and in all ways, not reject me from his honor, but may he preserve, strengthen, and establish me with his blessing.” Evil will abhor the bearer, and the stone deflects assaults.

Its bearer gains a prince’s favor.

See Topaz for the full entry and source citations.

Victory and protection in battle

Agate

A complete winged horse brings victory and prudence in all actions.

It also repels venomous creatures, strengthens sight, grants victory and strength, and repels tempests and lightning.

See Agate for the full entry and source citations.

Alabaster

It gives victory and preserves friendship.

See Alabaster for the full entry and source citations.

Alectorius

Alectorius gives strength and victory in battle and makes its bearer invincible. It brings honors and preserves them, helps restore lost kingdoms and conquer foreign ones, and gives eloquence, constancy, graciousness, and persuasive power.

See Alectorius for the full entry and source citations.

Amandinus

Amandinus counteracts or mitigates all poison and brings victory over adversaries.

See Amandinus for the full entry and source citations.

Amethyst

Amethyst extinguishes poison, protects soldiers, and brings victory over the enemy.

The image and its setting govern the following virtues.

A foaming horse ridden by a man holding a sceptre makes the seal useful in all things and wins the obedience of princes and powerful men.

See Amethyst for the full entry and source citations.

Beloculus

Beloculus is said to make its bearer invincible in battle.

See Beloculus for the full entry and source citations.

Beryl

Beryl protects against peril from enemies, overcomes disputes, and grants victory.

It brings wealth, honors, and victory over rivals.

When touched, it reconciles enemies and brings friendship to those at odds.

Dog — A leporini dog engraved on beryl brings great honors, fame, and benevolence.

See Beryl for the full entry and source citations.

Catochites

Catochites brings victory in battle.

See Catochites for the full entry and source citations.

Ceranius

It grants victory in legal cases and battles and makes dreams sweet and pleasant.

See Ceranius for the full entry and source citations.

Daemonius

It makes its bearer safe from enemies and victorious against them.

See Daemonius for the full entry and source citations.

Diamond

Worn on the left arm, it gives victory and courage and guards against enemies, wild beasts, savage men, weapons, quarrels, phantoms, and night terrors.

Engraved on a diamond, the head of a long-bearded man with blood about his neck gives victory and courage and protects the body from bodily harm.

See Diamond for the full entry and source citations.

Draconites

It also grants victory, courage, and invincibility.

See Draconites for the full entry and source citations.

Gagatromaeus

Worn by a leader going to battle, the stone grants victory by sea and land. Easterners set it into their sword hilts so that they do not go to war without it. Alcides braved every danger while protected by the gem and escaped unharmed, but after losing it the mighty victor soon fell.

See Gagatromaeus for the full entry and source citations.

Nitrum

It is used in instruments of war to launch stones.

See Nitrum for the full entry and source citations.

Pyrophilus

Pyrophilus brings victory and protects its bearer from poison.

The old tale—though it may be only a tale—says Alexander of Macedon wore the stone in his girdle during battle.

See Pyrophilus for the full entry and source citations.

Sard

It prevents terrible dreams, increases wealth and cheerfulness, restrains bleeding, and grants victory over enemies.

See Sard for the full entry and source citations.

Thunderstone

The stone also confounds enemies in lawsuits and battle and brings sweet sleep.

See Thunderstone for the full entry and source citations.

Success in lawsuits and disputes

Carnelian

A man holding flowers, engraved on Carnelian and set in a tin ring made on a day governed by the Moon or Venus, in the first, eighth, or twelfth hour.

A bearded man holding a flower, engraved on Carnelian and set in a tin ring made during the Moon’s phases, either on a day governed by Venus under the first Moon or on the eighth Moon.

See Carnelian for the full entry and source citations.

Chalcedony

Pierced and worn on the neck or hand, it secures success in lawsuits. When a hole is made in it and filled with an ass’s hair, it brings victory in civil suits and battle.

See Chalcedony for the full entry and source citations.

Chalcophonos

Carried with due reverence, Chalcophonos brings great advantage to the pleader.

See Chalcophonos for the full entry and source citations.

Emerald

It gives foresight, aids divination, and aids the foreknowledge of cases to be pleaded.

An image of a merchant carrying goods, or a man seated beneath a centurion, brings wealth, victory, and freedom from distress and evil. An armed horseman with a lance gives victory on the battlefield.

See Emerald for the full entry and source citations.

Onyx

When worn around the neck or on a finger, Onyx brings sorrow and fear, frightful dreams, and quarrels or lawsuits.

See Onyx for the full entry and source citations.

Ziazaa

Ziazaa makes its wearer litigious and causes the wearer to see horrible things in sleep.

See Ziazaa for the full entry and source citations.

Rulership, authority, and favor

Celidonius

They grant eloquence and persuasive power, soften the anger of rulers, and refresh weary sight.

See Celidonius for the full entry and source citations.

Galactite

Carried on the person, Galactite immediately appeases a king’s anger and restores his favor to one who has offended him. It brings victory in legal causes, makes its bearer astute, amiable, and eloquent, and guards against enchantment.

See Galactite for the full entry and source citations.

Hyacinth

They guard travelers from noxious air and contagious disease, defend against lightning and enemies, bring honor and wealth, and help secure a favorable hearing from princes.

Horse bearing a crocodile: carved on Hyacinth and bound in gold, it gives victory in civil lawsuits and makes its wearer well-mannered, kind, and amiable.

Jupiter enthroned with four men before him, hands raised to heaven and crowned: carved on Hyacinth and set in gold, it grants the wearer’s wishes from wise men and kings.

Crowned youth seated on a throne carried by four men, with hands raised beneath a circle: carved on white Hyacinth and set in a silver ring of equal weight, with mastic and turpentine resin beneath the stone, its wax seal brings the bearer whatever he seeks from kings, powerful people, and wise men of pure mind.

See Hyacinth for the full entry and source citations.

Kabrates

Jupiter — A figure of Jupiter, commonly seated with a baton and globe, or carved as a man with a ram’s head, when engraved on Kabrates, makes its bearer fortunate and amiable, helps him obtain what he desires—especially from clergy—and raises him to dignity and honor.

Eagle — An eagle engraved on Kabrates or Rock Crystal makes its bearer rich, victorious, and eloquent.

See Kabrates for the full entry and source citations.

Orphanus

Orphanus preserves royal honor.

See Orphanus for the full entry and source citations.

Phengites

One account says that a king built a windowless temple of this stone, whose transparency made the interior visible from outside.

See Phengites for the full entry and source citations.

Radaim

It heals its wearer, brings him honor, and aids him in governing.

See Radaim for the full entry and source citations.

Good fortune and success in undertakings

Coral

It also gives a happy beginning and successful end to undertakings.

See Coral for the full entry and source citations.

Crystal

Unspecified image (the referent is unclear) — Engraved on Kabrates or crystal. — Makes the bearer rich, victorious, and eloquent.

See Crystal for the full entry and source citations.

Evanthus

Whoever looks upon Evanthus as the sun rises will be victorious in all undertakings of that day.

See Evanthus for the full entry and source citations.

Pantherus

If one looks upon the stone early in the morning as the sun rises, success and victory follow. For that whole day it protects against enemies and keeps them from prevailing.

See Pantherus for the full entry and source citations.
