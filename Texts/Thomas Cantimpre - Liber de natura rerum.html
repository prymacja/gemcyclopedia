Thomas of Cantimpré — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV

Full text

Complete English translation of the supplied, cleaned Latin transcription

The translation follows the order and contents of the cleaned Latin file, including its prologue and its separately headed passages. Historical claims are translated, not endorsed. Where the surviving transcription is obscure or corrupt, the uncertainty is indicated in square brackets rather than silently resolved. The two entries headed “Crisolitus” are kept separate, as in the supplied text.

Prologue

First, we must speak generally of precious stones, in which great power and efficacy for healing seem to be found. Yet no human being, except God, knows whence they have this power, for all power comes from God. It is certain that almighty God directly bestowed power upon precious stones and, in accordance with their nature, granted them the favour of his will. Apart from the gift of healing, many great and wonderful marvels are experienced in gems: for instance, those concerning the magnet and the diamond, which seem hostile to one another in the attraction of iron; the diamond, which points out the star of the sea; the ostolanus, which renders a man invisible; the carbuncle, which dispels the darkness of night without the aid of fire; and many others, as the present book attests. The cause, therefore, of these marvels is the will of almighty God.

On this subject also the blessed Augustine says in Book XV of The City of God that demons are enticed to dwell in created things suited to the particular delight of each, through various kinds of stones, herbs, woods, animals, and incantations. Hence we also read that Solomon enclosed demons beneath gems in rings. We read, moreover, that Evax, king of the Arabs, wrote to Nero Caesar concerning the names, colours, powers, and differences of stones, and that this writing was subsequently adorned with metrical verses. We have set forth the meaning of those verses in plain prose, adding the opinions of other authors as well. At the end of the book we have placed certain opinions of the ancients, which we think should neither be believed in every respect nor rejected altogether.

There is a common opinion that the children of Israel engraved certain gems in the wilderness, especially carnelians, with such subtlety that no one afterward could imitate this work. Nor is there doubt that figures and images were engraved on gems in accordance with the efficacy of their powers. And if it is true that the children of Israel engraved precious stones with various figures, it is clear that [the passage concerning the rationale of these powers is syntactically defective in the supplied Latin]: those men, filled with the Spirit of God, were among the ones whom God chose for the mystery of every craftsman's and architect's work—namely Bezalel and Oholiab. Spiritually instructed in every craft involving gems, they wonderfully fashioned, by engraving and polishing, the breastpiece of judgement and the shoulder-piece upon Aaron's breast and shoulders. In these, according to the divine command, they set twelve precious stones and engraved upon them the names of the sons of Israel. From this it is evident that carvings [one word is corrupt in the Latin] are found upon gems. Yet we do not, on this account, believe that every carving is a sign of power or has a mystical significance.

Ametistus — Amethyst

Amethyst is one of the twelve stones. It has a violet colour, and that is the best kind. There is another variety resembling a drop of red wine diluted with water, and this kind is easier to engrave. It also acts against drunkenness. This stone would be very costly if it were rarer. It is found in Ethiopia and India, and also in Germany, although there it is of inferior quality and dark in colour.

Achates — Agate

Agate is one of the twelve stones, found in the eastern river Achates. It is black in colour, intermingled with white veins. It is said that, in engraved gems, it contains figures formed by veins of a single colour; but this is the work of nature, not of art. It drives away poisons, allays thirst, benefits the sight, strengthens health, and makes its bearer agreeable. When placed in fire it gives off a remarkable odour. King Pyrrhus of Egypt is said to have worn this stone on his finger; on it instruments together with the Muses appeared to be depicted, and Apollo stood in the midst of the Muses holding a lyre.

Adamas — Diamond

The diamond is a stone of which there are two kinds. It is found in the farthest parts of India among crystalline rocks, and resembles crystal in colour. It differs from crystal, however, in that it gleams like filed iron. It is so hard that neither iron nor fire can break it; but when warmed with the freshly shed blood of he-goats, it can be cut. With its sharp fragments the hardest gems are engraved. Its size does not exceed that of a hazelnut. Some people call this stone dyamas. It is reported to confer favour upon the person who bears it, if he has received it freely from a friend; but it does nothing for someone who acquires it by purchase, whether through entreaty or by payment. Its power is said to be greater if its setting is made of iron. Nevertheless, in keeping with the dignity of the stone, the ring is made of gold.

Another kind of diamond is found in Arabia, in the sea near Cyprus, and in the iron mine at Philippi. This kind, however, is far inferior in dignity and power. Diamonds of this kind are found in greater size, but are different in colour: they have a dark, rust-coloured appearance and can be broken without he-goat's blood. The diamond attracts iron and, if present, takes iron away from the magnet. It also reveals the star of the sea, which is called Maria, by the following method, in dark clouds by day or by night.

For when, at night, sailors cannot direct their course to harbour amid dark clouds, they take a needle and fix its point crosswise into a small straw, and place it in a vessel full of water. Then they move the diamond around it, and the needle's point immediately follows the movement of the stone in a circle. After they have quickly moved the stone all the way around, they suddenly withdraw it. At once the point of the needle, having lost its guide, directs its tip towards the star of the sea, and immediately comes to rest without moving a point. The sailors then direct their course to harbour according to the indication thus given.

This stone is also said to be useful in magical arts. It makes its bearer strong against an enemy, repels vain dreams, and drives away and reveals poison. Indeed, it is said to sweat if poison is brought near it. It benefits lunatics and those who are afflicted in the morning [the precise condition intended by the Latin is uncertain]. This stone should be worn on the left upper arm.

Albeston — Asbestos Stone

Albeston is a rust-coloured stone found in Arcadia. Once it has been kindled, it can never be extinguished, but always retains perpetual flames of fire. Isidore says of it that a device can be made whereby a lamp fashioned from this stone burns in the open air in such a way that no storm and no rain can extinguish it.

Amanthos — Amianthus

Amanthos is an eastern stone resembling alum. A silk garment coated with this stone shines in the fire, becoming bright merely from the brilliance it receives. When immersed in water, it withstands poisoners and the illusions of magicians.

Allectorius — Alectorius

Alectorius is a stone about the size of a bean, very like crystal but a little cloudier. It is formed in the belly of a cock which, after three years, has been castrated and then [has lived or grown for a further seven—units and verb are corrupt in the supplied Latin]. When carried in the mouth, this stone quenches thirst. It brings victory, restores peace, restores honours, makes a person eloquent and agreeable, and especially reconciles wives with their husbands. It accomplishes all these things when kept enclosed in the mouth.

Abscinthus

Abscinthus is a black stone streaked with snow-white veins and exceedingly heavy. If it has been heated by fire, it is said to retain the heat for seven days.

Alabandina — Alabandine

Alabandine is a precious and beautiful stone. Its colour resembles that of garnet, though it is deeper and more like ruby. There is also a kind of alabandine that resembles sard, found in the region of Asia called Alabanda.

Andromanda

Andromanda is a square stone, exceedingly hard like diamond, silver in colour, and found in the Red Sea. It possesses such power that it can calm a mind burning with fury. It also restrains lust.

Berillus — Beryl

Beryl is one of the twelve stones. It has a pale colour like seawater. The best specimens, however, resemble clear oil, especially those that have no blemishes inside. There are very bright beryls which nevertheless have tiny, elongated markings within, like little hairs. If a beryl has a hexagonal form, it produces the colours of the rainbow when struck by a ray of sunlight. If it is round like an apple and moistened with water, in the heat of the sun it sets alight dead coals, blackened burnt linen, or dry growths on a tree.

Hence a certain versifier says:

The power that shapes me into fire has made me remarkable;
I have received the sun's splendour, but not the fire's heat.
Thus the eternal light protects from the heavenly height;
It was incarnate without the mother being so defiled.

For it is said to cure the disease called quinsy, and by rubbing it on swellings of the glands arising from unhealthy humours it removes them while they are still increasing. It also reconciles the love of spouses and brings distinction to those who wear it. Washed in water, it benefits weak eyes. If that water is taken as a drink, it relieves belching, sighing, and pains of the liver. There are nine kinds of this stone, some of them as clear as crystal. India produces it.

Borax, that is, Crepaund — Toadstone

Borax is a precious stone, so called because an animal named borax, a kind of toad, carries it in its head. There are two kinds: one white, which is better and rarer, and another dark and black, with an eye-like mark in its centre, approaching a sky-blue colour. A specimen of this latter kind is better if it has that mark. When the stone is taken from a living, still-throbbing toad, it is said to possess the eye; but when it is extracted from a toad that has been dead for some time, that mark fades and disappears, and the stone is spoiled.

This stone, when taken as food, is said to cure internal disorders in a human being. It passes through the intestines and, once the cure is accomplished, exits through the lower part of the body. It is said that the complete stone of the white variety possesses this power. The French call this gem crepandine, and it resists poisons.

Carbunculus — Carbuncle, Ruby, and Balas

The carbuncle is the most precious of all stones and one of the twelve said to have been placed on Aaron's vestment. Its colour is fiery. It shines more at night than by day, since its brilliance is obscured in daylight, but it dispels the darkness of night. In Greek it is called anthrax, and it is found in Libya, the land of the cave-dwellers.

There are three varieties: the first, carbuncle; the second, ruby; and the third, balas, which is of lesser worth. Ruby is fiery in colour, but incomparably less so than carbuncle; consequently, it does not dispel the darkness of night at all. It is also unequal to carbuncle in its powers. Nevertheless, among other stones it is more precious and more elegant, and has a star-like colour. Balas, which is the third variety, is much inferior in colour, power, and price, yet it is considered superior to sapphire and jasper.

Calcedonius — Chalcedony

Chalcedony has a dull, dense, and invariably pale colour. Its hue lies between that of hyacinth and beryl. It is said that anyone who wears it around the neck or on a finger will win legal cases. There are said to be three varieties of this stone.

Corallus — Coral

Coral is a red stone, though not as translucent as carnelian. Its shape resembles a stag's antler or the root of a plant with many branching roots. This is not surprising, since it is at first a plant in the sea and, when brought out by ships or taken up by human activity and exposed to liquids [possibly a corrupt reading], it hardens into stone. It is found no longer than half a foot.

It withstands lightning and storms. Hence the ancients, led by this belief, used to scatter crushed pieces of the stone with seed over a field or hang it in olive trees, so that their crops might not suffer the destructive dangers of hail. It is terrifying to demons, perhaps because the spreading of its branches frequently gives it the shape of a cross. It averts misfortunes and bestows benefits. Hence Ovid says:

So too coral hardens as soon as it touches [the air];
At first it was a soft plant beneath the waves.

Crisopassus — Chrysoprase

Chrysoprase is a stone of two colours: it glitters with little golden drops, mingled with the colour of leek juice. They say it is found very rarely and is therefore held in greater esteem. Nevertheless, it is found in India.

Celidonius — Chelidonian Stone

The chelidonian is a small, shapeless stone found in the bellies of swallows. Sometimes it is reddish, sometimes black. Young swallows carrying this stone in their livers are recognised by the following sign: they sit face to face, beak to beak, as a sign of mutual peace.

When the reddish kind is found, it calms lunatics and the insane, as well as long-lasting illnesses. It makes a person eloquent and agreeable. It must be wrapped in a linen cloth, and the stone should be worn on the left side. When the black kind is found, it too must be carried wrapped in a linen cloth, and it is useful for merchants. It moderates anger and, when dissolved in water, brightens the eyes. When carried in a saffron-coloured cloth, it allays fevers and harmful humours.

Cacephanus

Cacephanus is a black stone. If carried by a chaste person, it makes the voice sweet and resonant and protects the throat against hoarseness. The stone itself also gives a sweet, ringing sound like bronze when struck with iron or bronze.

Cristallus — Crystal

Crystal is formed from ice hardened over many years. Solinus, however, seems to maintain the opposite, for he says that crystal is found in certain parts of the world where frost or ice has never been seen. This stone is exceedingly clear, like ice. If it has a rounded shape, as we have said above concerning beryl, it sets tinder alight when exposed to the sun. Crystal, ground up, mixed with honey, and taken as a drink, fills the breasts with milk.

Crisolitus — Chrysolite (first passage)

Chrysolite resembles gold or amber and has a more pleasing colour in the morning hours. If it is brought near fire, it immediately catches fire and, having seized upon the material, springs back from the fire as though fleeing it. This stone sends out fiery sparks of its own accord and drives away every kind of serpent.

Ceraunius — Thunderstone

Ceraunius is a blue stone, so named, as it were, from the Greek word for a thunderbolt, because it falls from heaven in a thunderbolt. Lightning is said not to fall where this stone is present. It is usually pointed at one end.

Corneolus vel Cornelius — Carnelian

The stone called corneolus or cornelius is said to be red, but of a dull colour resembling cut flesh. It calms anger and stops blood flowing from a limb or the nose, and especially bleeding in a woman suffering a discharge. It is said that the children of Israel engraved a great number of these stones, namely carnelians, in the wilderness, and not without good reason.

Crisolitus — Chrysolite (second passage)

Chrysolite is one of the twelve stones, mentioned above. It gleams like gold and sparkles like fire. It resembles the sea, having a certain greenish tint in its darker shades. If worn in gold, it is effective against fears of the night. If it is pierced and the hole filled with hairs from a donkey, it terrifies and drives away demons. It should be worn on the left upper arm.

Dracontides (Chalcophonos) — Dragonstone

Dracontides is a stone extracted from the brain of a dragon, and it does not become a gem unless it is cut out while the dragon is alive. This is done as follows: courageous men seek out a dragon's cave and suddenly pierce the head of the sleeping dragon. Thus, while it is still throbbing with full vitality, the gem is extracted. The kings of the East take particular pride in this gem. It has a translucent colour.

Dyonisia — Dionysias

Dionysias is an eastern stone, dark in colour and sprinkled with snow-white spots. When ground in water, it causes wine to flare up [the Latin wording is obscure]. Yet, contrary to nature, drunkenness is driven away by its smell, although the smell of wine naturally produces drunkenness even without drinking it. Dionysias gleams black, studded with snowy-white spots.

Diadocos — Diadochos

Diadocos is a stone that, when cast into various waters, produces the likenesses of demons, so that [they may give] answers to those who seek them. If it is brought near the body of a dead person, it withdraws, as though it had the power to feel and recoiled from anyone's death. It resembles beryl in colour.

Emathites — Haematite

Haematite is a stone from Ethiopia or Arabia, rust-coloured and intermingled with blood-red veins. Broken up and dissolved in water, it heals those who spit blood. It also cures menstrual bleeding and looseness of the bowels. Its powder mixed with wine cures ulcers and is effective against poisonous bites. It also cleanses the eyes like an eye-salve. Taken as a drink, it breaks up stones in the bladder. Its powder also eats away and removes dead flesh from wounds.

Echites — Eagle-stone

The eagle-stone is carried by the eagle to its nest from the farthest regions of the world. For the eagle knows by nature that this stone is a protection for its young. Others say that the eagle's eggs would become overheated by excessive warmth if the stone, which is naturally cold, were not present. The stone is scarlet in colour and contains a small stone within, which makes it rattle.

It helps pregnant women most effectively, preventing miscarriage and excessive suffering in childbirth. It delights in being worn on the left upper arm [the Latin reading for “left” is uncertain]. It bestows sobriety, increases riches, grants favour, makes the bearer victorious, and keeps children unharmed.

Elitropia — Heliotrope

Heliotrope is a stone which, when placed beneath the rays of the sun in a vessel full of water, makes the sun appear blood-red, as though it were undergoing an eclipse. Immediately the vessel in which the stone lies seems to boil and to scatter water like showers of rain. When this happens, people seized by a frenzy, if any are present, are carried out of themselves and foretell future events.

This stone prolongs the life of its bearer, staunches bleeding, drives away poisons, and protects against deceit. Furthermore, if the herb called heliotrope is placed beneath the stone and consecrated with the proper incantation, the gem renders a person invisible. This is the herb heliotrope, which some identify with the plant called solsequium (“sun-follower”). Ethiopia, Cyprus, and Africa produce this gem. It is green like emerald and sprinkled with blood-red spots.

Clidros (Enhygros)

Clidros is a stone resembling crystal, which in a wonderful manner continually drips tears. More wonderful still is that the substance of the stone does not dissolve, nor does the stone diminish; rather, it remains hard and entirely intact in every respect.

Granatus — Garnet

Garnet is a most beautiful stone, almost resembling ruby in colour, but with a deeper colour like that of a rose. It is exceedingly difficult to engrave. It shines more brightly if a black colour is placed beneath it. It drives away sadness and makes the mind cheerful. It is found in Ethiopia and near Tyre, among the sands cast up by the waves of the sea. There is a variety called violet-coloured, because it has violet mixed with red. This kind is more precious and closely resembles balas.

Gagas — Jet

Jet is a precious stone found in Lycia and Britain. It is shining, black, and light. When heated, it attracts pieces of straw. It burns when washed with water and is extinguished by oil. It helps those suffering from dropsy and, when washed with water, strengthens loose teeth. Fumigation with it restores menstruation; when burned it benefits those suffering from the falling sickness, and [the Latin also mentions “elidros”, whose meaning here is uncertain]. It drives away demons; and if they speak through the mouth of a possessed person, its smoke forces them either to depart or to be silent. It helps an upset stomach and is effective against magical illusions.

Water in which the stone has lain for three days also benefits pregnant women: that water brings about a speedy delivery. It reveals virginity, for if the woman is a virgin she remains still, but if she has lost her virginity she immediately passes urine.

Gelasia

Gelasia is a stone possessing the whiteness of hail and an unconquerable hardness like that of diamond. It can never be heated by fire, but always remains cold.

Gecolitus

Gecolitus is an eastern stone, resembling an olive pit. It is unremarkable in appearance but precious in virtue. Dissolved in water and drunk, it dissolves a stone in the bladder and purges the sand accumulated there.

Galartides (Galactites)

Galactites is a stone resembling ashes. When ground with milk and drunk, it increases milk and relieves pregnant women. It drives away scabies. [The following words are corrupt in the cleaned Latin and cannot be translated securely: vitello locus ovi patrat bona gerenti.] It is found in the river Nile.

Gagatromeus

Gagatromeus is a stone resembling the skin of a roe deer. It makes its bearer victorious in battle, and nothing can withstand him either on land or at sea. It is related that Hercules overcame many dangers with this stone, but succumbed to his enemies whenever he did not have the gem with him.

Jaspis — Jasper

Jasper is one of the twelve stones and is green, though it also has blood-red spots. The best kind is the most translucent. If the person wearing it is chaste, it drives away fevers and dropsy. It also helps women in childbirth. If consecrated, it makes its bearer safe and agreeable. It repels harmful apparitions. Its power is greater if it is worn in silver. There is a variety that is very red, fiery, and translucent, and this is precious. Another is red but not translucent, and this is of lesser worth.

Jacinctus — Hyacinth

Hyacinth is a blue stone, cloudy in gloomy weather and bright in clear weather; for it is said to respond to the condition of the air. The best kind is neither too bright nor too dark. It is so hard that it resists cutting or engraving, though it is sometimes engraved with fragments of diamond. It is exceedingly cold, especially when put in the mouth.

It strengthens its bearer, drives away sadness and various suspicions, and keeps travellers safe. It is a protection against pestilence, serpents, and poisons. It makes its bearer pleasing both to God and to men. Ethiopia sends forth this gem. Garnet belongs to the genus of hyacinth.

Isciscos

Isciscos is a stone resembling saffron, with a slight lustre. If a garment is woven from this stone, it resists fire, so that it will not burn even when placed in the flames. Rather, it takes on brilliance and shines, sometimes resembling lightning. It withstands all poisoners, especially magicians.

Iris

Iris is a stone resembling crystal and having a hexagonal shape. When placed in the rays of the sun inside a house, it produces colours like those of the rainbow upon the wall. Its power is against lightning; it grows in the sea like a bush [the syntax of the final Latin clause is uncertain].

Hyena — Hyena-stone

The hyena-stone is a very precious gem, named after the animal called the hyena because it is taken from that animal's eyes. It enables its bearer to foretell the future when it is carried in the mouth beneath the tongue.

Ligurius — Lynx-stone

Ligurius is one of the twelve stones. It is produced from the urine of a lynx, which hardens into stone. Its colour is fiery and reddish, like carbuncle, except that it does not shine at night, as Pliny says. Evax says that this gem resembles amber, whereas the lapidary says that it is green. Washed in water, it benefits those suffering from constipation, loosens the bowels, restores lost colour to the face, and attracts straws like jet. It is useful for those suffering from stones in the urinary tract.

Margarita — Pearl

The pearl is found in shells. White pearls are better than pale or reddish ones. Since two are never found in one shell, they are called uniones, or “single ones.” A pearl is soft in water, but when taken out it hardens into stone. They are not found larger than half an ounce. The highest praise of their colour is that they gleam like alum. Some are oblong, while others are round. Placed in vinegar, they dissolve more quickly. This stone, ground up and prepared in the prescribed manner, cures weakness of the stomach. It also has the power to bring harmony and to produce health of mind and body—health of mind because it makes the wearer chaste.

Magnes — Lodestone

Lodestone is a rust-coloured stone. It attracts nearby iron when diamond is absent, but cannot do so when diamond is present. They say that this stone is useful in magical illusions and the magical arts. If someone wishes to know whether his wife is an adulteress, he places the lodestone under the head of the woman as she lies in bed. If she is chaste, she will seek her husband and embrace him even while asleep; but if she is an adulteress, she will suddenly fall from the bed as though thrust by a hand. Thus the stone reveals a concealed offence. It is also said to reconcile wives to their husbands.

If someone enters a house at night intending to steal, and the thief places glowing coals in four places in the house and sprinkles fragments of lodestone over them, a cloud of smoke will immediately disturb the minds of the inhabitants so much that it will cause them to flee, and the thief will carry away the things in the house unpunished.

This stone, ground up and mixed with milk, cures dropsy. Reduced to powder, it heals burns. It is found among the Troglodytes and the Indians. It is better the bluer it is. It attracts glass just as it does iron. By means of this stone, as Saint Augustine says, an iron image was suspended in the air without any support.

Memphites — Memphite Stone

The Memphite stone takes its name from Memphis, a place in Egypt. It has a colour like fire. Ground up and mixed with vinegar, it is given to drink to those who are to be cauterised or cut, so that it may render the body insensible and prevent it from feeling the pain.

Melonites

Melonites is a stone from Arabia, having a deep green colour, very like emerald but not so bright. It strengthens tender limbs and protects against unfortunate accidents.

Medus — Median Stone

The Median stone comes from the region of the Medes and is somewhat green. Dissolved in the milk of a woman who has given birth to a boy, it restores sight to the blind and cures gout. It also cures those suffering from frenzy when dissolved in water and drunk, and causes the lung to be vomited up. Whoever washes his forehead with this water becomes blind. Evax says that the stone is entirely black.

Onichinus — Onyx

Onychinus is a most precious stone, one of the twelve. It resembles a human fingernail; hence it is so named because onyche means “nail” in Greek. It is effective against scabies, whitens the face, and produces a most pleasant smell.

Onica

Onica is a kind of gum which hardens into stone, like amber. When it is applied to a diseased eye, it enters of its own accord without doing harm and passes around all the interior parts of the eye until it has expelled the harmful humours. This seems marvellous because nothing is so tiny that it will not damage the eye if it enters it. Hence the proverb: “The eye is a narrow dwelling which admits no guest.”

Ostolanus

Ostolanus, or ostaines, is a stone that is the patron of thieves, because it makes its bearer invisible and draws a cloud over the eyes of those standing nearby, while leaving the bearer's own vision unimpaired. The author does not reveal the colour of this stone, lest it be easily found.

Orithes

Orithes is a green stone with white spots. It resists adversity. Another kind is black and round, and heals eyes afflicted by poison. Anointed with oil, it also drives away the effects of bites from serpents and wild beasts. There is yet another kind of the same stone, resembling a small plate of iron, which makes a woman pregnant or, if she is already pregnant, causes her to expel the foetus.

Perites, or Pederotes

Perites, also called pederotes, is a tawny stone. If it is gripped too tightly in the hand, it burns the hand that holds it; but it allows itself to be touched gently and cautiously. Another kind is almost green, like the juice of leeks mixed with water. This variety is very noble and is effective against spasms.

Pantera — Panther-stone

Pantera is a stone named after the animal called the panther. It displays almost every colour. Anyone carrying it should look at it in his hand at earliest morning, as the sun rises, so that he may be victorious and successful in every undertaking. It calms rage and is found in India. It is said to have as many powers as it has colours.

Parasius, or Prasius

Prasius is a beautiful green stone, but its colour is denser than that of emerald. It is of no use except that it looks attractive in gold. Emerald is cut from this stone.

Saphirus — Sapphire

Sapphire is a most precious stone, one of the twelve, and is blue in colour. The most precious kind comes from India and is not transparent. It preserves the body and its members intact and strengthens the inner organs. It cools internal heat, restrains sweating, removes pain from the eyes and forehead, heals and soothes ulcers of the tongue, and drives away the dreadful disease called noli me tangere (“do not touch me”). Yet it is said to spoil its colour after the disease has been driven away.

It is especially effective against deception, envy, and terror. It is also favourable to peace. Whoever wears it should guard chastity with the greatest care, lest the stone lose its powers. Eastern sapphires are the best, especially those that shine with an intense blue colour and have, as it were, little clouds of deeper colour upon their surface. There are also certain eastern sapphires containing an admixture of the stone called ruby; these are more valuable and more powerful than the others.

Smaragdus — Emerald

Emerald is a precious stone, one of the twelve. It is greener than anything else that is green. The best is found in Scythia, where the Arimaspi take it from griffins. The finest stones are those through which the eye can see, whose radiance makes the neighbouring air appear green, and whose greenness is not dimmed by sun, lamplight, or shade. The best shape is flat.

If worn chastely, it drives away the falling sickness, which is called li entericus [the disease-name in the Latin is uncertain]. It refreshes the sight and cleanses the eyes. If it is washed with wine or anointed with oil, it makes a person pleasing in speech. It increases riches, helps those who investigate hidden matters, averts storms, and restrains lustful impulses.

Sardonix — Sardonyx

Sardonyx is one of the twelve stones and is formed naturally from onyx and sard. One part is red, derived from sard; another is white and another black, and those two colours come from onyx. It has no power except that onyx cannot do harm when sardonyx is present. It has a most beautiful appearance where the different colours are intermingled. This stone does not pull away wax [when used as a seal]. It makes its wearer prudent and humble. It withstands the powers of onyx: for whereas onyx causes apparitions of demons to appear in dreams, and stirs up and increases quarrels, sardonyx drives away all such things.

Sardius — Sard

Sard is one of the twelve stones. It was first discovered at Sardis. It is red, although some specimens resemble red earth. It staunches the flow of blood, and onyx cannot cause harm when sard is present. Some say that this is the stone called carnelian.

Samius — Samian Stone

The Samian stone is named after the island of Samos. It is heavy and white. Gold is polished with this stone. When carried, it calms dizziness and restores disturbed minds. It has this harmful property: when tied to the breast of a woman in labour, it is said to hold back childbirth.

Succinus (Ambra) — Amber

Amber, which the Greeks call electron, is saffron-coloured and sometimes shines or is translucent like glass. According to Isidore, it is the sap of a pine tree, and for this reason it is called succinus; in common speech it is also called ambra. When rubbed or warmed between the fingers, it attracts leaves, straws, and the fringes of garments, just as the magnet attracts iron. It may be handled in whatever way one wishes, for it is dyed with the root of a certain herb called attusa [the plant-name is uncertain]. It is said to confer chastity on those who wear it. Its smoke is said to help women in labour and to drive away serpents.

Silonites

Silonites is a stone carried by a tortoise in India. It is beautiful with white, red, variegated, and purple colours. There is a kind of it that is green like grass. When carried in the mouth, it foretells future events. For example, if you wish to know whether something will or will not happen, place the stone beneath your tongue and consider in your mind whether the event must happen or not. Immediately one of these alternatives will take hold in your heart so firmly that it cannot be removed.

This stone has its power in the morning and at the sixth hour when the moon is waxing. On the first day [of the moon] it has power for only one hour; on the tenth day [the text appears to specify] the first and sixth hours. When the moon is waning it lacks power, though its power remains on the preceding day [the timing is uncertain in the supplied Latin]. It can never be broken by fire. It reconciles those who hate one another in love. It cures sufferers from consumption and those weakened by long illness. It is found in Persia.

Topazius — Topaz

Topaz is one of the twelve stones. Its colour resembles gold, and this is the more precious kind. Another kind is much brighter and paler in colour, but is inferior. It helps those suffering from haemorrhoids. It is said to respond to the moon and to calm boiling waters. It is also reported to restrain anger and lust. It is found in Arabia. Pliny says that a topaz was found of such a size that Pholoneus Philadelphus [the ruler’s name as transmitted; the intended name may be Ptolemy Philadelphus] made from it a statue four cubits high.
