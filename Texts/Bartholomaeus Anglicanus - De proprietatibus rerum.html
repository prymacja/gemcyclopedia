INCIPIT LIBER XVI. DE LAPIDIBVS Preciosis.

For the properties of the Earth and of the parties thereof, is described in general: by help of God, is now our purpose to plant here somewhat, of the beauty & ornament thereof in special. Of things, that beautify the earth, some be clean without soul & without feeling, as all thing that grows under the ground, & is gendered in veins of the earth, as stones, colours, and metal: and of these we shall first speak by order. And some with life and soul, as roots, herbs, and trees, and some with soul and feeling, as men, and other beasts, of whom we shall last speak. And we shall show first by order of A.B.C. the things that be gendered in the earth, & in the veins thereof.

Of Arena. chap. 1.

Gravel is called Arena, and has that name of dryness. And if it were thrust and bruised together in a man's hand, it should rattle & make noise for dryness & hardness. And if gravel be thrown on a white cloth, it defiles it not, as Isi. says li. 15. ca. 4. Also gravel of the sea is washed of all manner slime and ooze, and of gleamy fen, as Aristotle says liber de proprietatibus elementorum. And therefore because of great dryness it runs not, nor turns not perfectly into stone. Also li. 4. Mcth. he says, that pure earth turns not perfectly into stone: for it continues not, but breaks. And therefore dryness overcoming all moisture, suffers not earth to turn into solidity of stone. Therefore it is the gravel & sand is more hard in substance then common earth, & more soft then a stone, and may be departed in many parts. And so gravel and sand is cold & dry & small, & most heavy in weight when it is thrust together, & though of it self it be small & barren for default of heat & of moisture, & mastery of cold & dryness, when a shower falls thereon, it breaks not, but fenny things is washed away & wasted, & gravel & sand is more pressed together, & is more pure & solid: for the parts cleave more together. Also the waves of the sea is prevented with multitude of gravel and sand, & withstood with heaps thereof, & constrained that it passes not the meares & bounds that God has ordained thereto, as Jerome says super le. cap. 5. He set gravel & sand mark & mear to the sea, &c. Also he has universally kind of during and of cleansing. And so water that runs through gravel is most clean and fresh, as Constantine says.

Also it has virtue to cleanse metal, and to cleanse always the rustiness of metal. Gold, Brass, and Iron is made bright with rubbing of Gravel and Sand.

Also it has virtue to cool and to smite inward, and to stop and to assuage swelling, as it fares of Thimalia, that is, gravel and sande shaven or bruised of a Smiths stone, which is good for all the foresaid things, as Constantine says plainly, and has virtue like to ore of metal. And so among gravel and sand both of rivers and of the sea, is found golden gravel oft times, and also bright polished stones, and precious stones are found in the sea cliffs among gravel. Also gravel of rivers and of the sea, be gathered to heaps with contrary winds, that raises parts thereof hetherwarde and thitherward, and stops and tarries oft the waters of the rivers, of course and running into the sea, as Iero. says super Amos. 8. Ibi destuct sicut riuus Aegypti, &c. There it is openly said, that the water of Nilus is lot with heaps of gravel, that are gathered together in the month of the river, and suffers not the water of Nilus to run into the Sea: but constrains it openly, and makes it turn again and overflow the plains of Egypt: but the more the water is prevented, and compelled to turn again with those heaps of gravel, with the more course and might it runs and falls into the sea: and it is said that it falls the more deep into the sea, when those heaps be departed and avoided, as Jerome. says there. Also it is hard and most peril to fall and smile on heaps of gravel, hid in the sea under water, as it is rehearsed before, where it is shown of perils of the sea.

Of Clay. chap. 2.

Clay is called Argilla, which is tough earth, glewie and gleamy, apt and meet to diverse works of Potters, and has that name Argilla of Arges, as Isidore says lib. 16. cap. primo. For among them were first earthen vessels made of such clay: and as it is said there, by working of heat that consumes and wastes the moist parts, and dries and hardens the earthy parts, such clay turns into [?]ile. Also often it happens, that cold freezes the water in such fenny earth, and the earth turns to stone: Also changing of unctuous sen into stone, falls often by heat running & hardening the matter, as it said 4. lib. Metheorer. Unctuous fen is able to such changing and turning into stone, by burning & hardening of heat: for an unctuous thing is mean between a gleamy, and vaporative thing that passes out of things, in breathing or smoking. And the thing that is unctuous has moisture in it self, and so because of the part that is vaporative, it may congeal and be made hard by heat: and because of the part that is gleamy, it may not all passe out in vapours, but be more thrusted together and made thick and hard, and so tourned into stone, as the Commentour says. Clay by his coldness staunches blood at the nose, if clay be tempered with strong vinegar, and laid to the temples.

Of Alabastro. chap. 3.

As Isidore says li. 15. ca. 5. Alabaster is a white stone, with streaks of diverse colours. Of such stone the ointment box that the Gospell speaks of, was made: and of this stone, the ointment vessel is made at best without corruption, and that stone that breeds about Thebes, in Egypt and Damascus, is more white, than other: but the best comes out of India. And Dioscucides calls this stone Intomar, and it is said, that this stone helps to win victory, & mastery. Also he says, that this genders and keeps friendship.

Of Gold. chap. 4.

Gold is called Aurum, and has that name of Aura, still wether, as Isidor. says lib. 26. For it shines most, in air that is bright: for it is kindly, that shining of metal blase the more, if they be shined with other light. Therefore things that make shining the more blassing, be called Aurarij, and gold that is most blasing is called Obrisum, for it shines with bright beams, and is of best colour. And the Hebrews call it Ophar, and the Greeks name it Chrusos.

And a thin plate, of the which golden threds are cut out, is called Brathea. Huc vsque Isi. cap. de mettallis. In li. Metheororum parum ante fine Aristotle says, that gold, as other metal, has other matter of subtle brimstone and red and of Quicksilver subtle and white. In the composition thereof is more solidity of brimstone, then of air and moisture of Quicksilver: and therefore gold is more solid and heavy than silver. In composition of silver, is more commonly Quicksilver than white brimstone as he says. Then among metal, nothing is more solid in substance, or more better compact than gold: and therefore though it be put in fire it wastes not by smoking & vapours, neither lessees the weight, and so it is not wasted in fire: but if it be melted with strong heat, then if any filth be therein, it is cleansed thereof, & that makes the gold more pure & shining. No metal stretches more with hammer work than gold: for it stretches so, that between the anfield and the hammer without breaking and renting in pieces, it stretches to lease gold. And among metal, there is none fairer in sight than gold, and therefore among Painters, gold is chief and fairest in sight, and so it darkeneth shape and colour of other metal. Also among metal is nothing so effectuall in virtue, as gold. Plato describes the virtue thereof and says, that it is more temperate & pure than other metal, for it has virtue to comfort, and for to cleause superfluities gathered in bodies: and therefore it helps against leprosy and leprosy. The filing of gold taken in meat or in drink, or in medicine preserueth, & and prevents breeding of leprosy, or namely hides it and makes it unknown: for often use of gold prevents & tarieth the breeding of Lepra, or makes it unknown, as Avicenna says. Also the some filing and powder, with the juice of Borage, and with the bone of the heart of a Harte, helps against swooning, and against cardiaele passion. Also thin plates of gold, fire hot, quenched in wine, makes the wine profitable against the evil of the spleen, & against many other evils and passions melancholic: and also burning made with instruments of gold, is better than with other manner of metal, for it keeps the place that is burnt without stinking. Also powder of the offall of gold heals spots of eyes, if it be therein. Also gold comforts sore limbs, though it be not corporate therein, and though it norisheth them not, yet it does away with the sharpness thereof, superfluities that growe in members, & cleanses them in that wise as Plat. says. Also hot gold does away hair of members: and what that is burnt or rubbed therewith, grows no more, as Consta. says. Also gold smitten, foundeth not as brass does and silver, nor creketh nor cleaves, but strecheth even abroad, except other strong metal be mingled therewith in some wise. For as long as brass or latten is mingled with the substance of gold, it cleaves always under the hammer, & is inobedient to take out shape by beating of the hammer: no therefore it needs that all superfluity be put away, and the gold cleansed by melting & made pure, that it may be able to be shaped with a hammer, to make thereof some vessel with convenable shape as Gregory says. And when a plate of gold shall be mingled with a plate of silver, or joined thereto, it needs to beware namely of three things, of powder, of wind, and of moisture: for if any hereof come between gold and silver, they may not be joined together, the one with the other: and therefore it needs to meddle these two metals together, in a full clean place & quiet, and when they be joined in due manner, the joining is inseperable, so that they may not afterward be departed asunder.

(*Gold makes wise men glad: and spendthrifts mad: Gold vpholdeth kingdoms: Gold is preferred in many common wealths before Justice: & therefore to those that put their delight therein, it is called Auri sacra fames, of the misvse thereof. It is reported by late Writers, of the golden mountains of Cibana, aud of the wonderful riches of gold that the king of Spain has yeerly from the West and East Indies, gathered by a multitude of slaves, ordained for that purpose: notwithstanding the vsurers that inhabit Britain, doe make moe slaves that are free borne, by raising of rents, ingrossing of lands, and hindring of tillage, for the quantity of ground in one year, then is subject among the Indies in three years, to the utter ouerthrowe of lands, goods, wife and children.)

Of Latten. chap. 5.

Latten is called, Auricalcum, and has that name, for though it he brass or copper, yet it shines as gold without, as Isid. says, for brass is Calco in Greek. Also latten is hard as brass or copper for by mingling of copper, of tin, and of Auripigment, and with other metal, it is brought in the fire to colour of gold, as Isid. says. Also it has colour & likeness of gold, but not the value. Also of latten be composed diners manner of vessels and fair, that seem gold when they be new, but the first brightness dimmeth some and some, and becommeth as it were rusty, and so both in colour and in smell of copper, the first matter thereof, is known. Meat and wine kept in such a vessel, takes an horrible savour and smell of the vice of brass: but Collyria and medicines for the eyes be profitably kept in such vessel, and be made sharp by the strength of brass, as Plat. says.

Of Auripigmento. cap. 6.

Avripigmentum is a vein of the earth, or a manner of free stone that cleaves and breaks, and it is like to gold in colour. And this is called Lapis Arcenicus by another name, and is double: red and citrine. And as Dioscorides says, it has kind of brimstone, or burning and drying, & if it be laid to brass, it makes the brass white, and burns and wastes all bodies of metal, except gold, as Diosc. says. And Plat. says, that Auripigmentum is hot and dry in the fourth degree, and is a vein of the earth: and thereof is double kind, red and citrine: and the citrine helps to medicine, and has virtue of dissolving and tempering, of drawing and of cleansing: and so it helps Phthisic and Etike, and those that have evil breath, that comes of moisture, if they gave thereon, & take the smoke thereof. Of Auripigment is made Pisilo cracium, an ointment that does away hair and makes them fall, when it is fodde in hot lime. Also it helps against tetars and scabs, if the place be softly anointed with sope and with Auripigment: but the place must be washed anon, for if the ointment lye long thereto, it will fret the good flesh as well as the evil, and stay away the skin, and it has kind of slitting, taruing, and of fretting, and makes soul and unseemly nails fall, that better nails may grow, as Platea says.

(*Auripigmentum, a colour called Orement, of some Orpine or Arsenike, & daungerous poison. The red and ftretting Mercury is of the like operation.)

Of Silver. chap. 7.

Silver is called Argentum, and has that name as Isi. says of the speech of Greek, for silver is called Argiron in Greek: and wonderfully it fares. For though silver be white, yet it makes black lines and streaks in the body that is scored therewith or ruled therewith, as Isi. says. In composition thereof is Quicksilver and white brimstone, and therefore it is not so heavy as gold, as Richardus Rufus says. There be two manner of silvers, simple and compounded & the simple is fleeting, and is called, Quicksilver. The silver compounded is massie & solid, and is compounded of Quicksilver pure & clean, & of white brimstone not burning, as Aristotle says.

Of Quicksilver. chap. 8.

Quicksilver is a watry substance mingled strongly with subtle earthy things, and may not be dissolved, & that is for great dryness of earth, that melts not on a plain thing, & therefore it cleaves not to the thing that it touches, as does the thing that is watry. The substance thereof is white, & that is for clee[?]nes of cleere water; & for whitnes of subtil earth that is well digested. Also it has whitenes of mingling of air with the foresaid things. Also Quicksilver has the property, that it cruddeth not by it self kindly; without brimstone: but with brimstone and with substance of lead, it is congealed, and fastened together. And therefore it is said there; that Quicksilver and brimstone is the element, that is to wit, matter, of which all melting metal is made. All this said openly, lib. Metheororum. And also Richardus Rufus, expounds it openly, in this manner: Quicksilver is matter of all metal, & therefore in respect of them it is a simple element. Of the virtue and kind thereof, diverse men speak diuerslye: But what the meaning of the Physicians is thereof, it may be known by these words. Quicksilver (as Plato says) is hot and moist in the fourth Degree, though some men deem that it is cold in the same degree. That it is hot, it is known by effect of working: for it dissolves, pierces and carves, but for it seems full cold in touch, some men deem it amiss, and mean that it is cold. But some men mean, that Quicksilver is made by great heat of a veyne of the earth: but it is known that that is false, for by heat of fire, it is soon wasted away in smoke: but of earth it is gendered, and is kept as it were fleeting water, & is full long kept in a cold vessel & sound, & cleaves so together, & so strong that it may to nothing be mingled, but it be first quenched, and it is quenched with spettle when it is rubbed therewith: and namely when powder is mingled with spettle, and specially when powder of bones of an Henne is mingled therewith: and Quicksilver passes out by evaporation is seething & in smoking. The smoke thereof is most grievous to men that be thereby, for it breeds the Walste and quaking, shaking and softening of the sinews. If it be taken in at the mouth or into the care, it pierces and slays the members. Against that peril, Goates milke is best, dronke in great quantity, with continuall moving oof the patient: for the same specially wine is good, in the which, wormwood and Isop is sod. Huc vsque Plat. And it is called Quicksilver, for it congeals matter in which it is done, as Isi. says li. 15. And also there he says more to this. He says it is fleeting, for it runnenth, & is specialy found in silver forges, as it were drops of silver molten, & it is oft found in old dirt of sinks, & slime of pits: and also it is made of Minis put in caverns of iron, and a patent or a shell done there under, and the vessel that is noynted therewith, shall be compassed with burning coals, and then the Quicksilver shall drop. Without this, silver nor gold, nor latten nor copper, may be ouergilte: and it is of so great virtue and strength, that though thou put a stone of an hundred pound weight, upon Quicksilver of the weight of two pound, the Quicksilver anon withstands the weight, and if thou doest thereon a scruple of gold, it consumes into it self the lightness thereof: and so it appears it is not weight, but Nature to which it obeys. Taken in drink, it slays because of weight, & is best kept in glass vessels: for it pierces, bors, and frets other matters. Huc vique Isidorus lib. 16. chap. de metallis.

Then silver is white and cleere, & sounding and pure, and may well be wrought with hammer, but not so well as gold, and keeps and saves well balm, and helps the virtue in the stone Iaspis, that has more virtue in silver then in gold, as it is said in Lappidario, and it will melt: and when it is molt in the fire, it has the colour of fire, nevertheless when it is cold, it is again white and hard. And though it be of more great and coarse matter then gold, yet among metal it bears the price after gold, in worthiness and value: and is medicinal, and helps against many euills. For the some thereof heals wounds, & fulfills the place, & frets away superfluity of dead flesh, & prevents it not grow, & brings in the whole flesh, & cleanses filth and hoare of bodies, a marking instrument of silver, kepeth from stinking, and comforts feeble members, and lays and makes them steadfast, & rusts, if it touches the earth, and takes his colour again, if it be scoured with gravel, sande, and salt.

Of Adamant. chap. 9.

Adamas is a little stone of India: and is coloured as it were iron, and shines as Crystal, but it passes never the quantity of a walnut. Nothing overcomes it, neither iron nor fire, and also it heats never: for of the Greeks it is called, a virtue that may not be daunted. But though it may not be overcome, & though it despise fire and iron, yet it is broke with new hot blood. Engravers use the pieces thereof to sign and to rule, & to hollow precious stones. This stone is contrary to Magnes: for if an Adamas be set by iron, it suffers not the iron to come to the Magnes, but it draws it by a manner of violence from the Magnes, so that though the Magnes draws iron to it self, the Adamas draws it away from the Magnes. It is said, that this stone warns of venom, as Electrum does, and puts off diverse dreads and fears, and withstands witchcraft. Huc vsque Isi. li. 15. ca. de Christallis. Dioscorides says, that it is called a Precious stone of reconciliation and of love. For if a woman be away from her husband, or trespasseth against him, by virtue of this stone Adamas, she is the sooner reconciled to have grace of her Husband. And heereto he says, that if a very Adamas, be privately laid under a womans head that slepeth, her husband may know whether that she be chaste, or no: For if she be chaste by virtue of the stone, she is compelled in her sleep to embrace her husband: and if she be untrue, she leaps from him out of the bed, as one that is vnworthye to abide the presence of that stone. Also us Dioscorides says, the virtue of such a stone borne in the left shoulder, or in the left arme pit, helps against enimars, against madness, chiding and strife, and against frends that anoy men that dream in their sleep, against fantasy, against dreaming and venom.

[Batman's addition] ( Adamas, A Diamond, whereof there be diverse kinds, (as in Pliny) and in other it appears. Cooper) that a Diamond laid by a Needle, causes that the Load stone cannot draw the needle. No fire can burt it, no violence break it, vnles it be moysted in the warm blood of a Goat.)

Of the whitest of these, they make many smal Diamonds, which cannot be known from the true, saving by touching of such are skilfull in that practise.)

Of Ametisto. chap. 10.

Ametistus is a stone of India, & is chief among precious stones of purple colour, as Isi. says. Amatistus is purple in colour, mingled with colour of violet, as it were a blasing rose, and tightly rasting out as it were glisning beams of light. Isidore says that the other kind thereof is much like to blue, and is not all fiery, but it has colour of wine. And five kinds be thereof, as Isidore says. But as Dioscondes says: The Purple red is most noble and better then the other. For the virtue thereof helps against drunkenness, and makes waking: and removes away idle thoughts, and makes good understanding, and is soft, so that men may grave and write therein.

[Batman's addition] ( Amethistus, a stone called an Amethist, of colour purple: but the more Rose colour the better.)

Of Achate. chap. 11.

Achates is a precious stone, and is black, with white veins, and has that name Achates of a river, that is called so: the which is in Sicily. By the which river that stone was first found, as Isid. says: and is now found in many regions and lands: and a manner kind of that stone is in Creta, as Dioscorides says, with blue veins. And another kind is in India speckled with red drops, as Isid. says. Men believe that the first manner thereof helps witchcraft. For therewith tempest is changed: and slinteth rivers and streams, as it is said. Also Dioscorides says, the same kind is good to the shape of Images of Kings, and to show likens of sleeps. And the manner kind of Creta changes perils, and makes gracious and pleasing, and fair showing and speaking, and gives might and strength. The third manner stone that is of India, comforts the sight, and helps against thirst and venom, and smells sweet if it be near, as Dioscorides says.

[Batman's addition] ( Achates, a precious stone, wheron are represented diverse figures. Some have the Image of the nine Muses, some of Venus. It was one of the 12. stones, that God willed to be put in Aarons vestiment. It is supposed that the Magicians, by the smoke of that stone, did put away tempests & stormes. This stone is interlined with white, & light bluish lines, &c.)

Of Abeston. chap. 12.

Abeston is a stone of Archadia with iron colour: and has that name of fire: if it be once kindled it never quenches. Of that stone is made that crafty ingin, of the which nations taken with sacriledge wondred. For in a Temple of Venus was made a candle stick, on which was a lantern so bright burning, that it might not be quenched with tempest, neither with rain, as Isidore says, lib. 15. cap. de Gemmis.

Of Absciso. chap. 13.

Abscisus is a precious stone, black and heavy, straked with red vemes. If this stone be heat in the fire, it holds heat seauen days: as Isidore says. cap. de Gemmis.

Of Alabandina. chap. 14.

Alabandina is a precious stone, and has that name of a region of Asia, the which is called Alabandina: The colour thereof is like to the stone Calcidonius: but it is more tare, as Isi. says, cap. de Cristallis. And Dioscorides says, that Alabandina is a precious stone cleere & some deale red, as Cardius. The virtue thereof excites and increases blood.

[Batman's addition] ( Alabandina, a red stone, mixed with blue, provoking blood. D. Cooper.)

Of Argirit. chap. 15.

Argirites is a precious stone like to silver with golden specks. Andromaia is like thereto in colour: and is a stone with colour of silver, as Isidore says. And is four cornered in shape, as the corner of a Diamond. And Witches believe that it has that name for that it refrains sudden passions, and wrath of hearts, as Isidore says, cap. de Gemmis aureis.

Of Astrione. chap. 16.

Astrion is a precious stone of India, near like to Crystal. In the middle thereof shines a star with clearness of the full Moon: and has that name of Astris, stars: for if it be set in the stars light, it takes light of them, as Isidore says, cap. de Christal.

[Batman's addition] ( Astrios, a precious stone in India. Astroites, a stone like a Fishes eie.)

Of Allectoria. chap. 17.

Allectoria is a stone that is found in the mawes of capons, and is like dim Crystal: and the most quantity thereof is as much as a Beane. And as witches tell, it is supposed that in battle fighting, this stone makes men insuperable, that they may not be overcome, as it is said in Lapidario. Dioscorides speaks of this stone, and says, that Allectorius the stone excites the service of Venus, and makes a man gracious & steadfast, & vidor, wise and redi, and cunning in plea, and accords friends, and quenches thirst in the mouth.

[Batman's addition] ( Alectoria, a stone found in the maw or Gissard of a Cocke, of the bigness of a Beane, and couloured like Crystal: This stone is not to be found in every Cocke, until he be old, &c.)

Of Asterites. chap. 18.

Asterites is a precious stone, and is white, and conteyneth light therein closed, as it were a Star going within, and makes the Sun beams white.

[Batman's addition] (This Jewell is found in the maw of a pure maid, that is fifty years of age, or els among the Troglodites. Vesper.)

Of Amatides. chap. 19.

Amatides is a precious stone, if a cloth be touched therewith, the cloth withstands fire and burns not, though it put therein: But it receives brightness and seems the more cleere: and is lyks Alam, that may be brosed. And withstands all evil doing of witches, as Isidore says, li. 16. cap. 10.

[Batman's addition] ( Amatices, it is supposed that in the maw of the Cocke Turtle done this stone is to be found, and has virtue to increase concord and love.)

Of Bitumine. chap. 20.

Glew is slimy gleab of the earth, and gleaming and gendering, namely in marsh ground. And (as Isidore says, lib. 16. cap. 2. it is a manner of gleaming earth, and is found beside the lake Asphalti in Judea. The kind thereof is burning and of fiery kind, and holds so fast, and is so clongie, that it is not broke with water nor with iron, but only with menstruall blood, and it is good to joining of ships. Of this Glew is mention made in Plat. in this manner. Asphaltis glew of Judea is hot and dry in the third degree, and is earth of black colour, and is heavy and stinking. And some men mean, that it is earth gendered of the foam of the dead sea, and is made hard as earth, and has virtue of drawing, and sodering, and fastening, and consuming, and is full good to heal and close, and to sowder wounds, and Botches, if it be beat to powder and put in a dry wound, though the wound be full long and full wide. Also it is good to help the passions of the mother, that pierces and pierces the spiritual members, if it be put vppon coals, & the stinking smoke thereof be drawn in at the nose and at the mouth. Also it helps flumatike men, and sleeping Litergicis, that have the sleeping evil: For it purges well fleam out of the head, as Platea. says.

[Batman's addition] ( Bytume[?], a kind of naturall lime or clay, clammy like Pitch, in old time used in steed of Morteir, and in Lamps to burne in steed of Oil, being of the nature of Brimstone, it is a good siment for earthen pots and cups.)

Of Berillo. chap. 21.

Beryl is a stone of India, like in green colour to Smaragd: but it is with paleness, and is polished and shaped among the Indes in six corners shape: that dimness of colour may be excited by the rebounding of the corners. And otherwise shaped it has no bright seining. And of Beryl be ten manner kinds, as Isidore says, cap. de Viridibus Gemmis. Also there it is said, that Crisoberillus is a manner kind of Beryl. And the pale greennes thereof shines toward the colour of gold. And this stone comes to vs out of India. Dioscorides describes the virtues of Beryl and says, that among Beryl the stone that is most pals is best, the virtue thereof is good against peril of enemies, and against strift. And makes one so that he may not be overcome, and makes him mild, and gives good wit, and helps against diseases of the liver, and against fighthings and sobbings, and against bolkinges, and heals moist eyes, and burns his hand that bears it, if it be held before the Sun, & makes a man great of state, & joins wil love of matrimony.

[Batman's addition] (I suppose that the Beryl is none other than a grose Crystal, ingendred of the cold air and dry water hardened: as the North wind freezes the common streams, but with a more violent operation being either a thin green or yellow, is more earthy then airy, which is perceived by his peise and heaviness.)

Of Calculo. chap. 22.

Calculus is a little stone mingled with earth, and is round and most hard, and pure, and most smooth and plain in every side. And it has that name Calculus, for it is trod with feet without grief of his smoothness and plainness. And contrary heereto is a lyttle stone that is called Scrupulus chesell, and is most tough and sharpe, and full lightly if it still between a man's foot and the shoo, it grieves full sore. And so such stones that be sharpe and hard are called Scrupea, as Isidore says, lib. decimo quinto. ca. 3. And oft in the body of a beast, this stone breeds of hot humours and gleamy, now in the bladder, and sometimes in the reines, as Constantine says. Look before lib. 7. de Passionibus renum, cap. de Calculo.

[Batman's addition] ( Calculus is fallen. For the smooth Pibble stones, that net of many grose colours, but to small effect.)

Of a Whetstone. cap. 23.

A Whetstone is called Cos, and has that name, for it whetteth and sharps iron to cut and to carve. For Cotis is Greek, and is to say, carving, as Isidore says. And there be diverse manner of Whetstones. And some need Water and some need Oil for to whet, but Oil: makes smooth, and water makes the edge right sharpe, as Isidore says, lib. codem. capitulo. 3. The Whetstone has this property, that it serves another thing in whet[?]ing, and wastes himself some and some, as Gregory says. The powder of the Whetstone helps well in medicine, and has virtue of drying, and of smiting again repercussiue, and of staunching of blood, as Constantine says.

[Batman's addition] (The ponder of a Whetstone is as good for a fresh wound to stape the bleeding, as is a Pigges turd to the nose of him that bleeds: for the greet of the one is a pricking in the wound, & the stench of the other, anoyance to the head.)

Of Calce. chap. 24.

Lime is called Calx, & is a stone burnt, by mingling thereof with Sand and with water, Cement is made. And lime is called hot, as Isidore says. For while it is cold in handeling, it contains privately within fire and great heat. And when it is sprong with water, anon the fire that is within breaks out. In the kind thereof is some wonder: For after that it is burnt, it is kindeled in Water, that quenches fire. And lime is needful to building. For one Stone may not clean to another strongly, but they be joined with lime.

Lime that is made of white stone and hard, is best for walls: and Lime made of soft stone is best for roofs. Huc vsque Isidorus, lib. decimo quinto. ca. 3. And Plat. says, that Lime is hot and dry in the fourth degree: and Lime mingled with Oil helps whelks and wounds that be rotted, and closes and souders cutting, and frets superfluity of flesh in wounds, and suffers it not to grow. Hot Lime sod with Auripigment and water, makes hair to fall: For it opens the pores with sharpness thereof, as Constantine says and Plat. also.

Of Cemento. chap. 25.

Cement is lime, sand and water tempered together and mingled: And such mingling is most needful to join stones together, and to pergit & to white lime, walles. In painting and colouring of walls the first ground & chief to take colour is cement, & cleaves to wet walls, and namely if it be plaster stone. For as Isidore says, lib. 16. cap. 13. the best cement made of all stones is of the flint stone, or of plaster, that is called Gypsis: the which stone shines as it were Glass, also it will be broke into many small pieces.

Of Carbunculo. chap. 26.

Carbunculus is a precious stone, and shines as Fire, whose shining is not overcome by night. It shines in dark places, and it seems as it were a flame. And the kinds thereof be twelve, and the worthiest be those that shine and send out beams, as it were fire, as Isidore says, libro. 16. cap. 3. There it is said that Carbunculus is called Antrax in Greek, and is gendered in Libya among the Troglodites. Among these twelve manner kinds of Carbuncles, those Antraciles be the best that have the colour of fire, and be compassed in a white vein, which have this property: If it be thrown in fire, it is quenched as it were among dead coals, and burns if water be thrown thereon. Another kind of a Carbuncle is called Scandasirus: and has that name of a place of India in the which it is found. In this manner of kind as it were within bright fires, be seen as it were certain drops of gold. And this precious stone is of great price without comparison in respect of other. Another manner of kind there is that is called Signs, & has that name of bright burning of lamps. And this precious stone is called Remissus Carbunculus. And there is two manner of kinds: That one is with beams, as purple, & that other is of the colour of red silk. And if this be heated in the Sun with rubbing of fingers, it draws to it self straw and leaves of books. It is said, that it with stands engraving. And if it be sometimes graued and printed with waxe, it takes with him a part of the waxe, as it were with biting of a beast, as Isidore says there. Among manner of kinds of Carbuncles, Balagius is counted, that is red and bright, as Dioscorides says: and also it is said, that this manner Carbuncle is found in the vein of the Sapphire: & therefore through neerenesse of the Sapphire, Balagius has a manner mist, as it were sparkling about the fire, that compasseth him without. And this is openly seen if men take heed thereto.

[Batman's addition] (The Carbuncle orient, is of the colour of red Lead, and in the night sparkling like a coale.)

Of Crisoprasso. chap. 27.

Crrisoprassus is a stone of Ethiopia, & is hid in light, and seen in darkness. For it is fiery by night and golden by day, as Isidorus says. Therefore by day it is hid, as it were washed away, and loses his shining, and wars pals as gold. And there is another manner, kind that is called Crisoprassus, gendered in India: and this Crisoprassus is like in colour to a stone that is like to Prassius, and is green as a Leek and is besprinkled with certain, drops of gold, as it is said in Lapidario.

Of Calcidonia. chap. 28.

Calcidonius is a pale stone, and shows dim colour, as it were means between Beryl and Iacinct, as it is said in Lapidario. And is gendered among the Nasamos: and comes and is gendered of the rain of our Lord, as it is said: & is found by shining in the full of the Moon, as Isidore says, lib. 16. An[?]t found but of three colours, as the Lapidary says. And all kinds thereof withstands engraving, and draws straw to it self, if it be heated and chafed. Isidore says, this stone pierced and borne about one, makes him have mastery in causes, and helps against japes & scorns of fiends, and keeps & saves virtues, as Dioscorides says.

[Batman's addition] (Man's conjecture is far from the true operation of hidden properties.)

Of Crisolito. chap. 29.

Crisolitus is a little stone of Ethiopia, shining as gold, and sprinkling as fire. And is like to the sea in colour, and somewhat green. If it be set in gold and borne on the left arme, it fears fiends and chases them away, as it is said, and it helps night frayes and breads: And abates an evil that is called Menlancholy, or does it away. And comforts the understanding, as Dioscorides says. One manner of kind of Crisolitus, is Crisolimphis, the colour thereof is deemed golden by day, and firy by night. And another manner kind of Crisolitus is called Crisolentus, & is coloured as gold, and is right fair in sight in the morrow tide. And then as the day passes, his colour waxes dim. And this stone makes most soonest heat: For if it be set by the fire, anon it waxes on a flame, as Isidore says, li. 15. ca. de Gemmis aureis.

Of Celidonio. chap. 30.

Celidonio is a little stone, but it is precious. Hereof is double kind, black and red: And be taken out of a Swallows maw, and be little and unseemly: But in virtue they be no less of value then more stones and greater. But they passe many other in virtue. For the red helps against the passion Lunatic and against madness, & against old sorrow: and makes a man kind and pleasing. And the black helps against evil humours, and against Fevers and wrath. And if it be washed in water, it helps [?]ore eyes. And this is said in Lapidario.

[Batman's addition] (As touching these stones. I my self trieng an old rule, did find in the maws of the young Swallows, a black stone as bigge as a Thistle seed, and another red, and a third grey, spelled black, and after gave them away.)

Of Christallo. chap. 31.

Crystal is a bright stone and cleere, with watry colour. Men suppose that it is of snow or Ice made hard in space of many years. Therefore the Greeks gave a name thereto. It is gendered in Asia, & in Cyprus, & namely in the North mountains, where the Sun is most fervent in Summer: and they make this dure long, that is called Crystal. This stone set in the Sun takes fire, insomuch if dry tow be put thereto, it sets the tow on fire. His use is ordained to drink, & works none other thing, but what cold thing may do. Huc vsque Isi. li. 16. cap. 13. Dioscorides speaks of Crystal & says, that it is hardened & turned into stone, not only by virtue & strength of cold, but more by earthly virtue, and the colour thereof is like to Ice. The virtue thereof helps against thirst & burning heat: and if it be beaten to powder & drunk with hony, it fills breasts & teats full of milke, if the milke fails before because of cold: Also if it be drunk, it helps against Colica passio, & against the passion of evil guts, of the womb be not hard. The stone is cleere, & so letters and other things that be put therein, be seen clearly inough. That crystal materially is made of water. Gregory says super pri. Ezech. Water (says he) is of it self fleeting, but by strength of cold it is turned & made steadfast crystal: and so in Eccl. it is written. The Northern wind blew and made crystal freese, &c. And heerof Arist. tells the cause in li. Meth. There he says, the stony things of substance of ore, be water in matter, as Richardus Rufus says. Stone ore is of water, but for it has more of dryness of earth then things that melt, therefore they be not frore only with coldness of water, but also for dryness of earth that is mixed therewith, when the watry part of the earth & glassy has mastery on the water, and the foresaid rolde has the victory & mastery. And so S. Gregory his reason is true, that says, that Crystal may be gendered of water.

Of Ceraunio. cap. 32.

Ceraunius is a stone like to Cristall, and is infected with blue colour, and is in Germania. Another is of Spain, and shines as fire. These stones fall out of the place of Thunder. And it is said that they help against the strength of lightening, as Isidore says there. The same is said in the Lapidari. Where it is said that when it thundreth horribly, and the fiery air lightneth, and when clouds smite together, their stones falls from heaven. Who so bears this stone chastely, shall not be smitten with lightning, nor house nor Towne where this stone is therein. Either has virtue of helping in battailes, and to have mastery in war, and in causes of strife, and to make sweet sleeps, as it is said there.

Of Corallo. chap. 33.

Corall is gendered in the red Sea, & is a tree as long, as it is covered with water, but anon as it is drawn out of water, & touched with air, it turns into stone, and under water the boughs thereof be white and soft, & waxe red and turn into stone when they be drawn out of the water with nets, as Isidore says, cap. de gemmis rubeis. And if follows there: as precious as the Margarite of India is among vs, so precious and more is Coral among the Indes. Witches tell, that this stone withstands lightening, Isidore says, the same is said in Lapidario. His might and virtue, as says Zorastes, is wonderful, for it puts off lightning, whirl wind, tempest and stormes from Ships, and houses that it is in. And it is double, white and red. And is never found passing half a foot long. And the red helps against bleeding, and against the falling Evil, and against the fiends guile and scorn, and against diverse wondrous doing, and multiplies fruit, and speeds beginning and ending of causes and of needs.

Of Corneolo. chap. 34.

Corneolus is a red stone and dim, but it is full good and precicus, if it be hanged about a man's neck, or borne on a finger. In strifes it alayeth wrath that rise, and it staunches blood that runs of a member and all manner running. And namely such a running as women have, as it is said in Lapidario.

Of Dioniso. chap. 35.

Dionisius is a black stone or browne sprong with red veins, and has that name, for if it be ground and mingled with water, it smells as wine: and yet it withstands dronkenesse. And that is great wonder in that stone, as Isidore says, li. 16.

Of Diadoco. chap. 36.

Diadocus is a pale stone: bright as Beryl, and is apt to obtain answers of fiends, for it excites fiends and fantasies. And if it happens that it touches a dead body, it is said that it loses anon his good virtues, for it is the stone that hats and is squeimous of the thing that is overcome with death, as it is said in Lapidario.

Of Ere. chap. 37

Brass is called Es, & has that name of shining of air, as Isidore says. For in old time ere the use of iron was known, men eared land with brass, & fought therewith in war and battle. That time gold and silver were forsaken, and gold is now in the most worship, so age that passes and vadeth, changes times of things. Isidore says these words libro. 16. Brass and Copper is called Fa, for either is made of the same stone by working of fire, for a stone resolved with heat turns into Brass. Job. 28. Brass & Copper be made in this manner, as other metals be of brimstone & Quicksilver, & that hapneth when there is more of brimstone then of Quicksilver, & the brimstone is earthly & not pure, with red colour & burning, and Quicksilver is mean and not subtle. Of such mingling Brass is gendered, as Arist. says, and as Isidore says, ca. de Ere. If Brass be mingled with other metal, it changes both colour and virtue, as it fares in Latten & in Brass, that is called Conchium, that is joined to diverse metals, and some is white as silver, & draws some deale to browne colour, & seems gold, the third in which is temperatnesse of all. And another manner of Brass there is, that which is called Coronarium, that is beaten abroad into skin plates of brass, wrought with hammer, & is died with Buls blood, and is like to gold in crowns among posturing & painting Therefore it is called Coronarium, And Brass that is wrought with hammer is called Regular. And Brass, the only is melted, is called Fusil, but Brass that is fusil & molt, is brittle under the hammer: And when Brass is well purged & cleansed of all vices, then it is made able to be wrought with hammer & Regular. And all Brass is best molt in great cold: Brass rusts soon but it be anointed with oil. Also among al metal Brass is most mighty. Huc vsque Isi. li.16. And Brass accords most to the use of trumps and Belles, for sounding and during thereof. The stone of the which Brass is blown, is most solid and hard. And it breaks not easily, but it be burnt first with strong fire in the rocke, it is burnt: eight or nine times ere it be perfectly drawn out of the stone and purified, as they that cleanse Brass in places where it is made, tell certainly. Brazen vessels be some red and rusty, but they be oft stoured with sand: and have an evil se[?]uond and smell, but they be tinned. Also Brass if it be without Tin, burns soon, as Isidore says, cap. de Stanno, and Platea, says, that Brass burnt to powder has virtue of dissolving, of washing, of cleansing, & of purging of evil humours. And that powder heals wounds, and cleanses dimness of cien, and frets superfluity of flesh; and suffers it to growe no more, as Platea says, and Dioscorides also.

[Batman's addition] (Of the rust of Brass comes a blue substance called Verdigrese, which as it is a colour for Painters, so it is a fretting poison, used in ointments, on festured sores.)

Of Electro. chap. 38.

Electrum is a metal, and has that name, for in the Sun beam it shines more cleere then gold or silver. For in the Sun beam Electrum is effectual. And this metal is more noble then other metals. And heer of be three manner of kinds: One is such, that when it runs first out of the tree, it is fleeting and thin Gumm, but afterward with heat or with cold it is made hard as a cleere stone, as it were christal. That other manner kind is called metal, & is found in the earth, and in and in price, The third manner is made of the three parts of gold, & of the fourth of silver: and kind Electrum is of that kind, for in twinckling & in light it shines more cleere then all other metal: & warns of venom: for if one deep it therein, it makes a great chirping noise, & changes oft into diverse colours, as the rainbow, & that suddenly. Huc vsque Isi. lib. 15. de Metallis vlti Et super Eze. is mention shade of Electrum artificiall. Greg. says, that if hard gold be mingled with silver, then the filtier wiss more cleere, & the gold tempered with cloerenesse of silver, looses not his virtue, but only it wars somewhat pale, & less fair by mingling of silver. And some men cal very Glectrum Arpago. For if it be heted with fingers, it draws to it self all leaves & straws, & hems of clothes, as Magnis draws iron, as Isid says; li. 16. ca. de rubiis Gemis . And receives soon colour & hue, in what manner a man will. And so it is soon died with the rote of Auense, with Couchinello, as he [?]eth there.

Of Echites chap. 39.

Echites is a stone of India & of Persia, & is found in the cliffs of occean, both in the coasts of the sea of India & of Persia, & is a stone with red colour, as it is said in Lapidario, & there is two sorts, as Hil. says, male and female, & so always two be found in the Eagles nest, & the Eagle may not breed without these stones. And therefore the Eagles keeps these stones in her nest. The male thereof is hard, & is like to a gum that is called Galle, & this male is somewhat blasing. And the female is soft. And these stones bound to a woman that travails of child, makes her soon to be delivered: and makes them sometimes to bear dead children, if it be too long there, & somewhat passes of the private choice of the woman, except these stones be soon taken from her that travails of child, as Isi. says, li. 16. cap. 4. Also this stone Echites contains & breeds another stone within him, like as it were a woman with child: as Dioscorides says. And in Lapidano it is said: that this stone contains another stone, as a woman with child. The virtue of this stone Echites makes a man sober, and augmenteth & increases riches, and so it does love, & helps greatly to obtain & conquer victory, & favour. And prevents and withstands the falling of them, which have the falling evil. If there be any man suspect of fraud of poisoning, if he be guilty, this stoud put under his meat will not suffer him to swalow his meat; and if the stone be withdrawen, he shall not tarry to swallow his meats.

Of Emachite. chap. 40.

Emachites is a red stone and reddy and rusty, & is son in Africa and Iuda & in Arabia. And has that name, for if it be broken with blood, it tourveth anon into the colour of blood, as Isido. says. And is god against flux of the bladder, and for sore eyes, and against venom and against the biting of an Adder, and staunches, the flux of the womb▪ & restrains all bleeding, & namely the blood of women: & of the gums, as Dioscorid. says. And in Plat. it is said, that it is of cold & dry complection, & has virtue to stanch blood. And has that name Emachites, of Emach, that is blood, and Chits, that is stint. And so it helps Emoptoces, men that spue and cast blood, and is speciall remedy for the bloody flux.

Of Eliotropia. chap. 41.

Eliotropia is a precious stone, and is green and sprong with red drops and veins of the colour of blood, and has that name of effect and doing. For if it be put in water before the Sun beams, it makes the water seethe in the vesell that it is in, and resolves it as it were into mist, & soon after it is resolved into rainy drops. Also it seems that this same stone may doe wonders, for if it be put in a Basin with clere water, it changes the Sun beams by rebounding of the air, & seems to shadow them, and breeds in the air red and sanguine colour, and as though the Sun were in Eclipse and darked, the Moon set between vs and the Sun is darkned, and for it seems that it may change the clearness of the Sun, it is called Elitropia, that is turning away of the Sun, as Isidore says expresly & Dioscorides also. And in Lapidario the same meaning is said in this manner.

Ex re nomen habens est in elitropia gemma,
Quae solis radijs in aqua subiecta vacillo.
Sanguineum reddit mutato lumine solem.
Eclipsimque nouam terris effundere cogit.

And Isidore says, that this stone discovers the soily of inchauntenes of witches, that have liking and pride in their own wonders, for they begnile men's sight in those things that they work, as the foresaid stone does: and he sets an ensample thereof, and says, that an herb of the same name, with certain inchauntmentes, beguile the sight of men that look thereon; and makes a man that hears it not be seen. In many other things thy stone is good and commendable, for it staunches blood, and puts away venom, and he that bears this stone may not his beguiled.

Of Enidros. chap. 42.

Enidros is a little stone, and droppeth always, and melts not, nor it is never the less in any wise, and so it is said in Lapidario: Enidros that stone, weeps always as it were by springing of a full well with dropping tears and wells always. And there it is said, that it is hard to tell the cause of these doings. For if the drops were of the substance of the stone, why is not the stone less, or melts away? And if a thing enters into the stone, why is it that that thing that enters, puts not again that thing that goes out, but as seems me, it may be, that the virtue of the stone makes the air thick that is near thereto, and turns it into water. And so it seems that it comes out of the substance of the stone. Nevertheless it comes of the substance of the air that is about the stone.

[Batman's addition] Perpetui fletus lacrimis distillat Enidros.
Qui velut ex pleni fontis scaturigine manat.
Dissolving drops and tears full oft, that Enidros the stone does drop,
Which as out of a fountaine full, does always run and never stop.

Of Episte. chap. 43.

Epistes is a little stone bright and reddy, and makes a man safe that bears it in the heart side. And ceases guiles and decrites, and puts of long Flies and barren fouls, and mist & haile from the fruit of the earth. And if it be set in the Sun, fiery beams some out thereof. And if thou doest this stone in seething water, the seething thereof ceases, & the water cools soon, as Isidore says, and Dioscorides also.

Of Excoliceros. chap. 44.

Excoliceros is a little stone, so named to: it is distinguished and diuersed with forti colours, and is full little, and makes men's even that look thereon to quake. And it is found in Libya among the Treglodites.

Of Iron. chap. 45.

Iron is called Ferrum, and has that name, as Isidore says, of Ferrendum smiting: for it beats and smits, & by hardness thereof, over comes all kind of metal. And though iron come of the earth, yet it is most hard and solid, and therefore with beating and smiting, it suppresses and dilates all other metal, and makes it stretch on length and on breadth. And as Isidore says, it has the name of the land that it is digged in. And therefore it is diverse, for by reason thereof it is found more hard or soft. And after the mind of Aristotle, Iron is gendered of Quicksilver, thick and not clean, full of earthy hoales, and of brimstone great and coarse, and not pure. In composition of iron is more of the foresaid Brimstone, then of Quicksilver, and so for mastery of cold and dry and of earthy matter, iron is dry and cold, & full well hard, and is compact together in his parts. And for iron has less of airy and watry moisture then other metal: therefore it is hard to resolve and make it again to be soft in fire, as Ric. Rufus says. It is the manner to temper iron ware with Oil, least by coldness of water it be too much hardened, as Isid. says. Iron takes soon rust by touching of blood, and if it enters into the substance thereof, scarcely it is cleansed, as Isi says. The rust is a vice fretting iron, & comes thereto either of vncleannes of substance, or of touching of earth, & of near lyng thereto, or else of touching of blood, or of some other quality that is infected. And he says also, that with iron man's blood is spilt, and man's blood takes wreak of iron: For iron takes rust of nothing so soon as of man's blood, if it be [?]ointeb therewith. Iron has agreement with the stone Adamas, & so that stone Adamas draws iron to it self: and so iron drawn, by mean of silver vessel or of Brass, follows the stone, & is subject & obedient thereto. Iron well furnished, & annointed with Alam or with vinegre, shall be most like to Brass. And iron furbushed shall not rust, if it be anointed with Sewet, or with marrow of an heart, as Isidore says. Fiery hot iron if it be oft quenched in Wine or in milke, makes that wine or milke medicinal to them that have the evil of the spleen, and also to other sick men, as Constantine says. Also as Isidore says, iron that is made fiery hot is corrupted, but it is hardened with strokes. Iron that is red in the fire, is not profitable to carve nor to cutte, till it beginne to waxe white. Use of iron is more needful to men in many things then use of gold: though couetous men love more gold then iron. Without iron the comminaltie be not sure against enemies. Without dread of iron the common right is not governed. With iron innocent men be defended: & full hardiness of wicked men is chastised with dread of iron. And well near no handiwork is wrought without iron: no field is cared without iron, neither tilling craft used, no building builded without iron. And therefore Isidore says, the iron has this name Ferrum, for that thereby Farra that is corn and seed is tilled and sown. For without iron [?]read is not wonne of the earth, nor bread is not departed, when it is ready, without iron couenably to man's use. Also iron is whet with iron, as Salomon says. For when the coge of iron is dulled and blunted, with great rubbing it is oft made thin and sharpe and convenable to cut all thing the more easily. Sinder is called Scoria, and is the says of iron that is cleansed therfrom in fire: and has that name Scotia, see it is smitten and departed from the iron by violence of the fire, as Isidore says.

Of Ferrugine. chap. 46.

Ferrugo is the powder that falls from the iron with filing, as squama is that thing that leaps away from the fire with heating, and has virtue to make dry and thin, and therefore it is good against stopping of the spleen. Nevertheless it excites casting and spuing, when it is drunk, insomuch that sometimes it brings to death, but if the violence thereof be ceased with powder of the Adamas stone, or with water, in which the stone lies all night. Also it is said, that the powder thereof is best remedy against Emoroides: and also it staunches the flux of the womb, as it is said in Platea. Also filing or powder of iron is called Rubigo, also Corrosio, & has that name of Corrudendo, fretting and gnawing: but it is properly called Erugo, and has the name of Erodendo, filing, and not of Eramento, as Isidore says. And it infects and defiles the hand[?] that touches it, and makes it soul. The more pure and polished that iron is, the more rather it is defiled with rust, and worst to be cleansed. And rust is not soon done away, but in three manners. By burning of fire, or by fretting of a Salve or a File, or by rubbing of an hard stone, or by scouring of gravel and sand, and pullishing. And rust has this property, that infection thereof comes again by light occasion to the place in which it was first mored and rooted.

Of Gleba. chap. 47.

A Clot is gathering together of powder in a clustre, as Isidore says libro. 15. capit. primo. For earth bound and clonged together, is a clott, and if it be broken and departed, it is powder: Earth hardened into a clot, receives no seed, neither suffers the seed that is there sown to growe and to spring. A clott broken, couereth and nourishes seed, and if it be then tempered with rain, it genders a manner of fatness. & thereof seed takes nourishment and feeding in roots. And grows and springs, as Gregory says. Also sometimes a clot contains in it self a manner of kind of ore. Therefore diverse kinds of things be gendered and come of clots, as Gregory says, super illum locum. 24. Job. Sapphire stones is the place thereof, and clottes thereof is gold.

Of Gemma. chap. 48.

A Precious stone is called Gemma, and has that name, for it shines as Gum, as Isidore says. Precious stones adorn well gold, & make it well fair in diverse colours. Therefore Isidore says, that they be precious, for they be dear & scarce. For all that is scarce and seldom had, is called great and precious, as it is written. The word of God is precious, that is to say, scarce. Isidore says, that no man shall wene, that it is doubtful or false, that God has set virtue in precious stones. For it is said in Lapidario. Ingens est herbis virtus sed maxima gemmis. That is to say, Great virtue is in herbs, but most in precious stones. Of which precious stones some be found in veins of the earth, & be digged with metals. Some be cast out of the bottom of the sea, and the place of their generation is unknowen. And always such precious stones be found in cliffs of the sea, & in sand and in gravel of rivers. And some breed in bodies of souls and of creeping beasts. But from whence so ever precious stones come, they be found endued by the grace of God with passing great virtue, when they be noble & very. For Isidore ca. de Gemmis [?]urens says, in some kind of precious stones if is hard and great difficulty to know between the very precious stones and false. For oft those that be false & guilful, seem most like to them that be true & very, so that scarcely men may know between the true and guilfull. For as he says, sometimes men by craft array the stone that is called Vitrum, and put it forth in steed of Smaragdos, and beguile some men's eyes with false likeness of true & very Smaragdus. For no life of men is without guile, as Isido. says. And we use to cal al manner of precious stones that be not precious & shining, blind. For they be dimmed with their own thickness and boistousnesse. Isidore says, that they that deem so, be oft beguiled: for oft more virtue is hid in a little Adamant with colour of iron and darke, then in Sardio that is most bright, or in Berall.

Of Gagite. chap. 49.

Ieat is called Gagates, and is coarse stone, & nevertheless it is precious: And was first found in Sicily by the river that is called Gagas: and was thrown vp to the brink by the course of water. Therefore it has the name thereof, though most plenty & best be in Britain, as Isi. says: and is double, that is to say, yellow and black. The black is plain and light, & burns soon in fire, & drives away adders with smell thereof, when it is kindled, right as Thus or insence. This gines monition of them that have seendes within them. And is held contrary to seen[?]s: and gives knowledge of maydenhoode. For if a maid drink of the water thereof, she pisses not: and if she be no maid & drinks thereof, she pisses anon, and also against her will, as Dioscorides says: And so by this stone a maiden is anon proved, as diverse Authors affirm. Moreover the same stone both black & also yellow, being made hot with rubbing in the fingers, draws light straw and leaves to it self if it be put thereto, & a drink thereof helps them that have the dropsy: For it wastes evil [?]apours. And it is said, that it comforts the liver, & that is perchance by dryness thereof, & also by private virtue & quality. Also the powder thereof is good to feeble teeth & wagging, & strengthneth & fastneth them. Also it is said, that this stone helps for fantasies, & against veration of fiends by night. Also fumositie thereof excites Menstrua, if it be withdrawn by any day. Also it is said, that it assuages womb ach, when the stomach is misturned by any way. Also it helps against witchcraft, & fordoth hard inchantments, as it is said in Lapidario: and it speeds fast birth, & helps her that travails of child, as it is said in Lapidario. And so, if so coarse a stone does so great wonders, none should be despised for soul colour without, while the virtue that is hid within is unknown. And Isidore speaks of this stone & says, that this stone is kindled in water, & quenched in Oil, and that is wonder.

Of Galactile. chap. 50.

Galactiles is a stone with colour of Ashes, and has sweet tast and savour. If it be smit, a manner of white milke comes out thereof, as Isidore says. This stone closed in the mouth distroubleth the wit. And if it be borne about the neck, it makes breasts full of milke: and if it be bound to the thighs, it makes easy birth, and if it be mingled with water and salt, and sprong about the field: then the sheep be full of milke, and cleanses them of scabs, as Dioscorides says.

Of Gelacia. chap. 51.

Gelacia is a white precious stone, shaped as an haile stone: & it is so cold that it never heats with fire, as Isidore says, and Dioscorides also.

Of Geraticen. cap. 52.

Geraticen is a black stone, but it passes the colour in virtue. For if a man wash clean his own mouth, & bears the stone therein: he may anon tel what other men think of him, as it is said in Lapid. And makes a man that bears it well beloved: his virtue is proved in this manner. If a man be anointed with hony, and is set among many flies, if the stone be present, the flies grieve him not; & if the stone be away then the flies grieve, bite, suck, and hurt the body.

Of Iaspis. chap. 53.

Iaspis is a precious stone, & is green like to Smaragdus: but it is more dim of colour. And there be seuenteene kinds thereof, as Isidore says. For Iaspis that is green, is called Gemma Pinnasin, and though the chief colour thereof be green, yet it has many other colours mingled among. The virtue thereof destroys feaners and dropsy in them that bear him chastlye: and helps in trauailing of child: and drives away fantasies: and makes a man sure in peril, and abates heat within, and staunches bleeding and sweat, and withstands lechery, and prevents conception, and staunches menstruall blood and Emoroides, if it be in powder, and took with milke, it helps and heals old Torches and viles, and cleanses the eyes of foulenesse and filth: And sharps and comforts the sight, and withstands witchcraft and enchantments, and is more virtuous in silver then in gold. In the head of an Adder that is called Aspis, is found a little stone that is called Iaspis: and men suppose that it is a stone of wonderful virtue. And some men suppose that it has that name, as it were Aspis, and men suppose, that it has as many virtues, as diverse colours and veins, as Dioscorides says. And the best Iaspis is found in the mountains of Scythia, & Griphons keep this stone, as they doe Smaragdus, as Isidore says.

Of Iacincto. chap. 54.

Iacinctus is a blue stone some deale, & near of the colour of a Sapphire, and has that name of his own colour, as Isido. says. The stone Iacinctus that is found in Ethiopia is best, and is not too cleere, neither too dim, but mean & temperate between twain, shining. Isidore says, this stone shines not always like, for in cleere wether it is cleere before the eyes, and in darke weather it is dim and darke. Isidore says, in the mouth it seems cold, if it be therein, and is most hard to grave in. Nevertheless it may be engraved and written, and marked with the stone Ad[?]nae, as Isid. says. Dioscorides says, that the stone Iacinctus is now blue, now reddish, now purple, & now bright blue. And thereof be three manner of kinds, some be citrine, & some blue, but among such manner stones, the blue is best. The stone is wonderful, for it consumes it most to the air. For in dim weather it is dim, and in dright weather it is bright. And this stone has a singular virtue, as Authors write: for it gives gladness, and is contrary to melancholy quality: and has virtue of comfort, as Isidore says in the kind thereof. Iacirctus has virtue of comfort, & does away eleignesse and sorrow: and also vain suspition. And withstands diverse pestilences that come often of corrupt air, and gives strength and virtue to members, and life to the sinews, and gives good savor and sweet and wholsome, and is most like to the Sapphire. And Dioscorides says, that all men that treat of precious stones, mean, that the more like the Iacinct is to the Sapphire in colour & in substance, the more virtuous it is. And such Iacinctus withstands venom, and is contrary to poison, as Dioscorides says. Also an herb of the same name is like thereto in colour; and equal thereto in many things, though it be not all alike thereto in valewe, as Isidore sath.

Of Iride. chap. 55.

Iris is a six cornered stone, as Isidore says, and was first found in Arabia, by the red sea, and is now found in many a place, as in Germany, in Ireland, and many lands of the North, & is bright and cleere in colour, & like to Crystal, as Isid. says, and has name Iris of the likeness of the rain bow, for if it be in a house in the Sun, it makes the likeness of the colour of the rain bow, in the walls of the next house, as Isi. says. Men suppose that this stone has the same virtue that Beryl has, but that is not found in quantity, as Dioscorides says. Also it is said, that this stone helps women that travel of child, that they may the sooner be delivered, & better suffer their throws, and dread peril the less.

Of Ienia. chap. 56.

Jena is a precious stone that is found in the eyes of a beast that is called Hiena, & if this stone be put under a man's tongue, they say, that he shall tell many things that shall befall, as Isidore says.

Of Kamau. cap. 57.

Kamau is a stone, now white, now browne, now reddish, now divided with diverse colours, and has that name Kamau of Kamatis, that is to say, burning or kindling, for it is found in places of brimstone and of heat. And Dioscorides says, the virtue thereof heals the Dropsy, and is engraved with diverse Images and shapes.

Of Kalbrate. cap. 58.

Kalbrates is a passing shining stone, like to Crystal: and men suppose, that it gives fair speech and facunditie, worship and grace, and defends from griefes, and from noyous things and venemous, and cures and heals swelling of the liver, and of the spleen, as Dioscorides says.

Of Kalcophano. cap. 59.

Kalcophanus is a black stone, & makes cleere voice, and defends the grief of hoarcenesse, if it be borne in the mouth, as it is said in Lapidario.

Of Ligorio. cap. 60.

Ligorius is a stone like to Electurus in colour, and has that name, as Isidore says of a beast that is called, Linx. This stone Ligerius is gendered among the gravel of the brine of the beast, and the virtue thereof, draws straw to it self, and helps against ach of the stomach, & staunches flux of the womb, that is grieved. And helps them that have the iaundes, and that be discoloured: for it restores colour, that is lost, as Dioscorides says.

Of Lipparea. cap. 61.

Lipparea is a precious stone, and comes out of the countries of Sirtes. The property thereof is, that all kind of wilde beasts come to his presence, & behold thereon. And those beasts, that hunters may not take with running of hounds, they take and allure to them, with the sight and showing only of this stone Lipparea, as it is said in Lapidario.

Of Margarita. chap. 62.

Margarita, is chief of all white precious stones, as Isid. says, and has that name Margarita, for it is found in shells and in shell fish of the sea. It breeds in flesh of shel fish, and is sometimes found in the brain of the fish, and is gendered of the dew of heuen, the which dew shell fish receive in certain times of the year. Of the which Margarites, some be called Vniones, and have a convenable name, for only one is found, & never two or moe together. And white Margarites are better than yellow, & those that be conceived of the morrow dew, be made dim with the air of the even side. Huc vsque Isido. li. 16. And some are found kindly pierced, and those be better than other: and some be pierced by craft, as Plat. says. And they be best, that are white and cleere, bright and round. And they have virtue comfortatiue, either of all the whole kind, as some men say, or els because they are besprinkled with certain specialty, they comfort the lyins. for by constraining and coarting, they cleanse them of superfluous humours, & they help against the Cardiac passion, and against swooning or failing of heart, against feebleness that comes of the bloody flire, & against flire of the womb, as Plat. says. And in Lapidario it is said, that Margarites be gendered of dew, and the more of dew and air that is drawn in, the more and the greater they be. The Margarite is gendered of the dew, but it is supposed, that no Margarite grows passing of half a foot. Also it is said there: if that lightning or thundering fall, when the Margarite should breed of the dew that is drawn in, the shell closes by sodaine fear, and so the gendering falls and is cast out. The best Margarites come out of India, and out of the old Britain, as it is said.

Of Magnet. cap. 63.

Magnes is a stone of India, coloured somewhat as iron, and is found in India among the Troglodites, & draws to it self iron in such wise, that it makes as it were a chain of iron rings, as Isidore says. Therefore in the common speech, this stone is called, quick iron. Also it is said, that it draws glass molten as it does iron. The might and virtue thereof is so great as Austen says, that if that stone be set under a vessel of gold, or of brass, and iron set thereupon, by moving of the stone that is beneath, the iron shall moue that is above. And also there it is said, that in certain Temples is made an Image of iron, and it seems, that that Image hangs in the air. And in Aethiopia is another kind of Magnes, that forsaketh iron, and drives it away from him: also the same Magnes, draws iron to it in one corner, and puts it away in another corner, and the more blue the Magnes is, the better it is. Huc vsque Isid. And Dioscorides says, and also it is said in Lapidario, that this stone reconciles and accords men and their wives, and increases grace and fairenesse, in speech and in words. Also with drink made of honey and of wine, it heals the dropsy, and the spleen, and falling evil, & burnning. If the powder thereof be sprong and done upon coals, in four corners of the house, it shall seem to them that be in the house, that the house should fal anon: and that seeming is by moving, that comes by turning of the brain. Also Magnes is in likewise as Adamas, if it be set under the head of a chaste wife, it makes her sodainlye to embrace her husband: and if she be a spouse breaker, she shall moue her out of the bed sodainly by dread of fantasy. Platea says, that witches use this stone namely. This stone Magnes is hot & dry in the third degree, and has virtue of drawing of iron, and there be mountains of such stones, and therefore they draw to them and break ships, that be nailed with iron. Also namely the powder thereof layde to wounds, helps such as be wounded: for if it be laid to the wound, it draws out iron. Also the powder of Magnes in the quantity of two dragmes, with juice of fenill, is good against the drotsie, and against evil of the spleen, & against failing of the heart, as Avicenna says.

Of Melonite. chap. 64.

Melonites is a stone, and has that name, for sweet juice comes out thereof, as it were honey, as Isidore says: & has two colours, for it is green in the one side, and like to honey in that other side.

Of Menophite. chap. 65.

Menophites is a stone, and has that name of a place of Egypt, and is of the kind of precious stones as Isid. says. If this stone be bruised and ground, and laid upon the place that should be burnt or corued, or els with vinegar smered thereupon, it stonieth so the body, that it feels no sore neither grief of the burning, nor of carving.

Of Mirite. cap. 66.

Mirites is a precious stone, and has that name, for it is like to Mirra, in colour. And if it be wrong and pressed, it smells sweet, as Nardus.

Of Medo. chap. 67.

Medus is a precious stone, & is found in the land of Medes, and is sometimes green and sometimes black, as Dioscorides says. The virtue of this stone is against blyndnesse of eyes, and helps Podagre, if it be tempered with the milke of a woman that has a male child. Also it is good for ache of the reines and for frensie, and if the black stone be resolved upon a skin, and in hot water, and that water be given some man to drink, it slays spewing and ouerturning of the stomach: and if the forehead be washed therewith, it strippeth wonderfully of the skin, and grieves the sight, and takes it away every deale, as it is said in Lapidario.

Of Merochite. cap. 68.

Merochites is a green stone like to Smaragdus: but it is more boystous green than Smaragdus, and so it has the name of the colour of mallows, as Isid. says: and is bred in Arabia, and is full soft in substance, and nevertheless it is full profitable, as Dioscorides says. And it keeps and saves children, from harmful and evil happes.

Of Marmor. chap. 69.

Marble is called Marmor, and has that name of the Greeks, for greenness, as Isi. says. And he says also that marble stones be noble stones, and are praised for speckles and diverse colours: for of marble be endless many manner of kinds, but they be not all hewn out of rocks. But many manner of marble, is found in diverse manner of places under the earth: as marble of Lacedemonia that is green and precious. And marble is called Ophites, for it is speckeled like an Adder, & has therefore that name: & there is double kind, soft & white, and black and hard. And marble Purpurites comes out of Egypt, & is reddy with white points among: & has that name Purpurites, for it is red as Purple.

There is other manner kinds, as Alabastrum and Parium: thereof we shall speak innermore. Also another manner kind is called Coraliticum, and is found in Asia, and passes not two cubits in measure, and is white near as yuorye, and some black speckles, in diverse proportiens. Also another kind is called, Thebaicum, and is sprong with golden specks, and is found among Egyptians, and is kindly apt to grinde colours thereupon, and Collyria ointments that help the eyes. Other kinds of marble breeds in quarries and in rocks, as Marble that is called, Marmor Corintheum. Thereof is made great Pillars, pavements, and Towers. Also there is another manner kind, as Caristium, and is green and best: and has that name, of aspect, for men that grave love it wel, for green colour comforts the sight. Another kind is called Numidicum, & breeds in Numidia, and makes a thing that is rubbed therewith like to saffron, and has that name therefore. Lib. 16. Isidore sets ensamples of many other diverse Marbles: but these are sufficient at this time. But Marble is more hard and solid, more strong and fair, and more profitable than other stones. In veins thereof is diverse master found, and precious stones, and for hardness thereof, it is most hardly engraved & polished, and for coldness and solidiousnesse thereof, it is best to keep in spicerie, and ointments. Over all things we may wonder, that Marble stones be not hewed neither cloven with iron neither with steele, with hammer nor with sawe, as they be with a plate of lead, set between soft shingles or spones. For with lead & not with iron, Marble stones be hewn, and cloven, and plained, as shingles or small stones.

Of Nitro. chap. 70.

Nitrum (as Isidore says) is a stone some deale white, and may be hewn and cloven, and is full cleere. The virtue thereof dissolves and tempers, draws and cleanses, and wastes superfluities of humours. Lib. 16. cap. 2. Isidore says, that Nitrum has the name of the country of Nitria that is in Egypt. Thereof is medicine made, & there with bodies and clothes be cleansed and washed. The kind thereof is not far from the kind of salt, for it has that kind of salt, and is made right as salt in dryness in old cliffs.

The foam thereof, is called Affronitom, and is gendered dropping down in the country of Asia, and then dried with heat of the Sun, and what is best dried, is least heavy as Isidore says. And Platea says, the Nitrum is a veyne of the earth, and is hot and dry, light, red, or white or citrine, & is bitter, sour, and some deale salt in savour. Nitrum abates falnesse. If it be taken in the mouth, and consumes and wastes gleimie humours. Powder thereof consect in honey, clarifies & makes the face fair, & cleanses scabs and matter of the stomach and of the guts, if it comes of a postum, and cleanses lice, and head scabs, and slays worms of the ears, cleanses most perfectly the matter and scabs thereof. Fome thereof with vinegar, heals gnawing and swelling, and helps against the dropsy, and cleanses dimness of eyes, if it be mingled with honey, and slays venom, and destroys it, and withstands mightely the palsy of the tongue, as Plat. says and Dioscorides also. And Nitium is hot and dry in the ende of the third degree, and laxeth and cleanses, as Isidore says.

Of Noset. cap. 71.

Noset that is Orapondine, is a precious stone, some deale white, or of diverse colours. It is said that this stone is taken out of a Toads head, and is cleansed in the same head, & in strong Wine and water, as Dioscorides says: and sometimes the shape of a Toad seems therein with sharpe feet & broad. This stone helps against biting of Serpents and of creeping Worms, and against venom. For in presence of venom, the stone warms and burns his finger that touches him, as Dioscorides says.

Of Onichino. chap. 72.

Onichinus is a stone of India, and of Arabia, and has in it self colour mingled like the nail of mankind.

The Onix of India has colour of fire, with white veins & streaks, and the stone Onix of Arabia, is black of colour, and has white veins. And thereof if five manner of kinds: one is Sardonix, and has that name of company of two, of whiteness of the Onix, and redness of the Sardonius, as it is shown innermore of Sardonic. It is said, that this stone Onix has many harmful effects, for as Dioscorides says, if it be borne about the neck, or on the finger, it excites sorow, eleingnesse, and dread, and multiplies plea and strife, and mooueth the heart to contention and debate, and excites in children harmful superfluity of spettle, and may not grieve in presence of the stone Sardius. This stone Onix is cleere of the kind of mirrors, & therefore images & figures be seen therein, as it were in a mirror, but that is darkly, as Dioscorides says.

Of Optallio. cap. 73.

Optallio is called Oppalus also, and is a stone distinguished with colors of diverse precious stones, as Isid. says. Therein is the fiery colour of the Carbuncle, the shining purple of the Ametistus, the bright green colour of Smaragdus, and all the colours shine therein, with a manner diversity, and has the name of the Country. This stone breeds only in India, and is deemed to have as many virtues, as blewes and colours. Of this Optallius, it is said in Lapidario, that this stone Optallius keeps and saves his eyes that bears it, cleere and sharp and without grief, and dimineth other men's eyes that be about, with a manner cloud, and smits them with a manner blindness, that is called Amentia, so that they may not see neither take heed what is done before their eyes. Therefore it is said, that it is the most sure patron of thieves as it is said in Lapidar.

Of Orite. chap. 74.

Orites is a precious stone black and round, and mother manner kind thereof is green with speckles. The third manner of kind is white in the one side and plain in the other, & is in substance as it were a plate of iron. This stone borne upon a man, keeps him from biting of creeping worms, and of wylde beasts and other. Also this stone prevents a woman to conceive, if she bears it about her, and makes her to be delivered anon without due manner, if she be with child.

Of Petra. chap. 75.

A Stone is called Petra, and Petra is a name of Greek, and is to understand solid or steadfast, as Isi. says: for the substance of a stone is gendered, of solid and hard parts of the earth. A stone has this name Petra of Penetrando, pearcing: for he pierces the foot, when it is hard thrast and trod on: and is also pierced with drops of rain and of water, that falls down of spouts and of gutters. A stone has another name, and is called Lapis, and has that name of Ledens hurt and grief: for it hurts the foot with the hardness and sharpness thereof, as Isi. says. But commonly, a plain; soft, or a round stone is called, Lapis. And a stone that is hewn out of mountains is called Saxum. And a hard flint stone is called Silex, and has that name of Exihre, for fire leaps out there of Isid. says, though a stone be most cold of himself, yet fire comes out thereof when it is smitten with iron: For by strong violence and smiting of the air, between the iron and the stone, the ayre lepeth sparckling out of the flynt. Quarto Metheor. Aristotle says, that a stone genders not of earth alone, for dryness has mastery therein, and suffers it not to run. Stones are made either by congellation, or els by conglutination. By mingling together of water and of earth is clay made, that is mean between earth and stone, and turns into stone some and some, by constraining and fastening of parts, and so slimy clay is most meet for such transmutation: for if it it be not fatty, it will all to fall by mastery of dryness, and not contain and holde together.

Then by strong mingling and fastening of humour, earth turns into kind of stone, and takes diverse colours of the diverse quality of the earth. Also sometimes stone is gendered by freesing of water. In some place water shedd on the ground, turns into stone of diverse colours, and that by some virtue of ore, that is in that place, and freezes the water that is the matter of stones, as Aristotle says. Stone is bred of fat clay, by virtue of the sun that makes the fen privately run, and the parties cleave and fasten together. Other stones be bred of water frosen, by some virtue of ore, that brings therein kind & shape of stone, and be matter of stone, & some is soft and feeble of composition, and some strong: & some be gendered swiftly, and some slowly, and some strongly, as the might of the qualities that work more or less, and as the qualities that let and withstand, be less strong or more, as these qualities be mingled in substance of stones. Stones be diverse in virtue and in kind: For influence of heuenly virtue comes into their places, and puts therein the effects thereof, and after as it finds matter more able and obedient to his working, the more noble impression it prints therein. Therefore precious stones follow virtues of kind of Planets on effect and working, as it fares of Topasius, that follows the M[?]ne, as it is said, as Ambrose, Basilius, Isidore, Dioscorides and other tell, as it shall be known heereafter. The known properties of stones be these: for generally a stone is cold and dry, solid and fast hard and heuie, and moves downward by his own heaviness and weight, and lies heavy on the earth, and joins and mines together the parts of the earth, for it should not break and depart a sunder. Therefore Ambrose says, that stones be the bones of the earth: for stones doe in diverse parts of the earth, as bones doe in the body, for stones make the earth solid and sound, and holde the parts together; and helps that it falls not nor departs asunder, by ouermuch dryness thereof. Stones be not made soft with lauing and washing with water: nevertheless they be bored & made hollow with dropping of gutters, as Gregory says. Also if fire hot stones be quenched in wine, they corrupt the wine, and turn it into vinegere, as Isidore says. Also in stones generally needs purenes of matter, virtue, and precious colours, diverse figure and shape, and many manner moe profits: for stones be needful and profitable, to making and building of houses and of walls, of pavements & of bridges, & to put off enemies, wolves, and hounds, and other evil beasts, and to draw metal out of the substance thereof, and to healpe and heal men of diverse sicknesses and euills, and so make and to increase Towers of Kings, to build and to strengthen Cities, Castles and Towers, and for defence against wilde beasts. Also stones are first taken out of the quarry, and then be bewed, playned, and squared, and laid and set in order in work of building, the more under the less, and set together with Cement, and covered without, and playned with cement.

[Batman's addition] (At a place called Sutton in Kent, and at Boughton, are found a kind of stone, as if it were clusters of Periwrinkles growing together: which stone being wrought and polished, shows beyond Art, the shape of those shell fishes, after a curious and cunning manner. Which stones, if they were not to be had, but far off in some other Country, would here be esteemed of great price, & hard and fair Marble greet.)

Of Pario. chap. 76.

Parius stone is a kind of noble marble and precious, as Isidore says. This stone is found in the Island Paron, and is therefore called Parius. The quantity thereof, passes not Lances and Crateras, and is good to keep men's spicerie and ointments. The Gloss super Esay says, that Parius is a kind of most white marble, and betokeneth therefore chastity.

Of prassio. chap. 77.

Prassius is a stone as green as a leek, and comforts the feeble sight, and is sometimes found with red drops, and is sometimes distinguished with white drops. Of this stone it is said in Lapid. that no profit is therewith, but that it is green, and makes gold seemely.

Of Pirite. cap. 78.

Pirites is a red bright stone, like to the quality of the air: much fire is therein, and oft sparkles come out thereof, and this stone burns his hand that holds it right fast, therefore it has that name of Pir, that is fire. In Lapid. it is said, that he will easelye and softly be handled and held, for it burns if it be hard wrong.

Of Pionite. cap. 79.

Pionites is a stone that is said to be of female kind, as it is said. At certain time it conceiueth and bears such another stone, and helps women with child as Dioscorides says.

Of Panteron. cap. 80.

Panteron is a stone of diverse colours, sprong and distinguished, for it is seen black, red, green, pale, purple, yellow, & also bright green in colour. This stone makes a man bold and hardy, so that he shall not be overcome that day that he seethe this stone early at Sun rising, as it is said in Lapidario.

Of Plumbo. cap. 81.

Lead is called Plumbum, & has that name, as Isid. says de metal, lib. 16. for first with balles of lead, men assay deepness. He says that of lead are two manner kinds, white and black, and the white is the better, and was first found in the Islands of the sea Athlant in old time, and is now found in many places, for in Fraunce & in Lusitania is a manner black earth full of gravel, and of small stones, and is washed and blown, and so of that matter comes the substance of lead. Also in gold quarries, with matter of gold, be small stoanes found, and be gathered with the gold, & afterward departed from the gold, and blown by themselves, and turns all to lead, and therefore gold is as heauye as lead, as Isid. says. But of black lead is double kind, for black lead comes alone of a veyne, or is gendered with silver in mingled veins, and is blown, and in blowing, first comes tin, & then silver, and then what remains, is blown and turned into black lead, as Isi. says. But in India is neither brass nor lead found: but India is rewarded again with Margarites and precious stones.

Isido. says black lead is best in trauellous works, as in pipes and plates, and that Spaniards and Britons have proved. Huc vsque Isid. Quarto li. Metheo. Arist. says, that of brimstone, that is coarse and not swiftly purified, but troubly and thick, and of Quicksilver the substance of lead is gendered, and is gendered in minerall places, so of uncleanness of vnpure brimstone lead has a manner softness, & smooches his hand that touches it. And with wiping and cleansing this uncleanness of lead may be taken away for a time, but never for always, a man may wipe off the uncleanness, but always it is lead though it seem silver. But strange qualities have mastery therein and beguile men, & makes them err therein. Some men take Sal Armoniacum, as Aristotle says, and assigns cause of this uncleanness, and says, that in voystous lead is evil Quicksilver, heanie and fenny. Also that brimstone thereof is evil vapour and stinking.

Therefore it freezes not well at full. In li. 5. Alchimie Hermes says, if thou hang lead over vinegar, it hurts it, for vinegar shall pearce the substance thereof, & turn it into powder, and into white colour of flour of lead: and if thou powrest vinegar thervpon, it comes white, & destroys the might of vinegar. Burnt lead breeds red colour and dim, and if the fire be too strong, it turns into Citrine, and thereafter, with stirring, dryeng, and tempring with vinegar, it turns into white colour of flour of lead.

And if then makest strong fire, it turns all into the first matter of lead, & is made earth, as it is said there. Also lead mingled with other metal, gathers together the kind parts of the metal, & departs and cleanses away the other parts. Therefore lead is put with silver in the furnace, that the silver may be sooner cleansed: for the lead saves the silver parts from wasting, and the lead is wasted and burnt, and cleanses the silver, Jerome. says the same in Glosa super. 6 ca, Fere ibi consumptum est plumbum, &c. Also Hermes says, the lead in boiling, undoes the hardness of all solid and hard bodies, and also of the stone Adamant. And in li. Meth. Arist. speaks of lead and says that lead without doubt when it is molten, is as Quicksilver, but it melts not without heat, and then all that is molten seems red. Wonder it is, that though lead be pale or brown, yet by burning or resudation of vinegar, often it genders seemly colour and fair, as tawny, red, and such other: therewith women paint themselves, for to seem fair of colour. And Lead is good for medicine: For Lead is cold and moist in the second degree, as Constant. says, and helps wonderfully against burning of fire and hot postumes, and staunches bleeding wounds, and staunches and abates running of the eyes, & assuages smiting of Scorpions & Dragons, and prevents the service of Venus, if a plate of lead be laid to the 2. reines, and has other medicinal reasons.

Look thereof innermore De coloribus, of the colour that is called Stibium and Cerusa, and is also called flour of lead.

Of Puluere. chap. 82.

Powder is called Puluis, and has that name for it is puf[?]e with the wind, as Isi. says, for lightness thereof the wind takes it vp and blows it abroad.

Contrary windes comes into powder, and bears it about, & makes the whirl wind as Beda says. Powder beaten, shows the kind of the thing that it comes of: now by savour, now by colour, now by odour and smell. Therefore powder is made of spicerie, that the inner virtue thereof may show it self, that by medlyng and oning of parts, it may receive the larger virtue, that it may give foorth the more fragrant smell, and cause the sweeter taste, and that it may the more effectually dry and cleanse putrified wounds, & eate and fret away the dead flesh, that it grow no more, as powder of burnt lead dries and cleanses away superfluity of wounds, & genders and saves quick flesh, as Const. says. And therefore of powder plaisters, be made confections that be called Thimiamata, & other diverse medicines, and ointments: Powder is made of Thus, and of Mirrhe: with burning into powder, beasts were offred to our Lord God. Also of powder is made noble electuaries, that helps and heals bodies that be alive: also of powder is ointment made, that keeps and saves dead bodies, that they rot not, nor fall into powder: also sen is made by mingling of powder and of moisture, and turns into a clot by working of dryness, as Isid. says. Also powder defiles the face and grieves the eyes, and many manner worms breed of powder and be sed therewith, and powder is bread to the serpent, as Esa. says li. 12. Of powder, bodies with soul have beginning, and ends and turns into that it comes off, as it is said to man. Powder thou art, and into powder thou shalt turn. Also powder infects the air, and prevents often the eye, that it may not see the clearness of the Sun, for of powder mingled with moist air, oft the myst is gendered between vs and the Sun, whose coming hides the sun, that it is not clearly seen. Also moies in the Sun beam comes of powder, and the powder is not seen in the sun beam that comes in at the window.

Of Quirin. cap. 83.

Qvirin is a stone that is found in Lapwings neasts, as Dioscorides says. This stone bewrayeth and discovers in sleep counsayle and priuitie: for this stone laid and set under a man's head that sleeps, makes him tell as he thinks sleeping, & multiplies wonderfully fantasies. Therefore Witches love that stone, for they work Witchcraft therewith.

Of Quandros. cap. 84.

Qvandros is a stone of vile colour, but it is of great virtue, as Dios, says, and is found in the head of a vulture: and helps against all evil causes, and fills teats full of milke.

Of Rabri. cap. 85.

Rabri is a stone, & has another common name, and is called Bolus Armenicus, and is a stone or a veyne of earth, & is of cold and dry kind, & has a red colour, and is found in Armenia, & has virtue to constrain & make hard the womb, and to staunch blood out of what place of the body it runs.

[Batman's addition] Of Rubies.

The Rubies grow in India, and are found for the most part in a river named Pegu. These are of the best kind and finest, which they of the land of M[?]labar call Nunpuclo, and are well sold, if they be fair and clean without spots. In the Island of Zeylath, being in the second India, are found many Rubies, which the Indians name Manecas: the most part of these, are pale, and fleshly coloured, and very few in perfect beauty, which is as the red Rose and orient. If abiding their first triall in the fire, they grow to a sparkling cleere coale shining, called of the Greeks Anthrax, which signifies a coale of the Latins called, Carbunculus. Their values in the Indies are 50. shillings, but being brought hither, are sold for much more.

Of Reyben. cap. 86.

Reyben as Avicenna says, is a little stone and is found in a Crabs head, and is sometimes whife, and is sometimes some deale yellow, and is soft in substance, little harder than the black of a fishes eye, and is in shape round & plain without, and some deale hollow within, and is in virtue kindly cold & moist, and helps against biting of Scorpions, and of the wesell, if it be beaten & laid thereto in plaister wise. Also it is said, it helps against the biting of a mad dogge, if the powder thereof be taken in drink. If the powder thereof be burnt, it cleanses teeth, and dries wounds, and helps scabs, and prevents tears.

Of Saphiro. cap. 87.

Saphirus is a precious stone, and is blew in colour, most like to heaven in fair wether and cleere, and is best among precious stones, and most precious and most apt and able to fingers of Kings, and is found in many places, but that that is found in the East or in India is accounted best, and namely if it have as it were powder of gold mingled therein, and this Sapphire stone is thick and not passing bright, as Isi. says. This stone is most praised in Lapid. And for it is so noble and so excellent, it is called Gemma gemmarum, as it were chief of precious stones, for it lightens the body, and keeps and saves lims whole & sound, and has a bright star, and by brightness of that star, his virtue is known. Another manner Sapphire is called Sirtites, and is found fast by the place that is called Sirtes, among the gravel of the sea Libicum, as Diosc. says, & is also found in the veins of mines, where Lasurium is also found, and in the same veins of Sapphire, in the middle, as it were in the womb, is a certain kind of Carbuncle found. Therefore many men deem, that the Sapphire is the Carbuncles mother, for many men mean, that the Carbuncle is gendered in the Sapphires veins, and many men mean, that the Carbuncle is some deale beshadowed with a certain blue sparkle of the kind of the Sapphire, as Diosc. says: & Diosc. says that the Sapphire has virtue to rule & accord them that be in strife, & helps much to make peace & accord. Also it has virtue to abate unkind heat, therefore in Lapid. it is said of the sapphire, that it cools heat of the body within. For the Sapphire cools much the heat of burning fevers, if it be hanged near the pulse & the veins of the heart. Therefore he says, y[?] it helps against much fevers, y[?] have time of changing in access time. Also it has virtue to comfort & to glad y[?] heart, therefore it is said, that it helps against the Cardiac, & against all melancholik passions. Therefore it staunches running and sweat that comes of anguish, & other sweat also, as it is said in Lap. He staunches sweat that runs swift, &c. Diosc. says the same, & has virtue to staunch blood. And so a Sapphire of the East staunches bleeding at the nose, if it be laid to the temples. Also he has singular virtue to assuage blowing: For certain it is, that the Sapphire abates & assuages swelling of postumes, if it be soon laid thereto in the beginning of noiful drawing. Also the Sapphire helps against an evil postum, that is called Antrax, for it puts out the might of ventosity & mallice of that Postum, for it overcomes & puts out the madness thereof, & suffers not the smoak thereof come to the heart, nor the malice thereof to infect the spirits, as Dioscri. says. His virtue is contrary to venom, & quenches it euerye deale. And if thou put a Spider in a box, & hold a very Sapphire of India at the mouth of the bore any while, by virtue thereof the Spider is overcome & dies, as it were suddenly, as Diosc. says. And the same I have seen proved oft in many & diverse places. His virtue keeps & saves the sight, & cleanses eyes of filth without any grief: therefore it is written in Lapid. that it takes away filth out of eyes, and ach of the forhead. Also this virtue heals biles and botches: For as Dioscor. says, this stone beaten into powder, & mingled with milk, heals wounds, & this also has ben proved in wounds by experience. Also this stone was of so great authority in old time, that men held that it it was most worthy stone to their God, & so it was singularly hallowed to Apollo, for when nations asked counsel of Appollo in time of sacrifice, they hoped to be certified, & to have aunswere sooner, if a Sapphire stone were present, as Dioscorides says. And this is touched in Lapidario.

And they that use Nigromancie mean, that they have answer of God more thereby, then by other precious stones. Also Witches love well this stone, for they ween that they may work certain wonders by virtue of this stone, and also this is touched in Lapid. where it is said. This stone brings men out of prison bonds, & undoes gates and bonds that it touches. Authors write those doings and many other of the singular virtue of the Sapphire, & al Authors accord in this point & say, that the Sapphire is a precious stone, & loueth chastity, & therefore least the effect thereof be let in any wise by his uncleanness that him bears, it needs him that bears it to live chaste, as this story means, but he that it bears, is commanded to be most chaste. Also in Lapidario it is said, that this stone does always envy, & puts off dread & fear, and makes a man bold and hardy, & master and victor, & makes the heart steadfast in goodness, and makes meek & mild, and goodly. I ween that all this is said more in disposition then in effect and doing. But this suffices at this time.

Of Smaragdo. cap. 88.

Smaragdus of al green precious stones is the chief, as Isidore says. Men in old time gave thereto the third dignity after Margarites and unions. Smaragdus has that name of green colour, as he said there. For it is said that all green things is bitter. In no herbs nor in precious stone is more greenness then in the stone Smaragdus. It passes herbs & grass, twigs & braunches: And infects the air about it with passing green colour: And his green colour abates not in the Sun in any manner wise. Nothing comforts more their eyes that be engravers, then this stane: If the body thereof be straight and cleansed or polished, then Images be seen therein as it were in a mirror. Cesar Nero used to see fighting of sword players in this stone, as Isidore says. Thereof be 12. manner of kinds, but the most noble are found in Scythia, and in Bactria holds the second place: and Smaragdis be found among & under stones, and in chinnes thereof, when the Northern wind blows, for then the earth is vncouered, and Smaragdis shines among the stones, for in such wind gravel and sand is most moved. The Egyptians have the third. Other be found in metall or ore of brass, but they be gleyming, for they have speckles like to brass, or to lead, or to salt. Though the Smaragdus be green by kind, yet if it be mingled with wine or with oil, his green colour increases. There is a manner Smaragdus that goes out of kind though it be green, for it is somewhat unseemly by beines of brass, and is called Calcesmaragdus. Hue vsque Isid. li. ca. de viridibus gemmis. This stone is taken of and from Griphons, and plenty of Smaragdus may not be found: for great Griphons let the coming of men by the way that goes thereto, as Isid. says li. 13. cap. 3. And this stone multiplies his green colour, of him comes a beam that dies the air about him, and makes it green. The body thereof is cleere and of glassy kind, & shows figures, images and shapes of things that be near thereto, and has of gift of kind & goodness of virtue to heal diverse sicknesses and euills. Dioscorides says; it increases riches, & makes men have good words and fair evidence, in caose and in plea. If this stone be hanged about the neck, it helps the falling evil, and saves and comforts feeble sight, and represseth wanton motions of lechery, and makes good mind, and helps also against al fantasies & japes of fiends, and ceases tempest, and staunches blood: and it is said, that it helps them, that use to divine and gess what shall befall, as it is said in Lapid.

Of Sardio. cap. 89.

Sardius is a precious stone of red colour as it were red earth, & has that name, for it was first found in Sardis, as Isid. says, and the Gloss sup. Apoc. Though this stone be precious & fair, yet many account it least in value of precious stones: for as they mean, except shining, there is no profit therewith, but only that the stone Onix may not grieve in his presence: for as it is said, Onichinus, that has some evil properties, may not show them in deed, in presence of the stone Sardius. And Dioscorides says, that over this virtue, Sardius has many other virtues. Of Sardius be five manner of kinds, but the best comes out of Sardis, and is good: for it increases ioyes, and puts away dread, and makes men bold and hardy, and sharps the wit, and in his presence Onix may not grieve. Also he says, that Sardius, that is all red, saves his bearer from inchantment, and from witchcraft.

Of Sardonic. chap. 90.

Sardonix has that name, of company of two stones, of Sardius and Onix. as Isi. says, and is of three colours. For black is lowest, white the middle, red as vermilion is highest. This stone only takes nothing of the substance of the waxe, when it is printed therein, and is found in Arabia and in India. Hereof be five manner of kinds, but which of them has most colours and most divided, & the thickest, is best. It is said that it puts of lechery, and makes men meek and chaste.

Of Solis gemma. cap. 91.

The Sun stone is called solis gemma, and is white shining, and has that name for it shines with beams, as the Sun does shine in the world, as Isidore says De candidis gemmis.

Of Silenite, cap. 92.

Silenites is a stone of Persia, & is green as grass. His colour is like to the stone Iaspis, as it is said in Lapidario, and shines with a white speck, as though in brightness it contained the shape of the Moon: and the stone Silenites follows the Moon, and wexeth and wanes as the Moon does, as Isi. says, and Diosco. also. His virtue reconciles love and accord. It is supposed that it helps Phthisic and sick men.

Of Stanno. chap. 93.

Tin is called Stannum, and is a metal, and has that name of Etimologic of Greek as Isid. says. Tin departs, for in fire it departs metals of diverse kind, and it departs lead and brass from gold and silver, and defends other metal in hot fire: and though brass and iron be most hard in kind, yet if they be in strong fire without tin, they burne and waste away: if brazen vessels be tinned, the tin abates the venom of rust, and amends the favour. Also mirrors be tempered with tin, and white colour that is Cerusa is made of tin, as it is made of lead. Huc vsque Isidorus li. 16. cap. de Metallis.

Lib. Metheororum Aristotle says, that tin is compouned of good Quicksilver and of evil brimstone. And these twain be not well mingled but in small parts compounded, therefore tin has colour of silver, but not the sadnes thereof. In li. Alchemy Hermes says, that tin breaks all metals, & bodies that it is mingled with, & that for great dryness of tin. And destroys in metal the kind that is obedient to hammer work. And if thou medlest Quicksilver therewith, it withstands the crashing thereof, and makes it white, but afterward it makes it black and defiles it. Also there it is said, that burnt tin genders red colour, as Lead does: and if the fire be strong, the first matter of tin comes soon again. Also though tin be more soft then silver, & more hard then Lead, yet lead may not be soon soudred to lead nor to brass, nor to iron without tin: neither these may be soudered without greace or Talow, and Rosin.

Of Sulphur. cap. 94.

Brimstone is a vein of the earth, and has much air and fire in his composition, therefore it is called Sulphur, as it were Solum vrens burning of the ground. Fire is called Vr. for the firye virtue of brimstone is known in fervent waters, for water that runs and passes by veins of brimstone, takes whiteness or heat thereof, fauorinesse, effect, and smell. And hereof it comes that hot wels springing out of the earth bring therewith the qualities of veins of brimstone. And nothing is so soon set a fire as Brimstone, and breeds in the hot Island Eloijs between Sicily and Italy, and some men mean, that those Islands burne, and Brimstone is found and digged in other places, as Isidorus says: of Brimstone there be four kinds. One is called Viuum, the which when it is digged shines and flourishes, the which all only among all the kinds thereof, Physicians use, as Isidore says. Another is called Gleba, and serves only for fullers. The third is called Liquor, and is good and profitable to cast and sparple on Wooll: for therewith they make soft and white: the fourth serves to light of lanterns, as Isidor. says. And says thereto, the the virtue of brimstone is so great, the certain sicknesses are perceived with brightnes thereof. If the flame thereof comes straight into a man's face, it breeds foul and evil paleness to their sight that look thereon, to the likeness of dead men, as Isid. says, li. 16. cap. 1. Avicenna & Pla. mean, that brimstone is hot & dry in the fourth degree, & is turned into kind of brimstone in part of water, of earth, & of fire, and that brimstone is sometimes great & coarse, & full of dross, and sometimes pure, white, cleere and subtle, and sometimes mean between both.

And by this diverse disposition, diverse metal is gendered of Brimstone and of Quicksilver, as it is said 4. Metheororum. There it is shown that Brimstone and Quicksilver is the matter of metals. Some Brimstone is called quick brimstone, such as it is when it is taken out of the earth: and some is dead Brimstone or quenched, and is made by craft, and put in potts or in other vessels for medicine. The best is the quick with heat bright & shining white, or green without stone, and that makes green colour, and if it be put in the fire, it has virtue of tempering and departing, of consuming and wasting, and of making subtle and thin, and of restoring. Therefore it prevents the cough, and helps them that have the falling evil, and cleanses scabs, and withstands venom, and awaketh men that have the sleeping evil, and helps for the gowtes, Podagre, & the palsy, if the remedy thereof be used in due manner and medicinal, as Avicenna, Dioscorides, Platearius, and other Authors say.

Of Sale. chap. 95.

Salt is called Sal, and has that name of Saliendo, leaping: for it leaps out of the fire, and flis the fire, though it be fiery kind, as Isidore says. Other men mean, that it has that name Sal, of Sale, or of Sole, of the sea, or of the Sun. For it is gendered of sea water by working of the Sun: for some of the Sea abides at cliffs, and is dried with the Sun, and is sometimes drawn out of salt pits, and sodde till water turn into hardness of salt, that was fleeting before, and so made hard and thick with heat. And is sometimes gathered among gravel and Sande in waring of the Moon by night. For oft in Cerenia salt is found under Gravel and Sand. Also in some places be rocks of Salt, and out thereof stones be hewn with iron, that turns afterward into kind of Salt. As it fares in Arabia, & in Pannonia. Also those stones be so hard, that they make houses of them, and the common salt craketh & sparks in fire, leaps out thereof, but Sal agrigentinum of Sicily suffers fire, and melts in fire against kind, & starts and leaps out of water. And salt is diverse in colour: for Sal memphiticum is red. In a country of Cicllia, where mout Etna is, is pure salt. In the same Sicily in Pathmos is so bright & cleere salt, the Images be seen therein. In Capado, is yellow salt digged and mined, as Isidore says. Also salt varies, and is diverse in savour as he says. For in some place it is sweet in savour, and in some place most salt, and in some most bitter. And the more bitter salt is, the more hot it is, or is the more hotter deemed, as Avicenna says. Salt is most needful, for without Salt near all meat is wearish and vnsauory. Salt makes Potage and other meat sauorie, and excites good appetite in all meat. With salt al meat is made savory and liking. Men ween it has this name Salt of the Sun: for nothing is more profitable then the Sun & salt. And so we see diverse beasts come to pasture most for liking of Salt. Also milke and theese be the more abundant, for goodness of salt. Also salt hardneth and dries things, and keeps and saves dead bodies from rotting: Huc vsque Isidorus. libr. 16. cap. 3. Also Plat. and Avicenna tell, that Salt has generally virtue to vndoe, cleanse, and waste rotted humours. Also to depart and destroy ventositie, and namely if powder of salt be sod and laid all hot in a bagge to the mouth of the stomach. Also this virtue joins and saves kind moisture in the body, & wastes & destroys vnkinde moisture therein. And so water of Salt wells dissolves and wastes swelling and boiling, and also the Dropsy, as he says. Also salt frets away dead flesh, and namely if the Salt be burnt. For then it withstands best rotting: and dries, cleanses, & thirieth into the inner parts, as he says. Also salt softens the womb, and brings out supersluitie, & namely salt that is called Gemma, and has that name, for it is cleere as a precious stone, & works wonderfully in ordeining of the guts. And softens what is hard, and putieth out superfluity, and so does Armoniacum & common salt also. Also salt mingled with honey, bread, and wine, heals the Postum Carbunculus or Antrax, as Avicenna says. Also Salt does away speckles of the face, if it be tempered with water, and Camphora, and the face be washed therewith. Also Salt cleanses the body of scabbes and Traters, namely if it be mingled with Sope. Also Salt heals the venimous biling and stinging of Scorpions and creeping worms, if it be mingled with honnye and nuts, and with other certain things, as Aucien says. Salt has these virtues and many moe, that were too long to reckon all arowe: but these shall suffice at this time.

Of Topazio. cap. 96.

Topazius is a precious stone, & has another name Topazion indeclinable, as Isidore says, lib. 16. And is of shining kind, and shines with all colours, and was first found in an Island of Arabia in which Island when the Troglodites were diseased with hunger & tempest, they digged vp roots of herbs, and they found this stone therewith, and called it Testam nebulis. After that ship men sought and found the stone, and called it Topazim in the language of Troglodites. Therefore this stone that was so sought & sound is called Topazius, and has that name of the Island. Topazim in their language, is to say Siche, & is greatest of precious stones. Plinius wrote, that a stone of this kind was found so great, that Philadech made thereof an Image of four cubits long. In the Gloss super fixem Apost. it is said in this manner. The more scarce Topazius is, the more precious it is. And has two colours, as it were of gold and of cleere air, and shines most when it is smit with the Sun beam, & passes in clearness all other precious stones, and comsorteth men and beasts to behold and look thereon. And if thou wipe this stone, thou darkest it, and if thou leadest him to his own kind, he is the more cleere. And in treasury of kings, nothing is more cleere nor more precious then this precious stone. For clearness thereof takes to himself the clearness of other precious stones that be about him, and it is said, that he follows the course of the Moon: and helps against the passion Lunatik. And so it is said, the as the Moon is more full or less, so his effect is more or less, as it is said in Lapid. and staunches blood, and helps them that have Emoroides, & assuages fervent water, & suffers it not so boil, as it is said in Lapidario. Dioscorides says, that it assuages both wrath and sorrow, and helps against evil thoughts and frenzy, and against sudden death. And has the shape of a mirror, aud the Image that is therein, is seen in a hollow mirror.

And of these are small Diamonds, counterfeit.)

[Batman's addition] Of the Turquesses. cap. 97.

Tvrquesses are found in Exer, in a place of Siech Ismael. Their mine is a dry earth, that is found upon a black stone, which the Moores take of in small pieces, and carry them to the Island of Ormus, from whence they are brought to diverse parts of the world by sea and by land. The Islands call them Perose. They are soft stones, of small weight, & not much cold, and to know that they are good and true, in the day they shall appear like the cleere sky blue, and by candle green, and the best sorts are not without some black spotte of the Mine whereon they grewe. The Indians know none other virtue but this fayrenesse.

Of Turgot. chap. 98.

Tvrgotes that is called Torcois also, is a white yellow stone, and has that name of the Country of Turki, there it is bred. This stone keeps and saves the sight, and breeds gladness and comfort.

Of Terra Sigillata. Chap. 98.

A Certain vein of the earth is called Terra sigillata, and is singularly cold and dry. And Dioscorides calls it Terra saracenica and argentea, and is some deale white, well smelling & cleere. The chief virtue thereof binds and staunches. And powder thereof tempered with the white of an Egge, staunches bleeding at the nose: and helps against swelling of the feet, and against the gowte, if it be laid in a plaster thereto, as it is said in Lapidario.

Of Tartaro. chap. 99.

Tartarum is Wine drastes, and like to a soft stone, cleaning hard to the sides of the tunnes. Whose kind is hot and dry in the third degree, and is good against scab and scall, and uncleanness of the head: and it has virtue to make thin, to waste, to cleanse, and to laxe, as it is said in Plat.

Of Vitro. chap. 100.

Glass (as Avicenna says) is among stones, as a fool among men. For it takes al manner of colour and painting, and is called Vitrum, as Isi. says. For by his virtue he is bright and cleere, & light shining, there through all that is conteined inward in other mettalles and in veins of earth is hid. In glass all manner liquor is seen outward such as it is within, & is shown as it were to closed eyes, that look thereon, as Isidore says. And glass was first found beside Tholomeida, in the cliffe beside the river that is called Vellus, that springs out of the foot of mount Carmelus, at which shipmen ariued. For upon the gravel of that river shipmen made fire of clots mingled with bright gravel, & thereof ran streams of new liquor, that was the beginning of glass, as Isi. says. Now glass is made of ashes of trees and of herbs, with strong blast of fire, with the which it is mingled, now glass, now brass, & now both, & so turns into glass. When glass is molt in the furnace & perfectly cleansed, then it takes pureness, brightness, & clearness. Glass is died with all manner of colour, so that it follows Iacinctus, Smaragdus, & other precious stones in colour & brightness. Also it is so pliant that it takes anon diverse and contrary shapes by blast of the Glasier, & is sometimes beaten, and sometimes engraved, as silver, as Isid. says: and no matter is more apt to make mirrors then is glass, nor to receive painting. But most workmanship is in white glass, that is next to Christal in colour. For it is often chosen before silver & gold to drink in as Isi. says. Also there he says, that the stone Obseanus is reckoned among kind of glass. And this stone is sometimes green, & sometimes black, & is cleere and bright. And is called Specularis, and is with fatly sight. Of this stone many men make precious stones, as Isidore says. And all manner glass has this property, that it is most pliant, while it is melting hot and soft, and most brittle when it is cold and hard. And if it be broken, it may not he amended without melting again. But long time passed, there was one that made glass pliant, which might be amended and wrought with an hammer, as Isidore says. And brought a Viole made of such Glass before Tiberius the Emperor, and threw it down on the ground, and was not broken, but bended and folded. And he made it right and amended it with an hammer. Then the Emperor commanded to smite off his head anon, least that his craft were known. For then Gold should be no better then fen, & all other metal should be little worth, for certain if glasen vessels were not brittle, they should be accounted of more value then vessels of gold, as Isid. says. Then glass is clean and pure, and specially bright & cleere. And Images & shadows be seen therein, and is pliaunt when it is melting hot, and brittle when it is cold and hard. And receives all colours, and follows precious stones more in colour then in value, and cleanses away superfluity and filth as Avicenna says. Powder thereof cleanses the teeth, & does away weue of the eyes, and helps greatly against the stone of the bledder and of the reines, if it be drunk with Wine, as Avicenna says.

[Batman's addition] (But the powder of glass must be very fine, else will it frett a sunder the guts and suddenly kill the patient, for the powder made of glass mixed in Butter, writ and Mice, it may as soon kill men, take some other medicine)

Of Ydaci. cap. 101.

Ydachites is a precious stone of red colour, and round in shape. And has another stone within him, and sounds by tinking thereof. Nevertheless wise men mean, that tinkling is not the inner stone, but some spirit that is within, as Dioscorides says: This stone sweats water, so that it seems that it contains a manner well spring within it self. Therefore some men tell, that this is the stone that is called Enidros. Look before in the same book.

Of Yrachite. cap. 102.

Yrachites is a stone, the man that bears it shall not be bit wish any flies, neither stung with Bees, as Dioscorides says. And so men ween, that it helps against venom.

[Batman's addition] (That is when he can get such a stone, that has such a virtue.)

Of Zimiech. chap. 103.

Zimiech is a stone or vein of the earth, whereof Lapis Lazurij is made; as it is said in Lapidary. The more liker this stone is to the colour of heaven, the better it is, and has small pieces, as it were of gold mingled thereon. Those that be whitest be most earthy, and therefore they be not so precious. These may be long kept without corruption: and help against many evils, and against swooning, that comes of melancholic fumositie, if they be given in due manner to the patient. It shall never be given without it be ground full final, & oft washed full clean. And the sign of perfect washing thereof is, if the water that it is washed in, be little or naught died. This stone cleanses & looses wonderfully the quarteine, and shall not be given with the decostion. For then it should fall to the bottom, but after or before, & that, with whey, as Dioscorides says, as it is said in Plat. And as Cerusa is made of plates of iron: so is Lazurum made of plates of silver with vinegar. Oft the stone Lazurius is found among silver ore, & among gold ore. And oft in the veins of Lazarius be found Sapphires, Iacincts, and other such blue stones.

Of Zingutte. cap. 194.

Zinguttes is a stone with glassy colour, if it be borne about the neck, it helps against Victipolam. And staunches blood, and puts off raving and frenzy. And if it be held to a tree that burns, it quenches the flame thereof as Dioscor. says.

[Batman's addition] (There are many mo stones, whose hidden virtues are unknown for want of experience, which grow in fishes, beasts, birds, & men, as the stone in the Crabs head, the stone that grows in the Snail, and the stone called Bulgoldo[?], sort of a beast in Indes, and there are good against all po[?]sons, the stone that grows in the maw[?] of an Ox; or in the gall, the stone that is dred in the kindness of a man, and all these serve to great and secret uses.)
