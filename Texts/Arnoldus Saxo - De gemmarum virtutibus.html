Arnold of Saxony — On the Virtues of Gems

To remove the uncertainties and errors many people have about gems, stones, their images and their powers, I have labored for the common benefit. I have selected the most useful, best and most notable material scattered through Aristotle, Aaron, Evax, king of the Arabs, and Dioscorides, and arranged a brief lapidary for beginners and advanced readers alike, as I have done in my accounts of other things and according to what I have tested. Stones have properties that are not attributed to any mixture. When the first simple constituents are mixed, a single power arises from them, as the attracting power in the magnet, which draws iron from one side and repels it from the other. In this way different specific powers are attributed to different gems, stones and their engraved images.

Abeston (asbestos). A stone of iron color, brought from Arabia. Its power is that, once kindled, it is never extinguished.

Abscintus. A black gem with red veins. Once kindled, it retains heat for seven days.

Adamas (diamond). A stone darker than crystal, with a gleaming color. It is dissolved by neither iron nor fire, but by the bloody flesh of a he-goat or by lead. It penetrates iron and gems and draws iron to itself. It is larger than a hazelnut. The Arabian, Cyprian and iron-bearing kinds are softer and darker. Its power, when set in gold or silver or iron, breaks the nature of the magnet. Worn on the left arm, it protects against enemies, madness, the untamed, quarrels and disputes, poisons, vain dreams, phantoms and incubi.

Agathes (agate). A black stone with white veins. One kind resembles coral; there is also a Cretan kind with saffron-colored veins, and an Indian kind, mottled with blood-red drops. The first kind is suited to depicting royal figures and images seen in dreams. The second, Cretan kind overcomes dangers and makes its bearer welcome, pleasing and persuasive, good in color and eloquent; it gives protection and strength. The third kind strengthens sight and helps against thirst and poison; when burned, it gives off a fragrant odor.

Alabandina. A gem named after its region. It has a bright, clear red glow like sardius. Its power is to provoke and increase the flow of blood.

Alectorius. A stone resembling dark crystal. It is taken from the stomach of a capon after its fourth year; it is about the size of a bean. It stirs sexual desire and makes its bearer welcome, steadfast, victorious, discerning and eloquent. It reconciles friends and, held in the mouth, quenches thirst.

Amandinus. A varicolored stone. It extinguishes every poison, makes its bearer victorious over opponents and a good interpreter of dreams.

Amethyst. A purple gem, like a rose, brought from India. There are five kinds, but this one is more useful than the others. It is soft enough to carve. It protects against drunkenness, keeps a person alert, drives away evil thoughts and gives good understanding.

Androdamanta. A silver-colored stone from the Red Sea. Its shape is like a gaming piece and its hardness like diamond. It counters fury and agitation of the mind.

Balagius. A red, translucent gem, inferior to carbuncle; its color and all its powers are weaker and milder.

Beryl. A pale stone like water, hexagonal in shape, brought from India. There are twenty kinds, but the palest is best. It protects against dangers from enemies and against lawsuits. It makes a person invincible and gentle, and grants good judgment; it helps against laziness, liver pain, sighing and difficulty urinating. It heals watery eyes, though it burns the bearer. If set before the sun and turned, it kindles fire. It magnifies a person and brings about marriage.

Chalcedony. A pale stone. It should be pierced together with the power of the sineritis stone and worn around the neck. It counters demonic illusions and helps its bearer prevail fully in lawsuits against opponents.

Carbuncle. A very red gem that shines in darkness like a coal; in Greek it is called antrax. It comes from Libya. In color and powers it surpasses all fiery gems, for it alone has all the powers found in them. There are twelve kinds; the rest are less useful than this one.

Calcofanes. A black stone. It clarifies sight and prevents hoarseness.

Ceraunius (thunderstone). One kind resembles crystal and is tinged blue, coming from Germany; another comes from Spain and glows like flame. They fall from places struck by thunder. They help bring sweet sleep, prevail in battles and win lawsuits. Every place where they are kept is protected from lightning and storms.

Celydonius. There are two kinds, black and red, taken from a swallow’s stomach. They are small. The red one is wrapped in linen or calfskin. Worn under the left arm, it counters madness, old illnesses and the falling sickness, and makes its bearer eloquent, welcome and pleasing. The black one counters harmful humors, fevers, anger and threats. Washed in water, it heals the eyes and brings a task to its intended end. Wrapped in a celandine leaf, it dims sight.

Celonites. A purple and green stone sent by the tortoise. Whoever carries it under the tongue becomes a diviner. This power is present only as the new moon begins to wax and when it has waned for twenty-nine days. The stone is not harmed by fire.

Cegolitus. A stone like an olive pit. Dissolved in water and drunk, it heals kidney and bladder stones.

Coral. There are red and white kinds, like little branches, taken from the sea. It helps against every flow of blood and epilepsy. Worn around the neck, it protects from vain apparitions, demons, lightning, storms and hail. Sprinkled over crops, it multiplies the fruit and helps bring undertakings to a successful start and finish.

Cornelian. A dark red stone, like the washings of flesh. Worn around the neck or on a finger, it calms anger and helps against every flow of blood, especially menstrual bleeding.

Chrysoprase. A stone like leek juice, shining with golden drops and brought from India. It clears sight, drives away greed and gives perseverance in all good things.

Chrysolite. A sparkling golden gem brought from Ethiopia. Set in gold and worn on the left arm, it counters demons and nighttime fears, drives away melancholy and foolishness, and grants wisdom.

Crystal. A stone hardened more by earthly force than by cold, whose color resembles ice. When cold, it catches fire if placed in the sun’s rays; when hot, it does not. It counters thirst and burning. Ground with honey and drunk in milk, it fills the breasts with milk.

Chrysolechus. A golden-colored gem, most beautiful to the eye in the morning and entirely different at other hours. It catches fire quickly.

Chrysopasion. A gem from Ethiopia. It shines in darkness and vanishes in the light. Its color shifts without settling, like rotten oak and a glow-worm.

Demonius. A two-colored stone. It helps those with fever, drives away poisons and makes its bearer safe and victorious.

Dyacodes. A pale stone like beryl. It strongly stirs demons and phantoms, but loses its power when applied to a dead person.

Dionysia. A black stone sparkling with red drops and smelling of wine. Its smell drives away drunkenness.

Ethytes. A crimson-colored stone found on the shores of the Ocean, in an eagle’s nest or in Persia. It contains another stone. Suspended on the left arm, it helps pregnant women and counters fever; it increases wealth and love, makes its bearer victorious, lovable and safe, prevents falling sickness, miscarriage and the labor of childbirth. If you suspect someone of poisoning you, place the stone beneath that person’s food: if guilty, the person cannot swallow it. Once the stone is removed, the food will at once be swallowed.

Heliotrope. A green gem like emerald, with blood-red drops, brought from Ethiopia, Cyprus and India. Set in water and exposed to the sun, it makes the water in the vessel boil and dissolve into mist; after a short time, the mist brings rain. It gives its bearer a good reputation, enables some foretelling, and keeps the bearer safe and long-lived. It helps against bleeding and poisons. If an herb of the same name is applied, a person can escape being seen by deceiving the eye.

Emathytes (hematite). A red, rust-colored stone from Africa, Ethiopia and Arabia. It heals bladder discharge, eye pain, snakebite, flux of the belly, excess flesh, menstrual bleeding and spitting blood. It heals wounds and ulcers, weak sight and rough eyelids. Its power is binding.

Enydros. A stone that continually drips. It never fully melts or grows smaller.

Epystrites. A gleaming red stone born in Bymari. Worn over the heart, it keeps its bearer safe and restrains riots. It keeps locusts and birds, barren mists, hail and whirlwinds from the crops. Held up to the sun, it gives off fire and rays. Thrown into boiling water, it stops the water from boiling, and the water later cools.

Ecacolitus. A varicolored stone. Placed in wine and drunk with it, it helps against iliac and colic illness.

Exacontalitus. A very small stone distinguished by sixty colors, found in Libya and among the Tragodytes. It makes a person’s eyes tremble.

Falcanos. Arsenic and orpiment are one and the same stone, red and citron-colored. It has the nature of sulfur: it heats and dries. Applied to something, it turns it white; it burns every metallic body except gold.

Filaterium. The gem Filaterium and chrysolite are one and the same and have the same power.

Gagates (jet). A very light stone, either gray-blue or black, found in Libya and Britain. Heated by rubbing, it attracts straw and burns easily. It helps those with dropsy and strengthens teeth. Its fumigation restores menstruation, removes falling sickness and drives away snakes. It helps against disorders of the belly and phantoms, and benefits those afflicted by demons. If water in which it has been placed is drunk, a virgin will not urinate, but a non-virgin will; in this way virginity is tested. It also helps women in labor.

Gagatromeus. A varicolored stone like goat skin. Its bearer is made victorious over enemies.

Galacia. A white gem shaped like hail. It is always cold and is never warmed by fire.

Galactydes. A stone like ash, sent from Nybas and Acholeos. It gives taste and milk to nursing women. Held in the mouth, it unsettles the mind. Worn around the neck, it fills the breasts with milk; tied to the thigh, it eases childbirth. If the sheepfold is sprinkled in the evening with water mixed with salt, the sheep fill with milk and their scabies is driven away.

Gerachites. A black stone. Whoever carries it in the mouth becomes a judge of thoughts and opinions and is made loved and beloved. Its power is tested this way: flies leave untouched a naked body anointed with honey and exposed to them, but if the stone is taken away, they suck the body and ruin it.

Jasper. A gem of many colors; the best is green and translucent. There are ten kinds, but this one is the most useful. It should be set in silver and comes from many regions. Worn, it drives away fevers and dropsy, hastens and helps childbirth, makes its bearer welcome and powerful, keeps the bearer safe and drives away phantoms. It restrains lust, prevents conception and helps against bleeding and menstrual flow.

Water hyacinth. A red or blue gem with three kinds: garnet, citron and Venetian. They are cold and very hard, and the Venetian is best. […] It helps against [reading uncertain]. Worn around the neck, it brings safety, strengthens the limbs, induces sleep and invigorates the nerves.

Sapphire hyacinth. It brings wealth, grants natural ability and joy, and counters poison.

Jena. A stone which, carried under the tongue, enables its bearer to divine and foretell certain things.

Jyrim. A crystal-like, hexagonal stone brought from the Red Sea. Exposed to the sun’s rays, it paints the nearby wall with the varied colors of a rainbow, such as red, yellow and citron.

Lygurius. An amber-colored stone formed when material drips from a lynx’s groin and solidifies. It attracts straw and helps against stomach pain, jaundice and flux of the belly.

Lypparia. A stone from Scythia. Its property is that every kind of wild beast quickly rushes to its presence and looks at it.

Pearl. A white gem taken from sea shells. Some are pierced, and thunder causes miscarriages in them. The best are sent from India and Britain. […] It helps against [reading uncertain].

[…] A passage about a green stone and its use in water is partly readable; the claims concerning eyes, kidneys and stomach are corrupt. […] Whoever drinks its water dies with vomiting and an upset stomach.

Molochites (malachite). A green, soft gem like emerald. It guards the body against harmful accidents, and likewise protects infants’ cradles.

Glass. A whitish, transparent stone. Its power is to dissolve and attract, and it helps against jaundice.

Nycomar or alabaster. The same white stone. […]

Onyx. A black gem; another kind is black with white veins. It is sent from Arabia and India. There are five kinds according to the veins and colors. Worn around the neck or on a finger, it stirs sadness, fears and phantoms in sleep; it multiplies quarrels and disputes and increases children’s saliva. In the presence of sardius, onyx does no harm.

Optallius. A stone that protects its bearer against every eye disease and preserves sharp sight; it blinds and clouds the eyes of all who stand nearby.

Orites. There are three kinds: a black, round one; a green one with white spots; and one rough on one side and smooth on the other. Its body is like a plate of iron. Worn and mixed with rose oil, it protects the body from misfortune and deadly bites of reptiles and other animals. Hung on a woman, it prevents conception; if she is pregnant, she miscarries.

Pantherus. A stone with many scattered, distinct colors: black, green, red, pale, purple and rose. It makes the sight dim.

Peanites. A stone born in Macedonia, of the female sex. At a certain time it conceives and gives birth to a similar stone. It helps pregnant women.

Prassius. A striking green gem that strengthens weak sight. One kind has blood-red drops, and a third is marked with white drops.

Quitin. A stone found in the hoopoe’s nest. It betrays secrets in sleep and increases phantoms.

Quanidros. A stone taken from a vulture’s brain. It helps against every harmful condition and fills the breasts with milk.

Ramuy. The same as Armenian bolus, a reddish stone. Its power is to bind the blood in dysentery and menstrual flow.

Radaym and Donatites. The same black, translucent stone, found in the head of a male rooster when it is given to ants to eat. Worn, it helps a person obtain anything.

Sapphire or Syrtites. The same gem, gleaming like the pure flame of heaven. The best is dense and not translucent. It is sent from India; another kind comes from the Syrtes. It makes its bearer chaste; heals pain in the forehead and tongue; removes wounds and eye dirt when smeared with milk; restrains sweat and cools inner burning. It appeases God and reconciles peace, loosens bonds by touch, makes a person bold, and removes envy and fraud. It strengthens the body and preserves the limbs intact; it makes the mind steadfast in good, gentle and humble, and serves magical arts.

Sardonyx. A black, white and red stone, with the red most prominent. The better kind has these colors more distinct and dense. There are five kinds, of which this one is the most useful; it is brought from Arabia and India. It drives away lust and makes a person chaste and modest.

Sardius. A bright red gem. There are five kinds, but this one is the most useful; it is brought from Sardis. It kindles joy, drives away fear, makes people bold and sharpens the mind. In its presence, onyx does no harm.

Sadda. A leek-green gem brought from Chaldea. It adheres to certain ships at sea and cannot be scraped off without the piece of wood to which it is attached.

Selenite. A gem like green grass, the color of jasper, born in Persia. It follows the moon’s motion: it grows as the moon waxes and shrinks as the moon wanes. It reconciles love; worn, it heals the sick and weak.

Emerald. A very green gem with twelve kinds: Scythian, British, Nile-grown, those found in copper veins, and [some names corrupt in the edition]. Scythian emeralds are best. […] A flat or concave shape is better. It increases wealth and gives more persuasive words in lawsuits. Worn around the neck, it heals quartan fever and falling sickness, strengthens weak sight and keeps the eyes unharmed; it restrains lustful impulses and restores memory. It helps against demonic illusions and enemies, turns away storms, and aids those who wish to divine and foretell certain things.

Topaz. There are two kinds: one the color of pure gold, the other a paler color. It is found in Arabia and on the island of the same name. It helps against hemorrhoids and the falling sickness; calms anger and sadness; helps against harmful impulses, frenzy and sudden death. It restrains boiling waves and prevents them from bubbling. It shows an image received from outside, reversed as in a concave mirror.

Turquoise. A yellow stone tending toward white, named for the region of Turkey where it is found. It preserves sound sight and, while kept on the person, protects from harmful external accidents; it brings cheerfulness.

Varach or dragon’s blood. According to Aristotle it is a stone; according to physicians, the red juice of a plant. It helps against every flow, especially bleeding. From it and quicksilver, algala is made.

Vernix. An Armenian stone of a whitish color. It helps against melancholy and disorders of the spleen, liver and heart.

Viriles or pyrites. The same fiery, gleaming gem. It should be touched lightly; if pressed too hard, it burns the fingers that touch it.

Zimech or heavenly stone. Yellow, with little golden particles, from which blue pigment is made. It helps against melancholy, quartan fever and fainting caused by melancholic vapors.

Zigrites. A glass-colored stone, the same as evas. Worn around the neck, it helps against noxious epilepsy, binds blood and drives away mental derangement. If held to a fire, the flame is extinguished.

If you find a ram, lion or archer engraved on a stone, those stones are fiery and eastern. They make their bearers pleasing to God and people, free them from daily fevers and dropsy, and make them eloquent and clever. If you find Gemini, Libra or Aquarius, these stones are warm, airy and western. They free their bearers from fevers and paralysis and make God favorable. If you find Cancer, Scorpio or Pisces, these stones are cold, watery and northern. They protect the bearer from hectic fever and [reading uncertain]; they are under perpetual consecration. If you find Taurus, Capricorn or Virgo on a stone, these are cold, southern stones that always keep their bearers safe from asynochia.

If you find a seal with a winged horse called Pegasus, it is excellent for soldiers, giving courage and speed in open battle. It is also said to free horses wearing the stone from illness and swelling of the legs. It presides over Aries and is called bellorifon, “source of wars.”

If you find Andromeda, a girl with loose hair and relaxed hands, the stone bearing this image has the power to reconcile love between a man and a woman, even between adulterers.

If you find a carved maiden seated on a chair, her hands extended in the shape of a cross within an angle above her head, the stone is comfort after labor and rest after illness. It restores tired, weak bodies and keeps them in perfect health.

The Serpent-Bearer, girded with a serpent whose head his right hand holds and whose tail his left hand holds, frees anyone from poison, whether the stone is worn or drunk before or after eating.

If you find Hercules kneeling, holding a club in his right hand and killing a lion or another monster, whoever carries this stone in open battle will be victorious.

If you find a bear or a serpent dividing two she-bears, the stone makes a person clever, strong, steadfast and pleasing to God and everyone.

A stone bearing Saturn with a sickle in his right hand makes its bearer powerful, and its power continually grows during his life. Saturn is a dark man, with little hair in his beard, unattractive and unsmiling.

Another stone bears Jupiter in human form with a ram’s head. Whoever has it will be loved by every creature and will obtain whatever he asks of another. It has wrinkles at the heel, a slender chest and scattered hair.

Another bears Mercury with wings on his feet and a staff in his left hand, like a coiled serpent. Whoever has it will be adorned with such wisdom and eloquence that no one can resist him; he will enjoy lasting health and be pleasing to God and all people. Mercury is a slender man with a beautiful, sparse, small beard and delicate lips and nose.

If anyone finds a serpent engraved on a stone, with a vessel on its back and a raven above its tail, whoever has the stone will abound in all good things and be clever and prudent about signs. This stone is believed to resist every harm. It has a crab beneath which it sends its head, directing its head up to the Centaur.

If anyone finds a stone bearing a centaur who holds a hare suspended by a knife in his left hand and a staff in his right, with a little animal fixed to the staff and suspended by a pot, the stone gives its bearer lasting sweetness or health. Hence it is said that the centaur was Achilles’ teacher because the young man carried this stone.

If you find a seal shaped like a shrine, a capsule containing sacred objects, the stone adorns its bearers with lasting virginity and makes them pleasing to God and people, even if a scorpion’s tail follows.

If you find a sign at the end of Pisces and the beginning of Aries, with a great trumpet on its back and another thing on its belly—namely, a serpent with a crested head above and below—whoever has this stone will be happier and more capable than all others on land and sea. If anything has been taken from him, he will recover it.

If you find a ship with a tall, fully spread sail, you will be safer in every undertaking. If you find a hare carved on a stone, no diabolical spell can harm you as long as you keep it with you. If you find Orion holding a sickle or sword, at the end of Taurus, you will be victorious in every battle. If you find an eagle that presides over Capricorn’s horn, the stone will preserve old honors and gain new ones. If you find the sign that presides over Aquarius, the stone will surely free you from paralysis and quartan fever. If you find Perseus holding a sword in his right hand and the head of the Gorgon in his left, God willing, it will protect its bearer from lightning, storms and all demonic envy.

On any stone, if you find a seal of a stag, dog, hare or hunter—or, on an asp, a stag, hunter, dogs or hares—the stone can heal the demon-afflicted, the frenzied, the melancholic and the lunatics.

On any stone, if you find Mars armed or Venus standing, holding laurel and wearing a great robe, the stone grants beauty, ease, power and perfection, and removes fear of water.

On any stone, if you find a lion, dropsy and cold illness will not harm the person carrying it. […]

If you find [an unclear figure] and a woman holding an apple in her right hand, her left hand placed against her breast like a square tablet, and on the other side of the stone a human image with a bird’s face and eagle’s feet, with certain letters engraved there, the stone has the power to reconcile love between a man and a woman. [The locus is corrupt in the digitized transcription.]

If you find a man holding a carved palm in his hand, the stone makes its bearer powerful and makes rulers favorable, cheerful and reconciled toward him.

On any stone, if you find the moon or sun carved or impressed in any way, the stone shows how to live and act well.

These stones were assigned in the temple of Apollo by the king of the Persians, on the counsel of all the astrologers, both Egyptian and Chaldean, according to the course of the signs and the hours of the planets.
