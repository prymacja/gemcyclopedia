Murra

NamesMurra; ¹ Murrina; ¹ Murina. ²
AppearancePurple and white varieties; ¹ ² other forms mix purple, white, and fiery red, with rainbow-like reflections. ²
Found inParthia, especially Carmania. ¹ Murrhine vases were brought to Italy. ²

Murra, also called Murrina and Murina, is formed from moisture or liquid compressed by heat. ¹ It appears in purple and white kinds, and in a mixture of purple, white, and fiery red colors that reflect like a rainbow. ¹ ² It is used to make beautiful vases.

Generic

Description

Murra is thought to be liquid compressed by heat beneath the earth. ¹ It is also said to form when the earth’s moisture is condensed by the sun’s heat. ² Its varieties include purple and white, while other descriptions give mixed purple, white, and fiery red colors with reflections like a rainbow. ¹ ²

It is useful for making vases, whose beauty is greatly prized. ² Pompey was the first to bring murrhine vases to Italy. ²

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, XII.6

Open cited source

Murra (murrina) is found in Parthia, but especially in Carmania. People think that it is a liquid that has been compressed by the heat under the earth, whence it takes its name. Its varieties occur in purple and white, and it has a fiery look, with reflections of colors such as are seen in a rainbow. Crystal is created by the opposite principle, when there is an extremely strong freezing of ice.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Murina

Open cited source

Murina is a stone of various colors all mixed together, such as purple, white, and fiery [red], with a kind of reflection one of the other, as seen in the rainbow. It is found among the Parthians. They believe that it is generated in the humidity of the earth condensed by the heat of the sun. Its virtue is not mentioned by the learned, but it is useful for vase making. Pompey was the first who brought murrhine vases to Italy, which were greatly prized for their beauty.
