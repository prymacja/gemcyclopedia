The Median Stone, also called Medius and Medus, is found in Media and has black and green kinds; ¹ the green kind is called Medinus. ³ It strengthens the weak and is said to treat chronic gout, dimness of the eyes, and kidney troubles. ² The black kind restores sight when prepared with a woman’s milk, and heals gout when ground with ewe’s milk and applied to the body; ¹ taken by mouth, it is deadly. ¹ ³ Other preparations heal or cause blindness and death. The green kind aids sight when prepared with gall ink, a Magnet, and rainwater. ³

Generic

Description

The stone is named Medius or Medus for the country in which it is found. ² It has two varieties, one black and one green; ² the green kind is called Medinus. ³ The Median Stone from Media’s plains is described as black. ¹ Its virtue is called “white” when it heals and “black” when it harms. ¹

Properties

Medius is said to be good against chronic gout, dimness of the eyes, and kidney troubles, and to strengthen the weak, weary, and feeble. ²

Subtype I: Black Median Stone

The image shows Onyx (source).

To restore sight, grind the stone in a green marble mortar with milk from a woman who has borne her first child. ¹ The remedy is said to restore sight even to a person blind for many years. ¹

For gout, mix the stone with ewe’s milk from a sheep that has borne a male lamb. ¹ It is also said to heal the liver and injured kidneys. ¹ The preparation is to be stored in glass or silver and taken fasting.

For harm, take a piece of the mortar, mix it with the stone, and grind them together. ¹ Drop the mixture into water and offer it to an enemy to wash the brow; this is said to seal the person’s eyes in eternal darkness. ¹ If the person drinks it, the source says the lungs are torn and death follows.

Fragments of the black kind dissolved in hot water are said to peel the skin if someone washes in the water. ² If the water is drunk, the person dies from vomiting.

Subtype II: Green Medinus

Grind the green kind with gall ink, a Magnet, and rainwater, then place it on the eyes for seven days. ³ This is said to aid sight and let the wearer discern things so small they are almost invisible. ³

Sources

1. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, XXXVI

Open cited source

The Median Stone dug up in Media's plains At once a source of health and death contains: This in a mortar of green marble brayed With woman's milk now first a mother made, Will to the blinded eye restore the sight Although for many a year denied the light. Mixed with ewe's milk that once has borne a male It remedies the gout's tormenting ail: It heals the liver in the panting breast. Or injured reins by racking pains opprest; Store it in glass or else in silver pure, And take it fasting 't is a sovereign cure. But yet if thou to harm thy foeman seek, With it a deadly vengeance canst thou wreak: Do thou a fragment of the mortar take And mix with this and both together break, Then dropped in water offer it thy foe And bid him bathe as with a wash his brow; Forthwith eternal darkness seals his eyes. Or if he drink, with riven lungs he dies. Black is the stone, not so its virtue shews: 'Tis white to heal us, black to slay our foes.

2. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, Medius

Open cited source

Medius is named from the country of the Medes, where much of it is found. There are two varieties of it, one black, the other green. They say it has power against chronic gout, and dimness of the eyes, and kidney troubles. And it is said to strengthen those who are weak and weary and feeble. They say that if fragments of the black kind are dissolved in hot water, and anyone washes in that water, the skin peels from his body; and if he drinks it, he will die of vomiting.

3. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Medus

Open cited source

Medus is a stone that takes its name from the country in which it was found. There are two species: black and green. The green is called medinus. If the black is placed in a green mortar and is liquefied in the milk of a woman who gave birth to a boy and is placed on the eyes, it restores lost sight. If ground in the milk of an ewe who has given birth to a male lamb, it heals the gout if placed on the affected parts; taken by mouth it is a deadly poison. Hence this stone is rightly said to give death and health. The green is called medinus; if ground and mixed with gall ink and with some magnet and rainwater, and then placed on the eyes for seven days, it aids sight and makes one discern things so small they are almost invisible.

4. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 67: Medo

Open cited source

Medus is a precious stone, & is found in the land of Medes, and is sometimes green and sometimes black, as Dioscorides says. The virtue of this stone is against blyndnesse of eyes, and helps Podagre, if it be tempered with the milke of a woman that has a male child. Also it is good for ache of the reines and for frensie, and if the black stone be resolved upon a skin, and in hot water, and that water be given some man to drink, it slays spewing and ouerturning of the stomach: and if the forehead be washed therewith, it strippeth wonderfully of the skin, and grieves the sight, and takes it away every deale, as it is said in Lapidario.

5. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Medus — Median Stone

Open cited source

The Median stone comes from the region of the Medes and is somewhat green. Dissolved in the milk of a woman who has given birth to a boy, it restores sight to the blind and cures gout. It also cures those suffering from frenzy when dissolved in water and drunk, and causes the lung to be vomited up. Whoever washes his forehead with this water becomes blind. Evax says that the stone is entirely black.
