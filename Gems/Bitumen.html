Bitumen comes in two forms: floating clumps gathered from Lake Dead Sea (also called Asphaltites) in Judea, and boiling mud that rises from the earth in Syria. ¹ The two condense into a dense, fire-related mass that resists water and iron and serves to caulk ships. ¹

Generic

Description

Bitumen comes forth in Lake Dead Sea (also called Asphaltites) in Judea, where sailors gather floating clumps in skiffs. ¹ In Syria it rises from the earth as boiling mud. ¹ Both forms gather and harden into a dense mass. Bitumen is fire-related and cannot be split by water or iron; ¹ menstrual blood alone can split it. It is used to caulk ships. ¹

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI

Open cited source

Bitumen (bitumen) comes forth in the lake Asphaltites (i.e. the Dead Sea) in Judea; sailors in skiffs collect floating clumps of it as they draw near. In Syria it is a mud that boils up from the earth in various places. Both kinds are compacted and combine into a dense mass, and the Greeks call both πισσάσφαλτος (pissasphaltos; i.e. a compound of asphalt and pitch). Its nature is burning and related to fire, and it cannot be split by water or iron, but only by female impurities (i.e. menses; see XI.i.141). It is useful for caulking ships.

2. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 20: Bitumine

Open cited source

Glew is slimy gleab of the earth, and gleaming and gendering, namely in marsh ground. And (as Isidore says, lib. 16. cap. 2. it is a manner of gleaming earth, and is found beside the lake Asphalti in Judea. The kind thereof is burning and of fiery kind, and holds so fast, and is so clongie, that it is not broke with water nor with iron, but only with menstruall blood, and it is good to joining of ships. Of this Glew is mention made in Plat. in this manner. Asphaltis glew of Judea is hot and dry in the third degree, and is earth of black colour, and is heavy and stinking. And some men mean, that it is earth gendered of the foam of the dead sea, and is made hard as earth, and has virtue of drawing, and sodering, and fastening, and consuming, and is full good to heal and close, and to sowder wounds, and Botches, if it be beat to powder and put in a dry wound, though the wound be full long and full wide. Also it is good to help the passions of the mother, that pierces and pierces the spiritual members, if it be put vppon coals, & the stinking smoke thereof be drawn in at the nose and at the mouth. Also it helps flumatike men, and sleeping Litergicis, that have the sleeping evil: For it purges well fleam out of the head, as Platea. says.
