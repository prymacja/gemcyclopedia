Sapphire

Sapphire

The image shows Sapphire Gem.jpg (source).

NamesSaphirus, Sirites, Sirtites, Sapphire, Homisitos, Sapphirus, Ornicus, Celonia, Sanctus Lapis, Holy Stone, Xiphino, Syrtites, Syrtitis. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ ⁹
AppearanceBlue, azure, purple, yellow, or clouded; ¹ ³ ⁵ ⁶ kinds include royal gold-marked, entirely purple, deep and pale azure, and yellow forms. ² ⁴ ⁹ ¹⁰
Found inIndia, Ethiopia, Media, Libya, and near Le Puy in Provence. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁹ ¹⁰

The sapphire shines in blue, azure, purple, and yellow forms. It cools heat and lust, heals the eyes and body, preserves health, and brings peace, devotion, and understanding. Prepared or engraved in particular ways, it also counters poison, bleeding, spirits, illness, and misfortune. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ The name Sirites also appears in accounts of Silenite, whose lunar properties are treated separately.

Generic

Description

The sapphire is blue like a clear sky, though purple, yellow, pale azure, and wine-dark forms are also known. ⁶ Some are clouded or flecked with gold; ³ ⁴ the royal kind bears dense golden marks, while an entirely purple kind is preferred in one tradition. ² The finest deep-azure kind is opaque, and the best stones have dusky clouds, sometimes reddish or white. ¹ ⁵

Properties

Healing and bodily strength

The sapphire cures abscesses and does not lose its virtue after curing one: a stone is said to have cured two abscesses nearly four years apart. It cools internal heat, checks sweating, cures headache and pain in the tongue, and invigorates the body. ¹ To remove dirt from the eyes, place the stone in cold water before touching it to the eye and again afterward.

Ground and mixed with milk, sapphire heals itching and fungal disease when applied to the affected places. ² A warmed, moistened sapphire touched to a sty morning and night for three days cures it. ⁴ For red and inflamed eyes or obscured vision, moisten the stone with saliva in the mouth and apply the saliva around and into the eyes. ⁴

Held in the mouth, sapphire stops gicht and severe oppression of head and body. ⁴ Moisten it in the mouth, warm it over wine vapour, and swallow its moisture to improve understanding and knowledge and help the stomach. ⁴ Rub it upon the tongue while fasting to improve understanding, provided the bearer intends no malice.

Conduct, peace, and protection

The sapphire makes a person chaste, cools lust, and quells wrath. ⁴ ⁵ The wearer becomes peaceful, lovable, pious, and devoted to good work; ⁶ the stone confirms the mind in goodness and brings peaceful agreements. ¹

It preserves health and turns away betrayal, harm, envy, and fear; it frees prisoners, promotes concord, and favours prayer and divination. It relieves illness, internal heat, sweating, ulcers, eye problems, headaches, and speech impairment. Milk serves in an eye remedy, and the wearer must be chaste. ⁵ A sapphire in a ring quells wrath only when set in pure gold without tin. ⁴

To torment and expel an evil spirit, enclose the stone with earth in a leather bag, suspend it from the neck, and say the prescribed prayer. To remove obsessive romantic passion, pour wine over the sapphire with the prescribed invocation and drink it for three or more days. ⁴

The sapphire is useful in magical arts and has great efficacy in necromancy. ⁶ It stops bleeding. ⁷

Engraving

  • An astrolabe engraved on sapphire increases wealth and foretells things to come. ⁶
  • A ram, or a bearded man's head, heals and frees the bearer from many infirmities, prison, and burdens. ⁶
  • A man engraved as if playing a musical instrument raises the bearer to greater honours and makes him gracious to all. ⁶

Varieties

Kind Distinguishing mark
Royal ² Purple, densely marked with gold.
Entirely purple Purple throughout; preferred to the royal kind in one account. ²
Deep azure ⁵ The finest kind is opaque.
Pale azure Very light, pure azure. ⁶
Yellow A deeper and more transparent yellow is better. ⁶
Clouded blue Dark reddish clouds or small white clouds in a translucent stone. ¹
Syrtitis (Syrtites) Named for the Syrtes; ⁸ a Lucanian kind is saffron-colored with faint inner stars that shine in cloudy weather.

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, Chapter 17: “Saphirus”

Open the cited text ↗

Saphirus is a very famous stone, and most of it comes from the East, from India. It is also found in an underground mine in the neighbourhood of the city of Le Puy, in Provence; but this is not so precious as to be exactly like the Oriental kind. Its colour is a transparent blue like a clear sky, but the blue colour predominates; the better Oriental kind is not quite transparent. The best has dark clouds with a reddish tinge; but a good kind is found that has small white clouds; its substance is like a dusky cloud, but rather translucent. I myself have observed the power of one that cured two abscesses. They say, too, that this stone makes a man chaste and cools internal heat, checks sweating, and cures headache and pain in the tongue. I myself have seen one put into the eye to remove dirt from the eyes; but it should be placed in cold water beforehand and likewise afterwards. As to the statement that it loses its power and colour after it has once cured an abscess, that is not true; for I have seen one that cured two abscesses in succession, with an intervening period of nearly four years. They say that it invigorates the body, and brings about peaceful agreements, and makes one pious and devoted to God, and confirms the mind in goodness. This stone is also called by another name sirites or, as others prefer, sirtites, because it is found in sandbanks (syrtis).

2. St. Epiphanius — On the Twelve Gemstones, fifth stone (Dan’s tribe)

Open the cited text ↗

The stone called Sapphire is purple-colored. Like gold brocade, in appearance with black-purple shades. Its types are also many: For there is of this a royal one, whose row is densely golden. But it cannot delight as much as the one that is purple-colored in its entirety. And this was found among the Ethiopian Indians. Therefore one says that the 365 steps of the Dionysus temple, which is among the Indians, are completely made of sapphire stone, although this matter is incredible. But one says that each individual of the steps is made of sapphire stones, according to their respective size. One has often also set it on outer garments and rings, especially the kings. For it is a wonderful stone, beautiful and delightful in appearance. And therefore some have called it Homisitos.

4. St. Hildegard of Bingen — Physica, Stones, VI: “Sapphire”

Open the cited text ↗

Sapphire (sapphirus) is hot and develops after noontime, when the sun burns ardently and the air is a bit obstructed by its heat. The splendor of the sun, from the extreme heat that it then has, pierces through the air. The splendor is not as full as it is when the air is a bit cool. Sapphire is turbid, indeed more fiery than airy or watery. It symbolizes a complete love of wisdom.

A person who has a sty in his eye should hold a sapphire in his hand and warm it, by holding it either closed in his hand or near a fire. He should touch the sty in his eye with the damp stone in the morning and at night, for three days, and the sty will grow smaller and disappear.

One whose eyes are red and painfully inflamed, or whose vision is obscured, should place a sapphire in his mouth before breakfast. It should become damp from his saliva. Then, taking on his finger the saliva which dampened the stone, he should smear it around his eyes, so that it even touches the inside of his eyes, which will be healed and will become clear.

↗ Open the matching source passage

One who is completely virgichtiget and unable to endure the great oppression in his head and the rest of his body should place a sapphire in his mouth, and the gicht will stop.

↗ Open the matching source passage

A person who desires to have good understanding and knowledge should place a sapphire in his mouth every morning, upon getting out of bed and while fasting. He should hold it in his mouth long enough for it to absorb the saliva which moistens it. He should take it from his mouth, then warm a bit of wine in a metallic vessel over the fire. He should hold the stone in the vapor of that wine so that, by sweating, it becomes damp. Then he should lick off some of that moisture and the saliva, which had heated the stone, and swallow it, and he will have pure understanding and knowledge. Even his stomach will be healed by this.

Its heat and strength, with the moisture of his saliva, will chase off noxious humors which oppress the person’s understanding, and the person will receive good understanding.

One who is much moved with wrath should immediately place a sapphire in his mouth. The wrath will be extinguished and will go from him. If this stone is placed on a ring of the purest gold, without tin, and there is nothing but gold under the stone, then a person may place the stone in his mouth as medicine, and it will not harm him. If anything but pure gold is in it, then it is of no use, and one should not place it in his mouth, because that ring is harmful.

He should say, “O you, most wicked spirit, quickly go from this person, just as, in your first fall, the glory of your splendor very quickly fell from you.” The evil spirit will be greatly tortured. He will depart from that person, who will be better, unless it is a very cruel and most good-for-nothing spirit.

If the devil should incite a man to love a woman so that, without magic or invocations of demons, he begins to be insane with this love, and if this is an annoyance to the woman, she should pour a bit of wine over a sapphire three times and each time say, “I pour this wine, in its ardent powers, over you; just as God drew off your splendor, wayward angel, so may you draw away from me the lust of this ardent man.” If the woman is unwilling to do this, then another person for whom that love is a problem should do it for her. He should give the wine to the man to drink for three or more days, whether he’s eating or not, and whether he knows about it or not. If a woman burns with love for some man, and this is an annoyance to the man, he should do the same thing with the sapphire and the wine, and the burning passion will go away.

5. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, section V

Open the cited text ↗

Fit only for the hands of kings to weai-,
With purest azure shines the Sapphire pure:
For worth and beauty chief of gems proclaimed,
And by the vulgar oft Syrtites named.
Oft in the Syrtes midst their shifting sand
Cast by the boiling deep on Libyan strand;
The best the sort that Media's mines supply,
Opaque of colour which excludes the eye.
By nature with superior honours graced,
As gem of gems above all othere placed;
Health to preserve, and treachery to disarm.
And guard the wearer from intended harm:
No envy bends him, and no terror shakes;
The captive's chains its mighty virtue breaks;
The gates fly open, fetters fall away.
And send their prisoner to the light of day.
E'en Heaven is moved by its force divine.
To list to vows presented at its shrine.
Its soothing power contentions fierce controls.
And in sweet concord binds discordant souls;
Above all othere this Magicians love,
Which draws responses from the realms above:
The body's ills its saving force allays
And cools the flame that on the entrails preys.
Can check the sweats that melt the waning force
And stay the ulcer in its festering course:
Dissolved in milk it clears the cloud away
From the dimmed eye and pours the perfect day;
Relieves the aching brow when racked with pain
And bids the tongue its wonted vigour gain.
But he who dares to wear this gem divine
Like snow in perfect chastity must shine.

6. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Book II, Chapter 7 (“Saphirus”), with related passages in Book III

Open the cited text ↗

Saphirus [Sapphire] is a yellow-colored stone, or a very light azure like the purest azure color; the deeper and more transparent the yellow, the better the quality of the stone. But the most precious one, which surpasses all, emits an almost burning brightness when struck by the sun; no image, however small, is ever found within it. It is found on the Libyan shoals, but the Indian ones are better. Some call it the gem of gems because of the grace of its color, but some say that it has received its name not from its color but from its virtue. It fortifies the body and gives it good coloring; it cools off the ardor of lust and renders man chaste and virtuous; it stops too much sweat. It removes filth from the eyes and aches from the forehead. If drunk with milk, it calms the spasms of the belly. It renders the wearer peaceful, lovable, pious, and devout, and it turns the soul to good work. It uncovers frauds and chases fears away; it is useful in the magical arts; and it is also said that it is of prodigious efficacy in necromantic magic. It discharges boils by touch alone. If touched to the eyes it preserves them and protects them from smallpox.

9. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 87: Saphiro

Open cited source

Saphirus is a precious stone, and is blew in colour, most like to heaven in fair wether and cleere, and is best among precious stones, and most precious and most apt and able to fingers of Kings, and is found in many places, but that that is found in the East or in India is accounted best, and namely if it have as it were powder of gold mingled therein, and this Sapphire stone is thick and not passing bright, as Isi. says. This stone is most praised in Lapid. And for it is so noble and so excellent, it is called Gemma gemmarum, as it were chief of precious stones, for it lightens the body, and keeps and saves lims whole & sound, and has a bright star, and by brightness of that star, his virtue is known. Another manner Sapphire is called Sirtites, and is found fast by the place that is called Sirtes, among the gravel of the sea Libicum, as Diosc. says, & is also found in the veins of mines, where Lasurium is also found, and in the same veins of Sapphire, in the middle, as it were in the womb, is a certain kind of Carbuncle found. Therefore many men deem, that the Sapphire is the Carbuncles mother, for many men mean, that the Carbuncle is gendered in the Sapphires veins, and many men mean, that the Carbuncle is some deale beshadowed with a certain blue sparkle of the kind of the Sapphire, as Diosc. says: & Diosc. says that the Sapphire has virtue to rule & accord them that be in strife, & helps much to make peace & accord. Also it has virtue to abate unkind heat, therefore in Lapid. it is said of the sapphire, that it cools heat of the body within. For the Sapphire cools much the heat of burning fevers, if it be hanged near the pulse & the veins of the heart. Therefore he says, y it helps against much fevers, y have time of changing in access time. Also it has virtue to comfort & to glad y heart, therefore it is said, that it helps against the Cardiac, & against all melancholik passions. Therefore it staunches running and sweat that comes of anguish, & other sweat also, as it is said in Lap. He staunches sweat that runs swift, &c. Diosc. says the same, & has virtue to staunch blood. And so a Sapphire of the East staunches bleeding at the nose, if it be laid to the temples. Also he has singular virtue to assuage blowing: For certain it is, that the Sapphire abates & assuages swelling of postumes, if it be soon laid thereto in the beginning of noiful drawing. Also the Sapphire helps against an evil postum, that is called Antrax, for it puts out the might of ventosity & mallice of that Postum, for it overcomes & puts out the madness thereof, & suffers not the smoak thereof come to the heart, nor the malice thereof to infect the spirits, as Dioscri. says. His virtue is contrary to venom, & quenches it euerye deale. And if thou put a Spider in a box, & hold a very Sapphire of India at the mouth of the bore any while, by virtue thereof the Spider is overcome & dies, as it were suddenly, as Diosc. says. And the same I have seen proved oft in many & diverse places. His virtue keeps & saves the sight, & cleanses eyes of filth without any grief: therefore it is written in Lapid. that it takes away filth out of eyes, and ach of the forhead. Also this virtue heals biles and botches: For as Dioscor. says, this stone beaten into powder, & mingled with milk, heals wounds, & this also has ben proved in wounds by experience. Also this stone was of so great authority in old time, that men held that it it was most worthy stone to their God, & so it was singularly hallowed to Apollo, for when nations asked counsel of Appollo in time of sacrifice, they hoped to be certified, & to have aunswere sooner, if a Sapphire stone were present, as Dioscorides says. And this is touched in Lapidario.

And they that use Nigromancie mean, that they have answer of God more thereby, then by other precious stones. Also Witches love well this stone, for they ween that they may work certain wonders by virtue of this stone, and also this is touched in Lapid. where it is said. This stone brings men out of prison bonds, & undoes gates and bonds that it touches. Authors write those doings and many other of the singular virtue of the Sapphire, & al Authors accord in this point & say, that the Sapphire is a precious stone, & loueth chastity, & therefore least the effect thereof be let in any wise by his uncleanness that him bears, it needs him that bears it to live chaste, as this story means, but he that it bears, is commanded to be most chaste. Also in Lapidario it is said, that this stone does always envy, & puts off dread & fear, and makes a man bold and hardy, & master and victor, & makes the heart steadfast in goodness, and makes meek & mild, and goodly. I ween that all this is said more in disposition then in effect and doing. But this suffices at this time.

10. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Saphirus — Sapphire

Open cited source

Sapphire is a most precious stone, one of the twelve, and is blue in colour. The most precious kind comes from India and is not transparent. It preserves the body and its members intact and strengthens the inner organs. It cools internal heat, restrains sweating, removes pain from the eyes and forehead, heals and soothes ulcers of the tongue, and drives away the dreadful disease called noli me tangere (“do not touch me”). Yet it is said to spoil its colour after the disease has been driven away.

It is especially effective against deception, envy, and terror. It is also favourable to peace. Whoever wears it should guard chastity with the greatest care, lest the stone lose its powers. Eastern sapphires are the best, especially those that shine with an intense blue colour and have, as it were, little clouds of deeper colour upon their surface. There are also certain eastern sapphires containing an admixture of the stone called ruby; these are more valuable and more powerful than the others.
