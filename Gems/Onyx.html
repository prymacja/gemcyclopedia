Onyx

Onyx

AppearanceNail-like pale layers or hues; black stone with white veining or bands; ¹ ² ³ Indian reddish and white-banded forms; ² other black and reddish kinds; ¹ ³ and a separate golden-yellow account. ⁴ ⁷ ⁸

Onyx bears the appearance of a human nail: it may show a pale layer or veins against black, reddish, or other colored stone. ² ⁴ The traditions differ over its colors and kinds, naming five varieties in several accounts and a golden-yellow Onyx in another. ⁴ It is found in India, Arabia, and Media. ¹ ² ³ Worn, it brings sorrow, fear, troubling dreams, quarrels, and excessive saliva in children; Sard is said to restrain this harm. ¹

Generic

Description

Onyx is translucent and seldom found alone. ³ It may bear a white layer like a fingernail, and its kinds differ according to their colors, bands, and the places where they are found. ² ³ Some reckon two kinds, others five. The accounts describe Indian and Arabian stones differently, and a separate tradition calls a golden-yellow stone Onyx. ² ⁴ These descriptions are kept distinct below where the sources distinguish them.

Properties

When worn around the neck or on a finger, Onyx brings sorrow and fear, frightful dreams, and quarrels or lawsuits. ³ ⁶ It increases saliva in children. ¹ A Sard or Sardian worn with it restrains its harmful influence. ⁶

Onyx worn around the neck also prevents falls in those who suffer from epilepsy. ³ Placed beneath a sick eye, it enters of its own accord, circles the eye, and extinguishes any illness it finds there; it also tempers noxious and contrary humors. ³ A smooth, polished stone may be put into the eye without harm, provided it does not touch the sensitive pupil. ¹

Onyx is hot and holds the heat of the air. ⁵ It forms around the third hour of the day in a thick cloud, when the sun is very hot but veiled by clouds. ⁵ It is born from a ray of the sun and congealed by the clouds, and it has power against infirmities arising from the air.

For foggy or otherwise ailing eyes, place Onyx in good, pure wine in a bronze, copper, or steel vessel and let it soak for fifteen or thirty days. ⁵ Remove the stone and each night gently touch the eyes with the wine; ⁵ they will become clear and healthy. ⁵

For pain of the heart or side, warm Onyx in the hands or against the skin. ⁵ Heat wine in a metal vessel, take it from the fire, and hold the stone over the steaming wine until its sweat mixes with it. ⁵ Put the stone into the wine and drink it at once; the pain will disappear. ⁵

For stomach pain, prepare wine with Onyx as above, make a broth from that wine, hens’ eggs, and flour, and eat it often; ⁵ it purges and heals the stomach. For ailing spleen, cook goat or young sheep’s meat and often eat it dipped in the Onyx-prepared wine; ⁵ the spleen will improve and swell fully.

For a strong fever, soak Onyx in vinegar for five days. ⁵ Remove the stone and season all food with the vinegar; the fever will cease. ⁵ The stone’s good heat mixed with the vinegar’s heat drives away the noxious humors that cause fever.

For sadness, gaze intently at Onyx and then put it in the mouth; ⁵ the oppression of the mind will cease. ⁵ To aid cattle during pestilence, hold Onyx over steaming water so its sweat enters the water, then leave the stone in the water for three days. ⁵ Give the water to the cows to drink, sprinkle their fodder with it, and mix bran with it for them to eat; ⁵ repeated use will help them.

Subtype I: Black Onyx

Black Onyx is black, sometimes streaked or banded with white; ¹ ² its better kind is black with white veins. ¹ One account locates it in Media and Arabia and distinguishes five kinds by their veins and colors.

Subtype II: Indian Onyx

Indian Onyx is described as having white sparkles in encircling bands. ² Another account gives an Indian kind as reddish with white veins; ³ these descriptions are retained as differing accounts of the Indian form.

Subtype III: Arabian Onyx

Arabian Onyx is black with white bands or veins. ² ³

Subtype IV: Other Color Kinds

Other color kinds include an Onyx whose true color mixes the hues of Amethyst, Carbuncle, and Chrysolite with white and black; an extremely black kind; ³ and a black kind mixed with reddish. ³ The traditions differ over whether there are two or five kinds.

Subtype V: Golden-Yellow Onyx

Golden-Yellow Onyx is distinctly golden-yellow. ⁴ This account distinguishes it from honey-colored stones similar in name to Beryl and from pure white marble that is wrongly called Onyx.

Related Named Stones

Onycha and Onychulus

Onycha, also called Onychulus, is sometimes black but more commonly the color of a human fingernail. ¹ It is said to be the same as Onyx or a variety of it. ¹ This stone may be put into the eye without being felt. ¹

Onychinus

Onychinus may be white, black, or reddish, but each kind is formed in a substance like a human fingernail. ¹ Drops of gum from a tree called Onycha harden into this stone, giving it an odor in fire. The soft drops readily take images, which remain when the gum hardens; it is therefore more often found marked with images than other stones. One account names Onychinus alongside Onyx. ⁵

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, Onyx and Onycha

Open Onyx · Open Onycha

Onyx is said to be a gem of a black colour; there is a better kind of it which is black, streaked with white veins. It comes from Media and Arabia. Five varieties are found, based on differences in their veining and colours. They say that, worn around the neck or on the finger, it induces sorrow and fear and terrible dreams in sleep; and it is reported to increase sorrows and dissensions; and they say that it increases saliva in children. But sard, if present, restrains the onyx and keeps it from doing harm. If onyx really has all these properties, surely this is because it has the power of affecting black bile, especially in the head; for all these disorders come from the motion and vapour of black bile.

Onycha, or onychulus, as some people say, is really the same as onyx, since it is very similar, or may be a variety of it. Its colour, however, is not always, but only sometimes, black; but it is more commonly like the colour of the human fingernail, as we have said above. But the stone named onychinus is found of many colours, white, black, and reddish; nevertheless, all these are formed in some substance that is very like the human fingernail. They say, too, that drops of gum from a tree called onycha harden into stone; and this is the reason why it has an odour in the fire. They also declare that this is the reason why, more frequently than other stones, it is found marked with images. For the drops are soft at first and easily formed into figures; and the gum retains these figures when it consolidates and hardens into stone. They say that this stone can be put into the eye without being felt, and this is marvellous. But I myself have seen a saphirus put into the eye, and a cockstone, and another stone whose name I do not know, without damage to the eye. For a smoothly polished thing does not damage the eye, unless it should touch the centre, or pupil, the sensitive part opposite the opening of the eyeball.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, VIII.3 Onyx

Open cited passage

  1. Onyx (onyx) is so named because it possesses a white layer mixed in it that is similar to the human nail. For the Greeks call the nail ὄνυξ (onyx). India and Arabia produce these gems, but the two types are distinct from each other, for Indian onyx has sparkles in white encircling bands, while Arabian onyx is black with white bands. There are five kinds of onyx.

3. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Onyx and engravings

Open Onyx · Open Raziel’s images · Open Hermes’s images

Onyx is a stone the color of a man’s nail, in fact onix in Greek and in Latin means nail. It is transparent and rarely found on its own. Its species varies according to the variety of colors [of the material] to which it is attached, and the place where it is found. Some say that there are two species, some five. The first, the true kind, has already been mentioned, the other is said to be extremely black. The third kind is black with white veins or bands. And this one comes from Arabia. And in India there is a reddish-colored species with white veins; the fifth type is black mixed with reddish. Some say that the true onyx is the color of amethyst, carbuncle, and chrysolite, and that these colors are mixed with white and black. This stone brings forth many horrendous things in sleep. Worn, it attracts quarrels and fights. It increases spittle in infants and accelerates birth. Worn around the neck it prevents falls for those who suffer from epilepsy. Onyx is said to be marvelous because of this: placed under a sick eye, it enters it of its own volition as if it were endowed of intellect, and it goes around it without any trouble; and if it finds in it any illness, it extinguishes it. It tempers noxious and contrary humors.

Camel; the image of this animal or of the head of two goats among myrtle trees, if sculpted on onyx, has the virtue of calling forth, congregating, and enslaving demons. And if worn, it makes a person see horrible things.

Man; a man shown standing and holding a dagger, if you find it sculpted on onyx, know that it has the virtue of rendering its wearer honored by all sovereigns and princes, as well as by all men.

Stag or Dove. If the image of these animals is found engraved an onyx know that it gives its wearer courage, it will chase demons away and force them to gather, and it pacifies all ill winds.

4. St. Epiphanius — On the Twelve Gemstones, The Twelfth Stone: Onyx

Open cited passage

The stone Onyx has a distinctly golden-yellow color. At this stone – as one says – the brides of kings and the daughters of the great lords delighted. Those who have these stones delight in them to the point of self-forgetfulness. But there also exist other stones similar in name to Beryl, which are honey-colored like wax; some say that they would be congealed from water drops. But Beryl is – those who investigate the name most truthfully say this – from the appearance of the nail of agile people, because its shimmer is similar to the nail, together with blood-red appearance. But the marble stone is also unnecessarily and falsely designated with the name Onyx because of its pure whiteness.

5. St. Hildegard of Bingen — Physica, III. Onyx

Open cited passage

Onyx (onychinus) is hot. It is formed around the third hour of the day, in a thick cloud, when the sun is very hot but when various clouds rise over the sun, so that it does not show through the abundance of water. Therefore, onyx does not have the great heat of fire, yet it holds the heat of air. It originates from a ray of the sun, is congealed by various clouds, and has great power against infirmities that arise from the air.

One whose eyes are foggy, or ailing in some other way, should pour good, pure wine into a bronze, copper, or steel vessel. He should place an onyx in that wine and let it soak for fifteen or thirty days. Then he should take out the stone and leave the wine in the vessel. Every night he should gently touch his eyes with the wine. They will become clear and healthy.

For an ailment of the heart or side, one should warm an onyx in his hands or against his skin. He should heat wine on the fire in a metallic vessel and, after taking the vessel from the fire, hold the stone over the steaming wine. The sweat coming out of it will mix with the wine. He should then put the onyx in the wine, and drink it right away. The pain of his heart and side will disappear.

One who has pain in his stomach should prepare wine with onyx, as already described. Then he should make a broth with that wine, hens’ eggs, and flour and eat it often. It will purge his stomach and heal him. But, one who ails in his spleen should cook goat meat or that of young sheep. He should eat the cooked meat, dipping it in the wine prepared with onyx, just as one customarily dips certain foods in vinegar. He should do this often, and his spleen will be better and swell up fully.

One who has strong fevers should place onyx in vinegar for five days. Then, having removed the onyx, he should prepare and season all his foods with that vinegar, and so eat them. The fever will cease and swiftly vanish, because the good heat of onyx, mixed with the heat of vinegar, chases away the noxious humors which give rise to fevers.

If you are oppressed with sadness, look at an onyx intently, then place it in your mouth. The oppression of your mind will cease.

If pestilence should infest and kill cattle, heat water in a vessel over the fire. Take it from the fire and hold an onyx over that steaming water, so that the sweat coming out of it mixes with the water. Then place the onyx in that water for three days. After taking out the onyx, frequently give the water to the cows to drink. Sprinkle their fodder with the water, and mix bran with the same water, and set it out for them to eat. Do this often, and they will be better.

6. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, IX. Onyx

Open cited stanza

Called by the Onyx round the sleeper stand Black dreams, and phantoms rise, a grisly band: Whoso on neck or hand this stone displays Is plagued with lawsuits and with civil frays; Round infants' necks if tied, so nurses shew. Their tender mouths with slaver overflow.

This the Arabian, this the Indian sends, And five the sorts to which its name it lends: Which name of Onyx, as grammarians teach, Comes from the usage of the Grecian speech, For what the name of nails amongst us bears Expressed in Greek as Onyches appears; Yet if a Sardian on thy finger shine 'T will quash the Onyx' influence malign.

7. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 72: Onichino

Open cited source

Onichinus is a stone of India, and of Arabia, and has in it self colour mingled like the nail of mankind.

The Onix of India has colour of fire, with white veins & streaks, and the stone Onix of Arabia, is black of colour, and has white veins. And thereof if five manner of kinds: one is Sardonix, and has that name of company of two, of whiteness of the Onix, and redness of the Sardonius, as it is shown innermore of Sardonic. It is said, that this stone Onix has many harmful effects, for as Dioscorides says, if it be borne about the neck, or on the finger, it excites sorow, eleingnesse, and dread, and multiplies plea and strife, and mooueth the heart to contention and debate, and excites in children harmful superfluity of spettle, and may not grieve in presence of the stone Sardius. This stone Onix is cleere of the kind of mirrors, & therefore images & figures be seen therein, as it were in a mirror, but that is darkly, as Dioscorides says.

8. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Onichinus — Onyx

Open cited source

Onychinus is a most precious stone, one of the twelve. It resembles a human fingernail; hence it is so named because onyche means “nail” in Greek. It is effective against scabies, whitens the face, and produces a most pleasant smell.
