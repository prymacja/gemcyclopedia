Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Gecolitus is reported to be a stone like the stone of an Eastern olive. If crushed and drunk with water, its power is said to break up and expel a stone from the bladder or kidneys.

2. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Gecolitus

Open cited source

Gecolitus is an eastern stone, resembling an olive pit. It is unremarkable in appearance but precious in virtue. Dissolved in water and drunk, it dissolves a stone in the bladder and purges the sand accumulated there.
