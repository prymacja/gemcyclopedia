Generic

Description

  • Resembles carchedonia, but has paler color. ¹
  • a reddish and cerulean-colored stone, like the sardius, to which is so similar that one can barely discern them by color; ² it takes its name from a part of Asia so called and where it was first found. ²

Properties

  • Its virtue is that of bringing on the flux of blood. ²

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville

Open cited source

Alabandina is named after the Alabanda region of Asia. Its color approaches that of the carchedonia, but is less intense. — Etymologies XVI.xiv.6

2. Camillo Leonardi

Open cited source

Alabandina is a reddish and cerulean-colored stone, like the sardius, to which is so similar that one can barely discern them by color; it takes its name from a part of Asia so called and where it was first found. Its virtue is that of bringing on the flux of blood. Drunk, it extinguishes all types of poisons. I find diverse opinions on the color of this stone, but all I have said is confirmed by the majority of sources.

3. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 14: Alabandina

Open cited source

Alabandina is a precious stone, and has that name of a region of Asia, the which is called Alabandina: The colour thereof is like to the stone Calcidonius: but it is more tare, as Isi. says, cap. de Cristallis. And Dioscorides says, that Alabandina is a precious stone cleere & some deale red, as Cardius. The virtue thereof excites and increases blood.

4. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Alabandina — Alabandine

Open cited source

Alabandine is a precious and beautiful stone. Its colour resembles that of garnet, though it is deeper and more like ruby. There is also a kind of alabandine that resembles sard, found in the region of Asia called Alabanda.
