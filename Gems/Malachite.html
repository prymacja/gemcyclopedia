Malachite is a soft, opaque green stone. ¹ ³ It is deeper and duller in color than Emerald, and takes its name from the color of the mallow. ² The Arabian kind is mallow-colored; ¹ ² ⁴ the Cypriot kind tends to gray; and the Persian kind is green with a brass hue. ³ ⁴

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, Melochites

Open cited source

Melochites (malachite), which some people call melonites, is an Arabian stone of a deep green colour, not transparent like smaragdus; it is soft. It is said to have the power of protecting the wearer from harm, and of guarding the cradles of infants.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, VII.11

Open cited source

Malachite (molochites), more densely green and duller than a smaragdus, takes its name from the color of the mallow (malva), and is valued for the seals it makes. It originates in Arabia.

3. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, LIV

Open cited source

The Molochites virtue keeps from hurt The infant's cradle, all mischance to avert. Lest spiteful witchcraft blast the tender frame. Virtue with beauty joined exalt its fame. Opaque of hue, with th' Emerald's vivid green It charms the sight, first in Arabia seen.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Malachite

Open cited source

Malachite is a stone that has the bright color of an emerald, with a kind of rough vigor without transparency; it takes its name from mallows, since it is almost the same color. It is a soft stone and is found in Arabia, Cyprus, and Persia but of different colors. Therefore, the Arabian type has the color of mallows, the Cypriote tends to a grayish color, and the Persian is of a green color still retaining a brass hue. The virtue of this stone is that of protecting infants from various accidents and hurtful phantasms, so that they may grow up in prosperity.

5. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 64: Melonite

Open cited source

Melonites is a stone, and has that name, for sweet juice comes out thereof, as it were honey, as Isidore says: & has two colours, for it is green in the one side, and like to honey in that other side.

6. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Melonites

Open cited source

Melonites is a stone from Arabia, having a deep green colour, very like emerald but not so bright. It strengthens tender limbs and protects against unfortunate accidents.
