Amethyst

Amethyst

The image shows Amethyst Gems (source).

NamesAmethyst, Amethystus, Ametistus. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶; Ametisto ⁷; Amatistus ⁷
AppearancePurple, violet, wine-red, rose, or diluted-wine shades; ³ ⁵ ⁶ five shades are distinguished, and some forms resemble Hyacinth. ¹ ² ⁷ ⁸
Found inIndia, Libya and its coast, Arabia, Cyprus, Egypt, Armenia, Galatia, and Tarsus. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Amethyst is a purple or violet stone with shades from deep wine-colour to pale rose. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ It checks drunkenness and evil thoughts, strengthens understanding, protects against poison and serpents, and bears virtues in medicine, hunting, warfare, and engraved images. ⁶

Generic

Description

The stone is purple, violet, wine-red, or a pale rose colour, with a dark or fiery transparency and rosy sparks. ³ ⁵ ⁶ Five shades are commonly distinguished. ¹ Some kinds resemble pure Hyacinth or Amphikochlon; ² these comparisons describe their appearance and do not establish that the stones are the same.

Properties

Amethyst checks drunkenness. ¹ ⁵ Worn over the navel, it lessens the effect of wine and drives drunkenness away. ⁶ It keeps the bearer awake at night, represses evil thoughts, gives good understanding of knowable things, and makes him skilled and expert in his actions. ¹

For spots on the face, rub the stone over them with saliva, then wash the face with water prepared with the stone; ⁴ the complexion clears. A fresh tumor diminishes when the moistened stone is applied. Passing amethyst over the flesh heals a spider bite, and the stone repels serpents and adders.

Water in which the stone has been washed makes barren women fruitful when drunk. ⁶ Amethyst extinguishes poison, protects soldiers, and brings victory over the enemy. ⁶ During a hunt it helps the bearer capture wild beasts and birds. ⁶

The stone foretells southern winds, winter weather, and rainfall. ²

Engraving

The image and its setting govern the following virtues. ⁶

  • A bear drives demons away and protects the wearer from drunkenness. ⁶
  • A man holding a knife and seated over a dragon, engraved on amethyst set in a lead or iron ring, compels spirits to obey, reveals treasures, and answers questions. ⁶
  • A foaming horse ridden by a man holding a sceptre makes the seal useful in all things and wins the obedience of princes and powerful men. ⁶
  • An armed man with a sword engraved on Sardion or amethyst gives the wearer a good and perfect memory and makes him wise. ⁶

Variants

Kind Distinguishing appearance
Five colour-shades ¹ ³ Purple, violet, wine-red, rose, and diluted-wine shades. ⁵
Hyacinth-like ² Resembles pure Hyacinth.
Amphikochlon-like ² Compared with Amphikochlon.
Indian ³ ⁵ A softer kind, valued for carving. ¹
Libyan ² Found by the Libyan coast.

Subtype I: Five colour-shades

Amethyst occurs in five recognized shades. ¹ ³ These range from Tyrian purple and darker violet to bright wine-red, pale rose, and a lighter wine color diluted with water. ³ ⁵ The accounts treat these as color differences among amethysts, not as different named stones. ³

Subtype II: Hyacinth-like

One amethyst form resembles pure Hyacinth. ² This is a comparison of appearance; it does not make the two stones identical.

Subtype III: Amphikochlon-like

Another amethyst form resembles Amphikochlon. ² The source distinguishes it by comparison but gives no further appearance details here.

Subtype IV: Indian

The Indian amethyst is softer and therefore easier to carve. ¹ ³ ⁵ It is ranked above other kinds for its color and beauty.

Subtype V: Libyan

The Libyan kind is found along the Libyan sea-coast. ² It is dense and fiery at its edge, while its interior has the appearance of wine. ²

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, Chapter 1: “Amethystus”

Open the cited text ↗

Amethystus (amethyst) is a very common gem. It is of a sort of purplish colour and a somewhat dark transparency. Many different varieties of this stone are found; but five are best known, all distinguished by their different shades of colour. One kind of this stone also occurs in India and is better for carving than other kinds because it is softer. It counteracts drunkenness, as Aaron says, and it keeps one awake at night and represses evil thoughts, and confers a good understanding of what is knowable.

2. St. Epiphanius — On the Twelve Gemstones, ninth stone (Issachar’s tribe)

Open the cited text ↗

Amethyst is flaming according to its edge, dense. But its glow is radiantly white and inside the body it shows wine-like appearance. But different is its form of appearance in the mountains. And I also think that it was found among the Libyans. For one is similar to pure hyacinth, the other to Amphikochlon. But this type is found on the shores of the sea of the Libyans. And the myth-makers say about this that it would indicate in advance the southern winds, winter and rainfalls.

3. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, IX.1

Open the cited text ↗

Among the purple gems Indian amethyst holds the place of honor. Amethyst (amethystus) is purple with a violet color mixed in; its brilliance is like that of a rose, and it gently gives out a sort of little flame. Another type of amethyst holds a lower place, down to that of the hyacinth-stone. People assert that the reason for its name is that there is something not entirely fire-like in its purple, but that it possesses rather the color of wine (cf. μέθυ (methy), “wine”). Amethyst is easily carved. There are five kinds.

4. St. Hildegard of Bingen — Physica, Stones, XV: “Amethyst”

Open the cited text ↗

Amethyst (amethystus) develops when the sun shows its circle, as though it were crowned, which it does when it prefigures some change in the vestment of the Lord, in the Church. Amethyst grows as a gum, and so there are many of them. It is hot and fiery and.a bit airy, since the air is a bit cool when the sun shows its circle, as described. A person who has spots on his face should dampen an amethyst with his saliva and rub it over his spots. Also, he should heat water over a fire and hold that stone over the water. The sweat coming out of it should be mixed with the water. Then he should place the stone in the water and wash his face with the water. If he does this often, his face will have smooth skin and beautiful color. If a fresh tumor is swelling on one’s body, he should dampen the amethyst with his saliva and touch the tumor all over with it. The tumor will grow smaller and disappear. Pass that stone over the place where a spider has pierced a person’s flesh, and it will be cured. Also, the serpent and adder flee this stone and avoid the place where they know amethysts to be.

5. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, section XVI

Open the cited text ↗

The Tyrian purple the rich Amethyst dyes.
Or darker violet charms the gazer's eyes;
Bright as the ruby wine another glows,
Or fainter blush that decks the opening rose;
Another yet displays a lighter shade,
Like drops of wine with fountain streams allayed.
All these supplied by jewelled India's mart.
Easy to cut, yield to the graver's art:
The gem, if rarer, were a precious prize.
But now too common it neglected lies;
Famed for their power to check the fumes of wine.
Five different species yields the bounteous mine.

6. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Book II, Chapter 7 (“Ametistus”), with related passages in Book III

Open the cited text ↗

Ametistus [Amethyst] is among those stones that are purplish and transparent, mixed with violet color, emitting rosy sparkles. There are five species of amethyst: and they all tend to be of a purplish color but mixed with various [other] colors. The Indian amethyst surpasses all others in color, beauty, and price; it is purple mixed with rose and somewhat violet tones. Those that are simply purple are not much valued. Those that tend to a violet color, or wine color, or a watery violet, are baser than the rest. The places where they are found give the stones their names. The Indian is the best, followed in order by the Arabian, the Armenian, the Galatian, the Egyptian, the Tarsian, and the Cyprian: even though the last two are the baser of them all, so much so that learned men do not even mention them. They are all fit to be engraved. Their virtue is to drive away drunkenness: and thus, placed over the navel, they lessen the effect of wine; and hence dispel drunkenness. They repress evil thoughts and encourage good understanding. They render man skillful and expert in his actions, they render barren women fruitful, if they drink water in which the stones have been washed. They extinguish poison, protect soldiers, and allow them to win against the enemy. During a hunt they make it easy for a man to capture various wild beasts and birds.

7. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 10: Ametisto

Open cited source

Ametistus is a stone of India, & is chief among precious stones of purple colour, as Isi. says. Amatistus is purple in colour, mingled with colour of violet, as it were a blasing rose, and tightly rasting out as it were glisning beams of light. Isidore says that the other kind thereof is much like to blue, and is not all fiery, but it has colour of wine. And five kinds be thereof, as Isidore says. But as Dioscondes says: The Purple red is most noble and better then the other. For the virtue thereof helps against drunkenness, and makes waking: and removes away idle thoughts, and makes good understanding, and is soft, so that men may grave and write therein.

8. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Ametistus — Amethyst

Open cited source

Amethyst is one of the twelve stones. It has a violet colour, and that is the best kind. There is another variety resembling a drop of red wine diluted with water, and this kind is easier to engrave. It also acts against drunkenness. This stone would be very costly if it were rarer. It is found in Ethiopia and India, and also in Germany, although there it is of inferior quality and dark in colour.
