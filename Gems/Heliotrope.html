Heliotrope, also called Eliotropia, Elitropia, Elitropus, or Bloodstone, is a green stone like Emerald, clouded and speckled with blood-red spots and veins. ¹ ² ⁴ Anointed with the juice of its namesake herb and set in water beneath the sun, it turns the sun blood-red, boils the water into mist, and brings rain. ¹ ³ It grants prophecy and long life, checks bleeding and poison, and, with its herb and secret rite, makes its bearer invisible. ¹ ³

Generic

Description

Heliotrope is a green, cloudy stone like Emerald, marked with reddish stars, blood-red drops, and veins. ² ³ It is found in Ethiopia, Cyprus, Africa, India, and Libya. ¹ ⁴ Necromancers call it the Babylonian gem. ¹ ⁴

The name Bloodstone also belongs to Haematite, which is iron-colored and blood-veined; that stone is distinct from the green Heliotrope described here. ¹ ³ ⁴

Properties

Sunlight, water, and rain

Anoint Heliotrope with the juice of the herb that bears its name, place it in a bronze basin full of water, and set it beneath the sun’s rays. The stone appears red and the sun turns blood-red, as in an eclipse. ¹ ³ ⁴ The water boils, rises as mist, clouds the air, and spills over as rain. ¹ When taken out of the water, Heliotrope receives the sun like a mirror and shows the eclipse and the advancing moon. ²

Prophecy and invisibility

Consecrated with an incantation and magical signs, Heliotrope makes those possessed speak prophetic words. ¹ ³ Magicians also consecrate it with verses and inscribe characters upon it to grant its owner foresight. ⁴ Joined with the juice of its namesake herb and the proper incantation, it hides its bearer from mortal sight.

Health and fortune

Heliotrope gives its bearer a good reputation, honor, health, and long life. ¹ ⁴ It checks the flow of blood, expels poisons, and protects against treachery. ³ ⁴

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Eliotropia (heliotrope, bloodstone) is a stone almost as green as smaragdus, sprinkled with blood-red spots. The necromancers say that this is a Babylonian gem and is called heliotrope because if rubbed with the juice of the herb of the same name and placed in a vessel full of water, it makes the sun look blood-red, as if there were an eclipse. And the reason for this is that it makes all the water boil up into a mist, which thickens the air so that the sun cannot be seen except as a red glow in the condensing cloud; and afterwards the mist condenses and falls as drops of rain. The stone must be consecrated by a certain incantation, combined with magic signs; and if any of those present are possessed, they speak words of prophecy. And therefore pagan priests commonly used this stone a great deal in their idolatrous festivals. And it is said to give a man a good reputation, and health, and long life; and to be good against bleeding and poisons. It is also said that if rubbed with the herb of the same name, as we have said before, it deceives the sight so much as to make a man invisible. It is found very often in Ethiopia, Cyprus, and India.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies XVI, VII.12

Open cited source

Heliotrope (heliotropia) is green in color and cloudy; it is strewn with reddish stars and has blood-colored veins. The reason for this stone’s name is from its effect, for when it is thrown into a bronze basin it changes the rays of the sun with a blood-colored reflection (cf. ἥλιος (hēlios), “sun”; τροπή (tropē), “change”), but when out of the water it receives sunlight like a mirror, and reveals an eclipse of the sun by showing the advancing moon. This stone also provides a most blatant example of the shamelessness of magicians, because they claim that someone carrying an herb blended with a heliotrope, once certain spells have been cast, cannot be seen. It originates in Cyprus and Africa, but the better heliotrope is from Ethiopia.

3. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, XXIX

Open cited source

The Heliotrope, or “gem that turns the sun,”
From its strange power the name has justly won:
For set in water opposite his rays
As red as blood 'twill turn bright Phoebus' blaze.
And, far diffused the inauspicious light,
With strange eclipse the startled world affright.
Then boils the vase, urged by its magic power,
And casts far o'er the brim the sudden shower;
As when the gloomy air to rain gives way
It storms evokes, and clouds the fairest day;
It gifts the wearer with prophetic eye
Into the Future's darkest depths to spy.
A good report 't will give and endless praise,
And crown thy honour'd course with length of days.
It checks the flow of blood, the wearer's soul
Shall laugh at treason or the poison'd bowl.
Though with such potent virtues grac'd by heaven
One yet more wondrous to the gem is given.
This with the herb that bears its name unite
With incantation due and secret rite.
Then shalt thou mortal eyes in darkness shroud
And walk invisible amidst the crowd.
The stone for colour might an emerald seem,
But drops of blood diversify the green.
'T is sent sometimes from Ethiopia's land,
Sometimes from Afric or the Cyprian strand.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Elitropia or Elitropus [Heliotrope] is a green gem, as some like to think, similar to the emerald, and spotted with blood-colored drops. Necromancers call it the Babylonian gem. It is found in Africa and Ethiopia. It is named after its virtue, by which it can be easily recognized. If it is placed in water, first anointed with the juice of the plant by the same name, and placed under the sun’s rays, it will appear red; and the sun will appear bloody, as if there were an eclipse. Water will start boiling and spill over the basin, as if boiled by fire. Placed outside the water, it absorbs the sun like a mirror; thus by looking into the eliotropia, we can see the sun’s eclipses. It can be found in Cyprus, but the most perfect may be found in Libya, as Solinus states. Magicians say that if consecrated with some verses, and if some characters are inscribed on it, it will allow its owner to foretell the future; and if anointed with the juice of the plant by the same name, it deceives sight by making the wearer invisible. Its virtue is of rendering its wearer safe and giving him a long life. It stops the flux of blood and expels poisons.

Bat; if this image is sculpted on heliotrope, the wearer will have power over demons; and it is useful for enchantments.

5. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 41: Eliotropia

Open cited source

Eliotropia is a precious stone, and is green and sprong with red drops and veins of the colour of blood, and has that name of effect and doing. For if it be put in water before the Sun beams, it makes the water seethe in the vesell that it is in, and resolves it as it were into mist, & soon after it is resolved into rainy drops. Also it seems that this same stone may doe wonders, for if it be put in a Basin with clere water, it changes the Sun beams by rebounding of the air, & seems to shadow them, and breeds in the air red and sanguine colour, and as though the Sun were in Eclipse and darked, the Moon set between vs and the Sun is darkned, and for it seems that it may change the clearness of the Sun, it is called Elitropia, that is turning away of the Sun, as Isidore says expresly & Dioscorides also. And in Lapidario the same meaning is said in this manner.

Ex re nomen habens est in elitropia gemma, Quae solis radijs in aqua subiecta vacillo. Sanguineum reddit mutato lumine solem. Eclipsimque nouam terris effundere cogit.

And Isidore says, that this stone discovers the soily of inchauntenes of witches, that have liking and pride in their own wonders, for they begnile men's sight in those things that they work, as the foresaid stone does: and he sets an ensample thereof, and says, that an herb of the same name, with certain inchauntmentes, beguile the sight of men that look thereon; and makes a man that hears it not be seen. In many other things thy stone is good and commendable, for it staunches blood, and puts away venom, and he that bears this stone may not his beguiled.

6. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Elitropia — Heliotrope

Open cited source

Heliotrope is a stone which, when placed beneath the rays of the sun in a vessel full of water, makes the sun appear blood-red, as though it were undergoing an eclipse. Immediately the vessel in which the stone lies seems to boil and to scatter water like showers of rain. When this happens, people seized by a frenzy, if any are present, are carried out of themselves and foretell future events.

This stone prolongs the life of its bearer, staunches bleeding, drives away poisons, and protects against deceit. Furthermore, if the herb called heliotrope is placed beneath the stone and consecrated with the proper incantation, the gem renders a person invisible. This is the herb heliotrope, which some identify with the plant called solsequium (“sun-follower”). Ethiopia, Cyprus, and Africa produce this gem. It is green like emerald and sprinkled with blood-red spots.
