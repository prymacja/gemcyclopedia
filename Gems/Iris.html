AppearanceCrystal-like, transparent, hard, and six-sided. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Iris, also called Rainbowstone or Iris crystal, is a hard, six-sided crystal that casts the shape and colors of a rainbow on a wall when sunlight strikes it indoors. ² ³ It is found by the Red Sea and in the mountains of Germany. ¹ ² ³ ⁴

Generic

Description

Iris resembles rock crystal and is transparent, extremely hard, and six-sided. ² ³ It grows within other stones: although naturally round, pressure from the surrounding material makes it hexagonal, like the cells within a honeycomb. It is formed from moisture dried out of red-clay stone, and its great dryness gives it hardness. ¹ It is found in Arabia and the Red Sea, and in the mountains of Germany between the Rhine and Treves. ¹ ² ⁴

Properties

Indoors, hold the stone so that one part receives sunlight while the other remains in shade. It casts a brilliant, many-colored rainbow onto the opposite wall. ¹ ² ³ ⁴

A similar but distinct substance forms in gypsum; ¹ it is extremely transparent and dry, and some use it in place of window glass. ¹

Engraving

An Armed Man with bow and arrow engraved on Iris protects both its bearer and the place where it is kept from every evil. ⁴

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Iris (rainbowstone) is a stone similar to rock crystal, and it is usually hexagonal. Evax says that it comes from Arabia and occurs in the Red Sea. But we have found a great many of these stones in the mountains of Germany between the Rhine and Treves. And although they are of different sizes, they are all hexagonal. They are formed in other stones and are made hexagonal by being closely compressed by the surrounding stone, though they are naturally round—just as the cells in the middle of a honeycomb are hexagonal, although those at the edges are round. This is a very dry stone, as its great hardness indicates. It is formed from dried-out moisture escaping from the material of a stone produced from red clay; and because this moisture has been intensely attacked by dryness, the stone is very dry and hard. If it is held up indoors so that part of it is in sunshine and part is kept in the shade, it casts a reflection of a beautiful rainbow on the opposite wall or anything else; and therefore it is called iris. The cause of this has been explained above. Another substance similar to this occurs in gypsum, which is extremely transparent and very dry; and some people use it instead of glass in windows.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies XVI, XIII.6

Open cited source

  1. The iris crystal is found in Arabia in the Red Sea. It is the color of crystal, with six sides. It is called iris (lit. “rainbow”) by analogy, for if it is struck by sunlight while indoors it recreates on the walls nearby the shape and colors of the rainbow.

3. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, XLVII

Open cited source

By the Red Sea the swarthy Arabs glean Th' Iris resplendent with the Crystal's sheen; Its form six-sided, full of heav'n's own light, Has justly gained the name of rainbow bright; For in a room held 'gainst the solar rays It paints the wall with many-colour'd blaze. And where the crystal its reflection throws The heav'nly bow in all its splendour glows.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Iris is a crystalline-colored stone, found in the Arabian Red Sea; nowadays it may be found in Germany, near the river Rhine, and it is hexagonal and extremely hard. Placing one part under the sun’s rays and the other in the shade, under a roof, it will throw beams of light on the opposite wall that are similar to those of the rainbow; it is from this effect that it takes its name.

Open cited passage: engraved Armed Man

Armed Man with a bow and arrow; if this image is found on iris, it has the virtue of protecting the bearer, and the locale in which it is placed, from all evils.

5. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 55: Iride

Open cited source

Iris is a six cornered stone, as Isidore says, and was first found in Arabia, by the red sea, and is now found in many a place, as in Germany, in Ireland, and many lands of the North, & is bright and cleere in colour, & like to Crystal, as Isid. says, and has name Iris of the likeness of the rain bow, for if it be in a house in the Sun, it makes the likeness of the colour of the rain bow, in the walls of the next house, as Isi. says. Men suppose that this stone has the same virtue that Beryl has, but that is not found in quantity, as Dioscorides says. Also it is said, that this stone helps women that travel of child, that they may the sooner be delivered, & better suffer their throws, and dread peril the less.

6. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Iris

Open cited source

Iris is a stone resembling crystal and having a hexagonal shape. When placed in the rays of the sun inside a house, it produces colours like those of the rainbow upon the wall. Its power is against lightning; it grows in the sea like a bush.
