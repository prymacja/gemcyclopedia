Galactite

Galactite

A pale pumice specimen illustrates the ash-colored generic form (source).

Galactite, also called Galactites, Galactitis, Galaricides, Milkstone, and by several other names, is an ash-colored stone that yields a milk-tasting fluid when ground. ¹ It comes from the Nile and Achelous rivers, and promotes milk in nursing women and ewes. ¹ Bound to the thigh, it eases childbirth; ² ³ held in the mouth until it melts, it disturbs the mind and memory. ² ⁴ A related form called Galatides or Gelatia is white, acorn-shaped, hard as Diamond, and heals feverish illnesses. ⁵

Generic

Description

Galactite is ash-colored, though some call it white like milk. ⁴ When ground or rubbed, it yields a white fluid with the color and taste of milk; ¹ ⁴ its powder also tastes of milk. ² ³ The stone is associated with the Nile and Achelous rivers; ³ another account names the Athaleus and Egypt. ³

Some call a white-veined Emerald Galactitis. ¹ ⁴ This is a separate naming account, and the sources do not establish that such an emerald is the same stone as the milk-exuding Galactite.

Properties

Milk and childbirth

Galactite promotes milk in nursing women. Mixed with mead, it is drunk by a nursing mother just after rising from the bath and before eating. ² Alternatively, string a perforated stone on wool from a ewe with young and bind it around the mother’s neck. Bound around the neck, it fills the breasts with milk. ² ³ ⁴ Tied around the thigh during the pains of childbirth, it eases labor; one account specifies a thread of wool from a pregnant sheep. ⁴

Hung around an infant’s neck, Galactite makes the child’s mouth water. ¹

Flocks and mange

To increase milk in ewes and protect them from disease, crush Galactite with salt and mix it with lustral water. ² Before sunrise, carry it around the fold; another account directs that the mixture be sprinkled at night around the sheepfold. ³ It makes the ewes’ udders abound with milk and drives away murrain or scab. ² One account adds that the sheep become fertile and free of mange, and says the stone also cures mange in people. ⁴

Mind, memory, and the affections

The juice of Galactite disturbs the mind when held in the mouth; ² ³ if left there until it melts, it can obliterate memory. ¹ Its effects also include forgetting past quarrels and troubles. When ground three times in water, dried, and drunk with clear water, it reconciles those at odds and joins them in a lasting love. ⁴ one account praises its virtues above those of all other stones, yet warns that it brings frenzy and hideous fancies when melted in the mouth.

Other virtues

Carried on the person, Galactite immediately appeases a king’s anger and restores his favor to one who has offended him. It brings victory in legal causes, makes its bearer astute, amiable, and eloquent, and guards against enchantment. It makes magical writings intelligible and compels summoned spirits to answer. ⁴ Tied around the neck, it relieves toothache.

Subtype I: Acorn-shaped white Gelatia

This form, also called Galatides or Galactica, is white and bright, shaped like an acorn, and hard as Diamond. ⁵ Its small pores keep fire from entering, so it is so cold that fire can barely warm it. It halts lust, calms wrath, and heals feverish illnesses. ⁵

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies XVI, X.20

Open cited source

Batrachites is easily split into layers, like a potsherd. Galactites is ashy in color and pleasant to the taste; it is so named because when it is ground up it emits a milky substance (cf. γάλα (gala), gen. γάλακτος (galaktos), “milk”).

Galactitis is milky (cf. γάλα (gala), gen. γάλακτος (galaktos), “milk”); when rubbed it gives off a white fluid tasting of milk. When tied onto nursing women it makes their breasts productive. It is also said to make an infant’s mouth water when it is hung around its neck. It becomes liquid in the mouth and obliterates memory. The rivers Nile and Achelous yield this stone. Some people call a smaragdus banded with white veins a galactitis.

2. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, XLII

Open cited source

The ashy Galactite, if mixed with mead,
Has likewise power milk in the breasts to breed:
Yet let the dame just rising from the bath,
Before she eats, the strength'ning potion quaff:
Or let the perforated stone be strung
On thread made from the wool of ewe with young;
Thus, round the neck of nursing mother bound,
It makes her breasts with plenteous milk abound.
Tied round the thigh in parturition's pains
The trembling wife an easy labour gains.
This, mixed with salt and lustral water, bear
Around thy fold, ere Phoebus first appear.
Then thy ewes' udders shall with milk abound
And murrains fell be banished from thy ground.
So high the ancients do its virtues raise
That all the rest combined its worth outweighs:
Yet, melted in the mouth, with frenzies blind
And hideous fancies it disturbs the mind.
From the bruised stone exudes a milky dew
Of milky savour — if report be true.
This potent gem Egyptian Nilus sends,
Which Achelous by its birth commends.

3. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Galaricides (milk-stone), which some people call galarictides, is a stone like ash, and it is mostly found in the Rivers Nile and Achelous. Its powder has a taste of milk; and its juice held in the mouth disturbs the mind. According to books on physical ligatures, if bound around the neck, it fills the breasts with milk; and if bound on the thigh it eases childbirth. The shepherds of Egypt say that if it is crushed with salt and mixed with water and sprinkled at night around the sheepfold, the udders of the sheep are filled with milk and the scab is driven away from them. In fact, it is generally said to be a remedy against scab.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Galactides or Galaricides is an ash-colored stone, or as some say, white like milk. It is found in the Nile and in a river called Athaleus. If ground in water, it has the color and taste of milk. Some say that it is an emerald encircled with white veins. Because of its different virtues, it has different names. Some call it elebon, magicians call it senochitem, some graffitem, others galbatem or anachitem. Magicians celebrate this stone with infinite praises, for they say that it renders magical writings intelligible and forces summoned spirits to give answers. It also makes man forget past quarrels and problems. If carried on one’s person, it immediately placates the anger and restores the lost benevolence of any king that a person might have offended. It renders man victorious in legal causes, astute, amiable, and eloquent, and makes it possible that he may not be charmed through magic. Hung by the neck, it fills [a woman’s] breasts with milk. Tied to the thigh with a wool thread from a pregnant sheep, it eases delivery. But it can disturb the mind, if held in the mouth until it melts. If ground and mixed with salt, and that mixture is spread over a sick sheep’s pen, as the Egyptian shepherds say, it fills the sheep’s’ udders with milk and renders them fertile and frees them from mange. They also say that it cures mange in men as well. Tied around the neck, it eliminates toothaches. It pacifies any discord it if is ground three times in water and, having been dried, is then drunk with clear water. It joins in love those who are at odds and thereafter their love can never more be rendered asunder or come to an end.

5. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Galatides

Open cited source

Galatides, Galactica, or Gelatia is a stone that has many different names. It is white, bright, shaped like an acorn, and very hard, just like a diamond; it is so cold that it can be barely warmed by fire. This is the result of its pores being very small, and thus not letting fire in. Because of its cold nature, it halts lust, calms wrath, and is a good remedy for all those feverish illnesses of the human body.

6. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 50: Galactile

Open cited source

Galactiles is a stone with colour of Ashes, and has sweet tast and savour. If it be smit, a manner of white milke comes out thereof, as Isidore says. This stone closed in the mouth distroubleth the wit. And if it be borne about the neck, it makes breasts full of milke: and if it be bound to the thighs, it makes easy birth, and if it be mingled with water and salt, and sprong about the field: then the sheep be full of milke, and cleanses them of scabs, as Dioscorides says.

7. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Galartides (Galactites)

Open cited source

Galactites is a stone resembling ashes. When ground with milk and drunk, it increases milk and relieves pregnant women. It drives away scabies. It is found in the river Nile.
