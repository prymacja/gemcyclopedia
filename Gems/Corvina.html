Corvina

Corvina

The image shows Otolito de corvina 02.jpg (source).

NamesCorvina; ¹ Corvia; ¹ Sinodontides. ¹
AppearanceThe fish-head kind is darkish white, oval, convex on one side, and concave with ridges on the other; ¹ the crow-nest kind is reddish. ¹

Corvina is known both as a stone found in the head of the corvi fish and as a reddish stone obtained through a crow’s nest. ¹ The two accounts give it distinct forms and virtues. The name Corvia and the name Sinodontides are also used for Corvina. ¹

Generic

Description

Two species of Corvina are found in the head of the corvi fish. ¹ One account describes a darkish-white, oval stone, convex on one side and concave with ridges on the other. ¹ A separate Corvia account describes a reddish stone obtained through a crow’s nest. Sinodontides is another name for Corvia. ¹

Properties

The fish-head Corvina keeps away intestinal disorders when carried against the body. ¹ Ground and drunk, it has the same virtue. ¹ It is taken from the still-living fish while the moon is waxing in May. ¹

The reddish Corvia is made by boiling eggs taken from a crow’s nest on the first day of April until they are hard. ¹ Let them cool, then return them to their original nest. The crow recognizes the change, caws, and flies far away in search of the stone. When it returns, it touches the eggs with the stone, making them fresh and fertile. Quickly take the stone from the nest. ¹ It increases riches, brings honors, and foretells future events. ¹

Sources

1. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open fish-head Corvina passage

Corvina is a stone found in the head of the corvi fish, and there are always two species. Its color is darkish white, it has an oval shape, and it is convex on one side; on the other is concave with some ridges in the middle. It is extracted from the still-breathing fish when the moon is waxing in the month of May. Carried so that it touches one’s body, it keeps away intestinal disorders. Ground and drunk, it has the same effect.

Open crow-nest Corvia passage

Corvia or Corvina is a reddish-colored stone. It is produced artificially in this manner: on the first day of April, boil the eggs taken from a crow’s nest until they become hard; left to cool, let them be placed back in the nest where they originally were. The crow will recognize this, will start cawing, and will fly a long way to find this stone. Once found, the crow will come back to the nest and touch the eggs with the stone, which then become again fresh and fertile. Then it is time to quickly take the stone out of the nest. Its virtue is that of increasing riches, bringing honors, and foretelling many future events.

Sinodontides is the same as Corvia.
