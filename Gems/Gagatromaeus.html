Gagatromaeus, also called Gagatronica, Garatronicus, or Galgatromeus, is a variegated stone, reddish and marked with saffron-colored veins like a brindled animal’s skin. ³ Worn in battle, it grants victory by land and sea. ¹ ² Alcides braved every danger under its protection, and Achilles always won while bearing it; ¹ ³ each was overcome after losing it.

Generic

Description

Gagatromaeus is variegated and brindled like a young goat or wild goat; ¹ ² it may also be reddish with small saffron-colored veins like a roe deer’s skin. ² ³

Properties

Worn by a leader going to battle, the stone grants victory by sea and land. Easterners set it into their sword hilts so that they do not go to war without it. Alcides braved every danger while protected by the gem and escaped unharmed, but after losing it the mighty victor soon fell. ¹ Achilles likewise always won while he carried the stone and was felled when he did not. ² ³

Sources

1. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus

Open cited source

Gagatromaeus, differing in dye,
Like brindled skin of kids delights the eye.
Worn by the leader who to battle goes
By sea and land he’ll crush his vanquished foes.
’Twas thus Alcides every danger braved
And scaped unharmed by its protection saved,
But lost the talisman (so sages tell),
The mighty victor soon a victim fell.

2. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Gagatronica is a stone of varied colour like the skin of a wild goat. Avicenna says that its power makes those who wear it victorious. They say that this was shown by the experience of the prince Alcides Hercules; for whenever he had this stone with him he was always victorious by land and sea; but when he did not have it, he is said to have succumbed to his enemies.

3. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Garatronicus or Galgatromeus, as some say, is a reddish stone sprinkled with small saffron-colored veins similar to those of a roe deer’s skin. This [stone] is useful to soldiers. They say that Achilles had this stone and carried it along in battles, and he was never beaten by anyone—in fact, he always won against his enemies—and when he did not carry it, he was felled by his enemies. Easterners have a great abundance of them, and they use it to make the hilts of their swords, so that when they go into battle they may not do without it, since their virtue is that of rendering the bearer victorious in battle.

4. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Gagatromeus

Open cited source

Gagatromeus is a stone resembling the skin of a roe deer. It makes its bearer victorious in battle, and nothing can withstand him either on land or at sea. It is related that Hercules overcame many dangers with this stone, but succumbed to his enemies whenever he did not have the gem with him.
