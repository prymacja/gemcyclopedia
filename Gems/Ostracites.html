Ostracites resembles a potsherd or oyster shell and is hard enough for its fragments to carve other gems. ¹ It is also used like pumice to smooth skin. ¹ ² Taken in a drink, it halts bleeding; ² ground with honey, it heals breast pains. ² Camites is named as the same stone.

Generic

Description

Ostracites takes its name from its likeness to a potsherd or oyster shell. ² It is stone-like in color and harder than a shell. ¹ ² A kind resembles Agate, though agate becomes shiny when polished. The hardness of Ostracites is such that fragments of it carve other gems.

Properties

Ostracites serves in place of pumice and is used to smooth the skin. ¹ Its fragments carve other gems. ¹ Taken in a drink, it halts bleeding; ² ground with honey, it heals pains of the breast. ²

Subtype I: Potsherd Kind

This kind resembles a potsherd and is stone-like in color. ¹ It is used in place of Pumice. ¹

Subtype II: Oyster-Shell Kind

This kind resembles an oyster shell and is harder than a shell. ¹ It is used like Pumice to smooth the skin. ²

Subtype III: Agate-Like Kind

This kind resembles Agate, though Agate shines when polished. ¹ Its fragments are hard enough to carve other gems.

  1. Ostracites is so called because it resembles a potsherd (cf. ὄστρακον (ostrakon, “potsherd”)); it is used in place of pumice.

  2. Ostracites is stone-like in color, and harder than a shell (cf. ostrea, “oyster”). Another type is similar to agate, except that polishing makes agate shiny. It has such strength of hardness that other gems are carved using its fragments.

Ostracites is a stone similar in shape to an oyster shell, from which it takes the name. It is used like a pumice stone to smooth the skin. Its virtue is that of halting bleeding if taken in a drink. If ground with honey, it heals breast pains.
