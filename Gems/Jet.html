Jet (Gagates, Kacabre) is usually black, smooth, and light, though it may also be yellow, citron-colored, or grey. ¹ ² ³ It is found in Sicily, Libya, Lycia, Britain, and along northern shores. ¹ It attracts straw when rubbed, burns with a strong odor, and its smoke and preparations are credited with many remedies and protections. A citron-colored kind is also called amber in one account; ⁴ this named kind is retained here as a form of jet, while Amber remains a separate article.

Generic

Description

Jet is black, smooth, and light, often with a polished surface. Some pieces are yellow or citron-colored, nearly transparent, or pale grey with a yellowish cast. ¹ The black kind is also called “black amber”; ⁴ the citron-colored kind is called amber in one account. ⁴ The names describe forms associated with jet, while Amber is treated separately in this encyclopedia.

Properties

Warmed by friction, jet draws straw and chaff to itself. ³ When ignited, it burns with a smell compared to incense or pitch. ¹ Its smoke is said to drive away serpents, demons, and spells; ⁴ when burned, it also chases snakes. ² ⁴ Its marks on earthenware cannot be erased. One account further says that fire can be kindled in it with water and put out with oil.

Jet is used against dropsy, stomach and belly disorders, and melancholy’s phantasms. ¹ It tightens loose teeth, and when the stone is powdered and mixed with water it is used to fix them; ³ ⁴ one account says that wearing it also keeps them firm. Its odor or smoke provokes menstruation. When washed in water, or when its fumes are applied from below, it brings on menstruation; ⁴ another account ascribes this effect to its smell. ⁴

For testing virginity, jet is steeped in water for three days, and the draught is given to the woman; ³ another account specifies water strained after washing the stone, with some scrapings mixed in. ¹ The drink is said to remain in the body of a virgin, while a woman who is not a virgin immediately urinates. Jet is also said to ease childbirth: the steeped draught eases a pregnant womb in travail, and jet ground in water and given to a pregnant woman hastens birth. One account adds that it cures epilepsy and that jet mixed with a stag’s marrow heals a snakebite. ⁴

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open Gagates

Gagates (jet) is kacabre, which I consider to be one of the gemstones. It is found in Libya and in Britain near the seashore; and a great deal is found in the sea along the northern coast of Teutonia. In England (Anglia), too, it is frequently found. It is of two colours, namely black and yellow, but the yellow is nearly as transparent as topasion. Some is also grey, rather pale with a yellowish tinge. If rubbed it attracts straws, and if ignited it burns like incense. It is said to benefit those who suffer from dropsy; and it tightens loose teeth, they say. It is known from experience that water in which it has been washed, or its fumes applied from beneath, will provoke menstruation in women. It is also reported to put serpents to flight; and it is a remedy for disorders of the stomach and belly, and for phantasms due to melancholy, which some people call 'demons'. They say, too, that experience shows that if water in which it has been washed is strained and given with some scrapings of the stone to a virgin, after drinking it she retains it and does not urinate; but if she is not a virgin, she urinates at once. And this is the way virginity should be tested. And they say that it is good against the pains of childbirth.

Open Kacabre

Kacabre (jet) is the same as gagates, as we have stated; but nevertheless some people say that kacabre is better, although really it differs from gagates neither in colour nor in powers.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, IV.3

Open cited source

Jet (gagates) is a stone first discovered in Sicily, cast out from the waters of the river Gagatis, whence it is named, although it is more plentiful in Britain. It is black, flat, and smooth, and burns when moved near to a flame. The inscriptions it makes on earthenware are indelible. It drives away snakes when it is burned. It reveals those who are possessed by demons, and it signals the presence of virginity, and, amazingly, it is set alight by water and extinguished by oil.

3. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, XVIII

Open cited source

Lycia her Jet in medicine commends;
But chiefest, that which distant Britain sends:
Black, light, and polished, to itself it draws
If warmed by friction near adjacent straws.
Though quenched by oil, its smouldering embers raise
Sprinkled with water a still fiercer blaze:
It cures the dropsy, shaky teeth are fixed
Washed with the powder'd stone in water mixed.

The female womb its piercing fumes relieve.
Nor epilepsy can this test deceive:
From its deep hole it lures the viper fell.
And chases far away the powers of hell;
It heals the swelling plagues that gnaw the heart
And baffles spells and magic's noxious art.
This by the wise the surest test is styled
Of virgin purity by lust defiled.
Three days in water steeped, the draught bestows
Ease to the pregnant womb in travail's throes.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Gagates

Open cited source

Gagates, even though many think it is a gum, is nonetheless listed among the stones. It takes its name from the place where it was discovered. There are two species: the citron colored, which is called amber and of which we will say more below; the other is black, which many call “black amber.” The latter is the true gagates, even though Pliny has a different opinion. It is found in Lycia. And Solinus writes that it is found in abundance in Britain. Gagates, as I said, is black, light, dry, bright, and not transparent. If set on fire, it almost gives off the smell of pitch. Warmed up by rubbing it, it attracts straws and chaff, and its smoke chases away demons and dissolves spells and enchantments. Worn, it is useful against dropsy. Ground in water, and given to pregnant women, it hastens the birth of a child, and in whatever manner a woman drinks it, it forces her to void foul urine, but it does not have this effect on a virgin. They even say that its smell brings forth menstruation in women, and cures epilepsy; it chases away snakes, it heals their bite if mixed with a stag’s marrow, and it keeps loose teeth in place.

5. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 49: Gagite

Open cited source

Ieat is called Gagates, and is coarse stone, & nevertheless it is precious: And was first found in Sicily by the river that is called Gagas: and was thrown vp to the brink by the course of water. Therefore it has the name thereof, though most plenty & best be in Britain, as Isi. says: and is double, that is to say, yellow and black. The black is plain and light, & burns soon in fire, & drives away adders with smell thereof, when it is kindled, right as Thus or insence. This gines monition of them that have seendes within them. And is held contrary to seens: and gives knowledge of maydenhoode. For if a maid drink of the water thereof, she pisses not: and if she be no maid & drinks thereof, she pisses anon, and also against her will, as Dioscorides says: And so by this stone a maiden is anon proved, as diverse Authors affirm. Moreover the same stone both black & also yellow, being made hot with rubbing in the fingers, draws light straw and leaves to it self if it be put thereto, & a drink thereof helps them that have the dropsy: For it wastes evil apours. And it is said, that it comforts the liver, & that is perchance by dryness thereof, & also by private virtue & quality. Also the powder thereof is good to feeble teeth & wagging, & strengthneth & fastneth them. Also it is said, that this stone helps for fantasies, & against veration of fiends by night. Also fumositie thereof excites Menstrua, if it be withdrawn by any day. Also it is said, that it assuages womb ach, when the stomach is misturned by any way. Also it helps against witchcraft, & fordoth hard inchantments, as it is said in Lapidario: and it speeds fast birth, & helps her that travails of child, as it is said in Lapidario. And so, if so coarse a stone does so great wonders, none should be despised for soul colour without, while the virtue that is hid within is unknown. And Isidore speaks of this stone & says, that this stone is kindled in water, & quenched in Oil, and that is wonder.

6. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Gagas — Jet

Open cited source

Jet is a precious stone found in Lycia and Britain. It is shining, black, and light. When heated, it attracts pieces of straw. It burns when washed with water and is extinguished by oil. It helps those suffering from dropsy and, when washed with water, strengthens loose teeth. Fumigation with it restores menstruation; when burned it benefits those suffering from the falling sickness, and. It drives away demons; and if they speak through the mouth of a possessed person, its smoke forces them either to depart or to be silent. It helps an upset stomach and is effective against magical illusions.

Water in which the stone has lain for three days also benefits pregnant women: that water brings about a speedy delivery. It reveals virginity, for if the woman is a virgin she remains still, but if she has lost her virginity she immediately passes urine.
