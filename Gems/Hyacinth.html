Hyacinth

Hyacinth

NamesHyacinth; ³ Hyacinthus; ⁵ Jacinth; Jacinctus; ⁶ Iacinthus; ² ⁴ hyacinth-stone; Aquatic or aquaticus; Saphirinus; ⁵ Thalassites; ¹ Rhodiaios; ¹ Natibos; ¹ Chauniaios; ¹ Pallios; ¹ Perileukios. ¹; Iacinctus ⁷; Granate 3 4; Venetian 4; Sapphirine 4
AppearanceMany-colored and transparent: blue, sea-green, purple, pomegranate red, citron yellow, watery red, or possibly green. ¹ ³ ⁴ Blue kinds brighten in fair weather and fade in clouds; the sapphire-blue kind is most prized. ² ⁴ ⁵ ⁷ ⁸
Found inScythia, Ethiopia, and Africa. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁷ ⁸

Hyacinth, also called Iacinthus or the hyacinth-stone, shines in many colors, from blue and sea-green to pomegranate red and citron yellow. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ Its virtues strengthen the body, dispel sorrow, protect travelers, and, in its garnet-colored kind, resist fire; ⁶ it also quenches flame, eases childbirth, guards against poison, and treats troubled eyes and the heart.

Generic

Description

Hyacinth takes its name from the flower and from its shining quality. ² ³ ⁴ Its hues vary: it may resemble bluish wool or sea-green tinged with purple, while other kinds are citron-yellow, pomegranate-red, or Venetian blue. Darker colors are nobler than paler ones. The Scythian kinds include Thalassites, Rhodiaios, Natibos, Chauniaios, Pallios, and Perileukios; ¹ Chauniaios is more water-colored than the preceding kind. ¹ The finest blue is neither too deep nor too pale, but holds purple and blue in balanced measure. ² ³ Hyacinth delights in daylight and fades in darkness. The name Pallios also belongs to a Jasper kind likened to snow or sea foam; ¹ they share a name but are treated as different kinds in Jasper. The identity of the biblical Ligyrion with Hyacinth remains uncertain.

Jacinth is born from fire at the first hour of the day, when the air holds a gentle heat. ⁶ It is more airy than fiery and senses the air and its heat in proportion to the air it holds, yet remains fiery because fire engenders it. ⁶

The watery kind, aquaticus, is pale blue and less valuable; ⁵ another watery kind is red. ⁵ Saphirinus is bright blue, without the watery quality, and more valuable; ⁵ it is sometimes called Sapphire. ⁵ A fourth kind, possibly green like Topaz, is extremely hard, nearly impossible to engrave, and generally worthless. The cold nature of this green kind benefits the body by restraining its powers.

The hyacinth-stone is probably a Sapphire; ² Iacinthizonta is treated separately as a stone that resembles Hyacinth.

Properties

All kinds strengthen the body and heart, drive away grief and vain suspicions, and protect their bearer from perils abroad. ³ ⁴ They guard travelers from noxious air and contagious disease, defend against lightning and enemies, bring honor and wealth, and help secure a favorable hearing from princes. ⁴

Hyacinth quenches a blazing fire without suffering harm. If wrapped in linen and laid over sparks, the stone keeps the cloth from burning or even singeing, though the hand holding the wrapped stone feels painful heat. The stone also eases childbirth and drives away nightmares. ¹

The cold Hyacinth benefits the body, induces sleep, and brings riches, cleverness, and happiness. ⁵ Worn at the neck or finger, it keeps travelers safe, wins the welcome of their hosts, and protects them in unhealthy regions. Saphirinus has a special power against poison. ⁵

For clouded, inflamed, or suppurating eyes, hold the stone in sunlight until it warms, moisten it with saliva, then quickly lay it on the eyes; ⁶ repeat often until they clear and heal.

For madness from delusions or magic words, warm rye bread and cut a cross into its upper crust without cutting all the way through. ⁶ Draw the stone down through the cut while saying, “May God, who cast every precious stone from the devil when he transgressed his command, now cast from you, N., every delusion and magic word, and free you from this madness.” Draw the stone across the warm bread while saying, “As the splendor the devil possessed was taken from him for his transgression, so may the madness tormenting you, N., through delusions and magic words be taken from you and disappear.” Give the patient the bread around the traced area to eat. ⁶ If bodily weakness prevents eating rye bread, bless warm unleavened bread with the stone and the same words. ⁶ Trace and bless the same cross over every food the patient eats—meat, vegetable purées, and the rest—and repeat this often. For heart pain, make the sign of the cross over the heart and recite the same words. ⁶

One warning stands apart: a contrary tradition holds that Hyacinth can cause miscarriage in pregnant women. ⁴

Subtype I: Scythian Named Kinds

The Scythian kinds are named Thalassites, Rhodiaios, Natibos, Chauniaios, Pallios, and Perileukios. ¹ Chauniaios is more water-colored than the preceding kind. These stones are found in the interior of Scythia. ¹

Subtype II: Garnet or Granate

This rare, prized kind is pomegranate-red, fiery, and often tinged with purple. ³ ⁴ It comes from Africa and withstands fire.

Subtype III: Citron

The citron kind is pale yellow with a little red and is judged inferior to the other kinds. ³ ⁴ No separate virtue is given.

Subtype IV: Venetian, Cerulean, or Aquatic

This pale-blue kind changes with the weather: it is clear and pleasing in fair weather and dims under clouds. ² ³ It is colder in the mouth than other kinds and especially hard to cut; ⁴ only a Diamond can engrave it.

Subtype V: Sapphirine

The Sapphirine kind is yellow and bright, with watery qualities. ⁴ It is judged the finest kind and is found in Ethiopia.

Subtype VI: Saphirinus

Saphirinus is brilliant blue and lacks the watery quality of the aquatic kind. It is more valuable, comes from Ethiopia, and has power against poison. It is sometimes called Sapphire. ⁵

Subtype VII: Green Topasion-Like Kind

This kind may be green like Topaz. ⁵ It is extremely hard and difficult to engrave, generally worthless, and cold-natured. ⁵

Engraving

The recorded engravings give these figures and uses:

  • Horse bearing a crocodile: carved on Hyacinth and bound in gold, it gives victory in civil lawsuits and makes its wearer well-mannered, kind, and amiable. ⁴
  • Woman above, fish below, holding a mirror and branch: set in a gold ring on the finger, the wearer turns the stone toward the palm and closes the hand to become invisible. ⁴
  • Jupiter enthroned with four men before him, hands raised to heaven and crowned: carved on Hyacinth and set in gold, it grants the wearer’s wishes from wise men and kings; it may be worn as a ring or as a wax impression around the neck. ⁴
  • Mermaid with a mirror and branch on sea-Hyacinth: set in a gold ring and covered with a wax seal, it makes the bearer invisible when the stone is held tightly in the palm. ⁴
  • Woman with loose hair and a man approaching in a gesture of love: carved on Hyacinth or crystal, set in gold with amber, aloe, and polium beneath it, it makes the wearer obeyed; touching a woman with it makes her obey, and placing it beneath the pillow brings desired dreams. ⁴
  • Crowned youth seated on a throne carried by four men, with hands raised beneath a circle: carved on white Hyacinth and set in a silver ring of equal weight, with mastic and turpentine resin beneath the stone, its wax seal brings the bearer whatever he seeks from kings, powerful people, and wise men of pure mind. ⁴
  • Woman with unbound hair and a threatening man before her: carved on Hyacinth, Garnet, or crystal, set in gold with amber, aloe wood, and polium beneath it, it makes the wearer merciful and obeyed; ⁴ placed beneath the head, it brings desired dreams.

1. St. Epiphanius — On the Twelve Gemstones

Open cited source

But I am extremely impressed that Holy Scripture also concerned itself with the decorations and the glorious beauty of royal crowns, and with those of priestly garments, to which the noble stones have given such adornment. But many other and unknown noble stones are also mentioned: I speak about the Ruby and the Emerald, about Amethyst and Agate, and about Beryl and Chrysolite. Although the stones are not mentioned by these names, neither is the Hyacinth, which is an important, noble, and glorious stone. Therefore, it is necessary to consider whether perhaps Holy Scripture calls this stone Ligyrion.

For the color appearances of hyacinth are manifold. And the darker the color of hyacinth is found, the nobler it is than other hyacinths. But it resembles a bluish sheep's wool cloth or also the sea-green with slightly purple shades; therefore Holy Scripture also says: With bluish and with purple the priestly garments were adorned.

The first stone is called Thalassites; and yet another stone is called Rhodiaios, and yet another Natibos. And yet another stone is more water-colored than this, which is called Chauniaios; and yet another is called Pallios, and yet another Perileukios. But these are found in the interior of the barbarians among the Scythians.

And as Scythia the northern directions are usually designated by the old writers: Where the Gundes are and the Vetens, and the Gonkes and Venens, and Ariosans and Arianosans; up to the Germans and Amazons, which translated means "Land of Women." And there in the interior of the desert is on a rocky height near great Scythia a deep gorge that is inaccessible to humans: The rocks are surrounded by clouds on this side and that, so that from above, from the summit of the mountains, looking down as onto a wall one cannot see the bottom of the gorge. Because of the deepest depth it is darkened like an abyss.

And those who were called by the kings there to find these stones were brought by force because they were condemned to death. And these, by killing and slaughtering sheep, scattered their meat from above from the rocks into the depths of the abyss. And the stones were, as one says, sacrificed together with the limbs of the slaughtered sheep. And when then the eagles that lived on the summits of the rocks ate the meat, the stones remained on the summits of the rocks, and one sent those condemned to death to the stones. And these, thanks to their observational ability, watching where the eagles brought the meat of the sheep, went thus and found the stones that had remained on the rocks. And they took and brought them to the clients.

But all these stones, whatever color they have, are after all valuable and extremely costly. And they have the following effect: If one throws them into a strongly blazing fire, they take no damage from it, rather they extinguish the fire. Not only that, but also if one takes these stones and wraps them in a cloth, or in another linen fabric, and with this linen lays them over the sparks, the same one who holds the wrapped thing in his hand will not endure the heat and will lament painfully from burning. However, the cloth in which the stone is wrapped and laid into the fire will take absolutely no damage and not even singe.

About this stone it is also said that it is useful for women in childbirth and eases the birth. It also drives away nightmares in the same way.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies XVI, IX.3

Open cited source

  1. The hyacinth-stone (iacinthus, probably a sapphire) is named after the flower called hyacinth. It is found in Ethiopia and has a blue color. The finest hyacinth-stone is neither clear nor dulled with opacity but glows and shines purple from both qualities, although this stone is not consistent in its color. In fair weather it is clear and pleasing, while in cloudy weather it loses its strength and fades before one’s eyes. When put into the mouth, it is freezing cold. It is very hard to carve, although it is not completely unworkable for it can be incised and engraved with a diamond.

  2. Iacinthizonta is from India, and closely resembles the hyacinth-stone. Certain crystals of these, similar to iacinthus, are discolored by crisscrossing veins, and from this flaw comes their name (cf. ζώνη (zōnē), “stripe”).

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Iacinthus [Hyacinth] is a stone the ancients say has three species. It takes its name from its shining quality. In fact, some are citron colored, some granate, and others Venetian [blue], but they are all transparent and can be recognized well enough by their names. As the citron ones are of a citron color, the granates the color of the pomegranate flower, and the Venetians of a cerulean color, which when they are placed in the mouth are colder than the others, thus these are also called aquatic. To these some add another species that they call sapphirine. All of these have shades of red and yellow mixed with the already-mentioned colors. This stone above all others delights in daylight and fades in the dark. The most prized ones are those that do not have too deep or too rare a color, but that possess in moderation both qualities; it shines of a perpetual light, yet not too glittery. However, Albertus writes that zaphirine jacinths are superior to all. It is yellow and bright, does have many watery qualities, and is from Ethiopia. Some think that the granates are better, because they resist fire and are of a purplish color. The citrons have little red in them. The worst type are the Veneti or the ceruleans, for they only have a subtle hint of citron with a bit of reddishness, yet they are harder than the others, are barely scratched by diamonds, and are very cold. Other species have warmth and dryness in the first degree. They all have similar virtue, even if they are of different colors. They invigorate the body and especially the heart. They chase sadness and imaginary suspicions away. They increase a mind’s ingenuity [ingenium], fame, and wealth. They make man safe from lightnings and enemies. They offer safety to travelers so that they are not hurt by pestilences of various countries; it makes its wearer achieve noble honors and makes them safe from all contagious diseases. However, Aristotle writes that it makes pregnant women miscarry.

5. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Hyacinthus (jacinth) is of two kinds, namely aquaticus (watery) and saphirinus (sapphire). The 'water jacinth' is pale blue, as if the clearness of water welled up from its transparent depths and struggled to predominate in it; and this is less valuable. There is also a watery red one of this sort, in which the transparency of water predominates. But the sapphire is a very bright blue, having nothing watery about it; and this is more valuable. Thus there are three names i.e. hyacinthus, aquaticus, saphirinus, for the jacinth is sometimes called sapphire. This is mostly found in Ethiopia. And some people say that there is a fourth kind green? like topasion. This is extremely hard and generally worthless because it can hardly be engraved. It is known from experience that it is cold, as a green stone is, and it benefits the body just like anything that is cold and restricts the powers of the body. In Physical Ligatures its use is that suspended from the neck or worn on the finger it keeps a traveller safe, and makes him welcome to those who entertain him, and protects him in unhealthy regions. And it is known from experience that it induces sleep because of its cold constitution. And sapphire is said to have a special property, and this is its power against poison. They say also that it confers riches and natural cleverness and happiness.

6. St. Hildegard of Bingen — Physica, II. Jacinth

Open cited source

Jacinth (jacinctus) is born from fire at the first hour of the day, when the air holds a gentle heat. More airy than fiery, it senses the air and its heat, in proportion to the air it holds. Nevertheless, it is fiery because it is engendered by fire. A person who suffers fogginess in his eyes, or whose eyes are agitated or suppurative, should hold a jacinth in the sun. It immediately remembers that it was born from fire and quickly heats up. He should then dampen it with his saliva and quickly place it on his eyes, so that it warms them. He should do this often, and his eyes will become clear and healthy.

If someone is bewitched by delusions or magic words, so that he is becoming crazy, take warm rye bread, and cut the shape of the cross in the top crust, not breaking it totally. Drawing a jacinth down through the cut, say, “May God, who threw off every precious stone from the devil when the devil contravened ‘his command, now throw off from you, N., every delusion and all magic words; and may he free you from the pain of this madness.” Then, drawing the same stone across the warm bread, say, “Just as the splendor which the devil had in him was taken from him because of his transgression, so also let this madness which torments you, N., by various delusions and magic words, be taken from you, and disappear.” The bread from around the area through which you drew the jacinth should be given to the afflicted person to eat. If he cannot eat the rye bread because of the debility of his body, then, using the jacinth and the same words, bless warm, unleavened bread in the same way, and give it to him to eat. In addition, draw the same shape of the cross through all foods which he will eat, namely through meats, vegetable purees, and the rest of his food. If you frequently make crosses on them and bless them with those words, he will be cured. One who has pain in his heart should make the sign of the cross over his heart and say the same words, and he will be better.

7. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 54: Iacincto

Open cited source

Iacinctus is a blue stone some deale, & near of the colour of a Sapphire, and has that name of his own colour, as Isido. says. The stone Iacinctus that is found in Ethiopia is best, and is not too cleere, neither too dim, but mean & temperate between twain, shining. Isidore says, this stone shines not always like, for in cleere wether it is cleere before the eyes, and in darke weather it is dim and darke. Isidore says, in the mouth it seems cold, if it be therein, and is most hard to grave in. Nevertheless it may be engraved and written, and marked with the stone Adnae, as Isid. says. Dioscorides says, that the stone Iacinctus is now blue, now reddish, now purple, & now bright blue. And thereof be three manner of kinds, some be citrine, & some blue, but among such manner stones, the blue is best. The stone is wonderful, for it consumes it most to the air. For in dim weather it is dim, and in dright weather it is bright. And this stone has a singular virtue, as Authors write: for it gives gladness, and is contrary to melancholy quality: and has virtue of comfort, as Isidore says in the kind thereof. Iacirctus has virtue of comfort, & does away eleignesse and sorrow: and also vain suspition. And withstands diverse pestilences that come often of corrupt air, and gives strength and virtue to members, and life to the sinews, and gives good savor and sweet and wholsome, and is most like to the Sapphire. And Dioscorides says, that all men that treat of precious stones, mean, that the more like the Iacinct is to the Sapphire in colour & in substance, the more virtuous it is. And such Iacinctus withstands venom, and is contrary to poison, as Dioscorides says. Also an herb of the same name is like thereto in colour; and equal thereto in many things, though it be not all alike thereto in valewe, as Isidore sath.

8. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Jacinctus — Hyacinth

Open cited source

Hyacinth is a blue stone, cloudy in gloomy weather and bright in clear weather; for it is said to respond to the condition of the air. The best kind is neither too bright nor too dark. It is so hard that it resists cutting or engraving, though it is sometimes engraved with fragments of diamond. It is exceedingly cold, especially when put in the mouth.

It strengthens its bearer, drives away sadness and various suspicions, and keeps travellers safe. It is a protection against pestilence, serpents, and poisons. It makes its bearer pleasing both to God and to men. Ethiopia sends forth this gem. Garnet belongs to the genus of hyacinth.
