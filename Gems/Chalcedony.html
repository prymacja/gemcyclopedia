Chalcedony, also called Chalcedonius, Calcedonius, or Calcedon, is a lustrous stone whose colors range from dark or Sapphire-like to dusky white and dull vermilion. ⁴ Pierced and worn, it aids success in causes and preserves the body’s strength; ¹ ² ³ each kind curbs lust and guards against tempests and adversity. ⁴

Generic

Description

Chalcedony is a lustrous gem with colors ranging between Hyacinth and Beryl. ³ One account calls it pale gray or dark. ¹ Another says it forms after sunset while the air is still slightly warm, drawing its heat more from the air than from the sun. Three principal hues are known: a noble Sapphire-colored kind, a dusky pale kind tending toward white, and a dull vermilion kind that is not transparent. These species may be mixed within one stone. ⁴

Properties

Pierced with Emery (smyris) and hung around the neck, Chalcedony is said to dispel fanciful illusions arising from melancholy. ¹ Pierced and worn on the neck or hand, it secures success in lawsuits. ³ ⁴ When a hole is made in it and filled with an ass’s hair, it brings victory in civil suits and battle. ⁴ It preserves bodily strength. All kinds curb lust and protect against tempests and similar adversity. ⁴

Worn against bare skin over a vein, Chalcedony turns away infirmities, strengthens the blood, and gives a mind strong against wrath. ² Its bearer becomes tranquil, difficult to provoke justly, and safe from unjust harm. To speak firmly and wisely, hold the stone, warm it with the breath until moist, and lick it. ²

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Chalcedonius (chalcedony) is a pale grey, or rather dark-coloured stone. If it is pierced by the power of the stone called smyris (emery) and hung around the neck, it is said to be good against fanciful illusions arising from melancholy. It enables one to win causes and preserves the powers of the body.

2. St. Hildegard of Bingen — Physica, Stones

Open cited source

Chalcedony (calcedonius) develops when it is past eventide, when the sun is almost gone and the air is still a bit warm. It draws its heat more from the air than from the sun, and it has good powers. If a person carries this stone, and he always has it with him, touching his skin, so that it is placed over a vein, the vein and its blood will receive its heat and strength and carry these to the other veins and the rest of the blood. That stone turns infirmities away from a human being and gives him a mind which is very strong against wrath. He will be so tranquil in his ways that almost no one will be able to find a way to provoke him to wrath which is justified or harm him unjustly.

One who wishes to have a consistent way of speaking, and to bring forth wisely the things he says, should hold chalcedony in his hand. He should warm it with his breath, so that it becomes moist. He should then lick it with his tongue, and he will be able to speak more firmly to people.

3. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus

Open cited source

Between the Hyacinth and Beryl placed,
With lustre fair is the Calcedon graced;
But pierced, and worn upon the neck or hand,
A sure success in lawsuits ’twill command.
Unlike the Jasper, of this precious stone
Three hues alone are unto merchants known.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Calcedonius or Calcedon, as some call it, is pale in color, but nobler still is the sapphire colored one. Scholars list three species of note, though some list more. Thus in our times Germany produces a diversity of species, [so much so] that it would be in vain to list them all. The sapphire-colored stone takes first place, followed by the dusky pale, tending to white. The last is a dead vermillion color, not transparent. Oftentimes these species are found in one stone alone that presents the various colors mixed together. All of Ethiopia produces the most perfect of them all. They are even found on the shores of the Adriatic Sea near your city, and these chalcedonies are white, pale, and even dusky, and are extremely hard. If worn around the neck, they drive away the fantastical illusions born out of melancholy. If worn with a hole in which has been placed an ass’s hair, that person will win all civil lawsuits as well as prevail in battle. It conserves the body’s strength. The black or sapphire-colored ones prevent a man’s hoarseness and renders the voice clear and strong. All species of this stone harness lust and spare men from tempests and similar adversities.

Open engraving source

Man; if the image of a man with his right hand lifted up to the sky is found on calcedonius, it will have the virtue of making man win civil wars and the wearer will remain healthy and it will preserve him from any contrary accidents while traveling.

5. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 28: Calcidonia

Open cited source

Calcidonius is a pale stone, and shows dim colour, as it were means between Beryl and Iacinct, as it is said in Lapidario. And is gendered among the Nasamos: and comes and is gendered of the rain of our Lord, as it is said: & is found by shining in the full of the Moon, as Isidore says, lib. 16. Ant found but of three colours, as the Lapidary says. And all kinds thereof withstands engraving, and draws straw to it self, if it be heated and chafed. Isidore says, this stone pierced and borne about one, makes him have mastery in causes, and helps against japes & scorns of fiends, and keeps & saves virtues, as Dioscorides says.

6. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Calcedonius — Chalcedony

Open cited source

Chalcedony has a dull, dense, and invariably pale colour. Its hue lies between that of hyacinth and beryl. It is said that anyone who wears it around the neck or on a finger will win legal cases. There are said to be three varieties of this stone.
