Sarcophagus

NamesSarcophagus; ¹ ² ³ Sarcophagus stone.
AppearanceEasily cleaved along a line; ² otherwise not described.
Found inThe region of Troy; ² similar flesh-eating rocks are found in the East.

Sarcophagus is a body-eating stone from which some ancient coffins were made. ¹ ² ³ It consumes a corpse within thirty or forty days; one account says that only the teeth remain. ³ The stone is easily split along a line of cleavage and is found near Troy. ² In the East, a kindred stone can eat the flesh of a living person when bound to him. ²

Generic

Description

Sarcophagus stone is named for consuming the dead body placed within it. ¹ ² Some of the ancients made coffins from it; ¹ ³ by extension, stone tombs are called sarcophagi, though not all are made of this stone.

Properties

A corpse is consumed within thirty days in one account and within forty days in others; ¹ one account says that the teeth alone remain. ³ The stone splits easily along a line of cleavage. ²

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open the cited text

Sarcophagus is a stone that devours dead bodies, for in Greek sarkos means 'flesh' and phagein 'eat'. Some of the ancients first made coffins for the dead of this stone because in the space of thirty days it consumed the dead body. For this reason our stone monuments are called sarcophagi.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, III.15

Open the cited text

Sarcophagus stone is so named because within forty days it consumes the body of a deceased person placed in it, for σορός (soros) in Greek means “funerary ark” and φαγεῖν (phagein) is “to eat” (see VIII.xi.85 and cf. XV.xi.2). It originates in the region of Troy, and is easily split along a line of cleavage. In the East there are rocks of the same kind, which eat away the body when bound even to living people.

3. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open the cited text

Sarcophagus, from which the ancients made their tombs, takes its name from its effect. In fact, in Greek sarco means coffin, and fagos, eater. Thus sarcophagus means body-eating coffin. Indeed, in the space of forty days it consumes the human body, except the teeth, so that nothing remains. Afterward tombs were not all made with this stone alone, but all sepulchers of stone were called sarcophagi. If this stone is bound to a living man, it has the virtue of eating away at his flesh.
