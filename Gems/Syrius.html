Syrius

NamesSyrius; ¹ ² Sirius; Syrus. ³
AppearanceNot specified. ¹ ² ³
Found inSyria. ¹ ² ³

Syrius, also written Sirius, is named for Syria. ¹ While whole, it floats in water; ² ³ once cut, it sinks because the air within escapes. ²

Generic

Description

Syrius takes its name from Syria. ¹ ² ³ The source gives no color or other visible features.

Properties

The whole stone floats in water, but a cut piece sinks. ¹ ² The stone contains air while whole; ² ³ cutting releases the air, leaving only the stone’s weight to pull it under.

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, IV.10

Open the cited text

Syrius stone is named after Syria, where it is found. It is said to be buoyant when it is whole, and to sink when it is broken up.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, “Syrius”

Open the cited text

Syrius is a stone so called from Syria, and as long as it is whole it cannot be sunk in water, but if cut it sinks to the bottom. The reason for this effect is that it retains air within, and because of the lightness of air it floats, but when broken, the air escapes and thus if placed under water, it sinks because all that remains is the stone’s weight.

3. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, “Syrus”

Open the cited text

Syrus is a stone from Syria, according to Isidore, which floats when it is whole and sinks when broken into bits. Surely the reason for this is that when it is whole, the pores contain air, which escapes from the powder of the broken stone.
