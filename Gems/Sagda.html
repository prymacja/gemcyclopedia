Sagda

A green Garnierite specimen illustrates Sagda’s stated color (source).

NamesSagda; ² ³ Sardo; ¹ Sadda; ⁴ Sagada. ⁴
AppearanceGreen like Prasius, leek-green or dark green. ¹ ² ³ ⁴
Found inThe sea’s depths; it is also said to be found among the Chaldeans. ² ³

Sagda, also called Sardo, Sadda, and Sagada, is a green stone from the sea’s depths that seeks passing ships and clings to their hulls. ² ³ ⁴ Like a Magnet to iron, it holds so firmly to the wood that it can be removed only by cutting away the plank. ¹

Generic

Description

Sagda is a dark or leek-green stone, like Prasius. ¹ ² ³ It rises from the sea’s depths and fastens itself to passing ships. ² ⁴

Properties

Sagda clings to a ship’s hull with a force compared to the Magnet’s attraction of iron. ¹ ² ³ Once attached, it cannot be removed unless the wood to which it holds is cut or scraped away. ² ⁴

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open the cited text

Sagda, which others call sardo, is a stone that is related to planks of wood as magnet is related to iron, and so it clings so tightly to the planks of ships that it cannot be removed except by cutting away the plank to which it clings. In colour it is green like prase.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, VII.13

Open the cited text

Sagda is a gem with a leek-green color, found among the Chaldeans. It has such power that from the ocean depths it seeks passing ships, and clings so tenaciously to the hulls that it can hardly be removed without scraping away part of the wood.

3. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, XXXV

Open the cited text

Rarest the Sagda saw the light of day— Did it not yield itself a willing prey: Sprung from the womb of the remotest deep By some strange force it seeks the passing ship Cleaves to the keel as to the port she dies, (The crew unconscious of their priceless prize,) But grasps the timber with so firm a fold If that’s not cut, it will not lose its hold. Dark green its colour like the verdant Prase, Its virtues high the learned Chaldeans raise.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open the cited text

Sagada or Sadda is a stone the color of prassius. It is so attracted to ships that it flings itself from the bottom of the sea up to the ships and fastens itself to it so solidly that it cannot be plucked off, unless one cuts away that part of the wood where it has fastened itself.
