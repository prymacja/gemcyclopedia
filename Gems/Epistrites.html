Epistrites, also called Epistides, is a glittering red stone found in the sea and at Corinth. ¹ ² Fastened over the heart with charms, it protects its bearer from misfortune; it also drives away locusts, harmful birds, and storms from the fields.

Generic

Description

Epistrites is a brilliant red stone. ¹ ² One account places it in the sea; another finds it at Corinth. ¹ ²

Properties

Worn over the heart with the proper ligatures and spells, Epistrites keeps its bearer safe, restrains sedition, and wards off misfortune. ¹ It drives locusts, harmful birds, clouds, hailstorms, and tornadoes away from fertile land and crops. ²

In direct sunlight, Epistrites emits fire and fiery rays. ¹ Thrown into boiling water, it stops the bubbling and soon cools the water; its intense coldness causes this effect.

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Epistrites is a brilliant reddish stone occurring in the sea. In [books on] incantations and physical ligatures it is said that, worn over the heart, it keeps a man safe, and restrains sedition; and it is also said to restrain locusts and birds and clouds and hailstorms, and to keep them off the crops. It has been found by experience that, if placed in direct sunlight, it emits fire and fiery rays. And it is said that if this stone is thrown into boiling water, the bubbling ceases and presently [the water] grows cold. The reason for this is merely that [the stone] is extremely cold, and when it is affected by the heat of the boiling water the coldness of its constitution begins to act.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Epistides or Epistrites is a glittering, red-colored stone. It originates from Corinth. They say that if it is fastened to the heart with appropriate magical ties and spells, it renders man immune to any misfortunes. It chases locusts and noxious birds away from fertile land, as well as bad clouds and tornadoes from wherever this stone will be placed.

3. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 43: Episte

Open cited source

Epistes is a little stone bright and reddy, and makes a man safe that bears it in the heart side. And ceases guiles and decrites, and puts of long Flies and barren fouls, and mist & haile from the fruit of the earth. And if it be set in the Sun, fiery beams some out thereof. And if thou doest this stone in seething water, the seething thereof ceases, & the water cools soon, as Isidore says, and Dioscorides also.
