Generic

Description

Chryselectrum is golden and tends toward amber. ² It is most pleasing in the morning and fades as the sun rises; ³ at other hours its appearance changes. One kind has a color between blue and red. ¹ Its behavior near fire is variously described: it may be destroyed and disappear, burst into flame, or quickly begin to glow.

A supposed variety formed from the solidification of a base substance is actually golden marchasita, described as an intermediate matter between stones and metals. ¹

Properties

The powder of Chryselectrum is said to cure scab and ulcers. ¹ Held in the hand, the stone reduces the heat of fever. ¹

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Chryselectrum is a gem of a golden colour; and in the morning hours it is very beautiful to see, but at other hours it looks different. It is destroyed and disappears in the fire and, as some people say, it bursts into flame: and therefore it is said to fear the fire. But some say that there is another variety of this stone that is formed by the solidification of an ignoble substance; and this is not true. It is really a golden marchasita, a substance in a way intermediate between stones and metals, as we shall show later. There is said to be a third kind with a colour between blue and red. The powder of this stone is universally said to be a cure for scab and ulcers. Held in the hand, it reduces the heat of fever.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, XV.3

Open cited source

Chryselectrus is similar to gold, but tending towards amber (electrum) in color. It is pleasing, but only when seen in the morning, and is very greedy for fire; if it should be near a fire it quickly begins to glow.

3. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, LIX

Open cited source

The Chrysolectrus shines with golden rays
Still verging on the brightest Amber's blaze;
At early morning pleasing to the eye
But fading still as Phoebus mounts the sky;
Of purest fire its hasty nature made.
In flames bursts forth if near a fire 'tis laid.
