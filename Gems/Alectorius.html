Alectorius is a small, crystal-like stone found in the crop, stomach, or intestines of poultry. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ It is no larger than a bean and may be white or clear, slightly cloudy, or marked with flesh-colored veins. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ It gives strength and victory to its bearer. ¹ ³ ⁶ It also makes its bearer invincible in battle. ² ⁴ ⁵ The accounts differ about the bird, body part, and age at which the stone is found. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Generic

Description

The stone forms in poultry, but accounts differ about the bird, body part, and age. ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ One tradition places it in the crop of a cock after the bird is more than four years old; some say more than nine. A weak, old cock yields the better stone. ³ Another places it in the intestines of a rooster or capon castrated at three and kept until seven. ² It should not be taken too early; it improves with age, and the capon stops drinking when it reaches perfection. ² It is also called Electria, as if the word were Alectoria; this account places it in the stomachs of poultry. ⁴ It is crystal-like, no larger than a bean, and may be white or clear, slightly cloudy, or veined with flesh color. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Properties

Alectorius gives strength and victory in battle. ¹ ³ It makes its bearer invincible. ² ⁴ ⁵

Held in the mouth, it quenches thirst. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶

It brings honors and preserves them. ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ It helps recover lost kingdoms and conquer foreign ones. ² ³ It frees its bearer from enchantments. ² It restores peace. ⁶

It grants eloquence, constancy, graciousness, amiability, wisdom, quickness of mind, and skill in argument; it reconciles friends and makes the bearer pleasing to his wife. ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ It also arouses sexual desire. ³ ⁵ A bride can use it to bind the affection of her husband. ¹

Sources

1. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus

Open cited source

Not least the glory of the gem renowned
Within the belly of the capon found,
Which, made an eunuch when three years have flown,
Through twice two more in swelling hulk has grown;
Its utmost size no larger than a bean,
Like purest water or the Crystal's sheen;
Hence Alectorius is the jewel hight.
For gifts of strength extolled, and matchless might.
If parched with thirst place this within thy mouth,
'T will in a moment quench thy burning drouth;
Aided by this on many a well-fought day
Crotonian Milo bore the palm away:
And many a prince, with laurel on his brow,
Returned victorious o'er a mightier foe.
The weary wretch who in far exile pines,
Restored to home, with pristine honours shines.
It gifts the pleader with persuasive art
To move the court and touch the hearer's heart:
Th' exhausted frame with youthful vigour filled
Exults once more with love's high rapture thrilled.
From this the bride full powerful aid may gain
To bind her spouse's heart with triple chain.
Borne in the mouth the virtues of the stone
And all its mighty works are quickly shewn.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Alectorius [Alectory] is a stone of a crystalline color, with a slight opacity, resembling limpid water; sometimes on its surface there are flesh-colored veins. Some call it gallinaceus, from its place of origin: since they are found in the intestines of roosters, or capons castrated at the age of three, and that have lived for seven years. This stone should not be extracted earlier, since the older it is, the better it is. When the stone has reached perfection, the capon will not drink. However, no stone has ever been found bigger than a fava bean. And as Solinus writes, this stone was first prized at the time of Tarquinius Superbus. Its virtue is that of rendering its wearer invincible. Held in the mouth, it quenches thirst, hence it is appropriate for fighters. It renders the wife pleasing to her husband. It bestows honors on those who do not have it, and preserves those honors already acquired. It frees those who have been bewitched. It makes man eloquent, constant, gracious, and amiable. It helps to regain lost kingdoms and to conquer foreign ones.

3. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Alecterius is a gem also called “cockstone,” and it is shining white, like a dull rock crystal. It is extracted from the crop of a cock after more than four years; and some say after more than nine, and that one extracted from a feeble old cock is even better. The largest one of these ever found was about the size of a bean. This stone has the power to arouse sexual desire, to make one pleasing and constant, victorious and distinguished; it confers the gift of oratory, and makes friends agree. And held under the tongue it quenches or mitigates thirst. This last is a matter of experience.

4. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, XIII.8

Open cited source

Electria, as if the word were alectoria, for it is found in the stomachs of poultry (cf. ἀλέκτωρ (alektōr), “cock”). It has the appearance of a crystal and the size of a bean. Magicians would have it that this stone makes people invulnerable in battle, if we may believe it.

5. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 17: Allectoria

Open cited source

Allectoria is a stone that is found in the mawes of capons, and is like dim Crystal: and the most quantity thereof is as much as a Beane. And as witches tell, it is supposed that in battle fighting, this stone makes men insuperable, that they may not be overcome, as it is said in Lapidario. Dioscorides speaks of this stone, and says, that Allectorius the stone excites the service of Venus, and makes a man gracious & steadfast, & vidor, wise and redi, and cunning in plea, and accords friends, and quenches thirst in the mouth.

6. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Allectorius — Alectorius

Open cited source

Alectorius is a stone about the size of a bean, very like crystal but a little cloudier. It is formed in the belly of a cock which, after three years, has been castrated and then. When carried in the mouth, this stone quenches thirst. It brings victory, restores peace, restores honours, makes a person eloquent and agreeable, and especially reconciles wives with their husbands. It accomplishes all these things when kept enclosed in the mouth.
