Sardonyx

Sardonyx

The image shows Red onyx - Handicraft (source).

NamesSardonyx, sardonycem, sardonix, Molochas, and Sardonius. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁶; Sardonic ⁷
AppearanceA composite or layered stone with black, white, and red bands; ¹ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ some accounts include a chalcedonian layer. The distinctness of its colors increases its value. ⁷ ⁸
Found inIndia and Arabia; ¹ ⁴ ⁵ One account says it is revealed there by rushing water. ⁷ ⁸

Sardonyx is a layered stone formed from Sard and Onyx, with black, white, and red bands; ¹ ⁴ ⁵ some accounts add a chalcedonian layer. When the colors are clear and distinct, the stone is most prized. ⁶ It is found in India and Arabia and is especially valued for seals, from which wax releases cleanly. ² ⁴ The sources differ over its virtues: some say it restrains lust and strengthens the senses, while one account assigns it no magical power. ³

Generic

Description

Sardonyx joins Sard and Onyx; ¹ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ another account says Chalcedony may also be present. Its bands are black, white, and red, with the red layer coming from Sard. ¹ ⁴ The most admired stones have clear, distinct layers, and greater distinction between colors marks the better stone. Five kinds are recorded, though the sources do not name those five kinds.

Properties

Sardonyx is prized for seals: it takes an impression clearly, and wax does not stick to it or tear away when the seal is removed. ¹ ⁴ ⁵ It was once esteemed by the Romans. ⁶

One account says Sardonyx is warmed by the sun after the sixth hour and until shortly after the ninth hour, when the air begins to cool. ³ To strengthen understanding, knowledge, and all the bodily senses, place the stone against bare skin and often bring it to the mouth so that the breath passes over it during inhalation and exhalation. ³ This also removes great wrath, stupidity, and illiteracy, and the devil flees its purity. ³ To relieve lust, a man should place Sardonyx on his loins and a woman on her navel. ³ After sweating from an acute illness and beginning to recover, place it on a finger in a ring to prevent the illness from returning.

Another account says Sardonyx, also called Molochas, alleviates Steatomat. ² Looking through it makes a yellowish object appear smaller than it is, especially during spring growth at the beginning of Easter.

Some sources say it removes lustful inclinations and makes a person chaste, modest, amiable, and merry. ¹ ⁶ another account, however, says it has no mystic powers and recommends it to a humble and pure-hearted wearer. ⁵

Variants

Form Description Distinctive properties
Three-banded Sardonyx Black at the base, white in the middle, and cinnabar-red above. ⁴ Not specified.
Sardonyx with chalcedonian band One account includes a chalcedonian color with the black and sardian bands; clearer, more distinct colors are better. ⁶ Not specified.

Subtype I: Three-Banded Sardonyx

This kind has three bands: black at the base, white in the middle, and cinnabar-red above. ⁴

Subtype II: Sardonyx with Chalcedonian Band

One account gives this kind a chalcedonian-colored band alongside its black and sardian bands; the clearer and more distinct its colors, the better the stone. ⁶

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, “Sardonyx”

Open the cited text

Sardonyx, which some call sardonycem, is composed of two stones, namely sard and onyx. Therefore it is red, and this colour predominates in it because of the sard; and it is also white and black and the colour of the fingernail, which it gets from the onyx. The kind that is more admired has these colours in distinct layers, and is somewhat more compact in substance. There are found five varieties, and perhaps more, based on different mixtures of colour and different compactness. Sardonyx is frequently found in India and Arabia. It is said to drive out licentiousness and to make a man chaste and modest. But its greatest power is due to the fact that, although there is onyx in it, it cannot do any harm because it has sard combined with its substance.

2. St. Epiphanius — On the Twelve Gemstones, The First Stone: Sardion

Open the cited text

There is also another, Sardonyx, which is called Molochas; it alleviates the disease Steatomat. But looking through it, the yellowish appears less large than it is, especially during spring growth, when it is the beginning of Easter.

3. St. Hildegard of Bingen — Physica, V. Sardonyx

Open the cited text

Sardonyx (sardonix) is hot and develops every day when the sixth hour has already passed, (until) just past one line of the ninth hour of the day. Then it is warmed by the sun, which is shining in its purity. Because the air then begins to grow cold, sardonyx is more fiery than from air or water. It holds strong powers in its nature and furnishes strength to a person’s five senses. It is a particular remedy for each of the senses, since it is born in the purity of the sun, when nothing soils its clarity.

When a person places it against his bare skin, and even frequently places it to his mouth, so that his breath touches it as he breathes in and out, he strengthens his understanding, his knowledge, and all the senses of his body. Great wrath, stupidity, and illiterateness are removed from that person. The devil hates this purity and flees.

If a man or woman has a nature strongly burning for the works of the flesh, then he should place sardonyx on his loins and she should place it on her umbilicus, and they will have relief from that lust.

After someone has sweated from an acute disease and considers himself better, he should soon place sardonyx on his finger, on a ring. He will not again fall into this sickness.

4. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, VIII.4

Open the cited text

Sardonyx is so called from a combination of two names, from the luster of onyx and from carnelian (sardius). It consists of three colors; the base is black, the middle is white and the upper layer is cinnabar-red. Only this stone removes none of the wax when making a seal. It is found in India and Arabia when it is uncovered by rushing water. There are five kinds of sardonyx.

5. Marbodus — De lapidibus, VIII. Sardonyx

Open the cited text

The Sard and Onyx in one name unite, / And from their union spring three colours bright. / O’er jetty black the brilliant white is spread / And o’er the white diffused a fiery red: / If clear the colours, if distinct the line, / Where still unmixed the various layers join, / Such we for beauty and for value prize, / Barest of all that teeming earth supplies. / Chief amongst signets it will best convey / The stamp impressed, nor tear the wax away. / The man of humble heart and modest face, / And purest soul the Sardonyx should grace; / A worthy gem, yet boasts no mystic powers: / ’T is sent from Indian and Arabian shores.

6. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, “Sardonyx or Sardonius”

Open the cited text

Sardonyx or Sardonius is a composite stone [made] of sardius and onyx, and often also of calcedonius; at times it is of three distinct colors, black, calcedonian, and sardian. The more distinct the colors, the better quality the sardonyx. It once was well esteemed by the Romans. Its virtue is that of removing lascivious inclinations and rendering a person amiable and merry. It is excellent for making seals, as wax does not stick to it.

7. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 90: Sardonic

Open cited source

Sardonix has that name, of company of two stones, of Sardius and Onix. as Isi. says, and is of three colours. For black is lowest, white the middle, red as vermilion is highest. This stone only takes nothing of the substance of the waxe, when it is printed therein, and is found in Arabia and in India. Hereof be five manner of kinds, but which of them has most colours and most divided, & the thickest, is best. It is said that it puts of lechery, and makes men meek and chaste.

8. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Sardonix — Sardonyx

Open cited source

Sardonyx is one of the twelve stones and is formed naturally from onyx and sard. One part is red, derived from sard; another is white and another black, and those two colours come from onyx. It has no power except that onyx cannot do harm when sardonyx is present. It has a most beautiful appearance where the different colours are intermingled. This stone does not pull away wax. It makes its wearer prudent and humble. It withstands the powers of onyx: for whereas onyx causes apparitions of demons to appear in dreams, and stirs up and increases quarrels, sardonyx drives away all such things.
