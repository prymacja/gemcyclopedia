Carnelian

Carnelian

NamesCarnelian; Corneolus; ¹ Corneleus; Cornelion; ² Cornelius. ⁵ Some accounts use “sardius” as an alternate name; ³ another distinguishes Sardius from carnelian. ³; Corneolo ⁶; Corneolus vel Cornelius ⁷
AppearanceRed, transparent or translucent, sometimes like flesh or meat juice; ⁴ Rhine stones may be vermilion-red. ¹ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷
Found inSand; ² the Orient; ⁵ and the Rhine region. One account says it was first found by the Sardinians. ³ ⁶ ⁷

Carnelian is a red stone, often transparent, and is valued for its powers against bleeding and discord. ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵ The name is linked with “sardius” in one naming tradition, while another account says that Sardius is darker and duller and is not the same stone; ³ see Sard.

Generic

Description

Carnelian is a red stone, from flesh-red and citron tones to vermilion. ¹ ³ It may be transparent; ⁵ when broken, one account says, its interior resembles the juice of meat. ¹ Another divides it into five kinds but does not name them.

Properties

Carnelian restrains bleeding, especially menstrual and hemorrhoidal bleeding; another account also assigns it a power against dysentery. ⁵ One account says the raw-flesh-colored variety stops bleeding both externally and internally. Worn around the neck or on a finger, it appeases quarrels and angry minds. ⁴ ⁵ Another account adds that it calms anger. ¹

For a nosebleed, heat wine, place Carnelian in it, and give the wine to the sufferer to drink; ² the bleeding will cease. ²

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open the cited text

Corneolus (carnelian), or corneolus, as it is called by some, is a stone the colour of flesh, that is, red; when broken it is like the juice of meat. This is very often found near the River Rhine; it has a very red colour like minium (vermilion), and when polished it shines brightly. It has been found by experience that it reduces bleeding, especially from menstruation or hemorrhoids. It is even said to calm anger.

2. St. Hildegard of Bingen — Physica, XXIII. Carnelian

Open the cited text

Carnelian (cornelion) is more from hot air than from cold and is found in the sand. If blood flows from someone’s nose, one should heat wine, place carnelian in it, and give it to him to drink. The blood will cease to flow.

3. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, VIII.2

Open the cited text

Carnelian (sardius) is so named because it was first found by the Sardinians. It has a red color superior to marble, but among gems it is quite base. There are five kinds of carnelian.

4. Marbodus — De lapidibus, XXII. Carnelian

Open the cited text

Let not the Muse the dull Carnelian slight / Although it shine with but a feeble light; / Fate has with virtues great its nature graced, / Tied round the neck or on the finger placed. / Its friendly influence checks the rising fray, / And chases spites and quarrels far away: / That, where the colour of raw flesh is found, / Will stanch the blood fast issuing from the wound; / Whether from mangled limbs the torrents flow, / Or inward issues, source of deadly woe.

5. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, “Cornelius (Carnelian)” and magical engravings

Open the carnelian entry · Open the magical images chapter

Cornelius [Carnelian] is a red and transparent stone that comes from the Orient. In the Rhine River there can be found very red ones, almost the color of vermillion. Some tend to a transparent citron color, similar to clean flesh tone. This stone halts menstruation and calms the pain of hemorrhoids. It heals those that suffer of dysentery. Worn around the neck, or on the finger, it appeases angry disagreements and fiery minds.

The image of a man beautifully adorned and holding any beautiful thing in his hand: if found on a carnelian it has the virtue of halting blood and of conferring honors on the wearer.

Man with a sword in hand; such an image, if found on a carnelian, has the property of protecting the locale where it is placed from lightning and tempests; and it protects the wearer from vices and enchantments.

The image of a man sitting and looking up to a woman standing in front of him, with loose hair all the way down to her lower back, if found sculpted on carnelian and someone is touched with this stone, the stone will bend that person to the will and obedience of the person who touched him; that person will conquer everyone’s benevolence. When this stone is set, there must be placed some amber and terebinth [trementine] resin under it.

Basilisk, fighting with a dragon, with above the head of a man; if this figure is found on carnelian and is worn at the neck, it will give its wearer the ability of prevailing over any land or sea creatures.

The image of a man holding flowers in the hand, carved on carnelian and made into a tin ring on a day governed by the moon or by Venus, on the first, eighth, or twelfth hour; afterward, if you touch anyone with that very ring, that person will obey you.

The image of a man sitting with a woman in front, whose hair reach her thighs and whose eyes are raised up, carved on carnelian, will make any man or woman touched with that seal obey all your wishes. It must be set in gold, equal in weight to the stone. Underneath it there should be placed bishop’s wort and amber.

Basilisk and Dragon; the image of these animals shown entangled, along with the head of a bull, if carved on a carnelian and placed around the neck, will make the wearer victorious in a fight against any woodland or sea beast.

The image of a bearded man holding a flower in his hand, engraved on carnelian and set in a tin ring made during the moon’s phases, either on a day governed by Venus under the first moon or on the eighth moon; touching whomever one wishes will bend them to one’s will.

Capricorn; if engraved on carnelian, or any other stone, and set in a silver ring and carried, the wearer’s enemies will not harm his person or wealth, nor will any judge pass an unjust sentence. His affairs and honors will be great and earn friendship; charms will be dispelled, and in battle no enemy will resist him.

One further carnelian passage begins “If this image is found engraved on a carnelian,” but its referent is unclear in the source context, so no effect is assigned to it here.

6. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 34: Corneolo

Open cited source

Corneolus is a red stone and dim, but it is full good and precicus, if it be hanged about a man's neck, or borne on a finger. In strifes it alayeth wrath that rise, and it staunches blood that runs of a member and all manner running. And namely such a running as women have, as it is said in Lapidario.

7. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Corneolus vel Cornelius — Carnelian

Open cited source

The stone called corneolus or cornelius is said to be red, but of a dull colour resembling cut flesh. It calms anger and stops blood flowing from a limb or the nose, and especially bleeding in a woman suffering a discharge. It is said that the children of Israel engraved a great number of these stones, namely carnelians, in the wilderness, and not without good reason.
