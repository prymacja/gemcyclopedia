Anthracitis

NamesAnthracitis; ¹ Antracites; ² Antrachas. ²
AppearanceFiery-colored like Carbuncle, encircled by a white vein. ¹ ²

Anthracitis, also called Antracites or Antrachas, is fiery in color like Carbuncle and circled by a white vein. ¹ ² Two forms are described: one loses its luster in fire and glows when doused in water; ¹ the other cannot bear fire, loses color when anointed with oil, and sparkles when wet. ² The latter protects its bearer from pestilential air and benefits swellings and abscesses.

Generic

Description

The name Anthracitis is tied to its fiery color, like Carbuncle. ¹ The stone is encircled by a white vein. ¹ ² Two descriptions differ in its response to fire, oil, and water; these forms are distinguished below.

Variants

Subtype I: Fire-Dulled Anthracitis Anthracitis; fiery-colored like Carbuncle, with white veins. Loses its luster in fire and begins to glow when doused in water. ¹ No separate virtue is stated.
Subtype II: Oil-Faded Antracites Antracites; Antrachas; fiery and sparkling like Carbuncle, encircled by a white vein. Cannot bear fire; loses color when anointed with oil and lights up and sparkles when wetted with water. ² Drives away pestilential air and protects its bearer; benefits swellings and abscesses when applied as a wetted stone to a wound.

Antracites cannot bear fire. ² When anointed with oil it loses its color; ² wetted with water, it lights up and sparkles. ² It drives away pestilential air and protects its bearer. It also benefits swellings and abscesses, which are treated as wounds are treated with Agapis: bind the stone to the affected place and wet it with water.

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies XVI.XIV.2, Anthracitis

Open cited source

Anthracitis is so named because it has a fiery color, like the Carbuncle, but is circled with white veins. A characteristic of anthracitis is that when thrown into the fire it loses its luster as if it were lifeless, while, on the contrary, it begins to glow when doused in water. — Etymologies XVI.xiv.2

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Antracites or Antrachas

Open cited source

Antracites or Antrachas is a stone of a fiery color that sparkles like the carbuncle. It is encircled by a white vein and cannot bear fire. Anointed with oil, it loses its color, but wetted in water, it lights up and sparkles. St. Albertus Magnus calls it a carbuncle. But it is not a carbuncle, as others sustain, even though it is similar to it in color and virtue. Its virtue is that of driving away pestilential air and thus rendering safe its wearer. It benefits [skin] swelling and abscesses called by its name [i.e., called anthraxes], in the same manner as we have said before of the agape [and wounds].
