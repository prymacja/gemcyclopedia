Jasper

Jasper

NamesJaspis; ¹ ⁵ Jasper; ³ Iaspis; ⁶ Jaspers; Pallios (the Jasper kind, distinct from the Hyacinth kind of the same name). ²; Ide ²
AppearanceTen kinds in one account and seventeen or more in others; ³ ⁶ colors range through green, yellow, red, smoky blue, and white. ² The finest is translucent green with red veins. ¹ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸
Found inThe Thermodon River (also called Thermodontos) and near Amatunt; ² Phrygia, Iberia, Hyrcania, Caspian lands, and Germany. It is also found in many places. ¹ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Jasper, also called Jaspis or Iaspis, has many colors; ² ³ ⁴ ⁶ its most prized kind is translucent green with red veins and should be set in silver. ¹ ⁵ ⁶ It checks bleeding and fever, aids childbirth, and drives away harmful phantasms. Engraved figures carry further protections and powers.

Generic

Description

Jasper is explained as “green gem” and resembles a dull Emerald. ³ ⁵ Sources count ten, seventeen, or more kinds. Its most valued form is deep, translucent green with red veins, and it should be set in silver. ¹ ⁶ Other forms are green with blood-red spots, roof-tile red or like Porphyry, citron-yellow, smoky and luminous, pale blue, or ice-like. Ide is transparent and wine-like, more golden-yellow than Amethyst. ²

Other forms are yellow, opaque, and copper-centered; rough and darker than sea flowers; ² transparent, wine-red Ide from Phrygia; smoky and brilliant, with an enduring blue glow; or yellow with central lines.

Properties

Jasper reduces or stops bleeding, including menstrual flow; ⁵ heals fevers and dropsy; ¹ ⁶ clears eyesight; ⁶ restrains lust; and guards against licentiousness. Incantations recited over it make the bearer pleasing, powerful, and safe. ¹ Silver setting increases its virtue.

Jasper is born in the late afternoon, after the ninth hour, warmed by the sun; it is more airy than watery or fiery, and its heat varies with the clouds. It calms disorders of the humors in the heart, kidneys, or elsewhere. For deafness, breathe warm breath onto the stone until warm and moist, put it in the ear, and cover the ear with thin cloth until its heat passes into it. For thick nasal discharge, warm and moisten it with the breath, set it in the nostril, and press the nose so its heat enters the head. ⁴ For gicht, press it against the affected part until it warms. ⁴

Carrying Jasper disperses phantasms and threats that appear in dreams, especially thunder and lightning. ⁴ A mother should keep it on her hand from childbirth through the child’s infancy to guard both from malign spirits. ⁴ Where a serpent breathes, place Jasper: it weakens the breath and causes the serpent to stop breathing there. ⁴

Traditions differ on conception and pregnancy. Some say Jasper prevents conception yet assists childbirth; ¹ the green kind with saffron veins is especially helpful to a pregnant woman. ⁶ Another account calls belief in Jasper’s protection of pregnant women superstition. ³

Worn at the neck, Jasper eases labor, guards against misfortune and night phantoms, and gains strength from blessing and silver. ⁶ One yellow kind delights the beholder so strongly that he seems beside himself. ² ⁵ All kinds are also held to share a common virtue.

Subtype I: Green with red veins

The most prized kind is deep green and translucent, with red veins. ⁵ ⁶ It should be set in silver. ¹ ⁶

Subtype II: Green with saffron veins

This green kind has saffron-colored veins. ⁶ It is especially helpful to pregnant women and aids childbirth. ⁶

Subtype III: Green with blood-red drops

This green kind is marked with blood-red spots or drops. ⁶ No distinct virtue is given for this appearance.

Subtype IV: Red Jasper

Red Jasper may be roof-tile red, dark red, or like red Porphyry. ⁶ No separate locality or virtue is specified.

Subtype V: Yellow and copper-centered

This kind is yellow like Emerald, opaque and foggy, with a copper-like core. ² It is found at the Thermodon River (also called Thermodontos) and near Amatunt; ² it so delights the beholder that he seems beside himself.

Subtype VI: Ide

Ide is transparent and wine-red, like a blood-red shell, and more golden-yellow than Amethyst. ² It is found in grottoes of the Phrygian mountains. ²

Subtype VII: Smoky, luminous kind

This kind is smoky and whiter than smoke, yet extremely luminous. ² Its bluish glow does not disappear. ²

Subtype VIII: Ice-colored Jasper

This kind resembles ice and is said to remedy fever phantasms. ² It is found among the Iberians, the Hyrcanians, and the peoples ruling the Caspian lands. ²

Subtype IX: Pallios

Pallios resembles snow or sea foam, and wild animals and monsters are said to fear it. ² Another, distinct kind of Hyacinth also bears the name Pallios.

Subtype X: Yellow with central lines

This kind is dim yellow, with lines running through its middle. ² No separate virtue is specified.

Subtype XI: Citron

The citron kind is pale citron-yellow and is least valued among the colors recorded. ⁶

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Jaspis (jasper) is a stone of many colours, and there are ten kinds of it. The best is translucent green with red veins, and it should properly be set in silver. It is found in many places. Experience shows that it reduces bleeding and menstruation. They say, too, that it prevents conception and aids childbirth; and that it keeps the wearer from licentiousness. In books on magic we read that if incantations are recited over it, it makes one pleasing and powerful and safe, and gets rid of fevers and dropsy.

2. St. Epiphanius — On the Twelve Gemstones

Open cited source

The stone Jasper is emerald-colored in its appearance, but is found at the edge of the mouth of the river Thermodontos and also near Amatunt. Not that which is in Cyprus, but a naturally large type that is called Amatheusion. But its appearance is as follows: According to the appearance of emerald it is yellowish, but opaque-foggy, pure-yellow; and in its core it resembles copper. But one delights in it as if one were out of one's senses, as the myths say.

Another type is rough and its color is of darker shading than the flowers of the sea. And there is also another type that is found in the grottoes of the Phrygian mountains, whose name is Ide, similar to a blood-red shell: colorfully transparent and more wine-like and more golden-yellow than amethyst.

For it does not correspond to one color and is not the same in effect, but one is smoky, and whiter than the smoke in the air, and extremely luminous. But its bluish glow does not disappear again. Similar to how from the winter-cold earth the water vapor rises, as soon as the south wind ceases. The other type resembles ice, and as the myth-makers say, it is a remedy for fever fantasies. This type has been found among the Iberians and among the Hyrcanians; and among those who rule over the land of the Caspians. And another type of Jasper – not very luminous – is yellow and has lines in the middle.

And another type of Jasper, which is called Pallios, resembles snow or sea foam. Therefore the mythmakers also say that the animals in the fields – and even the monsters – fear it.

Open cited source

is translated from the Greek into Latin as “green gem,” for ias means “green” and pinasin means “gem.” It is somewhat like a smaragdus, but with a dull color. There are seventeen varieties. Some people think the gemstone jasper provides good fortune and protection to pregnant women – but this belief belongs not to faith but to superstition.

4. St. Hildegard of Bingen — Physica, X. Jasper

Open cited source

Jasper (jaspis) develops when the sun is beginning to set, after the ninth hour of the day, and it is warmed by the fire of the sun. It is more from air than from water or fire. It has a varying heat because, when the sun is setting, its heat is often in a cloud.

A person who is deaf should hold a jasper to his mouth and breathe on it with his warm breath, so that it becomes warm and moist. Then he should put it in his ear, and place thin fabric over the stone, thus closing the ear, until its heat transfers to the ear. This stone develops from fresh air, so it breaks up various illnesses of the humors, and the person recovers his hearing.

One who has thick nasal discharge should put jasper near his mouth and breathe on it with his warm breath, so that it becomes warm and moist. Then he should set it in his nostril and press his nose with his hand. This makes its heat enter his head, so the humors in his head are loosened more quickly and gently, and he will be better.

The person in whom commotions of the humors, that is gicht, are rising, whether in his heart, kidneys, or any other bodily part, should place jasper on that area, and press until that area warms up. The torment will cease, since the good heat and strength of the jasper heals and calms humors which are too hot or too cold.

When thunder and lightning appear in dreams, it is good for a person to have jasper with him. Phantasms and menaces are dispersed and disappear.

When a woman brings forth an infant, from the time she gives birth through all the days of its infancy, she should keep a jasper on her hand. Malign spirits of the air will be much less able to harm her or the child. The tongue of the ancient serpent stretches out toward the sweat of the infant as it emerges from its mother’s vulva. At that time he is trying to ensnare the child as well as the mother. Also, if a serpent sends out its breath in any spot, place a jasper there. The breath will be weakened, so that it will be less harmful, and the serpent will stop breathing in that place.

5. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, IV

Open cited source

Of all green things which bounteous earth supplies Nothingin greenness with the Emerald vies; Twelve kinds it gives, sent from the Scythian clime, The Bactrian mountain, and old Nilus' slime; And some from copper mines of viler race Marked by the dross drawn from their matrix base: The Carchedonian from the Punic vale — To name the othere were a tedious tale. From all the rest the Scythian bear the palm Of higher value and of brighter charm, From watchful gryphons in the desert isle Stol'n by the vent'rous Arimaspian's guile. Higher their value which admit the sight.

And tinge with green the circumambient light: Unchanged by sun or shade their lustre glows, The blazing lamp no dimness on it throws. Such as a smooth or hollow surface spread Like slumbering ocean in its tranquil bed. These like a mirror the beholder's face Exactly image with reflected rays: And thus did Nero, if report say true, The mimic warfare of the arena view. But best the gem that shews an even sheen, Lustrous with equal never-varying green. Of mighty use to seers who seek to pry Into the future hid from mortal eye. Wear it with reverence due, 'twill wealth bestow And words persuasive from thy lips shall flow, As though the gift of eloquence inspired The stone itself or living spirit fired. Hung round the neck it cures the ague's chill. Or falling sickness, dire mysterious ill; Its hues so soft refresh the wearied eye, And furious tempests banish from the sky: So with chaste power it tames the furious mood And cools the wanton thoughts that fire the blood. If steeped in verdant oil or bathed in wine Its deepened hues with perfect lustre sliine.

6. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Iaspis [Jaspers], so called in both Greek and Latin, means green, because the green is of the best type and is valued above the others. As just mentioned, it is a green-colored stone with a certain depth since it has red veins; and of these there are many species. Therefore, some are translucent in a deep green. Some are green colored with blood-red drops. Some are red like a roof tile. Some are not dissimilar from red porphyry. They are of such varied colors that learned men say there are of seventeen species, though some say more. Thus today Germany is very rich in jaspers, and they export so many and in such variety of colors that it would be in vain to list them all. It is our intention to discuss only the most noble. As I said at the beginning, green emeralds with red veins are more valuable than the others, especially because they are rather transparent. After the green come the clearer ones tinted with red, and after these, the dark red. The citrons are of the lowest quality of them all, but all are of equal virtue. When worn it heals all fevers and dropsy. It clears eyesight and chases away noxious phantasms. It also eliminates lust and does not allow a woman to conceive; the green with saffron veins is particularly helpful to pregnant women, helping them to give birth. It renders its wearer powerful, victorious, and gracious, but above all other virtues it has the ability to stop the flux of blood from whatever part [of the body] it arises from. It must be set in silver, which increases its virtues.

Open cited passage: Green rooster-headed warrior

The magic or necromantic images engraved on stones by the ancients do not resemble [the zodiacal] signs, or heavenly constellations. Thus their virtues are understood only by those knowledgeable in scientific discourses. Nonetheless, it is very true, as it often happens, that no figure is sculpted on stones, if its function cannot be partially recognized through the stone’s virtue. For this reason, when a stone has more than one virtue, like stones on which are engraved images of various animal parts, these show the stone’s effect by [the principle of] similarity; as it is the case for my Jasper, on which there is a figure that has the head of a rooster, and the torso of a man in armor, holding a shield on the left hand and a whip on the right; and instead of thighs, legs and feet, there are two vipers. By carefully considering the parts of this figure, the virtues of the stone appear clear, as it has been said in the heading for Jasper. And since these magic and necromantic images have great virtue and power, and since we have mentioned here and there the numerous doctors [who write of these things], I decided to follow them and to list the images of the heavens as discussed by learned writers so that you may have knowledge of their virtues. Though, I do not think I may have assembled them all, thus if your excellency may discover more, they should be added here.

7. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 53: Iaspis

Open cited source

Iaspis is a precious stone, & is green like to Smaragdus: but it is more dim of colour. And there be seuenteene kinds thereof, as Isidore says. For Iaspis that is green, is called Gemma Pinnasin, and though the chief colour thereof be green, yet it has many other colours mingled among. The virtue thereof destroys feaners and dropsy in them that bear him chastlye: and helps in trauailing of child: and drives away fantasies: and makes a man sure in peril, and abates heat within, and staunches bleeding and sweat, and withstands lechery, and prevents conception, and staunches menstruall blood and Emoroides, if it be in powder, and took with milke, it helps and heals old Torches and viles, and cleanses the eyes of foulenesse and filth: And sharps and comforts the sight, and withstands witchcraft and enchantments, and is more virtuous in silver then in gold. In the head of an Adder that is called Aspis, is found a little stone that is called Iaspis: and men suppose that it is a stone of wonderful virtue. And some men suppose that it has that name, as it were Aspis, and men suppose, that it has as many virtues, as diverse colours and veins, as Dioscorides says. And the best Iaspis is found in the mountains of Scythia, & Griphons keep this stone, as they doe Smaragdus, as Isidore says.

8. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Jaspis — Jasper

Open cited source

Jasper is one of the twelve stones and is green, though it also has blood-red spots. The best kind is the most translucent. If the person wearing it is chaste, it drives away fevers and dropsy. It also helps women in childbirth. If consecrated, it makes its bearer safe and agreeable. It repels harmful apparitions. Its power is greater if it is worn in silver. There is a variety that is very red, fiery, and translucent, and this is precious. Another is red but not translucent, and this is of lesser worth.
