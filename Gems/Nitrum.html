Nitrum, also called Niter, is a clear, pale salt-like substance that approaches the solidity of stone, though it is not itself a stone. ¹ It dissolves and attracts, remedies jaundice, and when kindled in instruments of war it expands violently to hurl stones. ²

Generic

Description

Nitrum is a salt-like substance, not a true stone, though it is listed among stones. ² It approaches the solidity of stone and is clear, pale, and transparent, like salt. ¹ It is made artificially from earth’s salts where animals and people have urinated. ²

Properties

Nitrum dissolves and attracts, though the object of its attraction is not specified. ² It is a remedy for jaundice. ¹

It is used in instruments of war to launch stones. ² When kindled by fire, it rarifies and expands violently; ² by resistance and pressure it hurls stones into the air. A composite made by mixing three things in proportion is said to break, lead, drive away, and destroy whatever opposes it, but the ingredients are not named.

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, Nitrum

Open cited source

Nitrum approaches the solidity of stone. It is somewhat pale and transparent. And it has been proved to have the power of dissolving and attracting. It is a remedy for jaundice, and it is a kind of salt.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Nitrum (Niter)

[[Texts/Camillo Leonardi - Speculum Lapidum#Nitrum [Niter]|Open cited source]]

Nitrum [Niter] is listed by the learned among the stones, though it is not a stone: as we have said of others. It is clear and the color of salt. Its virtue is that of dissolving and attracting. It is created by artifice with the earth’s salts, there where animals and men have urinated. Its usefulness in instruments of war is well-known as it is notably used to launch stones. Thus, kindled by fire, it rarifies and violently expands; by means of opposition and pressure it launches stones in the air. It was never discovered by the ancients, but it is a discovery of modern inventiveness: by taking three things in proportion and mixing them together they make a certain composite that no force can resist, and thus it breaks, leads, drives away, and destroys everything.

3. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 70: Nitro

Open cited source

Nitrum (as Isidore says) is a stone some deale white, and may be hewn and cloven, and is full cleere. The virtue thereof dissolves and tempers, draws and cleanses, and wastes superfluities of humours. Lib. 16. cap. 2. Isidore says, that Nitrum has the name of the country of Nitria that is in Egypt. Thereof is medicine made, & there with bodies and clothes be cleansed and washed. The kind thereof is not far from the kind of salt, for it has that kind of salt, and is made right as salt in dryness in old cliffs.

The foam thereof, is called Affronitom, and is gendered dropping down in the country of Asia, and then dried with heat of the Sun, and what is best dried, is least heavy as Isidore says. And Platea says, the Nitrum is a veyne of the earth, and is hot and dry, light, red, or white or citrine, & is bitter, sour, and some deale salt in savour. Nitrum abates falnesse. If it be taken in the mouth, and consumes and wastes gleimie humours. Powder thereof consect in honey, clarifies & makes the face fair, & cleanses scabs and matter of the stomach and of the guts, if it comes of a postum, and cleanses lice, and head scabs, and slays worms of the ears, cleanses most perfectly the matter and scabs thereof. Fome thereof with vinegar, heals gnawing and swelling, and helps against the dropsy, and cleanses dimness of eyes, if it be mingled with honey, and slays venom, and destroys it, and withstands mightely the palsy of the tongue, as Plat. says and Dioscorides also. And Nitium is hot and dry in the ende of the third degree, and laxeth and cleanses, as Isidore says.
