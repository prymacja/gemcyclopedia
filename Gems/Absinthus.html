Absinthus, also called Abistos, is a black, heavy stone marked by vermilion and white lines and occasional small red spots. ¹ When heated, it retains warmth for seven days or more; a related account gives eight days. ¹ ² Like Asbestos, it retains heat for an extended time.

Generic

Description

Absinthus (Abistos) is a black stone with markings and small red spots; ¹ another account describes vermilion and white lines crossing its surface. It is heavier than its size would suggest. ²

Properties

When heated, Absinthus remains hot for seven days or more. ¹ Another account says it preserves heat for eight days. ²

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open the cited text

Absinthus is one of the black gems, with markings and sometimes little spots of red. Its power seems to be like that of asbestus, for absinthus remains hot for seven days or more, for the same reason that has been stated for asbestus.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open the cited text

Abistos is a black-colored stone, streaked and crossed by vermilion and white lines. Heated up it preserves the heat for eight days. It is weightier and heavier than it appears considering its size.

3. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 13: Absciso

Open cited source

Abscisus is a precious stone, black and heavy, straked with red vemes. If this stone be heat in the fire, it holds heat seauen days: as Isidore says. cap. de Gemmis.

4. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Abscinthus

Open cited source

Abscinthus is a black stone streaked with snow-white veins and exceedingly heavy. If it has been heated by fire, it is said to retain the heat for seven days.
