Turquoise, also called Turchois, Turchion, or Turchesia, is milk-veined: one account gives it a bright blue color, another a yellow-white hue. ¹ ² It is said to preserve sight and protect the bearer; ¹ ² horsemen carry it to keep their horses from tiring or suffering riding accidents.

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, “Turchois”

Open the cited text

Turchois (turquoise) is a stone of a brightly shining blue colour, as if milk had penetrated the blue colour and risen to the surface through it. They say that it preserves the sight and protects the wearer from misfortunes.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, “Turchion or Turchesia” and engravings

Open the stone entry · Open the bent-kneed-man engraving · Open the Aquarius engraving

Turchion or Turchesia is a yellow-colored stone tending to white, as if milk had been strained through yellow. It is very pleasant to behold and takes its name from its country of origin. Common opinion holds that it is useful to horsemen, so that as long as a rider wears it, his horse will never tire nor will he be bothered by any riding accidents. Its appearance strengthens sight, and it is also said that whoever carries it will be protected from outward and evil accidents.

The image of a man with bent knees, looking up, and holding a cloth in his hand, carved on turquoise, is useful in buying and selling various things and in behaving chastely.

The image of Aquarius carved on green turquoise makes its wearer gain much in selling or buying, to the point that buyers will go looking for him.

3. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 98: Turgot

Open cited source

Tvrgotes that is called Torcois also, is a white yellow stone, and has that name of the Country of Turki, there it is bred. This stone keeps and saves the sight, and breeds gladness and comfort.
