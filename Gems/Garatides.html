Description

Garatides, also called Gerachidem, is a black stone. ¹ ²

Properties

Worn, Garatides makes its bearer pleasant, amiable, and gracious, and reveals what others think of him. ¹ Held in the mouth, it enables him to pass correct judgments and discern well between differing opinions. ¹ ²

The stone is recognized by a trial with honey and insects. Anoint a person’s body with honey and place him where flies and bees gather while holding Garatides; ¹ they leave him untouched. ¹ When the stone is laid aside, the insects molest him. Another account specifies flies and wasps, and says that they fall upon the honey as soon as the wearer removes the stone. ²

Sources

1. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Gelachides or Garatides is a stone of a rather black color. It renders its wearer amiable, pleasant, and gracious. Held in the mouth, it allows a man to pass correct judgments and to well discern between various opinions. And furthermore, it has the benefit of letting him know what others are thinking of him. To recognize this stone, learned men give us this proof: anoint a human body with honey and place it where there are flies, holding the stone in the hand. If the body remains untouched by flies and bees but it is molested when the stone is let go of, then it is true garatides.

2. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Gerachidem is reported to be a stone of a black colour. The genuineness of the stone may be tested in this way: while wearing the stone, a man smears his whole body with honey and exposes himself to flies and wasps; if they do not touch him, the stone is genuine, and if he lays aside the stone, at once the flies and wasps fall upon the honey and suck it up. They say that if the stone is held in the mouth it confers the ability to judge opinions and thoughts. The wearer is said to be made agreeable and pleasing.

3. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 52: Geraticen

Open cited source

Geraticen is a black stone, but it passes the colour in virtue. For if a man wash clean his own mouth, & bears the stone therein: he may anon tel what other men think of him, as it is said in Lapid. And makes a man that bears it well beloved: his virtue is proved in this manner. If a man be anointed with hony, and is set among many flies, if the stone be present, the flies grieve him not; & if the stone be away then the flies grieve, bite, suck, and hurt the body.
