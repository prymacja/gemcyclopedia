Fire Stones

Fire Stones

NamesFire Stones; Piroboli; ¹ Terrobolem. ²
AppearanceTwo fire-bearing stones, one male and one female. ¹ ² ³
Found inThe East, upon a mountain. ¹ ² ³

Fire Stones, called Piroboli and Terrobolem, are a male and a female pair found in the East. ¹ ² While they remain apart, they do not burn. ³ When one draws near the other, fire breaks forth; ¹ ² the flames consume the stones and all around them, even the mountain.

Generic

Description

These are two fire-bearing stones, male and female, found in the East. ² One account names them Piroboli; ¹ another gives the Greek name Terrobolem. ²

Properties

While the stones remain apart, their fire does not ignite. ³ When one draws near the other, fire kindles between them and consumes both stones and everything around them. One account says the male approaches the female; ¹ ³ another says the female draws near the male. ² ³ The flames spread across the mountain and its surroundings.

Sources

1. Physiologus — Fire Stones (Piroboli)

Open cited source in vault · Primary source

Physiologus [ca. 4th century CE]: Piroboli rocks exist only in the East; they are igneous rocks of masculine and feminine gender. As long as they are separate from one another, they do not burn, but, if the male approaches the female, fire breaks forth and consumes all.

2. Aberdeen Bestiary — fol. 94r

Open cited source in vault · Primary source

On a certain mountain in the east, there are fire-bearing stones which are called in Greek terrobolem; they are male and female. When they are far from each other, the fire within them does not ignite. But when by chance the female draws near to the male, the fire is at once kindled, with the result that everything around the mountain burns.

3. Guillaume le Clerc — Bestiaire, chapter 3

Open cited source in vault · Primary source

In the East there far above / Are two stones on a high mountain / Which have a very strange nature, / For they bear fire and heat, / They are as male and female. / Did you ever hear a story / More wonderful or more true, / For the books make us believe it? / When these stones are far apart, / Fire does not issue for any purpose / But when by chance it happens, / That the one comes near the other, / They kindle and fire comes out / Which burns up both the stones, / And so greatly the fire waxes and grows / That it kindles all the mountain / And whatever there is on each side / Of the mountain kindles and burns.
