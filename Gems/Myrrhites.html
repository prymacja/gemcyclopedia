Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, VII.14

Open cited source

Myrrhites is so named because it has the color of myrrh. When it is compressed until it becomes warm it exudes the sweet smell of nard.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Mirites

Open cited source

Mirites is a stone similar to myrrh in both color and odor; if rubbed with a cloth, it emanates the sweet odor of muskroot.

3. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 66: Mirite

Open cited source

Mirites is a precious stone, and has that name, for it is like to Mirra, in colour. And if it be wrong and pressed, it smells sweet, as Nardus.
