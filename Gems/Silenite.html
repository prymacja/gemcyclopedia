Silenite

Silenite

A raw moonstone specimen shows a pale body with dark inclusions and sheen (source).

NamesSilenite, Silenites, Selenite, Selenites, Silonites, Sirites, Siderites, Lunarius, lunar stone, stone of the moon. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵
AppearanceTranslucent and pale with a honey-like brightness or the image of the moon; also green like grass with a white lunar mark, or red, white, purple, and variegated. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵
Found inPersia; some forms are associated with India, including a kind found in a shellfish and one carried by a tortoise. ¹ ² ⁴

Silenite, also called Selenite or Silonites, is a lunar stone found in Persian and Indian traditions. It appears as a translucent, honey-bright stone bearing the image or white mark of the moon, and also as a grass-green, red, white, purple, or variegated stone. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ Its light and power follow the moon; held beneath the tongue, it gives a firm inward sign of whether an intended act should be undertaken. ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵

Generic

Description

The stone is named for its relation to the moon: its interior light waxes and wanes with the lunar course. ¹ The names Selenites, Silenites, Silenite, and Silonites are used for this lunar stone; some accounts also call it Sirites or Siderites, while its descriptions differ from those of sapphire. ² ⁴ ⁵ A translucent kind has a clear, honey-like brightness and contains the image of the moon or a clouded star; it glitters in darkness. ¹ ⁵ Other forms are green, red, white, purple, or variegated. ² ³ ⁴

Found in

The stone is found in Persia. ¹ ³ ⁵ Some accounts place a kind in an Indian shellfish, while another says a tortoise carries it in India. ² ⁴

Properties

Silenite waxes and wanes with the moon. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ When carried beneath the tongue, it reveals whether a matter should be undertaken: what should be done is impressed firmly on the mind, while what should not be done quickly departs or produces an inward recoil. ² ⁴ ⁵ One account says to wash the mouth before placing the stone within it. ⁵

Its divinatory power follows appointed lunar hours. At the new moon it acts for one hour; on the tenth day it acts in the first and sixth hours. ² ⁴ While the moon waxes, it is powerful in the morning and at the sixth hour; while the moon wanes, it is powerless except on the preceding day. ⁴

The stone cannot be broken by fire. It reconciles those who are at enmity and is said to heal consumption and those weakened by long illness. ⁴

Subtype I: Persian Green Silenite

Description

This form is green as grass, resembles Jasper, and shines with a white mark shaped like the moon. It is found in Persia. ² ³ ⁵

Properties

It waxes and wanes with the moon, reconciles love and accord, and helps those suffering from consumption or illness. ³ ⁵ It is also useful for consumption while the moon waxes; as the moon wanes, it reveals future events. ¹ ⁵

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, IV.6 and X.7: “Selenite”

Open first cited passage · Open second cited passage

Selenite (selenites) is translated into Latin as ‘lunar stone,’ because people say its interior glow waxes and wanes with the moon (cf. σελήνη (selēnē), ‘moon’). It comes from Persia.

Selenite (selenitis) is translucent with a whitish honey-like gleam; it contains the image of the moon (cf. σελήνη (selēnē), “moon”). People claim that it wanes and waxes on particular days, according to the course of that celestial body. It originates in Persia.

2. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II: “Silenites”

Open cited source

Silenites (stone of the moon) is a stone of which there are varying reports. For some people say that it occurs in a certain kind of Indian shellfish, and is of most beautiful varying colours, red, white, and purple. But others say that it is green and is frequently found in parts of Persia. And they also say that it increases with the waxing of the moon and decreases with its waning. They say, too, that wearing it confers a knowledge of future events, if it is carried under the tongue, especially on the first and tenth days of the moon. For they say that on the rising of the new moon it has this power for only one hour; but on the tenth day of the moon, it has this power in the first and sixth hours. The method of divination is this: putting it under the tongue, one should think about whether some matter ought to be undertaken or not; if it should be, the heart is seized with a firm conviction that cannot be shaken off; but if it should not be, the heart immediately recoils from it. It is reported, too, that it cures languid and feeble consumptives.

3. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 92: “Silenite”

Open cited source

Silenites is a stone of Persia, & is green as grass. His colour is like to the stone Iaspis, as it is said in Lapidario, and shines with a white speck, as though in brightness it contained the shape of the Moon: and the stone Silenites follows the Moon, and wexeth and wanes as the Moon does, as Isi. says, and Diosco. also. His virtue reconciles love and accord. It is supposed that it helps Phthisic and sick men.

4. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, “Silonites”

Open cited source

Silonites is a stone carried by a tortoise in India. It is beautiful with white, red, variegated, and purple colours. There is a kind of it that is green like grass. When carried in the mouth, it foretells future events. For example, if you wish to know whether something will or will not happen, place the stone beneath your tongue and consider in your mind whether the event must happen or not. Immediately one of these alternatives will take hold in your heart so firmly that it cannot be removed.

This stone has its power in the morning and at the sixth hour when the moon is waxing. On the first day it has power for only one hour; on the tenth day the first and sixth hours. When the moon is waning it lacks power, though its power remains on the preceding day. It can never be broken by fire. It reconciles those who hate one another in love. It cures sufferers from consumption and those weakened by long illness. It is found in Persia.

5. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, “Selenites, Sirites, or Siderites”

Open cited source

Selenites, Sirites, or Siderites are synonyms for the same stone. Some say this stone is transparent with a clear and honey-like brightness and containing [within it] the image of the moon, or of a clouded star. It glitters in the dark. It takes its name from the place where it was found. Learned men list various species of this stone. The first has already been discussed. The second was discussed in the entry dedicated to Celonites, as some think it to be of the same species. The Persian [selenites] are similar to jasper because of their green color, and they duly follow the phases of the lunar progress; and, as if they were anxious of heavenly damages, they increase or decrease just like the moon increases or decreases. It is very powerful in reconciling love. During the time that the moon increases, it is very useful for those who suffer of tuberculosis. When the moon decreases, it shows marvelous effects, for it enables to foretell the future to come. Placed in the mouth, which has been first washed with water, it makes man think about those things he should be doing and those that he should not. Those things to be undertaken are thus exactly impressed in the mind so that they cannot be forgotten. And if they should not be undertaken, they quickly leave the mind.
