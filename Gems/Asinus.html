Asinus, also called Asinius, Asininus, and Onagari, is found inside the wild ass. ¹ It is nearly white, tending toward citron, round or oval, and about the size of an average nut; ¹ it is soft, with shallow crevices and a bright, citron-colored interior when broken. It has two kinds: the cephalic stone relieves head pain and epilepsy, while the maxillary stone in the jaw makes its bearer tireless in battle. ¹

Generic

Description

Asinus is found inside the wild ass. ¹ It is nearly white, with a citron tint, round or oval, and about the size of an average nut. It is soft and has shallow crevices; when broken, it resembles bright smalt with a citron-colored interior. The stone has two kinds, cephalic and maxillary. ¹

Properties

Asinus resists all poisonous animals and heals snakebites. ¹ Taken in wine, it keeps away quartan fever; ¹ children who take it in wine are freed of intestinal worms. It also makes harmless poisoned water that has already been drunk, helps women in childbirth, and aids the expulsion of a fetus that died in the womb.

Open cited source

Asinius or Asininus takes its name from the ass because it is found inside the wild ass. It is almost white and tends to a citron color with a round and oval shape; the size of an average nut, it is not hard and has some crevices that do not run very deep. When it breaks, is resembles bright smalt, tending to the citron colored. This stone is of two types: maxillary and cephalic. The cephalic, placed over the head, relieves pain and heals epilepsy. The maxillary, so called because it is found in the jaw, renders the wearer tireless, so that he shall never be defeated in battle, and on the contrary he will tire all his enemies as he will gain strength and beat them. It resists all poisonous animals; taken in wine, it keeps away the quartan fevers; and it has the marvelous property of killing worms that afflict children, when they, too, take it in wine. It renders innocuous poisoned water that has already been drunk, and it heals snake bites. It helps women in giving birth and aids in expelling a fetus who died in the womb.

Onagari is the same as Asinus, of which we spoke above, since in Greek onager means the same as the Latin asinus.
