Ranius, also called Rabri, Rami, and Vatrachius, is a heavy stone of livid color tending toward transparent white. ¹ It resists poison like Bolus Armenus, though it is distinct from that stone. ¹

Generic

Description

Ranius is a heavy stone whose livid hue tends toward transparent white. ¹ Some identify it with Bolus Armenus, but the difference in color marks Ranius as distinct.

Properties

Ranius resists poison, as does Bolus Armenus. ¹

Some accounts equate Ranius with Bolus Armenus, while its livid hue tending toward transparent white distinguishes it from the red Armenian earth also called Rabri or Bolus Armenicus. ¹ ² The shared name therefore belongs to two distinct descriptions.

Sources

1. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open the Ranius passage · Open the Vatrachius alias

Ranius, Rabri, and Rami are synonymous. According to some, it is also the same as the bolus armenus, but it seems different from the bolus armenus because it is of a livid color tending to transparent white. It is heavy, and its virtue is to resist poison, like the bolus armenus.

Vatrachius is the same as Ranius.

2. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 85: Rabri

Open cited source

Rabri is a stone, & has another common name, and is called Bolus Armenicus, and is a stone or a veyne of earth, & is of cold and dry kind, & has a red colour, and is found in Armenia, & has virtue to constrain & make hard the womb, and to staunch blood out of what place of the body it runs.
