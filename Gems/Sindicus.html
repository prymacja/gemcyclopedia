Sindicus

Sindicus, the Indian Stone, cures dropsy. Tie it to the dropsical person for three hours; ¹ the illness passes into the stone, which then outweighs the person. Expose the stone to the sun for three hours and it pours out the foul water, becoming clean again. ¹

Generic

Description

The source gives no color, form, or place of origin for Sindicus, but describes it as a stone that receives the foulness of dropsy and releases it again when placed in sunlight. ¹

Properties

To treat dropsy, bind the stone to the sick person for three hours. The foulness leaves the person and enters the stone. When the stone is untied, it outweighs the person on the scales. ¹ Then expose it to the sun for three hours: the stone pours forth the foul water it has received and becomes clean again. ¹

Sources

1. Physiologus — Indian Stone (Sindicus)

Open the cited text

There is a stone called sindicus which has the following nature: if a man is dropsical, the doctors seek out this stone, and when they find it, they tie it to the dropsical man for three hours, whereupon the foulness leaves him and is absorbed into the stone itself. When they have untied the stone from the man, they weigh them both and the moderate-sized stone outweighs the man in the scales. If it is exposed to the sun for three hours, however, the stone pours forth the most foul water, which it received from the dropsical man, and becomes clean again just as it was before.

The allegory identifies the stone with our Lord Jesus Christ.
