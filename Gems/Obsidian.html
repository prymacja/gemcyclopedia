Generic

Description

Obsidian is a translucent stone like glass, usually black but sometimes green. ¹ ² It can be polished and set into walls as a mirror, where it reflects shadowy images, and many gems are made from it. ¹ It is found in India, Italy, Spain, Libya, and Germany. ²

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, IV.21 and XII.5

Open India and Samnium account · Open India, Italy, and Spain account

Obsidian (opsius) is a black translucent stone with a resemblance to glass. It is used as mirrors on walls, to reflect shadowy images. Many people make gems from it. It originates in India and in Samnium in Italy.

The stone obsidian (obsianus) is counted as a type of glass. Sometimes it is green and sometimes black, and it is translucent. It looks rather dull and when used as a wall mirror it reflects shadows instead of images. Many people make gems out of it. People say that this stone occurs in India and Italy and in Spain near the Ocean.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Obsius or Obsianus

Open cited source

Obsius or Obsianus is a black stone that is translucent in color and similar to glass. When made even and polished, it shows images and shadows like a mirror. It is placed on the walls of buildings because of its beauty. It is equally found in Libya, Germany, and Italy.
