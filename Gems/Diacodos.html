Generic

Description

Diacodos, Diadocus, or Diadochus is pale and similar in color to Beryl. ¹ ² ³ It has a great command over devils and phantasms. ¹ The stone abhors dead bodies and loses its virtue if it touches a corpse. ¹

Properties

Diacodos is so effective in calling up phantasms that magicians make much use of it. ¹ When it is cast into water while the proper charm is spoken, various demons appear and answer questions. ² Held in the mouth, it summons any demon from hell, which then gives answers to questions put to it. ² Held in the hand, it commands spirits and makes the shapes of summoned demons appear within its orb. ³ It may be used to call water spirits and reveal their forms. But if it touches a corpse, it is deprived of its virtues. ¹

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Diacodos is a pale stone said to be somewhat similar to beryl. And it is said to be so effective in calling up phantasms that magicians use it a great deal; but nevertheless, if it touches a corpse it loses its force, so that it is declared to have a horror of death. A possible theory about these things comes from books of the magicians, Hermes and Ptolemy and Thebithen Corat; but I do not intend to discuss them here.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Diacodos or Diadocus is similar in color to the beryl, but pale. It especially commands the devils, making them appear in any matter. Therefore, thrown in water while one utters the proper charm, it makes various demons appear, who will answer those who question them. If held in the mouth, anyone may summon any demon they wish out of hell and will receive satisfaction to any question posed to it. It abhors dead bodies; thus, if the stone comes into contact with any dead body, it will be deprived of its virtues.

3. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus

Open cited source

If e'er thou seek where deep the rivers flow To force the water sprites the Fates to shew, Take the Diadochus within thy hand, No gem more potent doth the fiends command; Within its orb to thine affrighted eyes Will myriad shapes of summon'd demons rise; But if once brought in contact with a corse, Forthwith the stone shall lose its native force. Like to the Beryl shines the potent stone Which shuns the touch of one by death o'erthrown.

4. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 36: Diadoco

Open cited source

Diadocus is a pale stone: bright as Beryl, and is apt to obtain answers of fiends, for it excites fiends and fantasies. And if it happens that it touches a dead body, it is said that it loses anon his good virtues, for it is the stone that hats and is squeimous of the thing that is overcome with death, as it is said in Lapidario.

5. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Diadocos — Diadochos

Open cited source

Diadocos is a stone that, when cast into various waters, produces the likenesses of demons, so that answers to those who seek them. If it is brought near the body of a dead person, it withdraws, as though it had the power to feel and recoiled from anyone's death. It resembles beryl in colour.
