Orphanus

Orphanus

NamesOrphanus; ¹ Orfanus. ²
AppearanceViolet in one account; ² in another, a brilliant delicate wine-red, as if white snow shone within clear red wine. ¹
Found inNot found elsewhere than the Roman emperor’s crown; ¹ one account says only one had been found. ²

Orphanus, also spelled Orfanus, is the solitary jewel of the Roman emperor’s crown and preserves royal honor. ¹ ² Its color is described as either violet or a delicate wine-red, and the accounts differ on whether it still shines in darkness: one says it shines at night, another that it once did but no longer does. ¹

Generic

Description

Orphanus is the rare stone of the Roman emperor’s crown. ¹ It is prized for its beauty and value and preserves royal honor. ² The two accounts give different colors and differ on whether it still shines at night; these descriptions are retained separately.

Properties

Orphanus preserves royal honor. ² It was worn by Roman emperors as a crown jewel. ¹ ²

Subtype I: Violet Orfanus

This violet form is prized for its beauty and value and shines in darkness. ² It is called Orfanus because only one had been found at the time described. ²

Subtype II: Wine-Red Orphanus

This brilliant, delicate wine-red form is likened to clear red wine mingled with gleaming white snow and overcoming it. ¹ It was said once to shine at night, though it no longer shines in darkness.

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, Orphanus

Open cited passage

Orphanus is the stone in the crown of the Roman Emperor, and has never been seen anywhere else, and therefore it is called the orphan. Its colour is like wine, of a delicate wine-red, as if gleaming or shining white snow were mingled with clear red wine, and were overcome by it. It is a brilliant stone, and tradition says that at one time it used to shine by night; but nowadays it does not shine in the dark. It is said to preserve the royal honour.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Orfanus

Open cited passage

Orfanus is violet in color. Because of its beauty and value Roman emperors wore it on their crown. It shines in the darkness. It is called orfanus because at that time only one had been found. It is loved by emperors, as it preserves royal honors.
