Memphitis

Memphitis

NamesMemphitis; ² ³ Memphites. ¹; Menophites ⁴
AppearanceNo color or visible form is given; it is said to be as hot as fire. ¹ ⁴ ⁵
Found inMemphis, Egypt. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵

Memphitis, also called Memphites, is a gem from Memphis in Egypt. ¹ ² It makes the body insensible to pain before cutting, cautery, or amputation; mixed in a drink or ground with vinegar and applied to the limb, it numbs the place. ² ³ Its virtue is said to exceed opium.

Generic

Description

Memphitis takes its name from Memphis in Egypt. ² ³ No color or visible form is given; it is described as hot as fire. ¹

Properties

Memphitis renders the body insensible before cutting, cauterization, or amputation, so that pain is not felt. Crush it and mix it with water, then give it as a drink before the operation. Another preparation grinds it with vinegar and smears it onto the part that will be cut or burned. ² It may also be mixed into a drink. ¹ ³ The stone’s virtue is said to be greater than opium’s. ³

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, Memphites

Open cited source

Memphites is named from the city in Egypt called Memphis. It is said to be as hot as fire, with a power that is seen by its effect. For if crushed and mixed with water, and given as a drink to those who have to be cauterized or cut, it induces insensibility, so that the pain is not felt.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, IV.14

Open cited source

Memphitis is named from a place in Egypt (i.e. Memphis); it has the nature of a gem. When ground and mixed with vinegar and smeared on those parts of the body that have to be burned or cut it makes them so numb that they do not feel pain.

3. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Memphitis

Open cited source

Memphitis is so called from the city by the same name, in which it was first found; it is useful to surgeons for its virtue is greater than opium. Mixed in a drink, or ground in vinegar, applied to those limbs that need to be amputated or cauterized, it makes them numb so that the patient does not feel any pain.

4. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 65: Menophite

Open cited source

Menophites is a stone, and has that name of a place of Egypt, and is of the kind of precious stones as Isid. says. If this stone be bruised and ground, and laid upon the place that should be burnt or corued, or els with vinegar smered thereupon, it stonieth so the body, that it feels no sore neither grief of the burning, nor of carving.

5. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Memphites — Memphite Stone

Open cited source

The Memphite stone takes its name from Memphis, a place in Egypt. It has a colour like fire. Ground up and mixed with vinegar, it is given to drink to those who are to be cauterised or cut, so that it may render the body insensible and prevent it from feeling the pain.
