Sard

Found inNamed for Sardis. ¹ ³ One account lists five regional kinds: Babylonian, Indian, Arabian, Egyptian, and Cyprian; ⁴ it may occur embedded in other mined stones like a heart. ⁶ ⁷

Sard is a red, sometimes translucent stone associated with Sardis. ¹ ² ³ Its kinds differ in transparency and brightness; it is said to gladden the spirit, sharpen the mind, restrain bleeding, and counter the harmful influence of Onyx. The name is disputed: One account calls carnelian “sardius,” while one account explicitly distinguishes sardius from carnelian; see Carnelian. ⁴ ⁵

Generic

Description

Sard is a red stone, ranging from blood-red to a thick, somewhat translucent red. ¹ Five kinds are distinguished by their differing degrees of transparency. ¹ Another account ranks the Babylonian kind first, followed by the Indian, Arabian, Egyptian, and Cyprian; ⁴ it also describes the male stones as brighter and the female stones larger and dimmer.

Properties

Worn on a finger, Sard restrains the harm of Onyx. ¹ It gladdens the spirit and sharpens the wits; it prevents terrible dreams, increases wealth and cheerfulness, restrains bleeding, and grants victory over enemies. ⁴ one account gives it no virtue beyond beauty and the power to break Onyx’s malign influence. ³

One account prescribes Sard against illness and gives specific methods. To avert pestilence, carry the stone. For severe head illness, tie it atop the head in a hat, cloth, or leather pouch and recite the accompanying prayer asking God to cut away the illness and restore sound understanding. For hardened hearing, dip the stone in pure wine, wrap it in thin linen, fasten it over the deaf ear with a very thin tie, and repeat the treatment often. ² For jaundice, use the stone with urine at night, recite the same prayer, and repeat for three nights. To aid a woman unable to give birth, rub the stone over both thighs while saying the prescribed prayer, hold it at the birth opening and say the second prayer, then tie it in a belt and cinch the belt around her. ²

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open the cited text

Sardinus (sard) has been included since antiquity among precious stones. It is of a thick red colour, but somewhat translucent, as if red earth were imagined to have some transparency. There are five varieties, based on the different degrees of transparency in each. Perhaps this is the matrix of other stones, and the “house” in which they are produced. It is reported to have been found formerly near the city of Sardis, and therefore it is so named. And they say that it arouses the soul to joy and sharpens the wits; and by its counteracting powers it restrains onyx from doing harm.

2. St. Hildegard of Bingen — Physica, VII. Sard

Open the cited text

Sard (sardius) develops after midday from an inundation of showers, when the leaves of deciduous trees fall in autumn. The sun is very hot, the air is cold, and the sun’s redness warms the sard. Therefore, it is purely from air and water, and it is well balanced in a good moderation of heat. It averts adverse pestilences with its power.

If a person ails in his head from many diseases and illnesses, so that he is almost out of his mind from it, he should tie sard on top of his head, in either a hat, some cloth, or a leather sack. He should say, “Just as God threw the first angel into the abyss, so may he cut this illness from you, N., and restore good knowledge.” He will be cured.

One whose hearing has been hardened through some illness should dip the sard in pure wine. When it is thus dampened, he should put it in a thin linen cloth and fasten it over the deaf ear, with a very thin string or piece of material over the cloth. The heat of the sard will enter the ear. If he does this often, he will recover his hearing.

One who has jaundice should do a similar thing, using urine and sard at night. He should say the words as described above. After doing this for three nights, he will be cured.

If a pregnant woman is oppressed by pain but unable to give birth, rub sard around both her thighs and say, “Just as you, stone, by the order of God, shone on the first angel, so you, child, come forth a shining person, who dwells with God.” Immediately hold the stone at the exit for the child, that is at the female member, and say, “Open you, roads and door, in that epiphany by which Christ appeared both human and God, and opened the gates of hell. Just so, child, may you also come out from this door without dying, and without the death of your mother.” Then tie the same stone in a belt, and cinch it around her, and she will be cured.

3. Marbodus — De lapidibus, X. Sardian

Open the cited text

The blood-red Sardian to its birthplace owes / Its name, to Sardis, whence it first arose. / Cheapest of gems, it may no share of fame / For any virtue save its beauty claim: / Except for power the onyx’ spell to break: / Of this old sages five divisions make.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, “Sardius or Sarda”

Open the cited text

Sardius or Sarda is listed among the burning gems. The ancients frequently used it, even though it is worthless. It is red or bloodlike in color, but darker and duller than carnelian, as if it had [generated in] those places [rich] in red earth. It has a gender, which can be identified by its color. Since it was first discovered by the inhabitants of Sardis, it takes its name from them. There are five species, though all are surpassed by the Babylonian, followed by the Indian, the Arabian, the Egyptian, and last the Cyprian. In many places where stones are extracted, it is found within them as if it were a heart, as [it happens] on the island of Paros. The males are more resplendent than the females, and the females are bigger and have a murky shine. It binds the powers of onyx, for when it is present the other cannot hurt. It prevents one from seeing horrible things in one’s sleep. It increases wealth, brings about cheerfulness, sharpens the mind. It restrains the flux of blood and makes one victorious over his enemies. Some think that the sardius is the same as the carnelian, which is false.

5. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, VIII.2

Open the cited text

Carnelian (sardius) is so named because it was first found by the Sardinians. It has a red color superior to marble, but among gems it is quite base. There are five kinds of carnelian.

6. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 89: Sardio

Open cited source

Sardius is a precious stone of red colour as it were red earth, & has that name, for it was first found in Sardis, as Isid. says, and the Gloss sup. Apoc. Though this stone be precious & fair, yet many account it least in value of precious stones: for as they mean, except shining, there is no profit therewith, but only that the stone Onix may not grieve in his presence: for as it is said, Onichinus, that has some evil properties, may not show them in deed, in presence of the stone Sardius. And Dioscorides says, that over this virtue, Sardius has many other virtues. Of Sardius be five manner of kinds, but the best comes out of Sardis, and is good: for it increases ioyes, and puts away dread, and makes men bold and hardy, and sharps the wit, and in his presence Onix may not grieve. Also he says, that Sardius, that is all red, saves his bearer from inchantment, and from witchcraft.

7. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Sardius — Sard

Open cited source

Sard is one of the twelve stones. It was first discovered at Sardis. It is red, although some specimens resemble red earth. It staunches the flow of blood, and onyx cannot cause harm when sard is present. Some say that this is the stone called carnelian.
