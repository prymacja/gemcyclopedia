Phrygius is a pale, medium-weight stone from Cyprus, like Punic earth. ¹ Three times heated and sprinkled with wine, it turns red and dyes cloth. ¹ Its binding virtue calms swollen flesh, heals growing and malignant ulcers, and stops the flux of the eyes. ¹ It has three forms: the pale earth-like stone, brass dross, and an artificial red stone made by calcining Pyrite.

Generic

Description

Phrygius takes its name from Cyprus and is pale and of medium weight, resembling Punic earth. ¹ Three forms are distinguished: the pale stone, a brass dross, and an artificial kind made from Pyrite.

Properties

Heat Phrygius three times and sprinkle it with wine: it turns red and serves to dye cloth. The stone is styptic, or binding; ¹ it calms swollen flesh around a wound, heals growing and malignant ulcers, and stops the flux of the eyes. ¹

Subtype II: Brass-Dross Phrygius

This kind resembles burned brass and is its dross. ¹ No distinct property is given for it.

Subtype III: Calcined-Pyrite Phrygius

This artificial kind is made by calcining Pyrite in a furnace until it becomes reddish like blood. ¹ It is listed among the kinds of Phrygius, though it is not a natural species. ¹ No distinct property is given for it separately.

Phrygius takes its name from Cyprus, the province in which it is found. Its color is pale and it is of medium weight, like Punic earth. By heating it three times and sprinkling it with wine, it becomes red in color; it is useful to dye cloth. We find that there are three species. One we have mentioned, the other is similar to burned brass, for it is the scum [dross] of brass. The third type is not a true species since it is created by artifice, and it is made of pyrites by calcination in the furnace, until it acquires a reddish blood color; nonetheless it is listed among the phrigius species. Its virtue is styptic [binding]; it calms a wound’s swollen flesh, it heals growing and malignant ulcers, and it stops the flux of the eyes.
