Chrysolite

Chrysolite

Peridot, a modern gemstone also called chrysolite (source).

NamesChrysolite ¹; Crisolitus ¹ ²
AppearanceGold-bright and fire-sparkling, with darker sea-green shades; some kinds are golden by day and fiery at night. ¹ ²
Found inEthiopia. ¹ ²

Chrysolite shines like gold and sends out sparks like fire, with a darker sea-green tint. ¹ It drives away serpents; when worn in gold on the left arm, it wards off night terrors, and donkey hairs threaded through a pierced stone drive away demons. ²

Generic

Description

Chrysolite is a small, gold-bright stone, sparkling like fire and resembling the sea in colour, with a green tint. ¹ Crisolimphis is golden by day and fiery by night; Crisolentus is gold-coloured and fair in the morning, then grows dimmer as the day passes. ¹ Another account gives its darker shades a sea-green hue and compares the stone to gold or amber. ²

Properties

Set in gold and worn on the left arm, it drives away fiends and night terrors, abates melancholy, and comforts the understanding. ¹ Near a fire it catches flame readily. ¹ It sends out sparks of its own accord and drives away serpents. ² A hole pierced through the stone and filled with donkey hairs strengthens its power to terrify and drive away demons; it is worn on the left upper arm. ²

Sources

1. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 29: Crisolito

Open cited source

Crisolitus is a little stone of Ethiopia, shining as gold, and sprinkling as fire. And is like to the sea in colour, and somewhat green. If it be set in gold and borne on the left arme, it fears fiends and chases them away, as it is said, and it helps night frayes and breads: And abates an evil that is called Menlancholy, or does it away. And comforts the understanding, as Dioscorides says. One manner of kind of Crisolitus, is Crisolimphis, the colour thereof is deemed golden by day, and firy by night. And another manner kind of Crisolitus is called Crisolentus, & is coloured as gold, and is right fair in sight in the morrow tide. And then as the day passes, his colour waxes dim. And this stone makes most soonest heat: For if it be set by the fire, anon it waxes on a flame, as Isidore says, li. 15. ca. de Gemmis aureis.

2. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Crisolitus (two passages)

Open cited source · Open cited source

Chrysolite resembles gold or amber and has a more pleasing colour in the morning hours. If it is brought near fire, it immediately catches fire and, having seized upon the material, springs back from the fire as though fleeing it. This stone sends out fiery sparks of its own accord and drives away every kind of serpent.

Chrysolite is one of the twelve stones, mentioned above. It gleams like gold and sparkles like fire. It resembles the sea, having a certain greenish tint in its darker shades. If worn in gold, it is effective against fears of the night. If it is pierced and the hole filled with hairs from a donkey, it terrifies and drives away demons. It should be worn on the left upper arm.
