Marcasite

NamesMarcasite; ² Marchasites; ² Marchasita; ¹ Marchasida; ¹ Stone of Abesteni; ² Stone of Light. ²

Marcasite, also called Marchasites, Marchasita, Marchasida, and the Stone of Abesteni or Stone of Light, occurs in metallic colors: gold, silver, copper, or iron. ² It does not melt in fire but burns on its own. The metal color cannot be smelted from it; ¹ it leaves useless ash. ¹ It is valued by alchemists, and the Stone of Light is used for lost sight and kindles fire when struck with steel. ²

Generic

Description

Marcasite has many kinds, distinguished by the metals whose colors they bear: gold, silver, copper, and iron. ² Alchemists know it well. ¹ ² Though it does not melt in fire, it burns on its own. When heated, the metal that gives it color evaporates, leaving useless ash. ¹

Properties

The Stone of Light is useful when sight is lost. ² Some say it bears this name because, when struck with steel, it produces fire and kindles suitable material. ²

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, Marchasita

Open cited source

Marchasita, or marchasida, as some people say, is a stony substance, and there are many kinds of it: for it takes the colour of any metal whatever, and so it is called 'silver' or 'golden' marchasita, and so on for the other metals. But the metal that colours it cannot be smelted from it, but evaporates in the fire, leaving only useless ash. This stone is well known among alchemists, and is found in many places.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Marchasites (Marcasite)

[[Texts/Camillo Leonardi - Speculum Lapidum#Marchasites [Marcasite]|Open cited source]]

Marchasites [Marcasite], of which there are many different species, is different according to the diversity of metals. Thus some are the color of gold, others of silver, others of copper, and others of iron: they have more diversity in color according to the type of metal of which they are made. Alchemists know this stone very well. It is not liquefied by fire, but it burns on its own. Some call it the stone of abesteni, or of light, because it is useful when one has lost eyesight.

Some say that it is called the stone of light because when struck with steel it creates fire and kindles it in apt matter.
