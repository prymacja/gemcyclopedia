Cegolites, also called Cogolites, Cogolitus, Tegolitus, and Iudaicus, resembles an olive stone in color and size. ² Its shavings, or the ground stone, are dissolved in water and drunk to dissolve kidney or bladder stones; ¹ ² a proper quantity of water is also prescribed for strangury. ²

Generic

Description

Cegolites resembles an olive stone in color and size. ¹ Its interior is the color of alum or silver, and, though unpleasant to look at, it is useful in medicines. It is called Iudaicus because it is often found in Judea. ²

Properties

Shavings of Cegolites dissolved in water and drunk are said to dissipate a stone in the kidneys or bladder. ¹ When ground, dissolved in water, and drunk, it dissolves kidney stones and purges gravel from the bladder. ² Drunk with a proper quantity of water, it heals strangury. ²

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Cogolites or Cegolites is called Iudaicus (the Jewish stone) by medical doctors because it has often times been found in those lands. It is similar to an olive pit. Its insides are the color of alum or silver; it is not pleasant to see, but is useful in medicines. Ground and dissolved in water, and drunk, it dissolves kidney stones and purges the bladder of gravel. And drunk with a proper quantity of water it heals strangury.

Tegolitus is the same as Cogolites.

Iudaicus, so called from Judea, is the same as the Cogolitus [Cogolites].
