Generic

Description

Chrysoprase is green, its color likened to the juice of leek or cypress, and it is speckled with drops of gold. ⁴ Its name is explained through the Greek words χρυσός (chrysos), “gold,” and πράσον (prason), “leek.” Some consider it a kind of Beryl. ¹ The Indian kind is leek-green with golden spots. ¹ ³ The Ethiopian and African day-night kind is hidden by the light but revealed by darkness, fiery at night and golden by day. ¹ ⁵ Another dark kind shines in darkness, like rotten oak, and becomes a pale, dull gold in light. ⁴

Properties

When the moon is not yet full but has reached its middle size, Chrysoprase has nocturnal power. ² It aids sight, makes its bearer diligent in good works, banishes avarice, gladdens the heart, and removes strong passions. ⁴

Placed on bare skin over a part afflicted with gicht, Chrysoprase makes the pain cease. ² Held at the throat until it warms, it quiets anger and prevents wrathful speech. Wherever the stone is, death-bearing poison loses its strength. ² Carried by one who has epilepsy, it lessens the disease; airy spirits cannot mock the sufferer, who expels foam from the mouth.

For possession, pour water over Chrysoprase while saying: “O water, I pour you over this stone in that power by which God made the sun as well as the hastening moon.” Give the water to the possessed person, even if he is unwilling to drink it. ² It tortures and weakens the demon for a day. Repeat the treatment for five days; on the fifth day, pour the same water over a little bread and give it to the sufferer. If the demon is gentle, it will depart. ² A bitter and fierce demon is recognized when the sufferer is unwilling to speak, remains willfully mute and does not laugh readily, arches the hands, or spits foam. ² This stone has little power to expel such a demon, but it torments and weakens it, and it may be driven out when God wills.

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies XVI, VII.7 and XIV.8

Open Indian chrysoprase source

Chrysoprase (Chrysoprasus) is an Indian stone, with a color . . . recalling that of the sap of a leek, with intervening gold spots, whence it took its name (cf. χρυσός (chrysos), “gold”; πράσον (prason), “leek”). Some people have considered it as a type of beryl.

Open Ethiopian chrysoprase source

Chrysoprase (chrysoprasus) is an Ethiopian stone; light conceals it, but darkness reveals it, for at night it is fiery, and golden during the day.

2. St. Hildegard of Bingen — Physica, Stones, XIII

Open cited source

Chrysoprase (chrysoprasus) develops at the hour when the sun is totally withdrawn. Then the air and water are very turbid and colored green. When the moon receives the strongest power from the sun—that is when it is not yet full, but middle-sized, this stone has nocturnal power. Its great strengths have temperate and balanced heat, so that it is not too hot. On whatever part of the body gicht is afflicting a person, he should place chrysoprase there, on his bare skin, and the gicht will cease. If a person is very enraged, chrysoprase should be placed near his throat until it warms up. He will be unable to bring forth wrathful words while his ire is quieted. Wherever this stone is, any death-bearing poison loses its strength, becoming powerless and as weak as water. It loses heat in its debility, becoming less noxious. A person who has epilepsy should always have chrysoprase with him and that nocturnal disease will harm him less. The airy spirits around him will be unable to prepare their mockery, and the one suffering will expel the foam from his mouth. For a person possessed by the devil, pour water over chrysoprase, and say, “O water, I pour you over this stone in that power by which God made the sun as well as the hastening moon.” Give that water as a drink to the one possessed, in whatever way you are able, since he will be unwilling to drink it. For a whole day the devil will be tortured within him, will become weaker, and will not be able to manifest his powers in him, as he had done before. Do this for five days. On the fifth day prepare a bit of bread, with the same water poured over it, and give it to him to eat in whatever way you can. If the demon is not fierce, he will depart from that person. Airy demons can be distinguished as gentle or bitter in the following way: If the person laughs readily and looks at people kindly while gnashing his teeth, the airy spirit is gentle. If the person speaks unwillingly, is willfully mute, and does not laugh readily, and if he strongly arches his hands and spits foam from his mouth, it is a bitter and fierce demon. Chrysoprase is not much use in expelling a bitter demon, but will torture the bitter and fierce demon in the person, making it weak, and the demon will be expelled in some way, when God wishes.

3. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus

Open cited source

Parent of gems, rich India from her mines
The Chrysoprase, a precious gift, consigns,
As leaves of leeks in mingled shadows blent,
Or purple dark with golden stars besprent;
But what its virtue, rests concealed in night:
All things Fate grants not unto mortal sight.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open Indian Chrysoprase source

Crisoprassus or Crisopressus [Chrysoprase] is a stone whose green color is similar to the juice of cypress, with some golden drops that give it its name, as crisos in Greek means gold, since it is composed of gold and green colors. India and Ethiopia produce it. Its highest virtue is of aiding sight, it renders man assiduous in good works, it banishes avarice, it gladdens the heart, and it removes strong passions.

Open dark Chrysoprase source

Crisoprasius [Chrysoprase] is a stone that shines in the dark; it is of a fading and muddled color, like a rotten oak placed in a dark place, but in the light it withers and is of a pale golden color, without any brightness.

5. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, LX

Open cited source

In Afric springs the Chrysoprasion bright, Which day conceals but darkness brings to light: By night a shining fire, it lifeless lies Like golden ore when day illumes the skies. Reversed is Nature's law where light reveals Whate'er in darkness shrouding night conceals.

6. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 27: Crisoprasso

Open cited source

Crrisoprassus is a stone of Ethiopia, & is hid in light, and seen in darkness. For it is fiery by night and golden by day, as Isidorus says. Therefore by day it is hid, as it were washed away, and loses his shining, and wars pals as gold. And there is another manner, kind that is called Crisoprassus, gendered in India: and this Crisoprassus is like in colour to a stone that is like to Prassius, and is green as a Leek and is besprinkled with certain, drops of gold, as it is said in Lapidario.

7. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Crisopassus — Chrysoprase

Open cited source

Chrysoprase is a stone of two colours: it glitters with little golden drops, mingled with the colour of leek juice. They say it is found very rarely and is therefore held in greater esteem. Nevertheless, it is found in India.
