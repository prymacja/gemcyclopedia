Carchedonia is an inferior kind of Lychnis, resistant to carving and found by the reflected light of the full moon. ¹

Generic

Description

Carchedonia behaves like Lychnis, though it is much more base, or inferior. ¹ Like Lychnis, it resists carving. ¹

Properties

Carchedonia shares the virtues of Lychnis. When Lychnis is warmed by the sun or rubbed with the fingers, it draws chaff and scraps of paper to itself; ¹ it is also resistant to carving. ¹ Carchedonia resists carving in every kind.

Carchedonia is said to behave as Lychnis does, although it is much more base than Lychnis. It originates among the Nasamones in a divine rain, as they report; it is found by the reflected light of a full moon. Every kind of carchedonia is resistant to carving. — Etymologies XVI.xiv.5

Lychnis belongs to this class of fiery gems, and is so named from the glowing of lamps (cf. λύχνος (lychnos), “lamp”). It occurs in many places, but the most admired variety is found in India. Some people have called it a weaker carbuncle. It has two forms: one that glows purple and another that glows with the redness of scarlet. When heated by the sun or rubbed with the fingers, it is said to draw chaff and scraps of paper to itself. It is resistant to carving, and if it ever is carved, when it is pressed into the seal, it retains part of the wax, like the bite of an animal. It comes in four kinds.
