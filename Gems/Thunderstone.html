Thunderstone

A pale blue fluorite crystal shown as a visual analogue for the crystal-like Spanish Thunderstone (source).

NamesThunderstone; ¹ ² Ceraurum; ¹ Ceraunias. ²
AppearanceCrystal-like, tinted sky blue and red; ¹ ² Spanish kinds glow like fire, and Lusitanian kinds shine like Pyrope and withstand fire. ²
Found inGermany and Spain; ¹ another account also names Germania and Lusitania, and says the stone is found where lightning strikes. ²

Thunderstone, also called Ceraurum or Ceraunias, is cast from clashing clouds or found where lightning strikes the ground. ² It resembles crystal tinted blue and red; Spanish stones glow like fire. ¹ Worn in purity, it protects against lightning, and guards homes, farms, and ships from lightning and storms. ² It also confounds enemies in law and battle and brings sweet sleep. ²

Generic

Description

Thunderstone is said to fall from clashing clouds with thunder and to be found where a thunderbolt strikes the ground. ² It resembles rock crystal tinged sky blue; ¹ One account describes the German kind as crystal-bright with red and azure, and the Lusitanian kind as fiery and like Pyrope. ²

Subtype I: German Thunderstone

The German kind is crystal-bright, with red and azure blended together. ² It comes from Germany or Germania. ¹ No separate virtue is assigned.

Subtype II: Spanish Thunderstone

The Spanish kind is crystal-like and sky-blue, and it glows like fire. ¹ No separate virtue is assigned.

Subtype III: Lusitanian Thunderstone

The Lusitanian kind is fiery and resembles Pyrope. It defies fire. ²

Properties

Wear the stone in purity to be spared from lightning. ² Its presence protects a city, house, and farm from such harm; ² during a voyage over the sea, it guards the ship from lightning and destructive whirlwinds. The stone also confounds enemies in lawsuits and battle and brings sweet sleep. ¹

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, “Ceraurum”

Open the cited text

Ceraurum (thunderstone) is said to be like rock crystal, tinged with a sky blue colour. It is said to fall sometimes from a cloud with the thunder, and it is found in Germany and Spain, but the Spanish kind glows like fire. It induces sweet sleep, they say; and it is also said to be effective for winning battles and causes, and to protect against the danger of thunder.

2. Marbodus — De lapidibus, XXVIII. Thunderstone

Open the cited text

When flash the levin bolts from pole to pole, / When tempests roar, when awful thunders roll, / From clashing clouds the wondrous gem is thrown — / Hence styled in Grecian tongue the Thunderstone. / For in no other spot this treasure’s found / Save where the thunderbolt has struck the ground: / Hence named Ceraunias by the Grecians all, / For what we lightning they Ceraunus call. / Who in all purity this stone shall wear / Him shall the bolt of heaven ne’er fail to spare; / Its presence too protects from all such harm / His city mansion and his blooming farm. / Nor if he voyage o’er the briny deep / Shall lightnings strike or whirlwinds whelm his ship. / Thy foes in law, in battle, it confounds, / And with sweet sleep thy grateful slumbers crowns. / Two different species of this potent stone, / Two different colours, are to mortals known: / One, like the crystal bright, Germania sends. / Which with its red an azure colour blends. / The Lusitanian with the pyrope vies / In flamy radiance, and the fire defies.
