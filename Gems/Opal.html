Opal shines with many colors at once: a pale fire like Carbuncle, a purple sparkle like Amethyst, and a green gleam like Emerald. ¹ It is found in India, and one account says its size is no greater than a hazelnut. ² Another tradition names Paederos as a white gem second only to Pearl, while allowing that the name had become a general praise of beauty. ¹

Generic

Description

Opal is a variegated gem whose colors resemble the fire of Carbuncle, the purple of Amethyst, and the green of Emerald. ¹ ² The Paederos form is placed among the white gems, but its color is left uncertain; ¹ its name is also used more broadly as a promise of beauty. These descriptions are preserved as distinct forms in the variants below.

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, X.2 and XII.3

Open Paederos · Open Opalus

  1. The opal (paederos) holds second place among the white gems, after the pearl. One may ask of this gem under what color it should be classified, given that its name is applied to other sorts of beauty so frequently that it has become merely a term that promises beauty.

  2. The opal (opalus) is embellished by the colors of various gemstones, for it has the rather pale fire of a carbuncle, the sparkling purple of an amethyst, and the glittering green of a smaragdus, all glowing together with a certain variegation. It takes its name from the language of its native land (cf. Sanskrit upala, “gem”), for only India produces it.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Opalus

Open cited passage

Opalus [Opal] is a stone by a marvelous aspect, for it is composed of many diverse colors of shiny gems, like the carbuncle, amethyst, emerald, and many other gems of a certain variety that are equally shiny and wonderous to behold. It is found only in India. Its size is no bigger than a hazelnut. The high regard in which the ancients held it may be read in Pliny’s thirty-seventh book, where it says that its value was estimated at twenty thousand sesterces. Its virtues are useful against all sorts of eye diseases. It sharpens and strengthens sight. It is not improper to attribute it so many virtues, seeing that it partakes of the nature of many stones and colors.

3. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 73: Optallio

Open cited source

Optallio is called Oppalus also, and is a stone distinguished with colors of diverse precious stones, as Isid. says. Therein is the fiery colour of the Carbuncle, the shining purple of the Ametistus, the bright green colour of Smaragdus, and all the colours shine therein, with a manner diversity, and has the name of the Country. This stone breeds only in India, and is deemed to have as many virtues, as blewes and colours. Of this Optallius, it is said in Lapidario, that this stone Optallius keeps and saves his eyes that bears it, cleere and sharp and without grief, and dimineth other men's eyes that be about, with a manner cloud, and smits them with a manner blindness, that is called Amentia, so that they may not see neither take heed what is done before their eyes. Therefore it is said, that it is the most sure patron of thieves as it is said in Lapidar.
