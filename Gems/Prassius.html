Prassius

A deep-green emerald specimen shown as a color analogue for Prassius’s dark green form (source).

NamesPrassius. ¹ ²; Prassio ³
AppearanceDark or translucent green; ¹ varieties may have red spots or white lines and streaks. ¹ ² ³
Found inEthiopia near the Nile. ² ³

Prassius is a green stone, often the matrix of the Emerald, and is found in Ethiopia near the Nile. ¹ ² It has dark or translucent green forms, sometimes marked with blood-red drops or white lines. It benefits the sight and shares virtues with Jasper and emerald. ¹ An image of a bull carved upon it is useful for hexes and gives mercy in judgments.

Generic

Description

Prassius takes its name from the herb of the same name and resembles it in color. ² It is often the matrix or “palace” of the Emerald. ¹ ² It may be opaque and dark green, or translucent green with a dull cast; other forms are marked with blood-red spots or white lines and streaks.

Properties

Prassius benefits the sight. ¹ ² It has some of the virtues of Jasper and Emerald; ² another account gives it all the virtues of emerald, though less effectively.

Subtype I: Dark Green Prassius

This form is opaque and dark green like horehound. ¹ It benefits the sight and has some of the virtues of Jasper and Emerald.

Subtype II: Translucent Green Prassius

This form is transparent green, though somewhat dull rather than clear. ² It is described as the emerald's matrix and is said to comfort the sight and share the emerald's virtues, though less effectively. ²

Subtype III: Red-Spotted Prassius

This form is green and marked with blood-colored spots or drops. ¹ ²

Subtype IV: White-Marked Prassius

This form is green with white spots, white lines, or streaks resembling Chalcedony. ¹ ²

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open the cited text

Prassius is a stone which is very often the matrix and “palace” of smaragdus. It is of an opaque dark green colour like the plant prassius, or horehound. It is found sometimes with red spots, and sometimes with white. Experience shows that it benefits the sight, and it has some of the properties of jasper, and some of those of smaragdus.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open the cited text · Open the engraving passage

Prassius takes its name from an herb with the same name to which it is similar in color. They say that prassius is the emerald’s matrix [i.e., it is generated by the emerald]. They say that it is found in Ethiopia near the Nile River. There are three species. One, as just said, is transparent green with a degree of dullness and thus not clear. Another is green marked by blood-colored drops. The third is also green lined with white or calchedony’s streaks. It is of no small virtue: thus it comforts sight and has all the virtues of the emerald, though it is not as efficacious.

Bull; if this image is found on prassius, it is said to be useful for hexes [maleficia] and it gives mercy in judgments.

3. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 77: prassio

Open cited source

Prassius is a stone as green as a leek, and comforts the feeble sight, and is sometimes found with red drops, and is sometimes distinguished with white drops. Of this stone it is said in Lapid. that no profit is therewith, but that it is green, and makes gold seemely.
