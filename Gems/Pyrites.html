Pyrites, also called Perithe, Peridonius, Pyrite, Pirites, Piryte, Virites, Trapendanus, Pyr, or Ypestionus, is a firestone that sparks when struck. ¹ ⁴ Its true yellow kind is bronze-like, and a firm grasp burns the hand. ¹ ³ ⁴ Common pyrite kindles fire from sparks; ¹ ² yellow pyrite also helps the eyes and other illnesses. ⁴

Generic

Description

Pyrites takes its name from fire. ² The true kind is yellow, blunt, solid, and bronze-colored; ⁴ the action of sea waves makes its surface rough and polished like baked stone. ⁴ Struck with steel, iron, or stone, it gives off sparks. ² A firm grip burns the hand. ¹ ³

The name is also used broadly for stones that spark fire. Marcasite is called pyrites because it produces fire, and some call red Coral pyrites. ⁴ These are uses of the name and do not make the stones identical.

Properties

When struck, pyrites scatters sparks readily. Sparks from the common hearth kind can be caught on sulfur, fungi, or leaves, and they kindle a fire swiftly. ²

Pyrites burns the hand when grasped firmly, so it must be handled lightly and cautiously. ² ³ It is said to be good for coughs. ¹ The yellow kind benefits the eyes and is useful in medicines for sore eyes and many other illnesses. ⁴

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open the Perithe passage · Open the Virites passage

Perithe, or peridonius, is a stone of a yellowish colour. It is said to be good for coughs. And a marvellous thing is reported of this stone—that if it is strongly gripped in the hand, it burns the hand; and so it should be touched lightly and cautiously. There is said to be another variety of this which is similar to chrysolitus, except that it is greener.

Virites (pyrite) is the gem that we have called perithe above. Its colour is brilliant like fire, as we have said before. It should be touched lightly and cautiously, or it burns the hand of anyone who touches it. For indeed an animal that shines by night sometimes burns the hand, as I myself have often found by experience.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, IV.5

Open the cited text

Pyrite (pyrites) is a yellow Persian stone that mimics the qualities of bronze; it has a great deal of fire in it inasmuch as it gives off sparks easily. It burns the hand of anyone holding it if he squeezes it tightly. For this reason it takes its name from ‘fire’ (cf. πῦρ (pyr)). There is another, common, pyrite, which people call ‘living stone.’ It gives off sparks when struck by iron or by stone. These sparks may be caught by sulfur or various fungi or leaves, and it starts a fire quicker than one can speak. Common people call this the ‘hearth rock.’

Named from the fire the yellow Pyrite spurns The touch of man — and to be handled scorns: Touch it with trembling hand and cautious arm, For tightly grasped it burns the closed palm.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open the main entry · Open the Trapendanus alias · Open the Virites alias · Open Saturn engraving · Open armed horseman engraving · Open strawberry engraving · Open second horseman account

Pirites, also called Pyr (that is, “fire”) is commonly called the firestone, for struck with steel it sparks with fire. By some it is called Ypestionus—that is, Vulcan. Therefore, in a general sense, we may call all stones that spark fire pyrites. And thus, even the marcasite, because it produces fire, is called pyrites. Even coral for its redness is by some called pyrites. But the true pyrites is the one that as soon as it is struck sparks fire, is of a yellow color, rather blunt and solid, and becomes rough and polished like a baked stone because of the powerful action of the sea waves. Dioscorides affirms that it has the color of brass, and that ground and held between the fingers of the right hand it burns them. It is found in many places and is said to be of great use to make medicines; it particularly aids in healing sore eyes and many other illnesses as the learned state.

5. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 78: Pirite

Open cited source

Pirites is a red bright stone, like to the quality of the air: much fire is therein, and oft sparkles come out thereof, and this stone burns his hand that holds it right fast, therefore it has that name of Pir, that is fire. In Lapid. it is said, that he will easelye and softly be handled and held, for it burns if it be hard wrong.

6. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Perites, or Pederotes

Open cited source

Perites, also called pederotes, is a tawny stone. If it is gripped too tightly in the hand, it burns the hand that holds it; but it allows itself to be touched gently and cautiously. Another kind is almost green, like the juice of leeks mixed with water. This variety is very noble and is effective against spasms.
