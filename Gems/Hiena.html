Hiena

Hiena

NamesHiena; ¹ Hyaenia; ² ³ Hyena; ² ⁴ Hyena-stone. ² ⁴; Jena ⁵
AppearanceFound in the hyaena’s eye; ¹ ² it may be of various colors. ⁴ ⁵ ⁶
Found inIn the eye of a hyaena; no geographical origin is specified. ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Hiena, also called Hyaenia, Hyena, or Hyena-stone, is taken from the eyes of a hyaena. ¹ ² ³ Kept beneath the tongue, it grants foreknowledge; carried on the person, it protects against quartan ague and gout. ⁴

Generic

Description

Hiena is found in the eye of the hyaena, and comes in various colors. ¹ ² ⁴

Properties

Wash the mouth, then place the stone beneath the tongue: it grants the power of foretelling the future. ¹ ² ⁴ The ancient verse promises that futurity will become clear to the soul of its bearer. ³

Carried on the person, it guards against quartan ague and gout. ⁴

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Hiena stone is named from the beast called hyaena, because it is taken from a hyaena's eyes when they are turned to stone. But the ancient authorities, Evax and Aaron, say that if placed under the tongue it confers the power of predicting the future by divination.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies XVI, XV.25

Open cited source

  1. The hyena-stone (hyaenia) is found in the eye of that beast, the hyena (hyaena). If this stone is placed under a person’s tongue, they say he predicts the future.

3. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, XLIV

Open cited source

Torn from the eyes of the hyaena fell The Hyaenia, so the ancients tell. On mortals can prophetic gifts bestow And give the power the future to foreknow: Clear to his soul futurity appears Who 'neath his tongue this potent substance bears.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Hyena is a precious stone worthy of being protected; it takes its name from the animal by the same name, since it is found in its eyes. It is of various colors. Its virtue is such that, if reports be true, having washed one’s own mouth and having placed it under the tongue, it makes a man foretell the future. Whoever carries it with him will never suffer quartan ague or gout.

5. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 56: Ienia

Open cited source

Jena is a precious stone that is found in the eyes of a beast that is called Hiena, & if this stone be put under a man's tongue, they say, that he shall tell many things that shall befall, as Isidore says.

6. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Hyena — Hyena-stone

Open cited source

The hyena-stone is a very precious gem, named after the animal called the hyena because it is taken from that animal's eyes. It enables its bearer to foretell the future when it is carried in the mouth beneath the tongue.
