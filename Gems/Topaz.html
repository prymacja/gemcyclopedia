Topaz bears the names Topasion, Topatius, Topazius, Chrysolite, Chrysolithus, Chrysolitus, and Filacterium; it is sometimes called Chrysoberyl. Crisoletus, Crisolimus, Crisolensis, Crisolansis, Grisoletus, and Crisolitus belong to the same Chrysolitus tradition. Topaz is known in golden, gold-green, pale-green, and reddish forms. Some traditions identify Chrysolite with Topaz, while their varieties and origins are described differently. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ The red Indian Topazion and green Arabian Topazion are retained as distinct forms within this name tradition. ⁹ ¹⁰

Generic

Description

The traditions gathered here give Topaz and Chrysolite overlapping names and gold-like colors. Chrysolite is described as pale bright green, sparkling like a golden star in sunlight; ¹ golden with the color of the sea; ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ or gold-colored, with a gold-green kind from Babylon. ² ⁶ ⁸ It develops from the sun’s heat and the air’s humidity after noon, at the ninth hour. ⁴ Another topaz is clear like water and more gold than yellow. ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ Topaz is also described as reddish and like Ruby, while the Arabian kinds range from orange-gold to a fainter pale light. One classification distinguishes a gold-green oriental kind from a greener, duller occidental kind. ⁶ Crisoletus is transparent and golden like sparkling fire; ⁷ Ethiopian stones appear fiery in the morning and golden by day, while Indian and Arabian stones are darker, less transparent, and citron-colored. Crisolitus has a translucent gold-colored kind with blazing sparks, and a blue-green kind like seawater that reveals a golden star in sunlight; ⁸ it too is found in Ethiopia.

Some accounts keep Topaz and Chrysolite distinct in separate entries, despite the shared names used in other traditions. ¹¹ ¹²

Properties

Powdered Chrysolite eases breathing and is given for asthma. ¹ Pierced and threaded with an ass’s bristle, then bound on the left wrist, it drives away terrors and melancholy. ¹ Set in gold, it dispels phantasms, expels stupidity, and gives wisdom. ¹

Chrysolite strengthens newborn sheep and other animals when present at birth, helping them move sooner. For fever, hold the stone over steaming wine, mix its sweat into the wine, drink it warm, and briefly place the stone in the mouth; ⁴ repeat often. For heart pain, dip it in olive oil and rub the oil over the painful place. Carried always, it strengthens knowledge; ⁴ placed over the heart, it preserves knowledge and skill. Airy spirits recoil from it, and it holds the powers of the day’s seven hours. ⁴

Set in gold, Chrysolite drives away the terrors of night. ⁵ Strung on hairs from an ass’s tail and worn as a bracelet on the left arm, it makes even the mightiest demons quail. ⁵

Set in gold and carried in the left hand, Crisoletus drives away demons, nightmares, and melancholy visions. ⁷ It weakens the enchantments of evil women and ends their charms. Bore a hole through the stone and draw a hair from an ass through it to strengthen its power against demons. Held in the hand, it dispels feverish heat. ⁷

Set in gold, the Ethiopian Crisolitus drives away terrors of the night, gives wisdom and honors, and dispels folly. ⁸ Ground and drunk, it is useful for asthmatics. ⁸

Topaz resists heat and poison. ⁴ When poison is in food or drink, the stone sweats if brought near it; ⁴ while eating or drinking, hold a finger bearing the stone beside the food and drink and watch it. To clarify obscured vision, soak Topaz in pure wine for three days and nights, then moisten the stone in the wine and rub it around the eyes at night, letting a little liquid touch the inside of the eyes. ⁴ The wine may be kept for five days; renew it with the stone before further use.

To cure fever, make three moderately sized trenches in soft bread with Topaz and fill them with pure wine, adding more as it disappears. ⁴ Look at the face reflected in the wine and say: “I look at myself, just as the cherubim and seraphim look at God in a mirror, so that these fevers might be cast from me.” Repeat often. ⁴ For leprous lesions, heat a tile, place oat chaff on it to make smoke, and hold Topaz over the smoke until it sweats. ⁴ Smear the sweat on the lesion, then rub it with olive oil mixed with one-third violet juice. ⁴ Repeat often.

For ailments of the spleen or internal corruption, place Topaz in straight wine for five days. Remove the stone and boil the wine; ⁴ hold the stone over its steam until it sweats into the wine, then place it briefly in the warm wine. Prepare a broth or beverage from the wine without lard and sip it often. ⁴ For protection, place Topaz over the heart each morning and say: “May God, who is magnified above all things and in all ways, not reject me from his honor, but may he preserve, strengthen, and establish me with his blessing.” Evil will abhor the bearer, and the stone deflects assaults. ⁴

Topaz stops boiling water from bubbling so that it soon becomes cool enough to touch; one account says that water cooled by the stone drives away lasciviousness. ⁵ ⁶ It is a remedy for hemorrhoids and attacks of lunacy. ¹ Its surface reflects an inverted image like a concave mirror. ¹ When thrown into a boiling cauldron, it stills the bubbling, and it relieves hemorrhoids.

Topaz heals delirium and injured minds, hemorrhoids, and lunatic suffering; ⁶ it increases wealth and drives away anger and sorrow. ⁶ Bound above a wound, it stops bleeding; it also halts sudden death. ⁶ Its bearer gains a prince’s favor. ⁶

Subtype X: Indian Red Topazion

The image shows Topaz (source).

This Topazion is reddish and resembles Ruby. ⁹ It was found by stonecutters in the city of Topaz in India, where it lay within another stone. Its beauty delighted the workmen, who sold it cheaply to alabaster merchants; they sold it at a high price to the Thebans, who presented it to their queen for her crown. ⁹

When a physician grinds the stone, it yields liquid without red color or matching drips, even when enough gathers in a large bowl; ⁹ the stone loses neither weight nor size. The liquid treats eye disease, dropsy, and seasickness, and physicians give it to children as medicine to drink. ⁹

Subtype XI: Arabian Green Topazion

This Topazion is one of the green kinds of gem and glitters with many colors. ¹⁰ Troglodyte bandits, worn out by hunger and storms, found it on an Arabian island while digging for plant roots. ¹⁰ Later, when people sought it, clouds covered the island; sailors eventually found it. Its name comes from the Troglodyte word for “seek.” It is called the most abundant gemstone and the only precious stone that takes the polishing file; ¹⁰ two kinds are recorded.

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, Chrysolitus and Topasion

Open Chrysolitus source
Open Filacterium source
Open Topasion source

Chrysolitus is a stone of a pale, bright green colour, and in direct sunlight it sparkles like a golden star. It is not rare. It is said to come from Ethiopia. It has been found by experience that it eases the breathing and therefore it is powdered and given to those who suffer from asthma. It is also said that if it is pierced, and an ass bristle put through the hole, and is bound on the left wrist, it drives away terrors and melancholy: this is said in books on physical ligatures. And if it is worn in a gold setting, it drives away phantasms, they say. It is also affirmed that it expels stupidity and confers wisdom.

Filacterium, the jewellers say, is the same gem as chrysolitus and has the same power.

Topasion is a stone named from the place of its first discovery, which is said to have been an island [called] Topasis. It presents a certain similarity to gold. There are two varieties of these stones. One of them is entirely similar to gold, and this is more precious. The other is yellow, but more transparent than the colour of gold, and this is less valuable. It has been found by experience in our own time that if it is put into boiling water it makes the water stop bubbling, so that soon the hand can be put in, to take it out; and a member of our Order actually did this at Paris. They also say that it is a remedy for haemorrhoids and for attacks of lunacy. And it is certain that this stone is a mirror and reflects the image of an object [inverted] as a concave mirror does. And the reason for this is merely that it has grown together on the inside and hardened so that the surface is concave.

The stone Chrysolite, which some have called Chrysoberyl, is gold-colored. It is found at the water well on the shore of Babylon at the Achesenidic rock. This water well is called Babylonian and the rock Achesenidic. For they say that Achaimeneos, the father of King Cyrus, called it thus. As some historians have transmitted. There is also a gold-green one against stomach pains and diarrhea, which ground and drunk has a healing effect.

Chrysopis appears to be simply gold (cf. χρυσός (chrysos), “gold”; ὄψις (opsis), “appearance”). Chrysolite (chrysolithus, i.e. topaz; cf. λίθος (lithos, “stone”)) is similar to gold, with a resemblance to the color of the sea. Ethiopia produces this gem.

4. St. Hildegard of Bingen — Physica, Stones, VIII–IX

Open Chrysolite source
Open Topaz source

Chrysolite (chrysolithus) develops from the heat of the sun and the humidity of the air, after noon, at the ninth hour of the day. It is almost as if it contains living strength. If it is near when the offspring of a sheep or other beast is born, its strength fortifies the animal so that it begins to move sooner than usual.

A person who has fever should heat up some wine and hold chrysolite over the vapor of that wine. Its sweat should be mixed with the wine, and he should drink the warmed wine. He should also place the chrysolite in his mouth for a little while. If he does this often, he will be well.

One who ails in his heart should dip chrysolite in olive oil and then smear it over the place that hurts, and he will be better.

This stone strengthens knowledge in a person who always carries it with him. One with good knowledge and skill should place this stone over his heart. As long as it lies there, his knowledge and skill shall not fail him. Chrysolite has the powers which are in the day’s seven hours. Airy spirits shrink back from this stone a bit.

Topaz (topazius) develops just before the ninth hour of the day, in the heat of the sun. The sun, from the day’s heat and the variations of air, is then its purest. Topaz is hot and has a bit of air and water in it. It is clear, and that clarity is similar to water. Its color is more like gold than yellow. It resists heat and poison, and does not tolerate them, just as the sea is unable to bear any depravities in it.

If poison is present in bread, meat, fish, or any food, or in water, wine, or other drink, and there is topaz nearby, it will immediately sweat, just as the sea foams when there is filth in it. Therefore, when a person eats and drinks, he should hold his finger, with a topaz on it, next to the food and drink. He should frequently look at it. If there is anything poisonous in the food or drink, it will immediately sweat.

One whose vision is obscured should put topaz in pure wine for three days and nights. Then, at night when he goes to bed, he should rub his eyes with that topaz, so moistened. The liquid should touch the inside of his eyes a little bit. When the stone has been removed from the wine, it is possible to keep that wine for five days. Then, as often as he wishes to streak his eyes at night, he should dip the same stone in that wine and rub it, so moistened, around his eyes. He should do this often, always renewing the wine with the topaz after five days. Just as the best eye salve, it will clarify the eyes.

If a person has fevers, he should make with topaz three moderately sized trenches in soft bread, and pour pure wine in them. If the wine disappears, he should pour in more wine. He should look at his face in the wine which he poured into the trenches, as if he’s looking in a mirror. He should say, “I look at myself, just as the cherubim and seraphim look at God in a mirror, so that these fevers might be cast from me.” He should do this often, and he will be cured.

One who is leprous should heat a tile well, and place oat chaff on it, so that it smokes. He should hold the topaz over the smoke, so that it sweats, then smear the sweat over the leprous place. When he has done this, he should take olive oil and mix with it a third part of the juice of violets. He should rub it on the place which had been moistened with the topaz sweat. If he does this often the lesions will be broken, and the person will be better, unless he dies.

One who ails in his spleen or who has rotten matter inside himself, as if he is rotting internally, should place topaz in straight morach (wine) for five days. Then he should remove the topaz and boil the wine so that it steams. He should hold the topaz over the steam, so that it sweats and its sweat mixes with the wine. Then he should place the stone in the warm wine for a little while. Then he should take it out and prepare, without lard, a broth or beverage from that wine. He should do this often and sip it. His spleen will be healed, and his interior rot will diminish.

Also, every day, in the morning, place topaz over your heart and say, “May God, who is magnified above all things and in all ways, not reject me from his honor, but may he preserve, strengthen, and establish me with his blessing.” For as long as you do this, evil will abhor you. The very strong topaz stone has in it this virtue from God: Because it grows while the sun is sinking, it deflects assaults from a person.

6. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Topaz

Open cited source

Topatius or Topasion [Topaz] is a most splendid and noble stone among the blue-green burning gems, of which there are two species. One of a yellow color, bordering on gold with some green to it. This is the oriental: it defies the file and is more valuable than the others. The other is the occidental: greener than the former, of a dimmer gold hue, it erodes with use; it tolerates the file and is of lesser value. Some believe that the Crysopteron is of this species. This stone was first found on an Arabian island called Chitis [Cytis]. Some say that Troglodyte raiders were once driven here by a storm at sea, and since they lacked provisions, they found this stone while digging up herbs and roots to eat. Thus the stone took its name from the manner in which it was found, for topasion in the Arabian language means search. It is true that Pliny is of the contrary opinion about the origin of the name, for he says that the stone was first found on an island in the Red Sea, about three hundred stadia from the shore, which was naturally at an incline and always misty with fog; hence even though it was sought by sailors it was shielded from their sight. And thus the stone was called topazin from the act of searching for it. It is said that Ptolemy Philadelphus had a three-cubit topaz. It is also said that if a topaz is thrown in boiling water, it immediately cools it and that this tepid water drives away any lasciviousness. It heals the delirious [freneticos] and injured of mind; it heals hemorrhoids and cures and prevents lunatic suffering [madness]. It increases wealth and drives away anger and sorrow. It halts sudden death and stops blood flowing from wounds if it is bound above them. The bearer of this stone thus obtains the favor of his prince.

Falcon; if this image is found on a topaz it is useful to gain the benevolence of princes, kings, and monarchs.

If the image of a man of elevated rank, or a crowned man, is found on topaz, it renders its wearer good, obliging, and endears him to God and men alike, bestowing on him honors and dignity.

Chrysopteron is a species of topaz similar to the crisoprassio.

7. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Crisoletus

Open cited source

Crisoletus, Crisolimus, or Crisolensis [Chrysolitus] has a golden and sparkling fire color and is transparent. But it is properly called crisoletus from the Greek crisis [Chrysós], which means gold, and oletus, which means whole; thus in Latin it translates to wholly gold. Those that are produced in Ethiopia are the best. Those found in India and Arabia are baser and less transparent: they have a dusky quality and tend to be citron colored. In the morning, the Ethiopian stones appear to be on fire, and during the day they appear golden. Bound in gold and carried on the left hand, the stone chases away demons, nightmares, and melancholic visions. Its principal virtue is to render ineffectual the enchantments of abominable women and to put an end to their charms. If one bores through it and then passes through the hair of an ass, its virtue is all the greater in driving demons away. If held in the hand it dispels feverish heats.

Crisolansis is the same as Crisoletus.

Grisoletus it is the same as Crisoletus.

8. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, Crisolitus

Open cited source

Crisolitus. One species of this stone is translucent and the color of gold because of some blazing sparks. Another type, which is praised by many, is cerulean and green colored, like seawater tending more to the green. Under the sun’s rays, it shows a golden star. It was found in Ethiopia. Set in gold, it chases away night terrors. It offers wisdom and honors and dispels folly. Ground and drunk is useful to asthmatics.

9. St. Epiphanius — On the Twelve Gemstones, Second Stone: Topazion

Open the cited text

Topazion: It is reddish in appearance and resembles the stone called Ruby. But it was found among the Indians in the city of Topaz, who at a certain time cut stones there and found it in the middle of the stone. And it appeared to the stonemasons as splendid. And they rejoiced and showed it to some alabaster merchants and sold it at a low price. However, the alabaster merchants sold it at a high price to the Thebans. The Thebans in turn offered it to the queen who ruled at that time. And she set it on her crown and bound it to the middle of her forehead.

But this stone has the following effect: When a physician grinds the stone, it gives off no reddish water and does not drip according to its color; even when water collects in a large bowl, as much as the grinder wants. Even when so much dripped material accumulates, the stone does not decrease in its weight, nor does the measure of the stone become smaller in its appearance.

Moreover, what flows from it is useful for diseases of the eye. It is also effective against dropsy when someone drinks it. And also for those who suffer from seasickness. For physicians give the liquid obtained from the stone to children as medicine to drink.

10. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, VI.9, “Topazion”

Open the cited text

Topazion is one of the green kinds of gem, and glitters with all colors. It was first found in an island of Arabia, where Troglodyte bandits, worn out by hunger and storms, unearthed it when they dug out the roots of plants. When it was sought afterward, this island was covered with clouds, but was finally found by sailors. It is from this that both place (i.e. the island Topazus in the Red Sea) and gem took their name; for in the Troglodyte language τοπάζειν (topazein) has the meaning “seek” (cf. Greek ζητεῖν (zētein), “seek”). This is the most abundant gemstone; it alone of the precious stones is subjected to the polishing file. There are two kinds.

11. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 96: Topazio

Open cited source

Topazius is a precious stone, & has another name Topazion indeclinable, as Isidore says, lib. 16. And is of shining kind, and shines with all colours, and was first found in an Island of Arabia in which Island when the Troglodites were diseased with hunger & tempest, they digged vp roots of herbs, and they found this stone therewith, and called it Testam nebulis. After that ship men sought and found the stone, and called it Topazim in the language of Troglodites. Therefore this stone that was so sought & sound is called Topazius, and has that name of the Island. Topazim in their language, is to say Siche, & is greatest of precious stones. Plinius wrote, that a stone of this kind was found so great, that Philadech made thereof an Image of four cubits long. In the Gloss super fixem Apost. it is said in this manner. The more scarce Topazius is, the more precious it is. And has two colours, as it were of gold and of cleere air, and shines most when it is smit with the Sun beam, & passes in clearness all other precious stones, and comsorteth men and beasts to behold and look thereon. And if thou wipe this stone, thou darkest it, and if thou leadest him to his own kind, he is the more cleere. And in treasury of kings, nothing is more cleere nor more precious then this precious stone. For clearness thereof takes to himself the clearness of other precious stones that be about him, and it is said, that he follows the course of the Moon: and helps against the passion Lunatik. And so it is said, the as the Moon is more full or less, so his effect is more or less, as it is said in Lapid. and staunches blood, and helps them that have Emoroides, & assuages fervent water, & suffers it not so boil, as it is said in Lapidario. Dioscorides says, that it assuages both wrath and sorrow, and helps against evil thoughts and frenzy, and against sudden death. And has the shape of a mirror, aud the Image that is therein, is seen in a hollow mirror.

12. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Topazius — Topaz

Open cited source

Topaz is one of the twelve stones. Its colour resembles gold, and this is the more precious kind. Another kind is much brighter and paler in colour, but is inferior. It helps those suffering from haemorrhoids. It is said to respond to the moon and to calm boiling waters. It is also reported to restrain anger and lust. It is found in Arabia.
