Astrion, also called Asterites, Astrites, and Asterius, is a white Crystal-like stone with a star-shaped light enclosed within it. ³ It reflects white rays in sunlight; ¹ when held facing the stars, it catches their gleam and casts it back. ² Its central star shines like the full moon. It is found in India, Thrace, and Carmania.

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies XVI.x.3

Open cited source

Asterites is white and contains a light enclosed within it like a star floating about inside. It reflects white rays of sunlight, whence it finds its name. — Etymologies XVI.x.3

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies XVI.xiii.7

Open cited source

Astrion, quite close to Crystal, is from India, and in its center a star shines with the gleam of a full moon. It takes its name because when held facing the stars it catches their gleam and casts it back. — Etymologies XVI.xiii.7

3. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Astrites, Astrion, Asterius, or Asterites is a white stone, similar to Crystal; it encloses a light like a moving star; in the part where it receives the light there may be seen a shape similar to a blazing star or a burning flame. It takes its name from astrum, as its image is like that of a star and of Ion. They originate from Thrace, or in Carmania, where touched by the sun’s rays, it seems, they turn white.

4. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 16: Astrione

Open cited source

Astrion is a precious stone of India, near like to Crystal. In the middle thereof shines a star with clearness of the full Moon: and has that name of Astris, stars: for if it be set in the stars light, it takes light of them, as Isidore says, cap. de Christal.

5. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 18: Asterites

Open cited source

Asterites is a precious stone, and is white, and conteyneth light therein closed, as it were a Star going within, and makes the Sun beams white.
