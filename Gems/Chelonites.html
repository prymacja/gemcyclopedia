Chelonites

Chelonites

A purple variegated agate specimen illustrates the stated appearance (source).

NamesChelonites; ¹ Chelonitis. ²
AppearancePurple, variegated. ¹ ²
Found inThe eye of an Indian tortoise. ¹ ²

Chelonites, also called Chelonitis, is a purple-variegated gem from the eye of an Indian tortoise. ¹ ² Held beneath the tongue, it reveals future events at appointed lunar times and remains cold in fire, like Chalazia. ¹

Generic

Description

Chelonites is purple and variegated, and comes from an Indian tortoise’s eye. ¹ ²

Properties

Held beneath the tongue, Chelonites allows one to foretell the future. One account says this power is present while the moon waxes, lasting until the sixth hour; at the new moon it endures from dawn until nightfall; and on the fifteenth day it lasts as long as at the moon’s prime. ¹ While the moon wanes, the gift lasts only until dawn. The stone also remains cold in the hottest fire, like Chalazia. ¹

Sources

1. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus

Open cited source

The Indian tortoise yields a gem full bright
With varying purple, Chelonites hight:
Placed 'neath the tongue, as learned Magians shew,
It gives the power the future to foreknow.
To the sixth hour endures the magic boon
Whilst fills her crescent horns th' increasing moon;
But at new moon the prescient power, they say,
Lasts from the opening to the close of day.
When at her fifteenth day she rides through heaven
The same extent as at her prime is given;
But while her narrowing crescent nightly wanes
Not past the break of day this gift obtains.
Like the Chalazias it the fire defies
And cold remains where hottest flames arise:

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, XV.23

Open cited source

Chelonitis is the eye of an Indian turtle. It is a varicolored purple. Magicians imagine that with this placed on the tongue, one may predict the future.
