Borax, also called Nosa, Crapondinus, or Crapodinae, is a stone taken from a toad, sometimes from its head. ¹ ² It occurs in several colors: slightly grayish-white, black or dark blue with an eye-like center, and occasionally green. Swallowed after poison, it braces the intestines and expels the poison; it also serves against snakebites, fevers, and stomach and kidney ailments. ²

Generic

Description

Borax is a toadstone found within a toad, sometimes in its head. ¹ ² Its kinds include a slightly grayish-white form, considered best and rarer; a black or dark form with a cerulean hue and an eye-like center; ² and occasional small green stones or stones marked with images of toads. The accounts differ about when the toad should be opened: one says while it is alive and quivering, another when it is dead but its heart is still beating. Stones are better when taken from the toad than when they have long lain underground. ²

Properties

Swallowed after poison has been drunk, Borax braces the intestines, extinguishes the poison’s force, and carries it out through the lower parts. ² It is also said to cleanse the bowels of filth and excrement. ¹ For snakebites, fever, and ailments of the stomach and kidneys, drink water in which Borax has been placed. ² Some say it has the same effect when carried on the person. ²

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Borax (toadstone), as some say, is a stone named from a toad, which carries it in its head; and there are two kinds. One is slightly greyish-white in colour, the other is black. If it is extracted while the toad is still alive and quivering, it has in the middle, as it were, a blue eye. If swallowed, it is said to cleanse the bowels of filth and excrements. And in our own time a small green one was extracted from a toad. We have even seen some with pictures of toads in them, which were said to be of this kind. In common speech these are called toadstones (crapodinae).

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Borax, Nosa, and Crapondinus are all synonyms for the same stone extracted from a toad. They are of two species: white, which is the best, and is more rarely found; the other is dark black with a cerulean color, and its center resembles an eye. It must be taken out of a dead toad whose heart is still beating. These stones are better than those extracted after being underground for a long time. They have a marvelous virtue against poison: if someone has drunk poison, he should swallow this stone, which immediately will brace the intestines and extinguish the poisonous quality lodged in the intestines, then it passes through the lower parts and can be saved. It is helpful against snakebites, eliminates fevers, and heals ailments of the stomach and kidneys, if one drinks water in which it was put. Some say it has the same effect if carried on one’s own person.

Crapondinus is the same as Borax.

3. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 71: Noset

Open cited source

Noset that is Orapondine, is a precious stone, some deale white, or of diverse colours. It is said that this stone is taken out of a Toads head, and is cleansed in the same head, & in strong Wine and water, as Dioscorides says: and sometimes the shape of a Toad seems therein with sharpe feet & broad. This stone helps against biting of Serpents and of creeping Worms, and against venom. For in presence of venom, the stone warms and burns his finger that touches him, as Dioscorides says.

4. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Borax, that is, Crepaund — Toadstone

Open cited source

Borax is a precious stone, so called because an animal named borax, a kind of toad, carries it in its head. There are two kinds: one white, which is better and rarer, and another dark and black, with an eye-like mark in its centre, approaching a sky-blue colour. A specimen of this latter kind is better if it has that mark. When the stone is taken from a living, still-throbbing toad, it is said to possess the eye; but when it is extracted from a toad that has been dead for some time, that mark fades and disappears, and the stone is spoiled.

This stone, when taken as food, is said to cure internal disorders in a human being. It passes through the intestines and, once the cure is accomplished, exits through the lower part of the body. It is said that the complete stone of the white variety possesses this power. The French call this gem crepandine, and it resists poisons.
