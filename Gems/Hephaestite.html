Hephaestite

Hephaestite

Lustrous reddish-brown hematite, used as a close color-and-reflectivity analogue for Hephaestite (source).

NamesHephaestite; ² Hephaestitis. ¹
AppearanceRuddy and bright; ² mirror-like, reflecting images in red; ¹ emits fiery rays in sunlight.
Found inThe Isthmus of Corinth; ² Corycus. ¹

Hephaestite, also called Hephaestitis, is a ruddy, mirror-like stone that reflects images in red. ¹ Cast into boiling water, it stills the bubbling and cools the water; ² set in sunlight, it emits fiery rays and kindles dry matter. It protects crops from storms and blight, and guards its wearer from danger when worn over the heart.

Generic

Description

Hephaestite is ruddy and bright. ² It reflects images like a mirror, though it makes them red. ¹ Held to the sun, it shoots out fiery rays. ² It springs from the Isthmus of Corinth; ² another account places it at Corycus. ¹

Properties

Cast Hephaestite into a boiling cauldron, and its bubbling fury ceases; the water cools and settles into a tranquil pool. ² When placed in sunlight, it ignites dry material and emits fiery rays. ¹

It shields fields from locusts, hail, whirlwinds, and rust. ² It quenches sedition and arms its bearer against every danger, provided that it is worn over the heart.

Sources

1. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies XVI, XV.15

Open cited source

Hephaestitis has the property of a mirror in reflecting images, although it makes them reddish. The test of this gem is if, when added to boiling water, it immediately chills it or if, when placed in the sun, it ignites dry material. It originates in Corycus.

2. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus, XXXI

Open cited source

In Corinth's Isthmus springs the Hephaestite,
More precious than its brass, and ruddy bright.
The seething caldron bubbling o'er the blaze,
Cast in the stone, its fervent fury stays;
Tam'd by the virtue of the gem, as cool
It falls as water in a tranquil pool.
Nor flights of locusts, nor the scourging hail,
Nor whirlwinds fierce shall thy fair fields assail;
Nor felling rust the growing crops shall blight
That stand defended by its saving might.
Held to the sun it shoots out fiery rays
Dazzling the eye as with the furnace blaze:
This burning stone sedition's fury quells
And 'gainst all danger its possessor arms.
But let this precept in thy mind be borne —
O'er the heart this mineral must be worn.
