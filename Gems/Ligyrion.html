Ligyrion

NamesLigyrion; ¹ possibly Lungurion or Lingurion; Lingoros or Lanouros are proposed if they refer to Ligyrion.
AppearanceA rock with yellow points and a brighter, nobler vein in its depth; ¹ the points are compared to those of the animal called Lingion. ¹
Found inNot known with certainty; ¹ tentatively associated with Upper Thebes and the temple of Asclepius near Memphis. ¹

Ligyrion is the name of the seventh stone on the priestly breastplate, though its identity is uncertain. It may be called Lungurion or Lingurion; ¹ Lingoros and Lanouros are proposed only if these names refer to Ligyrion. The described stone has yellow points and a brighter, nobler vein within it. ¹ Its place is first said to be unknown, then tentatively associated with Upper Thebes and the temple of Asclepius near Memphis. It may be the Hyacinth, but that identification is also uncertain.

Generic

Description

The seventh breastplate stone is called Ligyrion, though its identity is uncertain. It may be Lungurion, called Lingurion in common speech; Lingoros or Lanouros may also name it if it is indeed Ligyrion. ¹ The stone contains yellow points unlike serpentine markings, with a vein in its depth nobler and brighter than the surrounding rock. ¹ It is more beautiful than the rock from which it takes its name. ¹

Its place is unknown, though Upper Thebes is proposed as its origin; it may come from the temple of Asclepius near Memphis in Egypt if these names refer to the same stone. ¹ Scripture may call Hyacinth Ligyrion, but this identity remains uncertain.

1. St. Epiphanius — On the Twelve Gemstones

Open discussion of the possible Hyacinth identification

Therefore, it is necessary to consider whether perhaps Holy Scripture calls this stone Ligyrion.

Open cited source

About the finding of Ligyrion we could quite simply find out nothing. Neither from the sources, nor from earlier testimonies, and also not from those who have dealt with it. However, we found Lungurion, a so-called stone, which some in the commonly known languages call Lingurion. Therefore I thought that this is Ligyrion, because in Holy Scripture the names are often given in changed form. For thus Scripture sometimes calls the Emerald the Green Stone, and names others with other names.

Either Lingoros or also Lanouros, if this is supposed to be Ligurion. I say once again that its location has not become known to us, however I have come to the understanding that it comes from the Upper Thebans. Therefore one says, if these are those stones, they are from such stones from the temple of Asclepius, from the so-called Memphis of the Egyptians. And in this stone there are inclusions, not similar to serpentine, but it has points in similarity to the 'wax-Kerinon' called stone. And its points are yellowish; so that we must recognize that in the middle of the depth of this rock there is a vein that is much nobler and brighter than this stone.

It is especially beautiful, more than the rock from which it received its naming. And it is translated as Lingoros because there is a certain animal that is called Lingion, whose color is similar to the ox calf or the Red Heifer. And its tail is short and has a multitude of yellowish points. Therefore its name was called Lunguros, which means 'tail of Liungos.'
