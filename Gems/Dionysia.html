Dionysia, also called Dionysius, Dionisia, or Dyonysia, is a black stone speckled with red; ¹ ² some describe dark or iron-colored kinds with white spots. ⁴ Rubbed or ground with water, it smells of wine and overcomes drunkenness. ² ³

Generic

Description

Dionysia is black as ebony or iron, with sparkling red spots. ¹ ³ Another description gives a dark or iron-colored stone with spots white as snow. ⁴ Its breath smells like wine. ¹ ² ³

Properties

When rubbed with water or ground and mixed with water, Dionysia gives off a wine-like fragrance that wards off drunkenness. ² Its own scent dispels the fumes of wine and makes the smell of wine evaporate. ¹ ³ ⁴

Steeped in water, the stone smells of wine and overcomes the madness that rises from wine. ³ Its force is even said to turn water into wine.

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II

Open cited source

Dyonysia is a stone black as iron, with sparkling red spots in it. Its breath is like wine; and yet that very odour of wine dispels drunkenness—a matter of wonder to many people. For the cause of this is that wine induces feeble drunkenness not by its odour but by its oppressive fumes; and the simple odour of this stone is active in clearing out and dispelling the fumes of the wine.

2. St. Isidore of Seville — Etymologies, Book XVI, IV.7 and XI.8

Open Dionysius passage

Dionysius is a dark stone speckled with red spots. It is named thus because if it is ground and mixed with water it smells like wine, and, what is marvelous about it, it fights off drunkenness.

Open Dionysia passage

Dionysia is black with mingled red spots. When rubbed with water it smells of wine, and its fragrance is thought to ward off drunkenness.

3. Marbodus of Rennes — De lapidibus

Open cited source

The Dionysia, black as ebon found, With ruddy spots diversifies its ground. In water steeped, fragrant of wine it smells. And yet the fumes of wine its force dispels. A thing opposed to nature's wonted course. Water to wine converted by its force: And yet the madness rising out of wine Completely vanquished by this gem divine.

4. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum

Open cited source

Dionisia is a black stone, scattered with red spots. Some say it has a dark or iron color sprinkled with spots white as snow. It is found in the East. If dissolved in water, it emanates the smell of wine; and with its own smell it dispels drunkenness, and it eliminates and causes the smell of wine to evaporate.

5. Bartholomaeus Anglicanus — De proprietatibus rerum, Book XVI, Chapter 35: Dioniso

Open cited source

Dionisius is a black stone or browne sprong with red veins, and has that name, for if it be ground and mingled with water, it smells as wine: and yet it withstands dronkenesse. And that is great wonder in that stone, as Isidore says, li. 16.

6. Thomas Cantimpre — Liber de natura rerum, Book XIV: “On Precious Stones”, Dyonisia — Dionysias

Open cited source

Dionysias is an eastern stone, dark in colour and sprinkled with snow-white spots. When ground in water, it causes wine to flare up. Yet, contrary to nature, drunkenness is driven away by its smell, although the smell of wine naturally produces drunkenness even without drinking it. Dionysias gleams black, studded with snowy-white spots.
