Varach, called Dragon’s Blood or sanguis draconis, is an extremely red stone whose powder is rough and shining. ¹ ² Some physicians say it is plant juice, but the stone account is supported by its broken-stone appearance. It remedies every kind of flux, especially bleeding; with quicksilver, it makes algala. ¹

Generic

Description

Varach is a deeply red stone, though some physicians say Dragon’s Blood is juice from a plant. ¹ One account favors the stone account because the powder’s rough, shining surface resembles little pieces of broken stone. ¹ Another account says that Varach is scarce and that physicians use Dragon’s Blood in its place. ²

Properties

Varach remedies every kind of flux, especially the flow of blood. Mixed with quicksilver, it makes algala. ¹ Another account likewise values it for stopping every sort of flux. ²

Sources

1. St. Albertus Magnus — The Book of Minerals, Tractate II, “Varach”

Open the cited text

Varach, which is called “dragon’s blood” (sanguis draconis), is a stone, according to Aristotle. But some medical men say that it is the juice of a certain plant. But Aristotle’s statement is proved correct by the appearance of the powder, for its surface is shining and rough, like stone broken into little pieces. And it is extremely red. It is a remedy for any sort of flux, especially of blood. From this and quicksilver, algala is made.

2. Camillo Leonardi — Speculum Lapidum, “Varach”

Open the cited text

Varach is a stone not easily found. Among us it is esteemed for its virtue of halting any type of flux. In its place doctors use dragon’s blood.
