Aetites

Aetites, also called Echites, Eaglestone, Ethices, Endes, Aquileus, Erodialis, Praegnus, and Echistes, is a red or scarlet stone found in eagles’ nests. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ Some kinds contain a smaller stone that rattles within. ¹ It protects pregnancy, detects poison, and grants victory.

Recorded localities: Eagles’ nests, shores of the Ocean, Persia, and the Persian Ocean. ¹ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Agate

Agate is a many-coloured stone: some are black with white bands or circles, others bear yellow veins, blood-like spots, coral-red colours, or figures of woods and beasts. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ Its virtues include healing venomous bites, preserving sight, strengthening the wearer, and averting tempests; ⁴ ⁶ these virtues belong to particular forms and preparations, as set out below.

Recorded localities: Sicily, Crete, India, Scythia, Egypt, Persia, Arabia, and Cyprus; ³ some forms are tied to particular places. ² ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Alabaster

Alabaster, also called Nicomar, is a shining white stone of marble kind. ¹ Its coldness preserves aromatic ointments and keeps corpses from becoming foul-smelling. It gives victory and preserves friendship. ¹

Recorded localities: Not specified. ¹ ²

Alabastrites

Alabastrites is white and variegated; it preserves ointments in vessels.

Recorded localities: Thebes; ¹ ² Egypt; ¹ Damascus; ¹ ² Syria; ¹ India; ¹ ² Cappadocia. ²

Amethyst

Amethyst is a purple or violet stone with shades from deep wine-colour to pale rose. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ It checks drunkenness and evil thoughts, strengthens understanding, protects against poison and serpents, and bears virtues in medicine, hunting, warfare, and engraved images. ⁶

Recorded localities: India, Libya and its coast, Arabia, Cyprus, Egypt, Armenia, Galatia, and Tarsus. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Astrion

Astrion, also called Asterites, Astrites, and Asterius, is a white Crystal-like stone with a star-shaped light enclosed within it. ³ It reflects white rays in sunlight; ¹ when held facing the stars, it catches their gleam and casts it back. ² Its central star shines like the full moon. It is found in India, Thrace, and Carmania.

Recorded localities: India; ² Thrace; ³ Carmania. ³ ⁴ ⁵

Beryl

Beryl, also called Beryllus or Berillus, has many kinds, from pale and transparent to opaque, sea-green, olive, or dark. ¹ ⁶ Some contain a moving, water-like light and shine when cut with six faces. ⁵ Beryl is found in India, Tauros, beyond the Euphrates, and Babylon. ² ³ ⁶ Its virtues include victory, concord, healing, and the expulsion of poison; ⁴ its use in sunlight and its many named varieties are set out below. ¹

Recorded localities: India; ¹ ³ Mount Tauros and lands beyond the Euphrates; ² Babylon. ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Callaica

Callaica, also called Callayca or Caldaicus, is a pale green, cloudy stone that appears more pleasing when set in gold. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: Icy cliffs in India and Germany; ¹ also on the bank of a cold, icy river in Media and Germany. ²

Chelonites

Chelonites, also called Chelonitis, is a purple-variegated gem from the eye of an Indian tortoise. ¹ ² Held beneath the tongue, it reveals future events at appointed lunar times and remains cold in fire, like Chalazia. ¹

Recorded localities: The eye of an Indian tortoise. ¹ ²

Chrysoberyllus

Chrysoberyllus is pale green and glitters with a golden gleam. ¹ Its name recalls Greek χρύσεος (chryseos), “golden.” It is found in India.

Recorded localities: India. ¹

Chrysocolla

Chrysocolla, also called Crisocollus or Antitaneus, resembles gold and has the nature of a Magnet. ¹ ² It is associated with regions where ants are said to extract gold; ² one account says it increases or coagulates gold, another that it increases wealth. ²

Recorded localities: India, where ants are said to dig out gold; ¹ also Media, where ants extract gold. ²

Chrysopassus

Chrysopassus is a rare and valuable stone from India, colored like hardened leek-green juice and speckled with gold. ¹ Its name recalls Greek χρυσός (chrysos), “gold.” It resembles Topaz.

Recorded localities: India. ¹

Chrysoprase

Chrysoprase is green with golden spots, its color compared to leek or cypress juice. ¹ ² ⁴ One kind comes from India; ³ another from Ethiopia is hidden by daylight and fiery at night. ⁵

Recorded localities: India, Ethiopia, and Africa. ¹ ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Diamond

The diamond is an exceedingly hard, bright stone, commonly small and crystal-clear or iron-coloured. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁹ It governs iron and the magnet, resists poison and sorcery, quiets fear and madness, and protects its bearer in contests and danger. ⁷ ⁸ The methods by which it may be cut or broken differ by kind.

Recorded localities: India, Arabia, Cyprus, Syria, Macedonia, Ethiopia, and Philippi. ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁹ ¹⁰ ¹¹

Emerald

The emerald is intensely green and translucent; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ some are smooth and clear enough to reflect an image. ⁵ Its virtues strengthen sight and memory, assist chastity and eloquence, increase wealth, and defend against illness, poison, demons, and tempests. ¹ ⁶

Recorded localities: Scythia, Britain, the Nile, India, Ethiopia, Phison, the Red Sea, Emerald Island, Beronike, Pelmi, Bactria, Egypt, Carchedonia, Persia, and Babylon. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Heliotrope

Heliotrope, also called Eliotropia, Elitropia, Elitropus, or Bloodstone, is a green stone like Emerald, clouded and speckled with blood-red spots and veins. ¹ ² ⁴ Anointed with the juice of its namesake herb and set in water beneath the sun, it turns the sun blood-red, boils the water into mist, and brings rain. ¹ ³ It grants prophecy and long life, checks bleeding and poison, and, with its herb and secret rite, makes its bearer invisible. ¹ ³

Recorded localities: Ethiopia, Cyprus, Africa, India, and Libya; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ necromancers call it the Babylonian gem. ¹ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Lichinus

Lichinus, also called Lychnis or Lychinites, is a lamp-bright fiery gem found in many places; ² the most admired kind comes from India. ¹ Red and purple forms are described. ² The purple kind attracts straw when heated by the sun or rubbed, and holds wax when a carved image is pressed into a seal. ²

Recorded localities: Many places; ¹ ² the most admired kind is found in India. ¹

Magnet

The magnet is iron-colored, or sometimes blue, and draws iron to itself, passing its power to the iron so that rings or needles may hang one from another. ² ⁷ It may guide a floating needle and compass toward the heavens, but garlic and Diamond oppose its attraction. ⁵ Certain kinds repel iron, attract human flesh, or are themselves drawn to iron. ¹ ³ ⁵

Recorded localities: India and the Indian Ocean, the country of the Troglodytes, Franconia, Ethiopia, Macedonia, Antioch, Alexandria, and Asia. ¹ ² ⁵ ⁸ ⁹ ¹⁰

Obsidian

Obsidian is a black or green translucent stone like glass. ¹ ² Polished, it reflects shadowy images and is used in mirrors and gems. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: India, Italy (including Samnium), Spain, Libya, and Germany. ¹ ²

Onyx

Onyx bears the appearance of a human nail: it may show a pale layer or veins against black, reddish, or other colored stone. ² ⁴ The traditions differ over its colors and kinds, naming five varieties in several accounts and a golden-yellow Onyx in another. ⁴ It is found in India, Arabia, and Media. ¹ ² ³ Worn, it brings sorrow, fear, troubling dreams, quarrels, and excessive saliva in children; Sard is said to restrain this harm. ¹

Recorded localities: Media and Arabia; ¹ ⁶ India and Arabia. ² ⁷ ⁸

Opal

Opal shines with many colors at once: a pale fire like Carbuncle, a purple sparkle like Amethyst, and a green gleam like Emerald. ¹ It is found in India, and one account says its size is no greater than a hazelnut. ² Another tradition names Paederos as a white gem second only to Pearl, while allowing that the name had become a general praise of beauty. ¹

Recorded localities: India; ¹ ² one account says it is found there alone. ³

Pantherus

Pantherus, also called Pantheros, gathers many colors in one stone: black, red, green, white, purple, and rose. ¹ ² It is found mostly in Media and is named for the spotted panther’s hide. ² Its colors bring many powers: the stone is said to impair sight, while looking upon it at sunrise or daybreak brings success, victory, and protection from enemies for that day.

Recorded localities: Mostly Media. ¹ ³ ⁴

Pearl

Pearl, also called Margarita, Unio, and Union, is a lustrous stone formed within shellfish and prized among the white gems. ¹ ² ⁴ Sea pearls are said to take their brightness and color from celestial dew, the sky, the season, and age; ² ³ ⁴ white and silvery kinds are most prized, while other pearls are yellow, reddish, or dark. ¹ ² A separate account describes pure pearls congealed from salty river waters. ⁵

Recorded localities: India and the Indian Ocean, Ceylon, Stoidis, Perimula, Arabia, the Persian Gulf and Red Sea, Britain and the British Sea, Flanders, Teutonia, the Moselle, and rivers in France. ¹ ³ A separate account places pearl formation in certain salty rivers. ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ¹⁴ ¹⁵

Ruby

Ruby, also called Carbuncle, Anthrax, Rubinus, and Sparks, is a hard, clear red stone whose fiery rays shine like a coal in the darkness. ¹ ³ ⁵ ⁶ A noble ruby glows by night; ² ³ ⁴ its radiance can reveal it even through clothing. Libya and the land of the Troglodytes yield carbuncles, while other accounts place ruby in Karchedon and the river Phison. ²

Recorded localities: Libya, among the Troglodytes; ¹ ³ ⁶ Karchedon in Africa; ² India; and the river Phison. ² ⁷ ⁸

Sandasirus

Sandasirus is found in India, at a place of the same name. Golden drops sparkle within it as in a transparent flame; ¹ the greater its stars, the greater its value. ¹

Recorded localities: India, at a place also called Sandasirus. ¹

Sapphire

The sapphire shines in blue, azure, purple, and yellow forms. It cools heat and lust, heals the eyes and body, preserves health, and brings peace, devotion, and understanding. Prepared or engraved in particular ways, it also counters poison, bleeding, spirits, illness, and misfortune. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ The name Sirites also appears in accounts of Silenite, whose lunar properties are treated separately.

Recorded localities: India, Ethiopia, Media, Libya, and near Le Puy in Provence. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁹ ¹⁰

Sard

Sard is a red, sometimes translucent stone associated with Sardis. ¹ ² ³ Its kinds differ in transparency and brightness; it is said to gladden the spirit, sharpen the mind, restrain bleeding, and counter the harmful influence of Onyx. The name is disputed: One account calls carnelian “sardius,” while one account explicitly distinguishes sardius from carnelian; see Carnelian. ⁴ ⁵

Recorded localities: Named for Sardis. ¹ ³ One account lists five regional kinds: Babylonian, Indian, Arabian, Egyptian, and Cyprian; ⁴ it may occur embedded in other mined stones like a heart. ⁶ ⁷

Sardonyx

Sardonyx is a layered stone formed from Sard and Onyx, with black, white, and red bands; ¹ ⁴ ⁵ some accounts add a chalcedonian layer. When the colors are clear and distinct, the stone is most prized. ⁶ It is found in India and Arabia and is especially valued for seals, from which wax releases cleanly. ² ⁴ The sources differ over its virtues: some say it restrains lust and strengthens the senses, while one account assigns it no magical power. ³

Recorded localities: India and Arabia; ¹ ⁴ ⁵ One account says it is revealed there by rushing water. ⁷ ⁸

Silenite

Silenite, also called Selenite or Silonites, is a lunar stone found in Persian and Indian traditions. It appears as a translucent, honey-bright stone bearing the image or white mark of the moon, and also as a grass-green, red, white, purple, or variegated stone. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ Its light and power follow the moon; held beneath the tongue, it gives a firm inward sign of whether an intended act should be undertaken. ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵

Recorded localities: Persia; some forms are associated with India, including a kind found in a shellfish and one carried by a tortoise. ¹ ² ⁴

Sindicus

Sindicus, the Indian Stone, cures dropsy. Tie it to the dropsical person for three hours; ¹ the illness passes into the stone, which then outweighs the person. Expose the stone to the sun for three hours and it pours out the foul water, becoming clean again. ¹

Recorded localities: Not specified. ¹

Topaz

Topaz bears the names Topasion, Topatius, Topazius, Chrysolite, Chrysolithus, Chrysolitus, and Filacterium; it is sometimes called Chrysoberyl. Crisoletus, Crisolimus, Crisolensis, Crisolansis, Grisoletus, and Crisolitus belong to the same Chrysolitus tradition. Topaz is known in golden, gold-green, pale-green, and reddish forms. Some traditions identify Chrysolite with Topaz, while their varieties and origins are described differently. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ The red Indian Topazion and green Arabian Topazion are retained as distinct forms within this name tradition. ⁹ ¹⁰

Recorded localities: Ethiopia; ¹ ³ ⁷ ⁸ Babylon at the Achesenidic rock; ² Arabia and islands in the Red Sea; ⁵ ⁶ ¹⁰ Topazion accounts name an Indian city and an Arabian island. ⁹ ¹¹ ¹²
