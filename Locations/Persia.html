Gems from the general area

Aetites

Aetites, also called Echites, Eaglestone, Ethices, Endes, Aquileus, Erodialis, Praegnus, and Echistes, is a red or scarlet stone found in eagles’ nests. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ Some kinds contain a smaller stone that rattles within. ¹ It protects pregnancy, detects poison, and grants victory.

Recorded localities: Eagles’ nests, shores of the Ocean, Persia, and the Persian Ocean. ¹ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Agate

Agate is a many-coloured stone: some are black with white bands or circles, others bear yellow veins, blood-like spots, coral-red colours, or figures of woods and beasts. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ Its virtues include healing venomous bites, preserving sight, strengthening the wearer, and averting tempests; ⁴ ⁶ these virtues belong to particular forms and preparations, as set out below.

Recorded localities: Sicily, Crete, India, Scythia, Egypt, Persia, Arabia, and Cyprus; ³ some forms are tied to particular places. ² ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Ammochrysus

Ammochrysus is Persian sand mixed with gold, containing tiny cubes made partly of gold leaf and partly of gold dust. ¹

Recorded localities: Persia. ¹

Coaspis

Coaspis is green and shines like gold. It takes its name from the Persian river where it is found. ¹

Emerald

The emerald is intensely green and translucent; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ some are smooth and clear enough to reflect an image. ⁵ Its virtues strengthen sight and memory, assist chastity and eloquence, increase wealth, and defend against illness, poison, demons, and tempests. ¹ ⁶

Recorded localities: Scythia, Britain, the Nile, India, Ethiopia, Phison, the Red Sea, Emerald Island, Beronike, Pelmi, Bactria, Egypt, Carchedonia, Persia, and Babylon. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Malachite

Malachite, also called Melochites, Melonites, and Molochites, is a soft, opaque stone of deep green, like the Emerald, and is found in Arabia. ¹ ³ ⁴ It protects its bearer and guards infants from mischance, witchcraft, accidents, and hurtful phantasms. ³ ⁴ It is also valued for making seals. ²

Recorded localities: Arabia, Cyprus, and Persia; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ Arabia is named by several accounts. ⁵ ⁶

Mithridax

Mithridax, also called Mithrax or Mitridax, sparkles in many colors when struck by sunlight. ¹ ² It is found in Persia and is also associated with the Euphrates.

Recorded localities: Persia and by the Euphrates. ¹ ²

Phengites

Phengites is a hard, white, translucent stone associated with Cappadocia and the power to admit light through closed buildings. ¹ A source tradition identifies Specularis, also called Mirrorstone, with Phengites or Fongites; ² their other accounts describe glass-like sheets, three colored forms, and a red kind that eases bodily aches and anger when carried in the left hand. ³

Recorded localities: Cappadocia; ³ around Segovia or Segorbe in Spain; ¹ Teutonia; ² France; ² and Persia. ³

Phlogites

Phlogites comes from Persia. ¹ It bears the appearance of flames burning within it, and those flames never go out.

Recorded localities: Persia. ¹

Pyrites

Pyrites, also called Perithe, Peridonius, Pyrite, Pirites, Piryte, Virites, Trapendanus, Pyr, or Ypestionus, is a firestone that sparks when struck. ¹ ⁴ Its true yellow kind is bronze-like, and a firm grasp burns the hand. ¹ ³ ⁴ Common pyrite kindles fire from sparks; ¹ ² yellow pyrite also helps the eyes and other illnesses. ⁴

Recorded localities: The yellow Persian kind comes from Persia; ² pyrites are also found in many places. ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Silenite

Silenite, also called Selenite or Silonites, is a lunar stone found in Persian and Indian traditions. It appears as a translucent, honey-bright stone bearing the image or white mark of the moon, and also as a grass-green, red, white, purple, or variegated stone. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ Its light and power follow the moon; held beneath the tongue, it gives a firm inward sign of whether an intended act should be undertaken. ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵

Recorded localities: Persia; some forms are associated with India, including a kind found in a shellfish and one carried by a tortoise. ¹ ² ⁴

Astrion

Astrion, also called Asterites, Astrites, and Asterius, is a white Crystal-like stone with a star-shaped light enclosed within it. ³ It reflects white rays in sunlight; ¹ when held facing the stars, it catches their gleam and casts it back. ² Its central star shines like the full moon. It is found in India, Thrace, and Carmania.

Recorded localities: India; ² Thrace; ³ Carmania. ³ ⁴ ⁵

Murra

Murra, also called Murrina and Murina, is formed from moisture or liquid compressed by heat. ¹ It appears in purple and white kinds, and in a mixture of purple, white, and fiery red colors that reflect like a rainbow. ¹ ² It is used to make beautiful vases.

Recorded localities: Parthia, especially Carmania. ¹ Murrhine vases were brought to Italy. ²

Jasper

Jasper, also called Jaspis or Iaspis, has many colors; ² ³ ⁴ ⁶ its most prized kind is translucent green with red veins and should be set in silver. ¹ ⁵ ⁶ It checks bleeding and fever, aids childbirth, and drives away harmful phantasms. Engraved figures carry further protections and powers.

Recorded localities: The Thermodon River (also called Thermodontos) and near Amatunt; ² Phrygia, Iberia, Hyrcania, Caspian lands, and Germany. It is also found in many places. ¹ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Callaica

Callaica, also called Callayca or Caldaicus, is a pale green, cloudy stone that appears more pleasing when set in gold. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: Icy cliffs in India and Germany; ¹ also on the bank of a cold, icy river in Media and Germany. ²

Chrysocolla

Chrysocolla, also called Crisocollus or Antitaneus, resembles gold and has the nature of a Magnet. ¹ ² It is associated with regions where ants are said to extract gold; ² one account says it increases or coagulates gold, another that it increases wealth. ²

Recorded localities: India, where ants are said to dig out gold; ¹ also Media, where ants extract gold. ²

Evanthus

Evanthus, also called Panthera, is a many-colored stone found in Media. ¹ Its markings may be black, red, pale, green, rosy, or purple. ¹ Looked upon as the sun rises, it brings victory in every undertaking that day; its further virtues vary with the stones whose colors are mingled within it.

Recorded localities: Media. ¹

Median Stone

The Median Stone, also called Medius and Medus, is found in Media and has black and green kinds; ¹ the green kind is called Medinus. ³ It strengthens the weak and is said to treat chronic gout, dimness of the eyes, and kidney troubles. ² The black kind restores sight when prepared with a woman’s milk, and heals gout when ground with ewe’s milk and applied to the body; ¹ taken by mouth, it is deadly. ¹ ³ Other preparations heal or cause blindness and death. The green kind aids sight when prepared with gall ink, a Magnet, and rainwater. ³

Recorded localities: Media, the country of the Medes. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵

Onyx

Onyx bears the appearance of a human nail: it may show a pale layer or veins against black, reddish, or other colored stone. ² ⁴ The traditions differ over its colors and kinds, naming five varieties in several accounts and a golden-yellow Onyx in another. ⁴ It is found in India, Arabia, and Media. ¹ ² ³ Worn, it brings sorrow, fear, troubling dreams, quarrels, and excessive saliva in children; Sard is said to restrain this harm. ¹

Recorded localities: Media and Arabia; ¹ ⁶ India and Arabia. ² ⁷ ⁸

Pantherus

Pantherus, also called Pantheros, gathers many colors in one stone: black, red, green, white, purple, and rose. ¹ ² It is found mostly in Media and is named for the spotted panther’s hide. ² Its colors bring many powers: the stone is said to impair sight, while looking upon it at sunrise or daybreak brings success, victory, and protection from enemies for that day.

Recorded localities: Mostly Media. ¹ ³ ⁴

Sapphire

The sapphire shines in blue, azure, purple, and yellow forms. It cools heat and lust, heals the eyes and body, preserves health, and brings peace, devotion, and understanding. Prepared or engraved in particular ways, it also counters poison, bleeding, spirits, illness, and misfortune. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ The name Sirites also appears in accounts of Silenite, whose lunar properties are treated separately.

Recorded localities: India, Ethiopia, Media, Libya, and near Le Puy in Provence. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁹ ¹⁰
