Amethyst

Amethyst is a purple or violet stone with shades from deep wine-colour to pale rose. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ It checks drunkenness and evil thoughts, strengthens understanding, protects against poison and serpents, and bears virtues in medicine, hunting, warfare, and engraved images. ⁶

Recorded localities: India, Libya and its coast, Arabia, Cyprus, Egypt, Armenia, Galatia, and Tarsus. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Amites

Amites is an alum- or nitre-colored stone, harder than alum. ¹ It comes from Egypt and Arabia; ¹ its Ethiopian variety is green and gives a milky appearance when dissolved in water. ¹

Recorded localities: Egypt and Arabia; ¹ the green variety comes from Ethiopia.

Basanites

Basanites, also called Basaltem, is an iron-colored stone found in Egypt and Ethiopia. ¹ When ground in water, it produces a saffron-like color. ¹

Recorded localities: Egypt and Ethiopia. ¹

Chalcedony

Chalcedony, also called Chalcedonius, Calcedonius, or Calcedon, is a lustrous stone whose colors range from dark or Sapphire-like to dusky white and dull vermilion. ⁴ Pierced and worn, it aids success in causes and preserves the body’s strength; ¹ ² ³ each kind curbs lust and guards against tempests and adversity. ⁴

Recorded localities: Especially fine stones come from Ethiopia; varieties are found in Germany and on the Adriatic shore. ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Chrysolampis

Chrysolampis is golden by day and fiery by night. ¹ Its name joins gold with Greek λαμπάς (lampas), “torch.” It is found in Ethiopia.

Recorded localities: Ethiopia. ¹

Chrysolite

Chrysolite shines like gold and sends out sparks like fire, with a darker sea-green tint. ¹ It drives away serpents; when worn in gold on the left arm, it wards off night terrors, and donkey hairs threaded through a pierced stone drive away demons. ²

Recorded localities: Ethiopia. ¹ ²

Chrysopagion

Chrysopagion comes from Ethiopia. ¹ It shines in darkness, then fades with the coming of light, retaining a faint, dull color tinged with hidden gold. As day and night alternate, its indeterminate color changes like rotten oak or a firefly.

Recorded localities: Ethiopia. ¹

Chrysoprase

Chrysoprase is green with golden spots, its color compared to leek or cypress juice. ¹ ² ⁴ One kind comes from India; ³ another from Ethiopia is hidden by daylight and fiery at night. ⁵

Recorded localities: India, Ethiopia, and Africa. ¹ ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Crystal

Crystal, also called rock crystal, quartz, crystallus, or cristallum, is clear and shining like ice or water. ² ⁵ It is formed by cold in some accounts and within the earth in others. ¹ ⁵ Set against sunlight, it kindles tinder; ⁴ it also eases thirst and serves in remedies for the eyes, throat, heart, stomach, and belly. ³

Recorded localities: Germany; ¹ Asia; ² ⁵ Cyprus; ² ⁵ the northern Alps; ² ⁵ Scythia; ⁵ the Ethiopian Alps; ⁵ and the Red Sea island of Meron off Arabia. ⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Diamond

The diamond is an exceedingly hard, bright stone, commonly small and crystal-clear or iron-coloured. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁹ It governs iron and the magnet, resists poison and sorcery, quiets fear and madness, and protects its bearer in contests and danger. ⁷ ⁸ The methods by which it may be cut or broken differ by kind.

Recorded localities: India, Arabia, Cyprus, Syria, Macedonia, Ethiopia, and Philippi. ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁹ ¹⁰ ¹¹

Emerald

The emerald is intensely green and translucent; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ some are smooth and clear enough to reflect an image. ⁵ Its virtues strengthen sight and memory, assist chastity and eloquence, increase wealth, and defend against illness, poison, demons, and tempests. ¹ ⁶

Recorded localities: Scythia, Britain, the Nile, India, Ethiopia, Phison, the Red Sea, Emerald Island, Beronike, Pelmi, Bactria, Egypt, Carchedonia, Persia, and Babylon. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Garnet

Garnet, or Granatus, is a transparent red stone of the Carbuncle family, like the flower of the wild pomegranate. ¹ ² Set in a seal ring with black beneath it, it shines more brightly. It gladdens the heart and dispels sorrow; ¹ some also say it protects its bearer from plague. ²

Recorded localities: Mostly Ethiopia; ¹ ² sometimes in the sands of the sea near Tyre. ¹ ² ³

Haematite

Haematite, also called Hematite, Ematites, Emathitis, Emathites, Sanguineus Lapis, or Sedeneg, is a blood-veined, iron-colored stone that turns blood-red when ground. ³ ⁴ It preserves rather than corrodes the body and stops bleeding; ¹ its many remedies treat the eyes, wounds, discharges, snakebite, and bladder stones. ²

Recorded localities: Ethiopia, Arabia, Afriia, Egypt, Babylonia, Spain, Libya, Phrygia, and Germany. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Hamonis

Hamonis is a golden stone shaped like a ram’s horn, counted among the most sacred gems. During contemplation, it shows the mind divine things. ¹ It comes from Ethiopia.

Recorded localities: Ethiopia. ¹

Heliotrope

Heliotrope, also called Eliotropia, Elitropia, Elitropus, or Bloodstone, is a green stone like Emerald, clouded and speckled with blood-red spots and veins. ¹ ² ⁴ Anointed with the juice of its namesake herb and set in water beneath the sun, it turns the sun blood-red, boils the water into mist, and brings rain. ¹ ³ It grants prophecy and long life, checks bleeding and poison, and, with its herb and secret rite, makes its bearer invisible. ¹ ³

Recorded localities: Ethiopia, Cyprus, Africa, India, and Libya; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ necromancers call it the Babylonian gem. ¹ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Hyacinth

Hyacinth, also called Iacinthus or the hyacinth-stone, shines in many colors, from blue and sea-green to pomegranate red and citron yellow. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ Its virtues strengthen the body, dispel sorrow, protect travelers, and, in its garnet-colored kind, resist fire; ⁶ it also quenches flame, eases childbirth, guards against poison, and treats troubled eyes and the heart.

Recorded localities: Scythia, Ethiopia, and Africa. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁷ ⁸

Magnet

The magnet is iron-colored, or sometimes blue, and draws iron to itself, passing its power to the iron so that rings or needles may hang one from another. ² ⁷ It may guide a floating needle and compass toward the heavens, but garlic and Diamond oppose its attraction. ⁵ Certain kinds repel iron, attract human flesh, or are themselves drawn to iron. ¹ ³ ⁵

Recorded localities: India and the Indian Ocean, the country of the Troglodytes, Franconia, Ethiopia, Macedonia, Antioch, Alexandria, and Asia. ¹ ² ⁵ ⁸ ⁹ ¹⁰

Marble

Marble (Marmor) is a noble stone in many colors, prized for its speckles and varied markings. Its kinds come from quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, and they take names from their colors, places, or patrons. ¹ ² ³ Among them, Parian Marble is exceptionally white, comes from the island of Paros, and preserves spices and ointments. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: Marble kinds are found in quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, including Lacedemonia, Egypt, Ethiopia, Asia, Paros, Syene, Thebes, the Cyclades, Thasos, Numidia, Melos, Chios, Arabia, Germany, and Carrara. ¹ ² ³

Prassius

Prassius is a green stone, often the matrix of the Emerald, and is found in Ethiopia near the Nile. ¹ ² It has dark or translucent green forms, sometimes marked with blood-red drops or white lines. It benefits the sight and shares virtues with Jasper and emerald. ¹ An image of a bull carved upon it is useful for hexes and gives mercy in judgments.

Recorded localities: Ethiopia near the Nile. ² ³

Sapphire

The sapphire shines in blue, azure, purple, and yellow forms. It cools heat and lust, heals the eyes and body, preserves health, and brings peace, devotion, and understanding. Prepared or engraved in particular ways, it also counters poison, bleeding, spirits, illness, and misfortune. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ The name Sirites also appears in accounts of Silenite, whose lunar properties are treated separately.

Recorded localities: India, Ethiopia, Media, Libya, and near Le Puy in Provence. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁹ ¹⁰

Topaz

Topaz bears the names Topasion, Topatius, Topazius, Chrysolite, Chrysolithus, Chrysolitus, and Filacterium; it is sometimes called Chrysoberyl. Crisoletus, Crisolimus, Crisolensis, Crisolansis, Grisoletus, and Crisolitus belong to the same Chrysolitus tradition. Topaz is known in golden, gold-green, pale-green, and reddish forms. Some traditions identify Chrysolite with Topaz, while their varieties and origins are described differently. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ The red Indian Topazion and green Arabian Topazion are retained as distinct forms within this name tradition. ⁹ ¹⁰

Recorded localities: Ethiopia; ¹ ³ ⁷ ⁸ Babylon at the Achesenidic rock; ² Arabia and islands in the Red Sea; ⁵ ⁶ ¹⁰ Topazion accounts name an Indian city and an Arabian island. ⁹ ¹¹ ¹²
