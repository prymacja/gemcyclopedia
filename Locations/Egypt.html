Gems from the general area

Aegyptilla

Aegyptilla, also called Egyptilla, is black at the base and blue upon its surface, with some kinds streaked by golden veins. Ground in water, it gives the water the color and taste of wine. ² It is named for Egypt. ¹

Recorded localities: Egypt. ¹

Agate

Agate is a many-coloured stone: some are black with white bands or circles, others bear yellow veins, blood-like spots, coral-red colours, or figures of woods and beasts. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ Its virtues include healing venomous bites, preserving sight, strengthening the wearer, and averting tempests; ⁴ ⁶ these virtues belong to particular forms and preparations, as set out below.

Recorded localities: Sicily, Crete, India, Scythia, Egypt, Persia, Arabia, and Cyprus; ³ some forms are tied to particular places. ² ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Alabaster

Alabaster, also called Nicomar, is a shining white stone of marble kind. ¹ Its coldness preserves aromatic ointments and keeps corpses from becoming foul-smelling. It gives victory and preserves friendship. ¹

Recorded localities: Not specified. ¹ ²

Alabastrites

Alabastrites is white and variegated; it preserves ointments in vessels.

Recorded localities: Thebes; ¹ ² Egypt; ¹ Damascus; ¹ ² Syria; ¹ India; ¹ ² Cappadocia. ²

Amethyst

Amethyst is a purple or violet stone with shades from deep wine-colour to pale rose. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ It checks drunkenness and evil thoughts, strengthens understanding, protects against poison and serpents, and bears virtues in medicine, hunting, warfare, and engraved images. ⁶

Recorded localities: India, Libya and its coast, Arabia, Cyprus, Egypt, Armenia, Galatia, and Tarsus. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Open the gem article and its source references

Amites

Amites is an alum- or nitre-colored stone, harder than alum. ¹ It comes from Egypt and Arabia; ¹ its Ethiopian variety is green and gives a milky appearance when dissolved in water. ¹

Recorded localities: Egypt and Arabia; ¹ the green variety comes from Ethiopia.

Arabica

Arabica, also called Arabus, is an ivory-colored stone named for Arabia, where it is chiefly found. ¹ It is also found in Egypt, where it takes on the color and odor of myrtle and serves as a scent; ² when burnt, it makes good toothpaste. It is also good for nerve pain. ²

Recorded localities: Chiefly Arabia; also Egypt. ¹ ²

Aromatitis

Aromatitis is found in Arabia and Egypt. ¹ It has the color and scent of myrrh, from which its name is derived.

Recorded localities: Arabia and Egypt. ¹

Basanites

Basanites, also called Basaltem, is an iron-colored stone found in Egypt and Ethiopia. ¹ When ground in water, it produces a saffron-like color. ¹

Recorded localities: Egypt and Ethiopia. ¹

Chalcosmaragdos

Chalcosmaragdos is a green stone mottled with copper-colored veins. ¹ Its name recalls Greek χαλκός (chalkos), “copper.” It is found in Egypt and on the island of Cyprus.

Recorded localities: Egypt and the island of Cyprus. ¹

Crisites

Crisites is an oyster-colored stone found in Egypt. ¹ It is also called Philoginos. ¹

Recorded localities: Egypt. ¹

Emerald

The emerald is intensely green and translucent; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ some are smooth and clear enough to reflect an image. ⁵ Its virtues strengthen sight and memory, assist chastity and eloquence, increase wealth, and defend against illness, poison, demons, and tempests. ¹ ⁶

Recorded localities: Scythia, Britain, the Nile, India, Ethiopia, Phison, the Red Sea, Emerald Island, Beronike, Pelmi, Bactria, Egypt, Carchedonia, Persia, and Babylon. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Galactite

Galactite, also called Galactites, Galactitis, Galaricides, Milkstone, and by several other names, is an ash-colored stone that yields a milk-tasting fluid when ground. ¹ It comes from the Nile and Achelous rivers, and promotes milk in nursing women and ewes. ¹ Bound to the thigh, it eases childbirth; ² ³ held in the mouth until it melts, it disturbs the mind and memory. ² ⁴ A related form called Galatides or Gelatia is white, acorn-shaped, hard as Diamond, and heals feverish illnesses. ⁵

Recorded localities: The Nile and Achelous rivers; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ another account also names the Athaleus and Egypt. ⁴ ⁶ ⁷

Open the gem article and its source references

Haematite

Haematite, also called Hematite, Ematites, Emathitis, Emathites, Sanguineus Lapis, or Sedeneg, is a blood-veined, iron-colored stone that turns blood-red when ground. ³ ⁴ It preserves rather than corrodes the body and stops bleeding; ¹ its many remedies treat the eyes, wounds, discharges, snakebite, and bladder stones. ²

Recorded localities: Ethiopia, Arabia, Afriia, Egypt, Babylonia, Spain, Libya, Phrygia, and Germany. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Ligyrion

Ligyrion is the name of the seventh stone on the priestly breastplate, though its identity is uncertain. It may be called Lungurion or Lingurion; ¹ Lingoros and Lanouros are proposed only if these names refer to Ligyrion. The described stone has yellow points and a brighter, nobler vein within it. ¹ Its place is first said to be unknown, then tentatively associated with Upper Thebes and the temple of Asclepius near Memphis. It may be the Hyacinth, but that identification is also uncertain.

Recorded localities: Not known with certainty; ¹ tentatively associated with Upper Thebes and the temple of Asclepius near Memphis. ¹

Marble

Marble (Marmor) is a noble stone in many colors, prized for its speckles and varied markings. Its kinds come from quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, and they take names from their colors, places, or patrons. ¹ ² ³ Among them, Parian Marble is exceptionally white, comes from the island of Paros, and preserves spices and ointments. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: Marble kinds are found in quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, including Lacedemonia, Egypt, Ethiopia, Asia, Paros, Syene, Thebes, the Cyclades, Thasos, Numidia, Melos, Chios, Arabia, Germany, and Carrara. ¹ ² ³

Memphitis

Memphitis, also called Memphites, is a gem from Memphis in Egypt. ¹ ² It makes the body insensible to pain before cutting, cautery, or amputation; mixed in a drink or ground with vinegar and applied to the limb, it numbs the place. ² ³ Its virtue is said to exceed opium.

Recorded localities: Memphis, Egypt. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵

Open the gem article and its source references

Nitrum

Nitrum, also called Niter, is a clear, pale salt-like substance that approaches the solidity of stone, though it is not itself a stone. ¹ It dissolves and attracts, remedies jaundice, and when kindled in instruments of war it expands violently to hurl stones. ²

Recorded localities: Made from the earth’s salts in places where animals and people have urinated. ² ³

Open the gem article and its source references

Prassius

Prassius is a green stone, often the matrix of the Emerald, and is found in Ethiopia near the Nile. ¹ ² It has dark or translucent green forms, sometimes marked with blood-red drops or white lines. It benefits the sight and shares virtues with Jasper and emerald. ¹ An image of a bull carved upon it is useful for hexes and gives mercy in judgments.

Recorded localities: Ethiopia near the Nile. ² ³

Open the gem article and its source references

Sard

Sard is a red, sometimes translucent stone associated with Sardis. ¹ ² ³ Its kinds differ in transparency and brightness; it is said to gladden the spirit, sharpen the mind, restrain bleeding, and counter the harmful influence of Onyx. The name is disputed: One account calls carnelian “sardius,” while one account explicitly distinguishes sardius from carnelian; see Carnelian. ⁴ ⁵

Recorded localities: Named for Sardis. ¹ ³ One account lists five regional kinds: Babylonian, Indian, Arabian, Egyptian, and Cyprian; ⁴ it may occur embedded in other mined stones like a heart. ⁶ ⁷

Alexandria

Asius

Asius is white and light as pumice. ¹ It tastes of salt when licked and crumbles easily when squeezed. ¹ Imported from Alexandria, it heals consumptives when taken with pink sugar as an electuary, and it heals scrofula, fistulas, and gout. ¹

Recorded localities: Imported from Alexandria. ¹

Magnet

The magnet is iron-colored, or sometimes blue, and draws iron to itself, passing its power to the iron so that rings or needles may hang one from another. ² ⁷ It may guide a floating needle and compass toward the heavens, but garlic and Diamond oppose its attraction. ⁵ Certain kinds repel iron, attract human flesh, or are themselves drawn to iron. ¹ ³ ⁵

Recorded localities: India and the Indian Ocean, the country of the Troglodytes, Franconia, Ethiopia, Macedonia, Antioch, Alexandria, and Asia. ¹ ² ⁵ ⁸ ⁹ ¹⁰
