Agate

Agate is a many-coloured stone: some are black with white bands or circles, others bear yellow veins, blood-like spots, coral-red colours, or figures of woods and beasts. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ Its virtues include healing venomous bites, preserving sight, strengthening the wearer, and averting tempests; ⁴ ⁶ these virtues belong to particular forms and preparations, as set out below.

Recorded localities: Sicily, Crete, India, Scythia, Egypt, Persia, Arabia, and Cyprus; ³ some forms are tied to particular places. ² ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Amethyst

Amethyst is a purple or violet stone with shades from deep wine-colour to pale rose. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ It checks drunkenness and evil thoughts, strengthens understanding, protects against poison and serpents, and bears virtues in medicine, hunting, warfare, and engraved images. ⁶

Recorded localities: India, Libya and its coast, Arabia, Cyprus, Egypt, Armenia, Galatia, and Tarsus. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Chalcosmaragdos

Chalcosmaragdos is a green stone mottled with copper-colored veins. ¹ Its name recalls Greek χαλκός (chalkos), “copper.” It is found in Egypt and on the island of Cyprus.

Recorded localities: Egypt and the island of Cyprus. ¹

Crystal

Crystal, also called rock crystal, quartz, crystallus, or cristallum, is clear and shining like ice or water. ² ⁵ It is formed by cold in some accounts and within the earth in others. ¹ ⁵ Set against sunlight, it kindles tinder; ⁴ it also eases thirst and serves in remedies for the eyes, throat, heart, stomach, and belly. ³

Recorded localities: Germany; ¹ Asia; ² ⁵ Cyprus; ² ⁵ the northern Alps; ² ⁵ Scythia; ⁵ the Ethiopian Alps; ⁵ and the Red Sea island of Meron off Arabia. ⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Diamond

The diamond is an exceedingly hard, bright stone, commonly small and crystal-clear or iron-coloured. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁹ It governs iron and the magnet, resists poison and sorcery, quiets fear and madness, and protects its bearer in contests and danger. ⁷ ⁸ The methods by which it may be cut or broken differ by kind.

Recorded localities: India, Arabia, Cyprus, Syria, Macedonia, Ethiopia, and Philippi. ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁹ ¹⁰ ¹¹

Heliotrope

Heliotrope, also called Eliotropia, Elitropia, Elitropus, or Bloodstone, is a green stone like Emerald, clouded and speckled with blood-red spots and veins. ¹ ² ⁴ Anointed with the juice of its namesake herb and set in water beneath the sun, it turns the sun blood-red, boils the water into mist, and brings rain. ¹ ³ It grants prophecy and long life, checks bleeding and poison, and, with its herb and secret rite, makes its bearer invisible. ¹ ³

Recorded localities: Ethiopia, Cyprus, Africa, India, and Libya; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ necromancers call it the Babylonian gem. ¹ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Malachite

Malachite, also called Melochites, Melonites, and Molochites, is a soft, opaque stone of deep green, like the Emerald, and is found in Arabia. ¹ ³ ⁴ It protects its bearer and guards infants from mischance, witchcraft, accidents, and hurtful phantasms. ³ ⁴ It is also valued for making seals. ²

Recorded localities: Arabia, Cyprus, and Persia; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ Arabia is named by several accounts. ⁵ ⁶

Morion

Morion is a very black, transparent stone found in Cyprus and France. ¹ It is used for gravestones. ¹

Recorded localities: Cyprus and France. ¹

Phrygius

Phrygius is a pale, medium-weight stone from Cyprus, like Punic earth. ¹ Three times heated and sprinkled with wine, it turns red and dyes cloth. ¹ Its binding virtue calms swollen flesh, heals growing and malignant ulcers, and stops the flux of the eyes. ¹ It has three forms: the pale earth-like stone, brass dross, and an artificial red stone made by calcining Pyrite.

Recorded localities: Cyprus. ¹

Sard

Sard is a red, sometimes translucent stone associated with Sardis. ¹ ² ³ Its kinds differ in transparency and brightness; it is said to gladden the spirit, sharpen the mind, restrain bleeding, and counter the harmful influence of Onyx. The name is disputed: One account calls carnelian “sardius,” while one account explicitly distinguishes sardius from carnelian; see Carnelian. ⁴ ⁵

Recorded localities: Named for Sardis. ¹ ³ One account lists five regional kinds: Babylonian, Indian, Arabian, Egyptian, and Cyprian; ⁴ it may occur embedded in other mined stones like a heart. ⁶ ⁷
