Bitumen

Bitumen comes in two forms: floating clumps gathered from Lake Dead Sea (also called Asphaltites) in Judea, and boiling mud that rises from the earth in Syria. ¹ The two condense into a dense, fire-related mass that resists water and iron and serves to caulk ships. ¹

Recorded localities: Lake Dead Sea (also called Asphaltites) in Judea; ¹ boiling from the earth in various places in Syria. ¹ ²

Cegolites

Cegolites, also called Cogolites, Cogolitus, Tegolitus, and Iudaicus, resembles an olive stone in color and size. ² Its shavings, or the ground stone, are dissolved in water and drunk to dissolve kidney or bladder stones; ¹ ² a proper quantity of water is also prescribed for strangury. ²

Recorded localities: Judea, where it is often found. ²

Haematite

Haematite, also called Hematite, Ematites, Emathitis, Emathites, Sanguineus Lapis, or Sedeneg, is a blood-veined, iron-colored stone that turns blood-red when ground. ³ ⁴ It preserves rather than corrodes the body and stops bleeding; ¹ its many remedies treat the eyes, wounds, discharges, snakebite, and bladder stones. ²

Recorded localities: Ethiopia, Arabia, Afriia, Egypt, Babylonia, Spain, Libya, Phrygia, and Germany. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Jewstone

Jewstone (Judaicus lapis) is white, about the size of an acorn, and marked with signs like letters, called grammata in Greek. ¹ It is frequently found in Judaea. ¹

Recorded localities: Judaea. ¹
