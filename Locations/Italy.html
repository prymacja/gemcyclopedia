Gems from the general area

Alabandicus

Alabandicus is black with a purple tinge. It melts in fire and is used in glassmaking to polish and whiten glass.

Recorded localities: Italy. ¹

Murra

Murra, also called Murrina and Murina, is formed from moisture or liquid compressed by heat. ¹ It appears in purple and white kinds, and in a mixture of purple, white, and fiery red colors that reflect like a rainbow. ¹ ² It is used to make beautiful vases.

Recorded localities: Parthia, especially Carmania. ¹ Murrhine vases were brought to Italy. ²

Obsidian

Obsidian is a black or green translucent stone like glass. ¹ ² Polished, it reflects shadowy images and is used in mirrors and gems. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: India, Italy (including Samnium), Spain, Libya, and Germany. ¹ ²

Sulfur

Sulfur, easily kindled by fire, comes chiefly from the Aeolian Islands between Sicily and Italy and is also dug elsewhere. ¹ Four kinds serve different ends: living sulfur is used by physicians, lump sulfur by fullers, liquid sulfur to whiten and soften wool, and a fourth kind for lamp wicks. ¹ The vapor of burning sulfur is said to cure the comitial sickness. ¹

Recorded localities: The Aeolian Islands between Sicily and Italy, and by excavation in other places. ¹

Veientana

Veientana is a black stone marked with white spots or lines. ¹ ² It is associated with Veii in Italy, though the same kind is also found elsewhere.

Recorded localities: Veii in Etruria; ¹ One account says it also occurs in many other places. ²

Aeolian Islands

Punicus

Punicus is found in the Aeolian Islands and has two species. ¹ The whitest is the most perfect and the heaviest. ¹ Burned, washed, and dried, the stone serves the eyes, cleanses ulcers, and helps them scab; taken before wine, it prevents drunkenness. ¹

Recorded localities: The Aeolian Islands. ¹

Carrara

Marble

Marble (Marmor) is a noble stone in many colors, prized for its speckles and varied markings. Its kinds come from quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, and they take names from their colors, places, or patrons. ¹ ² ³ Among them, Parian Marble is exceptionally white, comes from the island of Paros, and preserves spices and ointments. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: Marble kinds are found in quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, including Lacedemonia, Egypt, Ethiopia, Asia, Paros, Syene, Thebes, the Cyclades, Thasos, Numidia, Melos, Chios, Arabia, Germany, and Carrara. ¹ ² ³

Lucania

Sapphire

The sapphire shines in blue, azure, purple, and yellow forms. It cools heat and lust, heals the eyes and body, preserves health, and brings peace, devotion, and understanding. Prepared or engraved in particular ways, it also counters poison, bleeding, spirits, illness, and misfortune. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ The name Sirites also appears in accounts of Silenite, whose lunar properties are treated separately.

Recorded localities: India, Ethiopia, Media, Libya, and near Le Puy in Provence. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁹ ¹⁰

Sicily

Agate

Agate is a many-coloured stone: some are black with white bands or circles, others bear yellow veins, blood-like spots, coral-red colours, or figures of woods and beasts. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ Its virtues include healing venomous bites, preserving sight, strengthening the wearer, and averting tempests; ⁴ ⁶ these virtues belong to particular forms and preparations, as set out below.

Recorded localities: Sicily, Crete, India, Scythia, Egypt, Persia, Arabia, and Cyprus; ³ some forms are tied to particular places. ² ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Jet

Jet (Gagates, Kacabre) is usually black, smooth, and light, though it may also be yellow, citron-colored, or grey. ¹ ² ³ It is found in Sicily, Libya, Lycia, Britain, and along northern shores. ¹ It attracts straw when rubbed, burns with a strong odor, and its smoke and preparations are credited with many remedies and protections. A citron-colored kind is also called amber in one account; ⁴ this named kind is retained here as a form of jet, while Amber remains a separate article.

Recorded localities: Sicily and Britain; ² ⁴ also Libya, Lycia, England, and the northern coast of Teutonia. ¹ ⁵ ⁶
