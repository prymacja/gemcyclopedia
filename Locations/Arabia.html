Agate

Agate is a many-coloured stone: some are black with white bands or circles, others bear yellow veins, blood-like spots, coral-red colours, or figures of woods and beasts. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ Its virtues include healing venomous bites, preserving sight, strengthening the wearer, and averting tempests; ⁴ ⁶ these virtues belong to particular forms and preparations, as set out below.

Recorded localities: Sicily, Crete, India, Scythia, Egypt, Persia, Arabia, and Cyprus; ³ some forms are tied to particular places. ² ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Amethyst

Amethyst is a purple or violet stone with shades from deep wine-colour to pale rose. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ It checks drunkenness and evil thoughts, strengthens understanding, protects against poison and serpents, and bears virtues in medicine, hunting, warfare, and engraved images. ⁶

Recorded localities: India, Libya and its coast, Arabia, Cyprus, Egypt, Armenia, Galatia, and Tarsus. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Amites

Amites is an alum- or nitre-colored stone, harder than alum. ¹ It comes from Egypt and Arabia; ¹ its Ethiopian variety is green and gives a milky appearance when dissolved in water. ¹

Recorded localities: Egypt and Arabia; ¹ the green variety comes from Ethiopia.

Arabica

Arabica, also called Arabus, is an ivory-colored stone named for Arabia, where it is chiefly found. ¹ It is also found in Egypt, where it takes on the color and odor of myrtle and serves as a scent; ² when burnt, it makes good toothpaste. It is also good for nerve pain. ²

Recorded localities: Chiefly Arabia; also Egypt. ¹ ²

Aromatitis

Aromatitis is found in Arabia and Egypt. ¹ It has the color and scent of myrrh, from which its name is derived.

Recorded localities: Arabia and Egypt. ¹

Asbestos

The name Asbestos appears in two distinct traditions: an iron-colored stone that burns without being extinguished, and a fibrous stone identified with Amiantos that cannot be burned. ¹ ³ ⁴ ⁶ Related forms include the White Carbuncle or White Pebble, and the alum-colored Amiatus or Amiantus. ² ⁵ ⁷ ⁸ Their descriptions and properties are distinguished below.

Recorded localities: The flame-stone is found in Arabia and Arcadia; ¹ ³ ⁵ ⁶ Iscustos is found in Spain near Gades or the Gates of Hercules. ² ⁹ ¹⁰ ¹¹ ¹²

Aspilaten

Aspilaten is a black stone from Arabia, most often found in the nests of Arabian birds. ¹ Bound over the spleen with camel dung, it heals those suffering from spleen ailments. ¹

Recorded localities: Arabia, especially in the nests of Arabian birds. ¹

Crystal

Crystal, also called rock crystal, quartz, crystallus, or cristallum, is clear and shining like ice or water. ² ⁵ It is formed by cold in some accounts and within the earth in others. ¹ ⁵ Set against sunlight, it kindles tinder; ⁴ it also eases thirst and serves in remedies for the eyes, throat, heart, stomach, and belly. ³

Recorded localities: Germany; ¹ Asia; ² ⁵ Cyprus; ² ⁵ the northern Alps; ² ⁵ Scythia; ⁵ the Ethiopian Alps; ⁵ and the Red Sea island of Meron off Arabia. ⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Daemonius

Daemonius, also called Diamon or Daimon, is a double-colored stone like a rainbow and is named the Demon’s Bow. ¹ ² It heals fever, expels poison, and makes its bearer safe from enemies and victorious against them. ¹

Diamond

The diamond is an exceedingly hard, bright stone, commonly small and crystal-clear or iron-coloured. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁹ It governs iron and the magnet, resists poison and sorcery, quiets fear and madness, and protects its bearer in contests and danger. ⁷ ⁸ The methods by which it may be cut or broken differ by kind.

Recorded localities: India, Arabia, Cyprus, Syria, Macedonia, Ethiopia, and Philippi. ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁹ ¹⁰ ¹¹

Haematite

Haematite, also called Hematite, Ematites, Emathitis, Emathites, Sanguineus Lapis, or Sedeneg, is a blood-veined, iron-colored stone that turns blood-red when ground. ³ ⁴ It preserves rather than corrodes the body and stops bleeding; ¹ its many remedies treat the eyes, wounds, discharges, snakebite, and bladder stones. ²

Recorded localities: Ethiopia, Arabia, Afriia, Egypt, Babylonia, Spain, Libya, Phrygia, and Germany. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Iris

Iris, also called Rainbowstone or Iris crystal, is a hard, six-sided crystal that casts the shape and colors of a rainbow on a wall when sunlight strikes it indoors. ² ³ It is found by the Red Sea and in the mountains of Germany. ¹ ² ³ ⁴

Recorded localities: Arabia and the Red Sea; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ in Germany between the Rhine and Treves, including near the Rhine. ⁵ ⁶

Ligdinus

Ligdinus is a marvelously white stone, large as a cup or basin, first found in Arabia. Asia has a coralline kind no larger than two cubits and another kind of white like ivory. ¹

Recorded localities: First found in Arabia; ¹ Asia also produces two kinds.

Malachite

Malachite, also called Melochites, Melonites, and Molochites, is a soft, opaque stone of deep green, like the Emerald, and is found in Arabia. ¹ ³ ⁴ It protects its bearer and guards infants from mischance, witchcraft, accidents, and hurtful phantasms. ³ ⁴ It is also valued for making seals. ²

Recorded localities: Arabia, Cyprus, and Persia; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ Arabia is named by several accounts. ⁵ ⁶

Marble

Marble (Marmor) is a noble stone in many colors, prized for its speckles and varied markings. Its kinds come from quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, and they take names from their colors, places, or patrons. ¹ ² ³ Among them, Parian Marble is exceptionally white, comes from the island of Paros, and preserves spices and ointments. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: Marble kinds are found in quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, including Lacedemonia, Egypt, Ethiopia, Asia, Paros, Syene, Thebes, the Cyclades, Thasos, Numidia, Melos, Chios, Arabia, Germany, and Carrara. ¹ ² ³

Merochites

Merochites is a soft, strong-green stone, coarser in colour than Emerald, and is found in Arabia. It protects children from harmful events. ¹

Recorded localities: Arabia. ¹

Onyx

Onyx bears the appearance of a human nail: it may show a pale layer or veins against black, reddish, or other colored stone. ² ⁴ The traditions differ over its colors and kinds, naming five varieties in several accounts and a golden-yellow Onyx in another. ⁴ It is found in India, Arabia, and Media. ¹ ² ³ Worn, it brings sorrow, fear, troubling dreams, quarrels, and excessive saliva in children; Sard is said to restrain this harm. ¹

Recorded localities: Media and Arabia; ¹ ⁶ India and Arabia. ² ⁷ ⁸

Pearl

Pearl, also called Margarita, Unio, and Union, is a lustrous stone formed within shellfish and prized among the white gems. ¹ ² ⁴ Sea pearls are said to take their brightness and color from celestial dew, the sky, the season, and age; ² ³ ⁴ white and silvery kinds are most prized, while other pearls are yellow, reddish, or dark. ¹ ² A separate account describes pure pearls congealed from salty river waters. ⁵

Recorded localities: India and the Indian Ocean, Ceylon, Stoidis, Perimula, Arabia, the Persian Gulf and Red Sea, Britain and the British Sea, Flanders, Teutonia, the Moselle, and rivers in France. ¹ ³ A separate account places pearl formation in certain salty rivers. ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ¹⁴ ¹⁵

Sandastros

Sandastros, also called Sadasius and Garamantides, is a clear, fiery stone sprinkled with drops like gold. ¹ It resembles a star; the more brilliant its sparkle and the more shining drops within, the more valuable it is. ¹ Arabia yields it, and Chaldeans use it in ceremonies. ¹

Recorded localities: Arabia. ¹

Sard

Sard is a red, sometimes translucent stone associated with Sardis. ¹ ² ³ Its kinds differ in transparency and brightness; it is said to gladden the spirit, sharpen the mind, restrain bleeding, and counter the harmful influence of Onyx. The name is disputed: One account calls carnelian “sardius,” while one account explicitly distinguishes sardius from carnelian; see Carnelian. ⁴ ⁵

Recorded localities: Named for Sardis. ¹ ³ One account lists five regional kinds: Babylonian, Indian, Arabian, Egyptian, and Cyprian; ⁴ it may occur embedded in other mined stones like a heart. ⁶ ⁷

Sardonyx

Sardonyx is a layered stone formed from Sard and Onyx, with black, white, and red bands; ¹ ⁴ ⁵ some accounts add a chalcedonian layer. When the colors are clear and distinct, the stone is most prized. ⁶ It is found in India and Arabia and is especially valued for seals, from which wax releases cleanly. ² ⁴ The sources differ over its virtues: some say it restrains lust and strengthens the senses, while one account assigns it no magical power. ³

Recorded localities: India and Arabia; ¹ ⁴ ⁵ One account says it is revealed there by rushing water. ⁷ ⁸

Topaz

Topaz bears the names Topasion, Topatius, Topazius, Chrysolite, Chrysolithus, Chrysolitus, and Filacterium; it is sometimes called Chrysoberyl. Crisoletus, Crisolimus, Crisolensis, Crisolansis, Grisoletus, and Crisolitus belong to the same Chrysolitus tradition. Topaz is known in golden, gold-green, pale-green, and reddish forms. Some traditions identify Chrysolite with Topaz, while their varieties and origins are described differently. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ The red Indian Topazion and green Arabian Topazion are retained as distinct forms within this name tradition. ⁹ ¹⁰

Recorded localities: Ethiopia; ¹ ³ ⁷ ⁸ Babylon at the Achesenidic rock; ² Arabia and islands in the Red Sea; ⁵ ⁶ ¹⁰ Topazion accounts name an Indian city and an Arabian island. ⁹ ¹¹ ¹²
