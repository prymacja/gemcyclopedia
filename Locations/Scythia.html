Agate

Agate is a many-coloured stone: some are black with white bands or circles, others bear yellow veins, blood-like spots, coral-red colours, or figures of woods and beasts. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ Its virtues include healing venomous bites, preserving sight, strengthening the wearer, and averting tempests; ⁴ ⁶ these virtues belong to particular forms and preparations, as set out below.

Recorded localities: Sicily, Crete, India, Scythia, Egypt, Persia, Arabia, and Cyprus; ³ some forms are tied to particular places. ² ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Crystal

Crystal, also called rock crystal, quartz, crystallus, or cristallum, is clear and shining like ice or water. ² ⁵ It is formed by cold in some accounts and within the earth in others. ¹ ⁵ Set against sunlight, it kindles tinder; ⁴ it also eases thirst and serves in remedies for the eyes, throat, heart, stomach, and belly. ³

Recorded localities: Germany; ¹ Asia; ² ⁵ Cyprus; ² ⁵ the northern Alps; ² ⁵ Scythia; ⁵ the Ethiopian Alps; ⁵ and the Red Sea island of Meron off Arabia. ⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Cyaneus

Cyanica, also called Cyaneus, is a Scythian stone of cerulean blue with purple sparkles, golden drops, and sometimes shining spots of varied colors. It has male and female kinds; ¹ the male is clearer and purer, and its golden particles make it more pleasing.

Recorded localities: Scythia. ¹

Emerald

The emerald is intensely green and translucent; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ some are smooth and clear enough to reflect an image. ⁵ Its virtues strengthen sight and memory, assist chastity and eloquence, increase wealth, and defend against illness, poison, demons, and tempests. ¹ ⁶

Recorded localities: Scythia, Britain, the Nile, India, Ethiopia, Phison, the Red Sea, Emerald Island, Beronike, Pelmi, Bactria, Egypt, Carchedonia, Persia, and Babylon. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Hyacinth

Hyacinth, also called Iacinthus or the hyacinth-stone, shines in many colors, from blue and sea-green to pomegranate red and citron yellow. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ Its virtues strengthen the body, dispel sorrow, protect travelers, and, in its garnet-colored kind, resist fire; ⁶ it also quenches flame, eases childbirth, guards against poison, and treats troubled eyes and the heart.

Recorded localities: Scythia, Ethiopia, and Africa. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁷ ⁸

Lapis lazuli

Lapis lazuli, also called Zemech, Zumemellazuli, and Lazolus, is a blue stone gleaming with gold flecks. ³ Cyanea is a Scythian kind, either pure blue or flecked with gold. ² Lapis lazuli yields azure pigment and is a remedy for melancholic illnesses. ¹ ³

Recorded localities: Scythia (Cyanea). ² ⁴

Liparea

Liparea, also called Lippares or Liparia, draws wild animals. ⁴ Burned, it calls them; ¹ carried in the woods, it brings game to a hunter’s spear without hounds or snares. ² Hunted beasts run to it for protection. ³ One account says the beasts cannot be seen by dogs or hunters while they gaze upon the stone, but marks this marvel as uncertain.

Recorded localities: Scythia and Libya. ² ³ ⁴ ⁵
