Gems from the general area

Galactite

Galactite, also called Galactites, Galactitis, Galaricides, Milkstone, and by several other names, is an ash-colored stone that yields a milk-tasting fluid when ground. ¹ It comes from the Nile and Achelous rivers, and promotes milk in nursing women and ewes. ¹ Bound to the thigh, it eases childbirth; ² ³ held in the mouth until it melts, it disturbs the mind and memory. ² ⁴ A related form called Galatides or Gelatia is white, acorn-shaped, hard as Diamond, and heals feverish illnesses. ⁵

Recorded localities: The Nile and Achelous rivers; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ another account also names the Athaleus and Egypt. ⁴ ⁶ ⁷

Asbestos

The name Asbestos appears in two distinct traditions: an iron-colored stone that burns without being extinguished, and a fibrous stone identified with Amiantos that cannot be burned. ¹ ³ ⁴ ⁶ Related forms include the White Carbuncle or White Pebble, and the alum-colored Amiatus or Amiantus. ² ⁵ ⁷ ⁸ Their descriptions and properties are distinguished below.

Recorded localities: The flame-stone is found in Arabia and Arcadia; ¹ ³ ⁵ ⁶ Iscustos is found in Spain near Gades or the Gates of Hercules. ² ⁹ ¹⁰ ¹¹ ¹²

Chios

Marble

Marble (Marmor) is a noble stone in many colors, prized for its speckles and varied markings. Its kinds come from quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, and they take names from their colors, places, or patrons. ¹ ² ³ Among them, Parian Marble is exceptionally white, comes from the island of Paros, and preserves spices and ointments. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: Marble kinds are found in quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, including Lacedemonia, Egypt, Ethiopia, Asia, Paros, Syene, Thebes, the Cyclades, Thasos, Numidia, Melos, Chios, Arabia, Germany, and Carrara. ¹ ² ³

Corinth

Effesstis

Effesstis, also called Effestites, reflects images like a mirror. In water it becomes lukewarm; placed beneath the sun, it ignites combustible matter. It is found in Corinth. ¹

Recorded localities: Corinth. ¹

Epistrites

Epistrites, also called Epistides, is a glittering red stone found in the sea and at Corinth. ¹ ² Fastened over the heart with charms, it protects its bearer from misfortune; it also drives away locusts, harmful birds, and storms from the fields.

Recorded localities: The sea; ¹ Corinth. ² ³

Hephaestite

Hephaestite, also called Hephaestitis, is a ruddy, mirror-like stone that reflects images in red. ¹ Cast into boiling water, it stills the bubbling and cools the water; ² set in sunlight, it emits fiery rays and kindles dry matter. It protects crops from storms and blight, and guards its wearer from danger when worn over the heart.

Recorded localities: The Isthmus of Corinth; ² Corycus. ¹

Crete

Agate

Agate is a many-coloured stone: some are black with white bands or circles, others bear yellow veins, blood-like spots, coral-red colours, or figures of woods and beasts. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ Its virtues include healing venomous bites, preserving sight, strengthening the wearer, and averting tempests; ⁴ ⁶ these virtues belong to particular forms and preparations, as set out below.

Recorded localities: Sicily, Crete, India, Scythia, Egypt, Persia, Arabia, and Cyprus; ³ some forms are tied to particular places. ² ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Dactylus of Ida

The Dactylus of Ida, also called Idaeus, is an iron-colored stone from Crete, shaped like a human thumb. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: Crete, at Mount Ida. ¹ ²

Philippi

Diamond

The diamond is an exceedingly hard, bright stone, commonly small and crystal-clear or iron-coloured. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁹ It governs iron and the magnet, resists poison and sorcery, quiets fear and madness, and protects its bearer in contests and danger. ⁷ ⁸ The methods by which it may be cut or broken differ by kind.

Recorded localities: India, Arabia, Cyprus, Syria, Macedonia, Ethiopia, and Philippi. ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁹ ¹⁰ ¹¹

Samos

Samius

Samius is a white, heavy, fragile stone from Samos, used to polish gold. ² Taken in drink, it cures dizziness, calms the mind, and prevents miscarriage. Ground with milk and laid over the eyes, it treats eye ailments; carried in the left hand, it stops long weeping. Yet if bound to a woman’s hand during childbirth, it hinders the birth and keeps the child in the womb. ¹

Recorded localities: Samos. ¹ ² ³
