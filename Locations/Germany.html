Amethyst

Amethyst is a purple or violet stone with shades from deep wine-colour to pale rose. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ It checks drunkenness and evil thoughts, strengthens understanding, protects against poison and serpents, and bears virtues in medicine, hunting, warfare, and engraved images. ⁶

Recorded localities: India, Libya and its coast, Arabia, Cyprus, Egypt, Armenia, Galatia, and Tarsus. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Callaica

Callaica, also called Callayca or Caldaicus, is a pale green, cloudy stone that appears more pleasing when set in gold. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: Icy cliffs in India and Germany; ¹ also on the bank of a cold, icy river in Media and Germany. ²

Carnelian

Carnelian is a red stone, often transparent, and is valued for its powers against bleeding and discord. ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵ The name is linked with “sardius” in one naming tradition, while another account says that Sardius is darker and duller and is not the same stone; ³ see Sard.

Recorded localities: Sand; ² the Orient; ⁵ and the Rhine region. One account says it was first found by the Sardinians. ³ ⁶ ⁷

Ceranius

Ceranius, also called Cerraolus or Ombria, is a pyramidal stone that falls from clouds near lightning. ¹ Germany has the finest kind; Spain’s is next and shines like fire. It has crystalline saffron-colored and pyrite-colored kinds, while its black form is disputed. ¹ Worn, it prevents drowning, lightning, and tornadoes, grants victory in legal cases and battles, and makes dreams sweet and pleasant. ¹

Recorded localities: Falls from clouds near lightning; ¹ Germany has the finest kind, followed by Spain.

Ceraunium

Ceraunium has two kinds: a transparent blue stone from Germany that catches starlight beneath the open sky, and a fiery, reddish-bronze stone from the Lusitanian shore that guards against lightning. ¹ Its name is linked to Greek κεραυνός (keraunos), “lightning-bolt.” ¹

Recorded localities: Germany; the Lusitanian shore of Spain, near a place struck by lightning. ¹ ² ³

Chalcedony

Chalcedony, also called Chalcedonius, Calcedonius, or Calcedon, is a lustrous stone whose colors range from dark or Sapphire-like to dusky white and dull vermilion. ⁴ Pierced and worn, it aids success in causes and preserves the body’s strength; ¹ ² ³ each kind curbs lust and guards against tempests and adversity. ⁴

Recorded localities: Especially fine stones come from Ethiopia; varieties are found in Germany and on the Adriatic shore. ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Crystal

Crystal, also called rock crystal, quartz, crystallus, or cristallum, is clear and shining like ice or water. ² ⁵ It is formed by cold in some accounts and within the earth in others. ¹ ⁵ Set against sunlight, it kindles tinder; ⁴ it also eases thirst and serves in remedies for the eyes, throat, heart, stomach, and belly. ³

Recorded localities: Germany; ¹ Asia; ² ⁵ Cyprus; ² ⁵ the northern Alps; ² ⁵ Scythia; ⁵ the Ethiopian Alps; ⁵ and the Red Sea island of Meron off Arabia. ⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Daemonius

Daemonius, also called Diamon or Daimon, is a double-colored stone like a rainbow and is named the Demon’s Bow. ¹ ² It heals fever, expels poison, and makes its bearer safe from enemies and victorious against them. ¹

Draconites

Draconites, also called Dracontites, Dracontis, Snakestone, Dentrites, Draconius, Obsianus, or Vespertin Kimedius, is a gem taken from the head or brain of a living serpent or dragon. ² ³ It is described as translucent white in one account, while other accounts call it black, dark, mirror-bright, or crystalline. ² It is sought in the East; one stone was found beneath a serpent in Swabia. ¹ It dispels poison and is said to grant victory, courage, and invincibility.

Recorded localities: Taken from the head or brain of a living serpent or dragon. ³ It is brought from the East; ¹ ² one specimen was found beneath a serpent in Swabia, Germany. ⁴

Haematite

Haematite, also called Hematite, Ematites, Emathitis, Emathites, Sanguineus Lapis, or Sedeneg, is a blood-veined, iron-colored stone that turns blood-red when ground. ³ ⁴ It preserves rather than corrodes the body and stops bleeding; ¹ its many remedies treat the eyes, wounds, discharges, snakebite, and bladder stones. ²

Recorded localities: Ethiopia, Arabia, Afriia, Egypt, Babylonia, Spain, Libya, Phrygia, and Germany. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Iris

Iris, also called Rainbowstone or Iris crystal, is a hard, six-sided crystal that casts the shape and colors of a rainbow on a wall when sunlight strikes it indoors. ² ³ It is found by the Red Sea and in the mountains of Germany. ¹ ² ³ ⁴

Recorded localities: Arabia and the Red Sea; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ in Germany between the Rhine and Treves, including near the Rhine. ⁵ ⁶

Jasper

Jasper, also called Jaspis or Iaspis, has many colors; ² ³ ⁴ ⁶ its most prized kind is translucent green with red veins and should be set in silver. ¹ ⁵ ⁶ It checks bleeding and fever, aids childbirth, and drives away harmful phantasms. Engraved figures carry further protections and powers.

Recorded localities: The Thermodon River (also called Thermodontos) and near Amatunt; ² Phrygia, Iberia, Hyrcania, Caspian lands, and Germany. It is also found in many places. ¹ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Jet

Jet (Gagates, Kacabre) is usually black, smooth, and light, though it may also be yellow, citron-colored, or grey. ¹ ² ³ It is found in Sicily, Libya, Lycia, Britain, and along northern shores. ¹ It attracts straw when rubbed, burns with a strong odor, and its smoke and preparations are credited with many remedies and protections. A citron-colored kind is also called amber in one account; ⁴ this named kind is retained here as a form of jet, while Amber remains a separate article.

Recorded localities: Sicily and Britain; ² ⁴ also Libya, Lycia, England, and the northern coast of Teutonia. ¹ ⁵ ⁶

Ligurius

Ligurius, also called Lyncurius, Lyncurium, Lyngurium, and Ligure, forms when lynx urine hardens into stone. ² ³ ⁹ ¹⁰ It is usually yellow or brown, though red, dark saffron, and green kinds are also described. Rubbed or warmed, it attracts straw and leaves. ¹ ² ⁴ The green kind heals stomach pains, jaundice, bleeding, and the king’s evil. ³ ⁵

The lynx hides its urine beneath the sand, jealous that people might use the precious stone it forms. ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Marble

Marble (Marmor) is a noble stone in many colors, prized for its speckles and varied markings. Its kinds come from quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, and they take names from their colors, places, or patrons. ¹ ² ³ Among them, Parian Marble is exceptionally white, comes from the island of Paros, and preserves spices and ointments. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: Marble kinds are found in quarries, cliffs, and underground deposits, including Lacedemonia, Egypt, Ethiopia, Asia, Paros, Syene, Thebes, the Cyclades, Thasos, Numidia, Melos, Chios, Arabia, Germany, and Carrara. ¹ ² ³

Obsidian

Obsidian is a black or green translucent stone like glass. ¹ ² Polished, it reflects shadowy images and is used in mirrors and gems. ¹ ²

Recorded localities: India, Italy (including Samnium), Spain, Libya, and Germany. ¹ ²

Ophites

Ophites is a kind of Marble marked with serpentine spots. ¹ It has a soft white kind and a hard dark kind, the latter greenish and sprinkled with yellow spots. ¹ It is found in Germany and is worked into vessels, building decoration, and roofing planks. Hung by the neck, it heals headaches and serpent bites; ¹ its white kind restores the insane and lethargic. ¹

Recorded localities: Germany. ¹

Pearl

Pearl, also called Margarita, Unio, and Union, is a lustrous stone formed within shellfish and prized among the white gems. ¹ ² ⁴ Sea pearls are said to take their brightness and color from celestial dew, the sky, the season, and age; ² ³ ⁴ white and silvery kinds are most prized, while other pearls are yellow, reddish, or dark. ¹ ² A separate account describes pure pearls congealed from salty river waters. ⁵

Recorded localities: India and the Indian Ocean, Ceylon, Stoidis, Perimula, Arabia, the Persian Gulf and Red Sea, Britain and the British Sea, Flanders, Teutonia, the Moselle, and rivers in France. ¹ ³ A separate account places pearl formation in certain salty rivers. ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ¹⁴ ¹⁵

Phengites

Phengites is a hard, white, translucent stone associated with Cappadocia and the power to admit light through closed buildings. ¹ A source tradition identifies Specularis, also called Mirrorstone, with Phengites or Fongites; ² their other accounts describe glass-like sheets, three colored forms, and a red kind that eases bodily aches and anger when carried in the left hand. ³

Recorded localities: Cappadocia; ³ around Segovia or Segorbe in Spain; ¹ Teutonia; ² France; ² and Persia. ³

Thunderstone

Thunderstone, also called Ceraurum or Ceraunias, is cast from clashing clouds or found where lightning strikes the ground. ² It resembles crystal tinted blue and red; Spanish stones glow like fire. ¹ Worn in purity, it protects against lightning, and guards homes, farms, and ships from lightning and storms. ² It also confounds enemies in law and battle and brings sweet sleep. ²

Recorded localities: Germany and Spain; ¹ another account also names Germania and Lusitania, and says the stone is found where lightning strikes. ²

Magnet

The magnet is iron-colored, or sometimes blue, and draws iron to itself, passing its power to the iron so that rings or needles may hang one from another. ² ⁷ It may guide a floating needle and compass toward the heavens, but garlic and Diamond oppose its attraction. ⁵ Certain kinds repel iron, attract human flesh, or are themselves drawn to iron. ¹ ³ ⁵

Recorded localities: India and the Indian Ocean, the country of the Troglodytes, Franconia, Ethiopia, Macedonia, Antioch, Alexandria, and Asia. ¹ ² ⁵ ⁸ ⁹ ¹⁰
