Androdamas

The names Androdamas, Androdamantus, Androdamanta, Andromanta, Andromadas, Androa, and Andromantes are preserved in two differing forms. One is silver-white, cubical, and found by the Red Sea; ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵ the other is black, heavy, and hard, chiefly from Africa. ¹ ³ The first calms anger and passionate impulses; ¹ the second attracts metals and turns blood-red when ground or rubbed in water. ¹ ³

Recorded localities: The silver-white form is found in sand by the Red Sea; ¹ ² ⁴ ⁵ the black form is chiefly found in Africa. ³ ⁶

Chrysoprase

Chrysoprase is green with golden spots, its color compared to leek or cypress juice. ¹ ² ⁴ One kind comes from India; ³ another from Ethiopia is hidden by daylight and fiery at night. ⁵

Recorded localities: India, Ethiopia, and Africa. ¹ ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Coral

Coral is a branching growth of the sea. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ Green and soft beneath the water, it hardens in the air and is commonly red, though white and black kinds are also known. It protects against storms and lightning, and it brings fruitfulness to fields and trees. ¹ ⁴

Recorded localities: The sea, especially around Marseilles. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Crystal

Crystal, also called rock crystal, quartz, crystallus, or cristallum, is clear and shining like ice or water. ² ⁵ It is formed by cold in some accounts and within the earth in others. ¹ ⁵ Set against sunlight, it kindles tinder; ⁴ it also eases thirst and serves in remedies for the eyes, throat, heart, stomach, and belly. ³

Recorded localities: Germany; ¹ Asia; ² ⁵ Cyprus; ² ⁵ the northern Alps; ² ⁵ Scythia; ⁵ the Ethiopian Alps; ⁵ and the Red Sea island of Meron off Arabia. ⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Daemonius

Daemonius, also called Diamon or Daimon, is a double-colored stone like a rainbow and is named the Demon’s Bow. ¹ ² It heals fever, expels poison, and makes its bearer safe from enemies and victorious against them. ¹

Emerald

The emerald is intensely green and translucent; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ some are smooth and clear enough to reflect an image. ⁵ Its virtues strengthen sight and memory, assist chastity and eloquence, increase wealth, and defend against illness, poison, demons, and tempests. ¹ ⁶

Recorded localities: Scythia, Britain, the Nile, India, Ethiopia, Phison, the Red Sea, Emerald Island, Beronike, Pelmi, Bactria, Egypt, Carchedonia, Persia, and Babylon. ¹ ² ³ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸

Haematite

Haematite, also called Hematite, Ematites, Emathitis, Emathites, Sanguineus Lapis, or Sedeneg, is a blood-veined, iron-colored stone that turns blood-red when ground. ³ ⁴ It preserves rather than corrodes the body and stops bleeding; ¹ its many remedies treat the eyes, wounds, discharges, snakebite, and bladder stones. ²

Recorded localities: Ethiopia, Arabia, Afriia, Egypt, Babylonia, Spain, Libya, Phrygia, and Germany. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶

Hyacinth

Hyacinth, also called Iacinthus or the hyacinth-stone, shines in many colors, from blue and sea-green to pomegranate red and citron yellow. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ Its virtues strengthen the body, dispel sorrow, protect travelers, and, in its garnet-colored kind, resist fire; ⁶ it also quenches flame, eases childbirth, guards against poison, and treats troubled eyes and the heart.

Recorded localities: Scythia, Ethiopia, and Africa. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁷ ⁸

Iris

Iris, also called Rainbowstone or Iris crystal, is a hard, six-sided crystal that casts the shape and colors of a rainbow on a wall when sunlight strikes it indoors. ² ³ It is found by the Red Sea and in the mountains of Germany. ¹ ² ³ ⁴

Recorded localities: Arabia and the Red Sea; ¹ ² ³ ⁴ in Germany between the Rhine and Treves, including near the Rhine. ⁵ ⁶

Pearl

Pearl, also called Margarita, Unio, and Union, is a lustrous stone formed within shellfish and prized among the white gems. ¹ ² ⁴ Sea pearls are said to take their brightness and color from celestial dew, the sky, the season, and age; ² ³ ⁴ white and silvery kinds are most prized, while other pearls are yellow, reddish, or dark. ¹ ² A separate account describes pure pearls congealed from salty river waters. ⁵

Recorded localities: India and the Indian Ocean, Ceylon, Stoidis, Perimula, Arabia, the Persian Gulf and Red Sea, Britain and the British Sea, Flanders, Teutonia, the Moselle, and rivers in France. ¹ ³ A separate account places pearl formation in certain salty rivers. ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ¹⁴ ¹⁵

Topaz

Topaz bears the names Topasion, Topatius, Topazius, Chrysolite, Chrysolithus, Chrysolitus, and Filacterium; it is sometimes called Chrysoberyl. Crisoletus, Crisolimus, Crisolensis, Crisolansis, Grisoletus, and Crisolitus belong to the same Chrysolitus tradition. Topaz is known in golden, gold-green, pale-green, and reddish forms. Some traditions identify Chrysolite with Topaz, while their varieties and origins are described differently. ¹ ² ³ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ The red Indian Topazion and green Arabian Topazion are retained as distinct forms within this name tradition. ⁹ ¹⁰

Recorded localities: Ethiopia; ¹ ³ ⁷ ⁸ Babylon at the Achesenidic rock; ² Arabia and islands in the Red Sea; ⁵ ⁶ ¹⁰ Topazion accounts name an Indian city and an Arabian island. ⁹ ¹¹ ¹²

Keraunios

Keraunios, also called Keronion or Karchedonios, is described as slightly resembling Ruby. It is called wine-like Keronion, shares Ruby’s reported place of discovery near Karchedon in Libya, and is said to be “not delightful.” The Greek adjective Καρχηδόνιος means “Carthaginian,” which makes a derivation from Καρχηδών (Carthage) plausible; the connection to the gem name remains uncertain. ² No independent description of the stone is known beyond this passage. ¹

Ruby

Ruby, also called Carbuncle, Anthrax, Rubinus, and Sparks, is a hard, clear red stone whose fiery rays shine like a coal in the darkness. ¹ ³ ⁵ ⁶ A noble ruby glows by night; ² ³ ⁴ its radiance can reveal it even through clothing. Libya and the land of the Troglodytes yield carbuncles, while other accounts place ruby in Karchedon and the river Phison. ²

Recorded localities: Libya, among the Troglodytes; ¹ ³ ⁶ Karchedon in Africa; ² India; and the river Phison. ² ⁷ ⁸
