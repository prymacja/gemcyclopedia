Bitumen

Bitumen comes in two forms: floating clumps gathered from Lake Dead Sea (also called Asphaltites) in Judea, and boiling mud that rises from the earth in Syria. ¹ The two condense into a dense, fire-related mass that resists water and iron and serves to caulk ships. ¹

Recorded localities: Lake Dead Sea (also called Asphaltites) in Judea; ¹ boiling from the earth in various places in Syria. ¹ ²

Syrius

Syrius, also written Sirius, is named for Syria. ¹ While whole, it floats in water; ² ³ once cut, it sinks because the air within escapes. ²

Magnet

The magnet is iron-colored, or sometimes blue, and draws iron to itself, passing its power to the iron so that rings or needles may hang one from another. ² ⁷ It may guide a floating needle and compass toward the heavens, but garlic and Diamond oppose its attraction. ⁵ Certain kinds repel iron, attract human flesh, or are themselves drawn to iron. ¹ ³ ⁵

Recorded localities: India and the Indian Ocean, the country of the Troglodytes, Franconia, Ethiopia, Macedonia, Antioch, Alexandria, and Asia. ¹ ² ⁵ ⁸ ⁹ ¹⁰

Alabaster, also called Nicomar, is a shining white stone of marble kind. ¹ Its coldness preserves aromatic ointments and keeps corpses from becoming foul-smelling. It gives victory and preserves friendship. ¹

Garnet

Garnet, or Granatus, is a transparent red stone of the Carbuncle family, like the flower of the wild pomegranate. ¹ ² Set in a seal ring with black beneath it, it shines more brightly. It gladdens the heart and dispels sorrow; ¹ some also say it protects its bearer from plague. ²

Recorded localities: Mostly Ethiopia; ¹ ² sometimes in the sands of the sea near Tyre. ¹ ² ³
